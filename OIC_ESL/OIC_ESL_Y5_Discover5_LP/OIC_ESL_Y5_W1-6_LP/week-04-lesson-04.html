PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Show one or two short video(s) or several photos showing dentistry in the past. Search online for ‘dentistry historical photos’. Ask: What was the dentist like in the past? Why did they use these methods? Would you like to go to a dentist then? Show a short video or several photos showing the future of dentistry, focusing on virtual reality and technological developments. Search online for ‘future dentist virtual reality’. Ask: Would you like to go to the dentist in the future? Why? Explain that the link between this activity and the main activity is Past and Future. Children will learn new grammar skills so that they can talk about what they could or couldn’t do in the past and will or won’t be able to do in the future.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Teach modals of ability using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.33. In the past, I could / couldn’t ... Today, I can / can’t ... In the future, I will be able to / won’t be able to ...

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Discuss the meaning of ‘achievement’ and ‘aspiration’. Allow children to volunteer examples from their own lives. Children create a timeline on their whiteboard or on paper showing the following points: baby – 2 years old – [age now] years old – adult For each point on the timeline, ask children to add a positive and a negative statement using modals of ability. Model an example on the board.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into pairs. Ask them to read their sentences to their partner and check each other’s work. Take feedback. Tell children they should praise their partner’s hard work, whether they were right or wrong. Ask: What did your partner do well? Guide them to responses such as good effort or clear pronunciation rather than correct answers.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Encourage children to make some of their sentences about teeth and eating. Divide children into pairs. Ask them to read their sentences to their partner and check each other’s work. Take feedback. Tell children they should praise their partner’s hard work, whether they were right or wrong. Ask: What did your partner do well? Guide them to responses such as good effort or clear pronunciation rather than correct answers. Explore the unit topic further using modals of ability. Ask: What will you be able to do when you are older if you look after your teeth? What won’t you be able to do if you don’t? Could you brush your own teeth when you were a young child? When did you learn?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide sentence halves for them to match and place on their timeline: e.g. When I was a baby / I couldn’t* brush my teeth. *this word could be left blank Challenge more able children by asking them to produce more complex sentences: e.g. I couldn’t chew my food when I was a baby because I didn’t have any teeth. If I brush my teeth every day, I will be able to keep my teeth healthy.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the grammar practice in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.26–27. Create ‘My life story’ leaflets with three sections: Past achievements, Current abilities, and Future aspirations. Use modals of ability and illustrate the text.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to use modals of ability and practised them in writing and in speech, linking them to past and future events in their life.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
