PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Run a two-minute wellbeing survey. Ask: Are you feeling good? Are you tired? Are you hungry? Are you ready to learn? Do you feel calm? As you ask each question, perform an action which children must copy if they answer ‘Yes’ (such as tap your head, pat your stomach, close one eye). Then take a minute to close your eyes and breathe slowly. Remind children that they learn best when they have slept well, eaten well, and come with a positive attitude to learning. Explain to children that they are going to role-play interviewers and naturalists, talking about the naturalist’s life working with tigers. They are going to use questions and answers to practise a variety of different tenses that they know. By thinking again about tigers, they are also helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Review the simple present, present continous, and simple past tenses using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.21. Briefly revise vocabulary used for questions and opinions. Oxford Discover SB 5 p.22 ex.C can be used for reference.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Write ‘Who’, ‘When’, ‘Why’, ‘What’, ‘Where’, and ‘How’ on the board. As a class, write down possible questions for the interview using the opening words. Can children think of questions using all the tenses? Model answers for some of the questions. Divide the class into pairs. Ask each pair to decide which child will be the interviewer, and which the interviewee.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide the class into pairs. Ask each pair to decide which child will be the interviewer, and which the interviewee.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask children to spend several minutes role-playing the interview. Then ask them to switch roles. If time allows, ask volunteers to share their role-play in front of the class. Take feedback from children. Ask: Which role did you enjoy more? Did you freeze, or did you enjoy the challenge? Would it be easier if you had time to plan your questions or answers before the interview?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident children, pair them with a more confident speaker who can act as a mentor to motivate and support them. To support less able children, provide them with some model questions and answers that they can use or adapt. Challenge more confident children by asking them to give more than one sentence in their answers, and to consider contrasting views using ‘On one hand ... On the other hand ...’.

Oxford additional tasks: Show a video of an interview with an environmental campaigner or animal expert in your country. Build on children’s work in the main activity to plan and write a script for a television interview. They can perform it to the class or film it to share with others. Complete the grammar exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.14–15.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have experienced being the interviewer and the interviewee in a nature interview. They have used their knowledge and improvisational skills to ask and answer questions. They have revised the simple present, present continuous, and simple past.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

YEAR 5 Protecting wildlife
Week 2, Lesson 1: Asking questions about animals
Learning outcomes: 5.2b, 5.2c

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Protecting wildlife’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by revising their knowledge of vocabulary and different tenses to role-play an interview. They will have the opportunity to practise in a one-to-one activity which promotes experimentation and the ability to make mistakes.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
