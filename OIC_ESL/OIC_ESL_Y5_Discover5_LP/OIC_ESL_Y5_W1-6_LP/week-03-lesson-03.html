PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last two weeks, while working through the ‘Protecting wildlife’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Support less able children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Choose one of the following activity ideas: Plan a campaign (group) Divide the class into groups of four to six children. Each group chooses a local or global animal protection issue to campaign on. Suggestions of suitable campaign topics can be provided. Within each group, children organize the creation of posters, emails, a video, and a speech by different team members, all with the aim of persuading people to support the campaign. Children should work together to clearly establish their aims, objectives, and core message before each working on different parts of the campaign. Encourage children to consider creating a timeline for the project. Remind them to regularly review progress as a team, to ensure that all the different aspects of the campaign are well connected.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: At the end of the week, invite a local politician or campaigner to come to the school (or connect online) to see the groups present their campaigns and provide feedback. Design an animal sanctuary (individual) Each child can pick an endangered animal that appeals to them. Children research their animal’s needs and design an animal sanctuary to meet those needs. Create a leaflet to explain the role of the sanctuary and to raise awareness. Use drawings, maps, and text to communicate the sanctuary’s layout and habitats, its location, what staff would be needed, and other features. Mind map fundraising ideas (possibly with a partner) and add a section to the leaflet persuading people to donate their money or time to the sanctuary’s work.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Plan a campaign (group) Divide the class into groups of four to six children. Each group chooses a local or global animal protection issue to campaign on. Suggestions of suitable campaign topics can be provided. Within each group, children organize the creation of posters, emails, a video, and a speech by different team members, all with the aim of persuading people to support the campaign. Children should work together to clearly establish their aims, objectives, and core message before each working on different parts of the campaign. Encourage children to consider creating a timeline for the project. Remind them to regularly review progress as a team, to ensure that all the different aspects of the campaign are well connected. Mind map fundraising ideas (possibly with a partner) and add a section to the leaflet persuading people to donate their money or time to the sanctuary’s work. Explore careers in conservation (mix of individual, group, and class)

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: At the end of the week, display all the class materials as an exhibition. Explore careers in conservation (mix of individual, group, and class) In small groups, children research different types of conservation jobs. Individually, create a list of interview questions to ask someone who works in this field. Then practise conducting interviews in pairs. Organize a class visit to a local animal sanctuary or nature reserve. Arrange with the staff there to allow children to interview one or more staff members about their job and the animals they look after. Alternatively, organize an online tour or expert talk from an animal sanctuary in another part of the world, direct to the classroom. Search online for ‘Virtual school visits’. Children then write individual reports about the visit/talk and the staff interview(s). A selection of these reports can be published in the school newspaper or website, if available.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support, or those with less confidence, to provide them with the confidence and tools to plan their work and make a confident start. This guidance could be provided in a small group setting. Check on these children more frequently as they work. Ensure they have vocabulary banks and relevant sentence starters to hand. More confident children can be given more independence to manage their work. More able children can be given additional tasks, which contribute to the overall objectives of the week and require them to think about the learning process. For example, they could be asked to design and conduct a class survey, designing questions (with support if needed) which assess children’s learning experience in this unit. They can then collate the responses in a short report, using diagrams or charts.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the review section in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.20–21, as well as any activities from Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.2–19 which have not been completed.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
