PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Warm-up memory game: ‘I went to the forest and I saw ...’ Start by telling children something you saw in the forest. The next player repeats your sentence and adds something they saw to the end. The aim is to add as many things as possible to the list before a player forgets or makes a mistake. Repeat until everyone has had a turn. Explain that in this lesson children will think about things that humans have done (causes) which have had an impact on animals (effects). They will predict what happened using the knowledge they have learned in this unit.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Teach irregular verbs in the past tense using Oxford Discover SB 5 p.23 as well as your own subject knowledge. Pre-prepare sets of cause and effect statements on separate slips of paper or card to use as a matching game. Prepare enough sets for the class to use in pairs. Use a number of irregular verbs in the past tense. To find ideas for the cause and effect cards, search online for ‘human impact on animals’.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: To make the exercise harder, use two to three different effects for each cause, which need to be sequenced: e.g. Cause: The government sold the rainforest to farmers. Effect(s): 1. The farmers used the rainforest to make farms. 2. Tigers had a smaller habitat. 3. Farmers caught the tigers that came near the farms. Ask children to work in pairs. Distribute the cause and effect statements to each pair. Ask children to work together to match the statements. Before they start, read some of the cause statements as a class. Ask: What do you think the effect might be?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ask children to work in pairs. Distribute the cause and effect statements to each pair. Ask children to work together to match the statements.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Identify and praise pairs who are working well to solve the problem together. Encourage children to ask you or other pairs for help if they struggle. Check answers as a class. Ask: How did you decide how the causes matched to the effects? Ask children to use the cards to create longer sentences, and to tell each other their sentences. Use connective words such as ‘when’, ‘because’, ‘so’, ‘and’: e.g. When the government sold the forest to farmers, the farmers made farms, so tigers had a smaller habitat.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, only provide one effect statement for each cause. To support less confident children, do one example together in a small group before setting the pairs off on their own. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to find more irregular verbs in the past tense. Provide a news or magazine article for them to use for this investigation.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.17. Children create their own cause and effect statements using the irregular verbs in the past tense. This can relate to the unit topic or to their own lives.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned irregular past verbs and thought about cause and effect through playing a matching game.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

YEAR 5 Protecting wildlife 
Week 2, Lesson 3: Making predictions about animals
Learning outcomes: 5.2b, 5.2d, 5.2f

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Protecting wildlife’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills to include irregular past verbs, and think about linking sentences to show cause and effect. They will work in pairs to discuss the real-world implications of human development on nature. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
