PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Write ‘Hearing’, ‘Smell’, ‘Feeling’, ‘Sight’, ‘Taste’ on the board. As you call the words out, ask children to point to the appropriate body part (ears, nose, fingers, eyes, tongue). Go faster as children get used to it. Variations: Ask for volunteers to take the role of caller, or call two senses at a time. Explain that this lesson will look at our senses and how we feel about teeth.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Ask children to read the text in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.29 ex.D. Put the text on the class whiteboard and, working as a class, highlight vocabulary linked to the five senses. Discuss any new vocabulary. Ask: What effect does this word choice have on you as the reader? (ideas: you feel like you are there, you connect with the character, it’s more interesting, it’s persuasive) What word forms can be used to evoke the senses? (adjectives, verbs, adverbs)

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Create four work stations around the classroom. Place one of the large labelled sheets of paper at each work station, with room for several children to work on the paper at once. Have spare sheets ready if necessary. Divide children into four groups and send one group to each work station. Ask them to think about how they feel in each situation and add vocabulary to the paper which evokes the five senses. Ask: How can you categorize vocabulary? As a class, vote on whether to group words by sense or by word form.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into four groups and send one group to each work station. Ask them to think about how they feel in each situation and add vocabulary to the paper which evokes the five senses. Ask: How can you categorize vocabulary? As a class, vote on whether to group words by sense or by word form. Remind children to work neatly and to think how they should use the space available on the paper, both as a group and considering that other groups will also contribute.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Remind children to work neatly and to think how they should use the space available on the paper, both as a group and considering that other groups will also contribute. Divide the time available into four, and ask children to move clockwise between the workstations at the end of each time period. As a class, display and discuss children’s contributions. Celebrate their effort and highlight interesting ideas. Explain that this work will help them to write more interesting and descriptive sentences. Ask: Would you vote the same way on how to organize the words again? Why / Why not?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Depending on the nature of the class, during the work station task either: create groups made up of different ability levels, so that more confident children can support less confident ones; or divide children into less confident and more confident groups, so that less confident children are not overshadowed by their peers. Ask more confident children to use a dictionary to find and add synonyms. Provide less able children with a list of appropriate vocabulary grouped by sense. They can then decide which words fit each situation and add them to the paper.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.24–25. Write a paragraph describing one of the situations from today’s lesson, using as many senses as possible.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to analyse a short fiction text to identify sensory language, including how and why writers use different word forms to evoke the senses. They have applied this learning to describe real-world situations that affect them.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
