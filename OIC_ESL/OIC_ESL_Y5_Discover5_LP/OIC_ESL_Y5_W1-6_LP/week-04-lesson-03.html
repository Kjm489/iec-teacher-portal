PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Write five to eight anagrams on the board of vocabulary learned in Lessons 1 and 2 (e.g. LOMAR = molar). Give children two minutes to unscramble as many as they can. Share the answers and check understanding. Remind children that regularly revising vocabulary helps their long-term memory. Explain that in this lesson, they will analyse a longer text to identify the five senses. They will also learn how to recognize their own sensory responses to stressful events.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Ask children to read the poem about teeth in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.30 or search online for ‘dental poems children’ or similar. For specific poems, search ‘CLPE AF Harrold Teeth’ or ‘Poetry Foundation Roald Dahl Crocodile’. Ask children to write the five senses on their whiteboard. Tell them they are going to analyse the poem to look for information linked to each sense. Ask children to think about their confidence level and set a self-challenge to link one to five pieces of information. Invite children to re-read the poem and take notes as they read.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Take feedback. Ask: What did you learn about how the person in the poem feels? Was it easy or difficult? Did you meet your self-challenge? Give children more time to find information and to discuss it with a partner. Ask them to highlight everything linked to the senses. Take feedback and discuss their answers. Ask: Did you meet your self-challenge?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Give children more time to find information and to discuss it with a partner. Ask them to highlight everything linked to the senses.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask children to close their eyes and slowly read the following script to them: You are eating a delicious cake. [pause] But suddenly, you feel horrible pain. [pause] You have a cavity! Imagine how you feel ... [pause] Now you have to go to the dentist. [pause] You are sitting in the waiting room, waiting to go in. [pause] Now you are in the big chair, with your mouth open wide. Imagine how you feel ... Discuss with the class: How did you feel? Did you feel anything in your body? Are you worried about going to the dentist? What can you do to make it less stressful? (ideas: ear defenders to reduce noise, deep breaths to reduce stress, close your eyes to avoid bright lights, take a toy to hold/play with) What other situations are stressful for you?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Encourage children to choose a self-challenge that is realistic for their own level. Source an easier poem for less able children, which they can read and respond to at their own pace while the class continues the main activity.

Oxford additional tasks: Read the poem in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.31. Answer the questions on p.32. Create a colourful advice leaflet to help someone who is scared of going to the dentist manage their worries.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to analyse a text to identify the five senses. They have learned how to recognize their own sensory responses to potentially stressful events in their own lives, and have begun to think about potential solutions.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘What do you know about teeth?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by building on learning from Lesson 2, to analyse a more difficult text. They will consider how they can be more aware of their own senses, and how to respond in a stressful situation.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
