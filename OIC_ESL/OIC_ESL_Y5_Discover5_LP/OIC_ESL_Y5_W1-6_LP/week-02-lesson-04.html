ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Ask children to plan and write at least two paragraphs about one of the extinct animals on the board. They can use the topics and structure ideas on the board. Tell children they can access the internet to find more information about their chosen animal.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Ask: Can you copy text straight from the source? Why / Why not? What do we call this? (plagiarism) How can you make it your own? (e.g. taking key vocabulary and putting it into their own sentences) Remind children to use the appropriate tense for each sentence. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.21, or your own subject knowledge, as a reminder.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Set a timer for eight- to ten-minute intervals. Each time the timer goes off, ask children to stand up and shake out their bodies before continuing their work. Share some of the children’s work as a class.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Remind children of the importance of using their time effectively for planning, research, and writing. Encourage them to divide their time appropriately. Set a timer for eight- to ten-minute intervals. Each time the timer goes off, ask children to stand up and shake out their bodies before continuing their work. Share some of the children’s work as a class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide vocabulary banks and photo prompts. A template, with or without headings or sentence starters, could also be provided. Challenge more able children by asking them to write about two animals, comparing and contrasting them, and the reasons for their extinction. As an extension task for more able children, they can write a True or False quiz about their animal.

Oxford additional tasks: Divide children into groups of three to four. Children read their report aloud to their group, and their peers provide one positive comment and one suggestion for improvement. Each child then revises their text based on the feedback. Children record their text to use as a soundtrack to a slide show about their animal.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written a short text about an extinct animal using basic layout and paragraphing techniques.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Protecting wildlife’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using basic layout and paragraphing techniques to write a short text about an animal. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
