PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Explain to children that this week they will focus on how animals use their teeth. Today, they are going to review what they learned last week using new grammar, and then learn some new words. Teach adjectives with a preposition using Oxford Discover SB 5 p.35, or your own subject knowledge. Then ask children: What have you learned about teeth so far in this unit? Ask follow-up questions using the construction ‘adjective + preposition’, such as What part of this unit are you interested in? What are you excited about? What are you good at? Is there an animal you are afraid of? Encourage children to shake out their body, breathe, and relax before responding.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do the listening exercise in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.36. Alternatively, source a different listening exercise, or a text about animal teeth that you can read aloud, using the vocabulary listed on p.1 of this lesson plan. Let children follow along in the book or with the text. Ask children what they think the vocabulary on p.1 means. Remind them about using context to work out meaning. Ask: Why do you think that? Use the sentences and the pictures to help you. Replay the listening exercise and teach any remaining vocabulary.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Tell children they will create a quiz, in line with the following instructions: Use information from Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.36–37 and/or your own research. Write five questions about animal teeth for your partner. Model examples on the board, such as: Can you name an animal that [action] with their teeth? What do [animal] use their teeth for? Which animals have [type of teeth]?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Write five questions about animal teeth for your partner. Model examples on the board, such as: Can you name an animal that [action] with their teeth? What do [animal] use their teeth for? Which animals have [type of teeth]? Ask and answer with your partner, listening carefully to what they say. Check and correct your writing with your partner.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask and answer with your partner, listening carefully to what they say. Check and correct your writing with your partner. Take feedback from children. How did they decide to structure their quiz? Did it work well? Was it too easy or too difficult?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with model questions, and answers with gaps for them to complete. To support less confident children, have them work in pairs to create the quiz and challenge a different pair to answer. Challenge more able children to create a quiz on a theme, for example Big Cats’ Teeth.

Oxford additional tasks: Review and expand the animal teeth quiz. Create online, illustrated versions of children’s quizzes using presentation or quiz-building tools. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp. 29–31 (less able children) and/or pp.29 and pp.32–33 (more able children).

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned new vocabulary and connected it to their existing knowledge of the unit topic. They have created a quiz about animal teeth. They have learned the construction ‘adjective + preposition’.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘What do you know about teeth?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening, speaking, and writing skills by building on the vocabulary learned in Week 4. They will learn new vocabulary and apply it to create their own quiz. They will have the opportunity to reflect on their learning so far on this topic.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
