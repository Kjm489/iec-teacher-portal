PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Share a short video of young activists who are making a difference. Search online for ‘Just a Kid, UNICEF’, or for other videos featuring current well-known young activists in your country or globally. Ask: Why are these young people taking action? Remind children of the concept of altruism and link it to this task. Explain that children will be using everything they have learned in this unit to write an email to an official person or organization. They will ask them to take action to help to protect an animal and keep it safe in the future. They can choose their animal, proposed action, and who to write to.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Divide children into pairs. Give each pair slips of paper containing a short email which has been cut into five sections: salutation, introduction, body text, conclusion, and closing/signature. Ask children to put the sections (slips of paper) in order. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.24, Oxford Discover WB 5 p.18, or create a simple email in advance. Discuss how to structure an email. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.24–25 and Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.18–19, or a separate mentor text. In their pairs, ask children to highlight persuasive language in the model email. Discuss as a class (e.g. emotive language, personalization, specific and realistic demands). Remind children they have been working hard on time management in this unit. For this task, they can use the following steps (listed in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.19):

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: brainstorm (mind map) outline draft check

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into pairs. Give each pair slips of paper containing a short email which has been cut into five sections: salutation, introduction, body text, conclusion, and closing/signature. Ask children to put the sections (slips of paper) in order. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.24, Oxford Discover WB 5 p.18, or create a simple email in advance.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: finalize Tell children how long they have for this task, and ask them to estimate how much time they need for each step. They can use their learning today to help them with the next long writing task in Week 5, Lesson 5. Ask: If you feel worried during the task, what can you do? (take a few deep breaths, stretch out, ask someone for help) Children spend the rest of the lesson time writing a persuasive email of three paragraphs, following the model in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.18–19 if used.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a structured email template and ideas for the animal, suggested action, and organization to write to. Ask more able children to act as learning assistants to support their peers once they have finished, answering any questions and making suggestions.

Oxford additional tasks: Children read out their email to the whole class or in groups, practising using tone of voice, intonation, and gestures to persuade others. Contact a local activist or politician who uses email in their activities. Ask them to review a selection of the children’s emails and give them tips and advice, in person or by email.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written a formal email to persuade someone to take specific action to protect an endangered species.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
