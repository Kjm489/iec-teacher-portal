PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Explain to children that this is the first lesson of a new unit about teeth. First, they are going to think about what they know about teeth and learn some new words. Ask children to close their eyes and count their teeth. Ask: How many do you have? Are any missing? Do they all look or feel the same? Show a short video including a range of animal and human teeth to create enthusiasm for the topic. Use the video from Oxford Discover SB 5 p.27 and/or use the search term ‘KS2 teeth video’ or similar. Try SciShowKids. Ask children: What can you tell me about teeth?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do the listening exercise in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.28. Alternatively, source a different listening exercise, or a text about teeth that you can read aloud, ideally with the vocabulary listed on p.1 of this lesson plan. Let children follow along in the book or the text. Ask children what they think the vocabulary on p.1 means. Remind them about using context for understanding. Ask: Why do you think that? Use the sentences and the picture to help you.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Play the listening exercise again and teach any remaining new vocabulary. Divide children into groups of two to four. Share a range of photos of human and animal teeth, including the photo in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.26. Give a set to each group.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into groups of two to four. Share a range of photos of human and animal teeth, including the photo in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.26. Give a set to each group. Write the following questions on the board for children to discuss with their partner or group: Are teeth important? What are teeth good for? Do humans and animals use them differently? Do the teeth in the photos all look the same? Can you name them?

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Write the following questions on the board for children to discuss with their partner or group: Are teeth important? What are teeth good for? Do humans and animals use them differently? Do the teeth in the photos all look the same? Can you name them? Check answers as a class and take feedback. Ask: Do you agree with [Name]? Why / Why not? Do photographs tell us everything we need to know? Tell children that at the end of the unit, they will review their learning to see how much more they know.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident children, pair them with a more confident child who can act as a mentor to encourage and support them. To support less able children, provide them with a vocabulary bank alongside the photos. Challenge more able children by asking them to listen without seeing the text for the first time hearing the listening exercise.

Oxford additional tasks: Write the answers to the questions that children discussed in the main activity in a short paragraph. Draw a diagram of a human or animal mouth, labelling it using the appropriate vocabulary. See Oxford Discover SB 5 p.28 for an example or search for ‘diagram of human teeth’. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.22–23. Alternatively, provide a categorization exercise for children to put the vocabulary they have learned into groups, such as type of animal, type of tooth, type of action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned new vocabulary to provide them with a good foundation for the unit topic. They have made connections between newly learned vocabulary and photographic resources.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
