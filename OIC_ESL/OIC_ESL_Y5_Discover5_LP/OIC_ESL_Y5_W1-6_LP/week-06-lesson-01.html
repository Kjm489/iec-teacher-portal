PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last two weeks, while working through the ‘What do you know about teeth?’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Support less able children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Choose one of the following activity ideas: Plan a campaign (group) Divide the class into groups of four to six children. Each group will plan a Healthy Teeth campaign to promote good oral health and hygiene in young children. Within each group, children organize a simple flyer for children, a second flyer for their families, and a play, all with the aim of persuading children to look after their teeth. Children should work together to clearly establish their aims, objectives, and core message before each working on different parts of the campaign. Encourage children to consider creating a timeline for the project. Remind them to regularly review progress as a team, to ensure that all the different aspects of the campaign are well connected. Encourage children to consider the age of their audience. Think about making the play, and the children’s flyer, fun, colourful, and engaging. The flyer should include simple instructions. On the other hand, the adults’ leaflet should be more detailed and formal, perhaps with labelled diagrams.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: At the end of the week, each group should present their play and campaign materials to a class or classes at the lower end of the school. Write a non-fiction book about animal teeth (individual) For this task, children will produce an illustrated non-fiction book of 6–12 pages, with the number of pages and amount of text dependent on ability. This book should be divided into sections. Mind map these as a class. Ideas include Introduction, Types of Teeth, Different Diets, Case Studies, Fun Facts, Current Issues, Conclusion, and Glossary (word list). Children plan out the pages in their book, thinking about placement of text and illustrations or photos. Children recall knowledge learned in this unit and conduct further research as required in order to draft out each section. Children then publish their book, either by writing directly onto paper, or by typing up the text, then printing and sticking it in next to photos and drawings. Alternatively, it could be completed entirely online using publishing software.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Plan a campaign (group) Divide the class into groups of four to six children. Each group will plan a Healthy Teeth campaign to promote good oral health and hygiene in young children. Within each group, children organize a simple flyer for children, a second flyer for their families, and a play, all with the aim of persuading children to look after their teeth. Children should work together to clearly establish their aims, objectives, and core message before each working on different parts of the campaign. Encourage children to consider creating a timeline for the project. Remind them to regularly review progress as a team, to ensure that all the different aspects of the campaign are well connected. At the end of the week, each group should present their play and campaign materials to a class or classes at the lower end of the school. Explore careers in dentistry (mix of individual, group, and class)

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Remind children about giving credit for any photos or quotations used. At the end of the week, display all the books in the school library and/or share them with children in classes lower down the school. Explore careers in dentistry (mix of individual, group, and class) In small groups, children research different types of jobs connected to teeth (such as dentist, dental assistant, hygienist, dental technician, animal dentistry). Individually, create a list of interview questions to ask someone who works in this field. Then practise conducting interviews in pairs. Organize a class visit to a local dental practice or vet. Arrange with the staff to allow children to interview one or more staff members about their job. Alternatively, organize a local dentist or vet to visit the classroom, or connect via video conferencing software, to talk about their job and be interviewed by children. Children then write individual reports about the visit/talk and the staff interview(s). A selection of these reports can be published in the school newspaper or website, if available.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support, or those with less confidence, to provide them with the confidence and tools to plan their work and make a confident start. This guidance could be provided in a small group setting. Check on these children more frequently as they work. Ensure they have vocabulary banks and relevant sentence starters to hand. More confident children can be given more independence to manage their work. More able children can be given additional tasks which require them to communicate at a higher level of expertise. For example: Create a campaign aimed at teachers rather than children, asking them to take action to improve oral health within the school setting. Write their own lesson plans for younger children, covering the topic of animal teeth, thinking about how they can teach them the relevant vocabulary and facts in a fun and age-appropriate way. Collate questions for an interview with a dentist and structure the interview in a logical way.They can allocate questions to their peers, and be given the responsibility of running the interview during the visit or expert talk.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the review section in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.40–41. They can also complete any activities from Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.21–39 which have not been completed.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
