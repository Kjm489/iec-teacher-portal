PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Show photos of animals or objects which are out of focus, partially hidden, or otherwise difficult to guess. Search online for ‘picture quiz questions animals’ or similar, or use photo-editing software. Ask: Do you know what this is? Are you sure? Explain that in this lesson, children will learn a new form of grammar called ‘modals of certainty’. This will help them say how sure they are of something.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Teach modals of certainty (must be / has to be / might be / can’t be), using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.41. Ask children to assess how confident they are about modals of certainty on a scale of 1 (not at all confident) to 10 (confident). Play a guessing game. Pre-prepare small, printed photos of animals, enough for half the class. Use photos on Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.36–39.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Pair up the class in two circles, an inner circle and an outer circle. Each child in the inner circle (guesser) faces a child in the outer circle (partner). Give each partner in the outer circle a photo of an animal. Ask them to keep it hidden. Model the game for the class with one child. The guesser must ask a ‘Yes/No’ question to try to guess the animal which their partner has (asking questions such as Does it have ...?, Does it eat ...?). Their partner answers with ‘Yes’ or ‘No’ only.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Pair up the class in two circles, an inner circle and an outer circle. Each child in the inner circle (guesser) faces a child in the outer circle (partner). Give each partner in the outer circle a photo of an animal. Ask them to keep it hidden. The guesser must ask a ‘Yes/No’ question to try to guess the animal which their partner has (asking questions such as Does it have ...?, Does it eat ...?). Their partner answers with ‘Yes’ or ‘No’ only. Remind children to be encouraging if their partner is finding it difficult to guess.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: The guesser must then respond to this information, using It can’t be ..., It might be ..., or It must be ... with the name of an animal. They then ask another question, and keep following this pattern until they have guessed correctly. When everyone has guessed (or after a certain time), ask all guessers to move three places to the right. Partners stand still. Run the game again. After several more turns, the partners give their photos to the inner circle and become the guessers themselves. Remind children to be encouraging if their partner is finding it difficult to guess. At the end of the lesson, ask children to reassess their confidence by using modals of certainty using the 1–10 scale. Discuss why it might have changed.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To make the main activity easier for everyone, list the animals on the board, and add the name of the animal to each photo. Alternatively, give the list to less able children only. Allow less confident children to hold the photos first, so that more confident children model the questions to them first as guessers. Challenge more able children to produce complex sentences during the game: e.g. If it doesn’t have tusks, it can’t be an elephant.

Oxford additional tasks: Practise using modals of certainty by completing the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.34–35. Adapt the game to other types of vocabulary which the class needs to reinforce their knowledge of (e.g. classroom objects, transport, food).

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned to use modals of certainty in conversation by playing a guessing game. They have assessed their own confidence in this topic. They have practised how to construct questions and draw appropriate conclusions.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
