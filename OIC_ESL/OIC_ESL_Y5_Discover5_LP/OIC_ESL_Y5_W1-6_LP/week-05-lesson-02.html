PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play a slideshow of photos of different animals eating, including carnivores, herbivores, and omnivores. Ask children to write ‘carnivore’, ‘herbivore’, or ‘omnivore’ on their whiteboard and hold it up. Eliminate children with wrong answers until there is a winner, or until you reach the end of the slideshow. Explain that in this lesson they will find out about the different ways animals can use their teeth – not just for eating!

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Use the reading text in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.38–39, or source a long, illustrated non-fiction text about how animals use their teeth for a range of activities (such as fighting, attacking prey [including with venom], communicating, making shelter, and/or keeping clean). Give each child a copy of the text and divide the class into groups of four to six.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Remind children about the importance of working positively in a team. Ask: How will working positively or negatively affect the results you get? Tell children to look for information in the text about the different ways that animals use their teeth, and make notes. They have to decide how to organize the work within their group, and must decide on a spokesperson to feed back to the class. Tell the class how long they have for this task.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Remind children about the importance of working positively in a team. Ask: How will working positively or negatively affect the results you get? Tell children to look for information in the text about the different ways that animals use their teeth, and make notes. They have to decide how to organize the work within their group, and must decide on a spokesperson to feed back to the class. Tell the class how long they have for this task. Take feedback from each spokesperson about what their group has learned.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Take feedback from each spokesperson about what their group has learned. Ask: How did you divide up the work? Was it successful? What strategies did you use? (such as using visuals, scanning the page, highlighted words, sharing tasks, monitoring time). Review how to structure a non-fiction text using the reading text as a model (title, introduction, headings, paragraphs, conclusion). Remind children that regularly thinking about how texts are structured will help them to retain this learning, and apply it to their own writing.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Structure the groups according to ability and give less able groups more assistance with the task, such as providing suggested headings, allocating roles, and providing tips on how to scan a text for information (see strategies above).

Oxford additional tasks: Read the full text independently and answer the questions in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.40. If not completed in Lesson 1, complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.30–31 (less able children) and/or pp.32–33 (more able children). Expand the quiz from Week 5, Lesson 1 using information learned in this lesson.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to analyse a well-structured non-fiction text to extract information about a topic. They have worked in a team, distributing tasks, and collecting information together to present in one output.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
