PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Explain that children will be researching tigers today, using their research skills to make a fact file about tigers and their endangered status. Play a short video clip which shows the excitement, strength, and majesty of tigers in the wild.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Provide a number of written sources for children to use for their research, including: Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.17–19; photos, news and magazine articles, and books about tigers;

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: several graphs (such as population size over time); internet access with one or two suggested websites suitable for further research. Ask children to structure their research, learning from their experience in Lesson 3. Ask: What questions should you ask? How much information do you need to find out? How long should you spend on researching and writing? How should you make notes? What will you do if you see an unknown word?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Allow plenty of time for research and for writing, letting children know when they should be moving from the research stage to the writing stage. Ask children to make a short fact file as a result of their research. This could be on paper or as a simple online slide presentation.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Let children know how much time they have and explain that the activity must be completed within this time limit. Working in this time limit is challenging, especially with so much information available, but it helps their brain to grow. Remind children that they are in the stretch zone. Ask them to try to use the grammar they have learned this week, and model some examples on the board: e.g. Tigers have lived in India for thousands of years. Allow plenty of time for research and for writing, letting children know when they should be moving from the research stage to the writing stage. Ask children to make a short fact file as a result of their research. This could be on paper or as a simple online slide presentation.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide extra photos and simple texts which can be cut up and stuck together to create the fact file. Encourage less confident children to use photos as a starting point, thinking about the tiger’s appearance and habitat, before tackling more challenging aspects of the topic. Challenge more confident children by giving them less guidance on how to structure their research. You may set them off on the task while discussing this with the rest of the class. To challenge more able children, ask them to link their sentences, so that 2–3 sentences relate to the same subject (such as appearance, endangered status, hunting).

Oxford additional tasks: Children present their fact files to each other in small groups, or to the whole class. Read the text again on Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.17–19 and answer the questions on p.20. Conduct similar research into another type of big cat, ideally from the children’s own continent. Extend the fact file to compare and contrast this big cat with tigers.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned more about tigers, and about how to manage their time and effort in a timed research and presentation task.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
