PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display a picture of money on the board. Introduce the unit topic. Invite children to tell you any vocabulary and facts they remember about money from the previous unit. Ask: What do you think using money ‘responsibly’ means? Define the word ‘responsibly’ in this context (i.e. acting in a manner which is positive, trustworthy, and reliable).

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Spend the first half of the lesson on vocabulary work. Teach/review vocabulary related to money. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.140 exs.A–B, or use some or all of these words in sentences on the board: ‘bank’, ‘bills’ (or ‘notes’), ‘borrow’, ‘coins’, ‘currency’, ‘exchange’, ‘lend’, ‘owe’, ‘pay’, and ‘spend’. Encourage children to work out or check their meaning using context and dictionaries. Write the following verbs on the board: ‘borrow’, ‘pay (for)’, ‘lend’, ‘owe’, ‘buy’, and ‘sell’. In pairs, ask children to sort them into two groups. Then share answers as a class (‘borrow’, ‘lend’; ‘pay for’, ‘buy’, ‘sell’). Explain that ‘owe’ can be in either group: you can owe money for something you are buying, or you can owe someone money if you have borrowed it from them and they expect it back in the future. Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Say three things you would pay for and three things you would borrow. Who do you buy things from? Who do you borrow things from? What is the difference between borrowing and paying for something? (Discuss who ‘owns’ the item.)

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Write the following verbs on the board: ‘borrow’, ‘pay (for)’, ‘lend’, ‘owe’, ‘buy’, and ‘sell’. In pairs, ask children to sort them into two groups. Then share answers as a class (‘borrow’, ‘lend’; ‘pay for’, ‘buy’, ‘sell’). Explain that ‘owe’ can be in either group: you can owe money for something you are buying, or you can owe someone money if you have borrowed it from them and they expect it back in the future. Spend the second half of the lesson in small-group discussion. Form groups of four children. Ask them to think about the differences between borrowing an item and paying for it. They can also discuss the questions in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.140 ex.C and consider: Give each group six cards. Four have one word each: ‘borrow’, ‘buy’, ‘lend’, and ‘sell’. Two are blank. Instruct children to write the names of two objects on the blank cards. Then ask them to allocate the cards between themselves and to make two freeze frames. In turn, invite each group to explain their freeze frames using the lesson’s vocabulary. Ask: Who is the lender/borrower/buyer/seller? Check understanding of these roles in a transaction. Check that the objects they have chosen are appropriate to buy or borrow.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Why do people lend things to others? (e.g. to help others, to share, to save money) Give each group six cards. Four have one word each: ‘borrow’, ‘buy’, ‘lend’, and ‘sell’. Two are blank. Instruct children to write the names of two objects on the blank cards. Then ask them to allocate the cards between themselves and to make two freeze frames. In turn, invite each group to explain their freeze frames using the lesson’s vocabulary. Ask: Who is the lender/borrower/buyer/seller? Check understanding of these roles in a transaction. Check that the objects they have chosen are appropriate to buy or borrow. In their groups, encourage children to share a memorable experience of buying or borrowing items. Ask: What makes it a good memory? (e.g. They saved money for this item, or their friend was very kind to lend it to them.) Ask volunteers to share their experiences with the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability groups for the discussion task and encourage more able children to support them. As an extension task, challenge more able children to make a list of rules for good borrowing and lending between friends.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the vocabulary practice tasks in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.130–131. In pairs, encourage children to write as many words related to money as they can in one minute. They then compare their list with another pair’s list.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed vocabulary related to money. They have understood the difference between paying for and borrowing items in a discussion.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
