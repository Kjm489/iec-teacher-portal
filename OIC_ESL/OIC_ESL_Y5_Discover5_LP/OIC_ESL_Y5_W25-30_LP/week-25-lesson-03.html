PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Pass around some shells, feathers, and salt. In pairs, ask children to think about why you have brought these into class. Then tell them the answer: these have all been used in the past as currency! See if children can think more deeply about why these items might be valuable.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Tell the class they will be reading a text about the history of money. In pairs, ask them to predict what might be in the text, based on their previous learning about money. Allow them to share their ideas. Give children time to read the text in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.142–143.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Come together as a class after each section to check understanding. Ask children to complete the questions on p.144 exs.B–C (not ex.A). Then check answers.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: At the end of the reading task, divide children into groups of three or four. Ask each group to show a stage in the history of money through mime. Before doing this, work as a class to create a list of the different stages. Write them on the board. These might include bartering, using shells and feathers, using coins, using printed money, and using electronic money. Now give each group five minutes to create their mime. Share them with the class.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Remind children that if they are worried about tackling a longer text, they can take it step by step and divide it into shorter sections. They can use the title, headings, illustrations, and photos to help them understand what they read. At the end of the reading task, divide children into groups of three or four. Ask each group to show a stage in the history of money through mime. Before doing this, work as a class to create a list of the different stages. Write them on the board. These might include bartering, using shells and feathers, using coins, using printed money, and using electronic money. Now give each group five minutes to create their mime. Share them with the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, complete the reading activity as a small-group activity with adult support. Encourage less confident children by allowing each group to practise their mime before they perform it to the class. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to find out how much common items cost 10, 50, or 100 years ago (e.g. bread, shoes, a house). Challenge them to find out the meaning of the word ‘inflation’.

Oxford additional tasks: Read about using salt as a currency in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.132–133. Find out how coins are made. Search online for ‘how coins are made kids video’. Encourage children to share their learning with a partner.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned facts about the history of money. They have created a mime to illustrate one of the stages in its history.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Using money responsibly’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by applying their previous learning about money to help them make predictions about a text. They will use what they read to create a mime. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
