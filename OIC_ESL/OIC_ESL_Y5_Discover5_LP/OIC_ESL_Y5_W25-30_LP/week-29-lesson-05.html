PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play the Big Question Wrap Up video from Oxford Discover SB 5 p.169. Remind children that they watched this video in Week 28, Lesson 1. Check whether they now feel more confident in their understanding of the topic.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Tell the class that they are going to write a paragraph about their favourite way to express themselves. Optionally, share Oxford Discover WB 5 p.157 ex.B as an example. Throughout today’s lesson, encourage children to reflect on which creative activities are important to them, and to consider the positive effects this has on their self-development and personal growth. Allow 5–10 minutes for discussion. Pair up children. Ask them to discuss the following questions: What is your favourite thing to create? Describe it.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: How does creating this thing make you feel? Encourage children to take a few breaths and to think deeply before responding. There are many examples of self-expression they can choose from. Collaborate to create a short list of up to five success criteria. Take suggestions from the class. Add your own. Ideas might include: Clearly identify your favourite type of self-expression. Clearly explain how you like to express yourself through this activity. Use a sense verb + adjective to describe how it feels. Use the construction ‘It’s + adjective + infinitive’ to express an opinion.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Pair up children. Ask them to discuss the following questions: Invite children to read their text to a small group or to the class.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Write in full sentences with correct punctuation, capitalization, and spelling. Allow 20 minutes for the writing activity. Children write their text. In pairs, ask children to check each other’s work against the success criteria. Encourage them to identify mistakes, viewing them as part of the learning process. Allow five minutes for them to make improvements to their text. Invite children to read their text to a small group or to the class. Celebrate the variety of choices, talents, and skills in the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide vocabulary banks and a list of sentence starters using the target language. Challenge more able children to write more than one paragraph. They should divide the text into logical sections.

Oxford additional tasks: Spend more time discussing the variety of choices children have made. Ask: Why do you think we all express ourselves differently? What benefits does this have for our class, our local community, and for the wider world? Write a final copy of the text. Illustrate it. Put the work on display.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written about their personal experiences of a form of self-expression. They have used target language and grammar from the unit.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
