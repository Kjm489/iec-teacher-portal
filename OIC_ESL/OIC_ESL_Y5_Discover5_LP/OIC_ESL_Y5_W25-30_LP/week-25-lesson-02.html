PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Explain that today’s lesson will look at making decisions about buying a special product or experience (such as a day trip). This will involve comparing and contrasting different products (i.e. finding similar and different things about each product to help you decide). On the board, display a web page of a company selling products aimed at this age group (e.g. toys, electronics, or clothes). Encourage children to identify different pieces of information on the page, such as the price, size, category, or age group.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Form groups of two or three children. Explain (or review) how to create a Venn diagram. Using Oxford Discover SB 5 p.141 or the reading text provided, ask each group to create a diagram to compare and contrast the products in the text. Then check answers. Give groups time to answer the following questions. Then discuss as a class. What is the purpose of the text? (to help you choose which game/boat trip to buy) What kind of information does it have? (price, theme, what is included)

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Does organizing the information in a diagram help you to make a decision? Which one would you choose? Why? Hand out materials (in the form of leaflets, web page printouts, catalogues, etc.) selling products suitable for your class. Ask each group to explore the information they have and to discuss between themselves how two or more products are similar or different. Move around the room and observe each group as they work. Ask: Is this product good value for money? Why / Why not? Is this one better value for money?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Form groups of two or three children. Explain (or review) how to create a Venn diagram. Using Oxford Discover SB 5 p.141 or the reading text provided, ask each group to create a diagram to compare and contrast the products in the text. Then check answers. Hand out materials (in the form of leaflets, web page printouts, catalogues, etc.) selling products suitable for your class. Ask each group to explore the information they have and to discuss between themselves how two or more products are similar or different. Move around the room and observe each group as they work. Ask: Is this product good value for money? Why / Why not? Is this one better value for money?

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: As a class, share ideas about today’s lesson. Draw out children’s thinking about: how to use and evaluate the information they are given by companies how to make decisions about buying special products or experiences what ‘value for money’ means to them. Encourage children to think about why they are learning about comparing products. Help them to make the connection to building confidence in making financial choices.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, draw a Venn diagram on the board and fill in one or two pieces of information as a class, before finishing the task in groups. Challenge more able children by giving them a budget and list of criteria (e.g. I have $10 and want to see a historical film at 7 p.m. What’s the best choice?) Ask them to find the ‘best match’ (which may not be an exact one) from the materials provided. Allow a few minutes for children to explore the information provided in silence and to gather their thoughts ahead of the group discussion.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a short paragraph comparing and contrasting two products from the materials provided. Use the text from the Venn diagram activity as a model.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have assessed and categorized information to make decisions about which products and experiences to buy. They have considered how these skills support good money management.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Using money responsibly’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by working collaboratively to assess and categorize written information. They will have the opportunity to think about how financial decision-making skills will support them in life. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
