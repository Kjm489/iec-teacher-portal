PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Using money responsibly’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: Fundraising project (group/class) Present three local or international causes to the class. These can be charities, a school project, or other good causes. Give children time to discuss which cause they prefer with a partner. Then hold a class vote to choose a cause to support. In groups of five or six, ask children to discuss ways to raise money. Each group must choose one practical way to raise money that they can do during this week (ideas: make and sell craft items or cakes, clean teachers’ cars, do household chores, sell second-hand items, participate in a sponsored run in the school grounds). Each group should choose something that will challenge them. Groups plan out project steps, materials, and roles; and create a project timeline. Support them with thinking about steps they need to take to make it successful. Groups implement their project and collect funds in a manner appropriate to your school setting. At the end of the week, invite groups to tell the class about their project. Ask children to share things that went well and things that were more challenging. Tell children how much money was collected in total. Celebrate children’s contribution to the cause.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Research project (pairs/individual) Ask children to choose a topic to research from this list: How money is made and circulated 10 ways to save money on household bills A famous philanthropist (a rich person who donates money to good causes) and their projects Explain that these are challenging research topics. Remind children that struggle and challenge can help their brain to grow. Children will produce two pieces of work: A non-fiction report on the topic An illustrated poster highlighting key points

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Fundraising project (group/class) Present three local or international causes to the class. These can be charities, a school project, or other good causes. Give children time to discuss which cause they prefer with a partner. Then hold a class vote to choose a cause to support. In groups of five or six, ask children to discuss ways to raise money. Each group must choose one practical way to raise money that they can do during this week (ideas: make and sell craft items or cakes, clean teachers’ cars, do household chores, sell second-hand items, participate in a sponsored run in the school grounds). Each group should choose something that will challenge them.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Display all the reports and posters in the classroom or share with others in the school. Money management (class/individual) – if not used in Week 24 Arrange a visit to a local bank, or invite a representative from a local bank or money management charity to visit the class. Ask them to make a presentation about how to manage money responsibly. Before the visit, provide information about the bank or charity to the class. Tell children to prepare any questions they have about: how bank accounts work, saving and spending, budgeting. During the visit or presentation, encourage children to ask questions and to make notes. After the visit or presentation, discuss what children have learned. Tell them to write an illustrated leaflet to teach other children about managing money.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support in a small-group setting, to provide them with the tools to plan their work and make a confident start. Check on these children more frequently as they work. The research project can be completed in a small group, guided by an adult, with appropriately levelled materials chosen in advance. For the money management task, they may prefer to make a poster with less text, rather than a leaflet. More able children can be asked to stretch themselves in writing tasks by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar in their work. At the end of each task, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures. These pupils should be encouraged to do fundraising projects which require them to produce a greater amount of spoken English through interaction with others.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the end-of-unit review in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.140–141. Alternatively, create a short class quiz to check understanding of key vocabulary.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
