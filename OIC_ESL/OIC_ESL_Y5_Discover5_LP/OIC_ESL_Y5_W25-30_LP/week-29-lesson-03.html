PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Write five to six short anagrams on the board with clues. Use vocabulary learned in Week 29, Lessons 1–2. Examples: ‘covered in flowers’ = FROLAL (floral); ‘the actors in a play’ = SACT (cast). Give children two minutes to solve as many as they can. Share answers as a class.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Teach the use of the construction ‘It’s + adjective + infinitive as subject’ to express opinions. Use your own subject knowledge, Oxford Discover SB 5 p.165 and Oxford Discover WB 5 p.154. For example: It’s exciting to go on a rollercoaster. It’s fun to learn another language. It’s difficult/easy/dangerous/nice/cool/scary to …

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: As a class, put together a list of adjectives which can be used to express an opinion. Write them on the board. Do a speaking activity. Divide children into pairs. Ask each pair to look through their Student Book or Class Book. One child finds and indicates to a picture of an activity (e.g. riding a bicycle).

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ask each pair to look through their Student Book or Class Book. Their partner gives an opinion on it: e.g. It’s fun to ride a bicycle! Prompt them to use the grammar they have just learned. Extend the activity by asking the first child to respond to their partner’s opinion: e.g. I agree / I disagree; It’s boring to ride a bicycle! Repeat again several times. Encourage children to value their partner's opinions if they are different to their own. Play a class game. Invite a volunteer to the front. Ask them to give an opinion. The other children move to one side of the room if they agree, and to the other side of the room if they disagree. Ensure that each group is respectful of the other’s opinion. Repeat.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Their partner gives an opinion on it: e.g. It’s fun to ride a bicycle! Prompt them to use the grammar they have just learned. Then swap. Repeat several times. Extend the activity by asking the first child to respond to their partner’s opinion: e.g. I agree / I disagree; It’s boring to ride a bicycle! Repeat again several times. Encourage children to value their partner's opinions if they are different to their own. Play a class game. Invite a volunteer to the front. Ask them to give an opinion. The other children move to one side of the room if they agree, and to the other side of the room if they disagree. Ensure that each group is respectful of the other’s opinion. Repeat.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a choice of cards for each part of the phrase. Ask them to put together three cards: It’s + an adjective + an infinitive. Then say the phrase. Challenge more able and confident children by asking them to use their facial expression, gestures, and tone of voice to emphasize their opinion.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the grammar practice activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.154–155. Alternatively, write sentences expressing opinions about different activities linked to the ‘Self-expression’ unit (e.g. to write poetry, to create a sculpture, to paint a painting).

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have practised expressing opinions using the construction ‘It’s + adjective + infinitive as subject’.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Week 29, Lesson 3: Expressing an opinion
Learning outcomes: 5.2b, 5.2f

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Self-expression’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by building their repertoire of language used to express opinions. Through pair discussion and a class game, they will practise valuing differences of opinion. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
