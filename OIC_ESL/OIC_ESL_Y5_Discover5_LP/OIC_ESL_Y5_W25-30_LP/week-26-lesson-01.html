PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play audio of the buzz of conversation in a busy café. Search online for ‘people talking in a busy café video’. Encourage children to close their eyes, let their thoughts go, and pay attention to the breath. After several minutes, ask them to open their eyes. Discuss whether children found the sound of background conversation relaxing or not. Explain that today’s lesson will focus on listening to several people speaking – but luckily not all at the same time!

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Tell children that they will be asked to identify more than two speakers in a conversation. This task is challenging. Remind them that they are in the stretch zone. Do the listening activity in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.146 exs.A–B.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Briefly discuss how each speaker ‘prioritizes’ or ‘sets priorities for’ their money. Ask: How do you think these people decided what to spend the money on? What is important to them? (e.g. family, their community, education). Encourage children to consider what priorities they would have themselves. Invite them to tell a partner what is important to them and why. Form groups of four children. Space them well apart from each other. Give each group their writing from Week 25, Lesson 5 and one scarf or blindfold. Instruct one child in each group to cover their eyes.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Briefly discuss how each speaker ‘prioritizes’ or ‘sets priorities for’ their money. Ask: How do you think these people decided what to spend the money on? What is important to them? (e.g. family, their community, education). Encourage children to consider what priorities they would have themselves. Invite them to tell a partner what is important to them and why. Form groups of four children. Space them well apart from each other. Give each group their writing from Week 25, Lesson 5 and one scarf or blindfold. Instruct one child in each group to cover their eyes. The other group members slowly read out one sentence each from their work. The blindfolded child, with eyes still covered, tries to correctly match what they heard to the their group members. Then swap roles. If time allows, mix up the groups and run the activity again. Discuss as a class: Is it difficult to identify different speakers in a group? What do you need to listen for? What clues can help you?

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: The other group members slowly read out one sentence each from their work. The blindfolded child, with eyes still covered, tries to correctly match what they heard to the their group members. Then swap roles. If time allows, mix up the groups and run the activity again. Discuss as a class: Is it difficult to identify different speakers in a group? What do you need to listen for? What clues can help you?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a transcript of the first listening activity. In the second listening activity, choose more able children to cover their eyes first.

Oxford additional tasks: Look at a local newspaper or news website. How many stories are related to money?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have understood a conversation about money involving multiple speakers. They have thought about how people prioritize uses for money.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
