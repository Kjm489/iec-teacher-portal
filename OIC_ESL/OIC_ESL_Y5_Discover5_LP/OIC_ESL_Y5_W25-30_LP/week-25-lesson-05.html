PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to imagine places they would visit, or things they would do, if there were no limits – either in the real world or in their imagination. Encourage them to think big! Give children five minutes to doodle or draw one or more of their ideas on paper. They can also write if they prefer. Encourage them to show and tell a partner.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Tell children that when things aren’t possible now, we call them ‘hypothetical’ or ‘unreal’ situations. Write these words on the board. Ask them to put their hand up if they drew something hypothetical in the introductory activity. We use the unreal conditional for hypothetical situations. Teach the unreal conditional using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.145 and Oxford Discover WB 5 p.134. If I had lots of money, I would buy a farm with lots of horses.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: If I could/had/went/did(n’t)/met …, I would … Ask children: What would you do if you had a million dollars? (or equivalent) Tell them to make notes on their whiteboard, then share ideas with a partner. Elicit class responses and write some on the board. Then write: ‘save’, ‘spend’, ‘donate’, and ‘invest’. Invite children to define these, using prior learning. Categorize the responses on the board. Ask: Which is the most popular category? Why do you think that is? Ask children to think about the statement: I should spend all the money on myself. After all, it’s my money! Encourage them to discuss this in pairs.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ask children: What would you do if you had a million dollars? (or equivalent) Tell them to make notes on their whiteboard, then share ideas with a partner. Near the end of the lesson, ask children to check their writing with a partner. Discuss and correct common errors as a class. Keep these pieces of writing for Week 26, Lesson 1.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Discuss as a class and invite volunteers to share reasons for their decision. Give children the opportunity to change sides if they have heard something that changes their mind. Ask: Is it important to think about other people when deciding how to use the money? Why? How can we use the money responsibly? Ask children to write a paragraph using the unreal conditional to outline how they would spend a million pounds if they had it. Encourage them to include further ‘if’ statements as well as the initial question (e.g. If I gave half of the money to my Mum, I would feel happy. If I spent it all on myself, I wouldn’t be so content!). Near the end of the lesson, ask children to check their writing with a partner. Discuss and correct common errors as a class. Keep these pieces of writing for Week 26, Lesson 1.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children when teaching or practising the unreal conditional, provide statements cut into two (‘If’ statement + ‘I would’ statement) for them to match together. Spend more time explaining the construction of the sentence to ensure understanding. Introduce more able children to the irregular subjunctive: If I were …, I would … Encourage less confident children to vary the verbs they use in their ‘if’ statement by providing a list of possible verbs. Use Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.134–135 for inspiration.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the grammar practice in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.135 or prepare alternative worksheets to practise using the unreal conditional. Encourage children to ask and answer: What would you do if you had a million pounds?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used the unreal conditional to write about using money responsibly in a hypothetical situation.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Using money responsibly’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using the unreal conditional to write about what they would do in hypothetical situations. They will have the opportunity to think deeply about how to use money in a responsible way.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
