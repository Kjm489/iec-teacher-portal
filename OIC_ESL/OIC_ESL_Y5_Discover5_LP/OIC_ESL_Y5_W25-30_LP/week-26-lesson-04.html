PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Encourage children to warm up for the lesson by saying a world currency and its country and doing five star jumps. Then invite volunteers to say another currency and country, then choose another action. Finish with stretches and a few deep breaths.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Remind children of their prior learning from Week 25, Lesson 2, in which they compared information about products or experiences. Tell them that practising reading and analysing this kind of information again will help to build their confidence in this area. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.104 ex.C to teach how to make suggestions when planning a day out. Give children time to practise key phrases using the prompts provided.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Play (or pre-record) one or two simple radio/TV adverts for days out. Check understanding of key information (e.g. activity, price, location, time). Provide a range of leaflets/brochures advertising day trips, events, and activities. This time, encourage children to practise making suggestions using these materials. They can use dictionaries for support.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Give children a reasonable budget for a day trip. Instruct them to use their budget to choose one or two activities from the materials provided. They then tell a partner what they have chosen and why. Confident children can share their choice with the class.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Give children a reasonable budget for a day trip. Instruct them to use their budget to choose one or two activities from the materials provided. They then tell a partner what they have chosen and why. Confident children can share their choice with the class. Ask: Which day out is best if you are on a budget? Why? (The answer will depend on the materials selected.)

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, look at the texts and work together in a small group to highlight different categories of information in different colours (e.g. activity, price, time, equipment). Then use the colours to compare information. Challenge more able children by providing more difficult aural information, such as faster or more colloquial radio adverts.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a paragraph about a day trip based on the model in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.129.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have understood aural and written information about days out. They have made suggestions to plan a day out on a budget.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

YEAR 5 Using money responsibly
Week 26, Lesson 4: Planning a day out on a budget
Learning outcomes: 5.1f, 5.2b, 5.2f, 5.3g

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Using money responsibly’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening, reading, and speaking skills by using information they hear and read to talk about planning a day out. This will build on their prior learning in Week 25, Lesson 2. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
