PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display pictures of a credit card, a mobile phone, a contactless pay terminal, and some coins. In pairs, ask children to discuss what all these objects have in common and which is the odd one out. (You can pay with all of them, but coins are not electronic.) Show children a handful of real coins. Hold out your other hand and pretend to have coins in that hand. See if children know which handful is ‘visible’ and which is ‘invisible’. Remind them that some money – electronic money – really is invisible!

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Invite children to recall information learned in Lesson 3. Write key points on the board. Then ask them to open their books, or hand out the text. Add further points to the board. See if children can recall how to compare and contrast text using Venn diagrams. Draw a simple diagram on the board for coins and paper money. Invite volunteers to add facts to the diagram (e.g. coins – made of metal, heavy; both – used for payment; paper money – made of paper, light).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Pair up children. Tell them to create a Venn diagram for paper money and invisible (electronic) money, using information from the text from Lesson 3. Refer to ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.144. Draw a large Venn diagram on the board. Invite children to populate the diagram. Ask children to think about the statement: We should stop using paper money and coins, and only use electronic money. Encourage them to discuss this in pairs.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Pair up children. Tell them to create a Venn diagram for paper money and invisible (electronic) money, using information from the text from Lesson 3. Refer to ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.144.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask children to move to one side of the room if they agree, and to the other side of the room if they disagree. Invite them to share reasons for their decision. Give them the opportunity to change sides if they have heard something that changes their mind. Invite children to take a few moments to breathe deeply before they do the final task. Explain the writing task: children are going to write one or two paragraphs comparing and contrasting paper money and invisible money. Remind them to use their Venn diagram and the text for support. More able children should use their writing to justify their response to the above statement (i.e. for or against). Less able children can produce simple sentences comparing and contrasting facts from the text.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, simplify the writing task as suggested above. Provide a list of useful vocabulary for comparing and contrasting for them to refer to. As an extension task for more able children, divide them into two teams. Hold a debate for and against the statement: We should stop using paper money and coins, and only use electronic money. Then have a class vote.

Oxford additional tasks: If children read the text about salt as currency in Lesson 3, they may now complete the Venn diagram in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.133. Create a survey with questions about how often people use paper or electronic money, for what purposes, and which they prefer. Use the survey to ask family and friends, then report back to the class.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used information about paper and electronic money to write a text comparing and contrasting the two types of money.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Week 25, Lesson 4: Paper money and invisible money
Learning outcomes: 5.4a, 5.4b, 5.4d

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Using money responsibly’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by putting together skills practised in Week 25, Lesson 2 with the text from Lesson 3. They will write their own text comparing paper and invisible (electronic) money.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
