PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Self-expression’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: A class talent show (individual/groups) Invite children to contribute to a talent show which will be held towards the end of the week. Explain that they can take part as individuals or in small groups. Provide sufficient time for children to choose what they will do in the show and to rehearse their item. Give children who do not want to perform the opportunity to contribute in other ways (e.g. creating a set, organizing the running order and timings, managing technical equipment, or creating posters and invitations).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Hold the show during class time, or arrange a special time for other members of the school community to attend. After the show, allow time for children to discuss how it felt to take part in the show. Encourage them to be proud of their contribution. Ask them to write a paragraph reflecting on the project, and expressing their feelings about it. Explore careers in the arts (mix of individual, group, and class) In small groups, ask children to research different types of job in the arts (e.g. museums, theatre, music, fine art, dance, etc.). Encourage them to look at technical jobs (e.g. stage manager) as well as artistic ones. Individually, prompt children to create a list of questions to ask someone who has a career in the arts. Then practise conducting interviews in pairs. If possible, organize a class visit to a local theatre, art gallery, music studio, or any other artistic business to talk to someone working in this field.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Explore careers in the arts (mix of individual, group, and class)

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Children then write individual reports about the visit/talk and careers that they have researched. A selection of these reports can be published in the school newspaper or website, if available. Attending and reviewing an event (class/individual) Take children on a visit to a theatre or festival to watch a play, dance production, concert, film, art exhibition, or other artistic event. Alternatively, arrange for a similar experience to take place in school (such as a theatre company visit, visit from a poet, or a showing of a film in the classroom). Try to make any event in school feel like a special experience (e.g. provide popcorn or other snacks with a film and provide ‘tickets’ for the show). Before the visit, ask children to research what they are going to see or hear (and the venue, if an external visit). Encourage them to predict what the experience will be like. After the visit, tell children to write a personal review of their experience. Prompt them to consider all the senses and to include sense verbs and adjectives in their review. Ask: Did you experience ‘flow’ during the event? How do you know? Encourage pupils to share their reviews with each other. Discuss any similarities and differences in how they responded to the event.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support in a small-group setting, to provide them with the tools to plan their work and make a confident start. Check on these children more frequently as they work. The research project can be completed in a small group, guided by an adult, with appropriately levelled materials chosen in advance. More able children can be asked to stretch themselves in writing tasks by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar in their work. At the end of each task, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures. These pupils should be encouraged to perform talents in the talent show which challenge them, such as writing and performing a long narrative poem.

Oxford additional tasks: Spend one lesson thinking more deeply about the links between self-expression and wellbeing. This may be a good opportunity to support children with expressing themselves in ways that support their mental and emotional health. Invite an external speaker to run a workshop, or follow appropriate educational programmes and lesson plans.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have  participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Independent reflection

Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Self-expression’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

Main activity

Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

A class talent show (individual/groups)

Invite children to contribute to a talent show which will be held towards the end of the week. Explain that they can take part as individuals or in small groups.

Provide sufficient time for children to choose what they will do in the show and to rehearse their item.

Give children who do not want to perform the opportunity to contribute in other ways (e.g. creating a set, organizing the running order and timings, managing technical equipment, or creating posters and invitations).
