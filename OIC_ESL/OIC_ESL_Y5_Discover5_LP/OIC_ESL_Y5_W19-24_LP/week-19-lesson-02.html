PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Designate four places in the classroom as ‘land’, ‘water’, ‘air’, and ‘space’. Ask children to think of a journey that explorers might take. Children then move to the relevant place in the classroom (such as ‘swim across the English Channel’ = water). Invite several volunteers to share their examples. Then repeat the game. See if children can think of a journey from a different category.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Tell children they are going to plan an exploring trip. They will think about what to do, what to take, and where to go. First, they will think about activities that explorers do. In pairs, read ‘Emily’s blog’. Ask children to choose the activity from the blog they think is the most exciting. They should explain their choice to their partner. Invite volunteers to share their choice with the class. Ask: What general equipment do you need for exploring? Do you need special equipment for your activity? Why? Discuss how proper planning and equipment reduces risk in dangerous environments.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Tell children to draw the outline of a suitcase or bag on their whiteboard. They then make notes (pictures and/or words) to describe what they need to pack. Provide dictionaries for support. Children can also support their partner. Teach the use of colons to introduce a list after an independent clause, using Oxford Discover SB 5 p.117 and your own subject knowledge. For example: I need special equipment to explore a river: a canoe, a life jacket, water, and sun cream. On our caving trip, I brought several things: a rope, a helmet, a lamp, food, and water.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: In pairs, read ‘Emily’s blog’. Ask children to choose the activity from the blog they think is the most exciting. They should explain their choice to their partner. Invite volunteers to share their choice with the class. Tell children to draw the outline of a suitcase or bag on their whiteboard. They then make notes (pictures and/or words) to describe what they need to pack. Provide dictionaries for support. Children can also support their partner.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: See if children can turn their packing notes into one or more sentences, using a colon as shown above. Share examples as a class. Discuss and correct common mistakes. In their pairs, ask children to discuss where they want to do their activity (a country, or a place in their own country). Tell children they are now ready to create a single-page ‘planning journal’ for their trip. This can be in any format, but should have several sections, including: Activity, Place, and Equipment. Children can add other sections if they wish, as well as illustrations, maps, and any photos left from Week 19, Lesson 1. They should include their list of equipment in a sentence, introducing it with a colon. The planning journals can be started in this lesson and completed at the beginning of Week 19, Lesson 3.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a simple template (see ‘Additional information’ page) and encourage them to use annotated illustrations. Challenge more able children to choose a real-world challenge (such as taking part in the Vendée Globe sailing race). They can spend some time researching the challenge, the environment, and equipment before writing. 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a blog about exploring. They have learned how to use colons to introduce a list after an independent clause. They have used these inputs to write a plan for an exploring trip.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
