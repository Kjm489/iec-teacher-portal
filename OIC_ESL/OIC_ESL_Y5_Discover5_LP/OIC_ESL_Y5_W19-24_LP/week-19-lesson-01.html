PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Show children the photos in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.18–19, 46–47, 92–93, and 108–109; and/or any another photos which display a range of explorers and explored locations in modern or historical times. Use them to communicate enthusiasm and curiosity about exploring our incredible planet. Ask: What places can we explore? Why do people explore? Invite children to give their first thoughts. Encourage them to think about exploring in their daily lives as well as ‘bigger’ themes such as adventure sports, science, nature, or history.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Source a listening text about children exploring their city, or use the suggested listening script to read aloud. Ask children to listen for the ways exploring is mentioned in relation to Southampton and to Lisa’s activities. Tell them to write notes on their whiteboard. Check answers as a class. In pairs, ask children to talk about places they like to explore in their own city or town. Share results of the pair discussion as a class. See if children can talk about times they might have seen their city (or its surroundings) in new ways, either by exploring a new place, or looking at it with a new point of view. Encourage children to think about exploring on a very small level (such as noticing wild flowers in a street).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Divide children into small groups. Encourage them to change their point of view by thinking about the bigger global context for exploration. Revisit the questions: What places can we explore? Why do people explore? Provide each group with a large sheet of paper, scissors, glue sticks, and marker pens. Share photos of explorers and explored locations. Invite children to stick photos that inspire them to the paper. They then annotate them with different types of destination, methods of exploration, and reasons for exploration. Prompt them to think as widely as they can, and to collaborate on their answers. Discuss group answers as a class. Build on them to consider questions such as: Do you think exploring is important? Why?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Do you think all travellers are explorers? Do explorers always have to discover somewhere new? Does exploring have to be dangerous? What risks do explorers take?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, before the listening activity, give them more guidance on what to expect from the text as well as key vocabulary to listen for. Challenge more able children to think about how exploration might help or harm the world (e.g. positive: finding new plants to use in medicine, negative: causing physical damage to important habitats). Additional information: Week 19, Lesson 1

Exploring Southampton

Hi! I’m Lisa. I live in Southampton, which is a city on the south coast of England. Southampton is a very busy port. In the past, lots of ships left Southampton to explore the world. You probably know one of the most famous ships which left from Southampton: the Titanic!

Today, many people leave Southampton on huge cruise ships. Cruises are a very popular way to explore new places on holiday.

I prefer to be an explorer here in my city. I often explore the parks. In summer, there are music events and festivals here. In the city centre, there are many shops and restaurants to visit.

If you want to explore the area around Southampton, you can take a ferry to the Isle of Wight. The Isle of Wight is famous for dinosaur fossils. It is sometimes called ‘Dinosaur Island’ because so many fossils have been found there!

In the summer holidays, I explore the beaches with my family, looking for fossils. We haven’t found any fossils yet – but I hope we will!
