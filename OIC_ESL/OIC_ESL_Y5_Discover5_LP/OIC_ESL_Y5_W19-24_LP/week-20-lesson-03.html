PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Teach the word ‘maze’. Show examples on the board. (These should have a choice of paths and at least two entry/exit points.) Model navigating a simple maze. Divide children into pairs. Give each pair a sheet of dotted paper. Give them 5 minutes to create their own maze. Remind them that there must be at least one possible path from entry to exit. Then tell pairs to swap papers. See if children can solve the maze. Show an example of a ‘labyrinth’ on the board. Explain that this is a type of maze with only one path, from an entry point to the centre. It isn’t a puzzle like a maze.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Use the story in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.112. First, tell children to read the text for gist. Check understanding of the main events. Next, ask children to look more closely at the characters in the story. Focus on themes of self-motivation and leadership. Encourage children to explain their reasoning. Useful questions might include: What are the characters’ reasons (motivations) for exploring the [maze]/[mountain]? What problems did the characters have? How did they solve them?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Describe how the characters’ feelings change throughout the story. Why do you think they feel this way? What goal or reward motivates you to explore? How do you keep yourself motivated during a difficult task? Which character do you relate to in the story? Who do you think is the leader? Why? Divide children into groups of three or four. Ask each group to make a list of ways to be a good leader in an exploring activity. Invite them to use the events and dialogue in the story for inspiration. Take feedback. Draw out deep thinking from the class about qualities needed to be a good leader. Encourage children to be considerate of each other’s perspectives. Ask:

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into groups of three or four. Ask each group to make a list of ways to be a good leader in an exploring activity. Invite them to use the events and dialogue in the story for inspiration. In a team, can different people be leaders at different times?

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Do you think everyone can be a good leader? How can we help people to be good leaders? How does it feel to be a leader? In a team, can different people be leaders at different times? Would you like to lead the activity in the story? Why / Why not?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children using Oxford Discover WB, read the story together, explaining vocabulary and checking understanding of each paragraph before moving on. Create mixed-ability groups for the leadership group task. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write a paragraph about a real-life leader that they admire. Encourage them to explain the qualities they see in this leader.

Oxford additional tasks: Tell children a version of the story of the Minotaur from Greek mythology. Create a ‘human maze’ in a large, open space. All children except one stand in a grid formation and raise their arms to form ‘paths’. The remaining child sees if they can find a path through the maze.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a text about an exploring trip. They have used the text to think more deeply about their own self-motivation and the qualities needed for leadership.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
