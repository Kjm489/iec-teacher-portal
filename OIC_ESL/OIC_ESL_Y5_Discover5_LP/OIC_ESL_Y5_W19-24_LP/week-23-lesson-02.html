PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Write on the board: ‘When I grow up, I want to be a millionaire.’ Ask children to discuss whether they agree with this statement with a partner. Then take feedback. Elicit children’s reasons for their choice. Tell children that ‘grow up’ is a special kind of verb. See if they can tell you why (it is a phrasal verb).

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Review the use of phrasal verbs, in particular those linked to money and household chores. Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.139. Write a sentence using each phrasal verb on the board. Use ‘put aside’, ‘put away’, and ‘put on’ to show how the preposition changes the meaning of the verb. Suggestions: Money: ‘save up’ (synonym of ‘save’), ‘put aside’, ‘pay back’, ‘splash out (on)’;

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Chores: ‘wash up’, ‘put away’ (toys, shopping), ‘put on’ (the laundry, the dishwasher), ‘take out’ (the rubbish), ‘tidy up’, ‘hang out’ or ‘hang up’ (the clothes). Invite children to tell you which of these phrasal verbs they do, or have done, themselves. Divide children into pairs. Ask them to tell their partner one phrasal verb that they do always, usually, sometimes, and never.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into pairs. Ask them to tell their partner one phrasal verb that they do always, usually, sometimes, and never. Play a memory game. Give each pair a set of flashcards. Ask them to put them face-down on the table. One child turns over two flashcards. If they match (such as ‘put’ and ‘away’), they say a sentence using that phrasal verb and keep the cards. If not, they turn them face-down again. Then their partner has a turn.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Play a memory game. Give each pair a set of flashcards. Ask them to put them face-down on the table. One child turns over two flashcards. If they match (such as ‘put’ and ‘away’), they say a sentence using that phrasal verb and keep the cards. If not, they turn them face-down again. Then their partner has a turn. Observe children as they play the game. Identify common mistakes. As a class, discuss common mistakes made during the game. Remind children that everyone makes mistakes and it is good to share and learn from them.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, add pictures to the flashcards. Put them in a pair with a more able child. As an extension activity for more able children, ask them to look up one or more of the following words in the dictionary: ‘set’, ‘get’, ‘give’, ‘call’, ‘stand’. They should find two or three different phrasal verbs for each and write their definitions.

Oxford additional tasks: Extend learning of phrasal verbs using Oxford Discover WB 5 p.128.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned a limited range of phrasal verbs to talk about money and chores. They have practised using them in sentences by playing a matching game.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Why use money?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by using a limited range of phrasal verbs linked to the unit topic. They will practise these in a matching game. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
