PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Explain that today’s lesson will focus on drama. Invite children to warm up by imagining they are explorers. Give them an environment to explore – such as mountains, desert, or rainforest – and ask them to mime being an explorer in that environment. Then invite children to suggest different environments. Include a local environment, such as ‘our city’ or ‘our school’. Ask: What factors did you think about when deciding how to act? (e.g. temperature, landscape, equipment used) Which environment was the most difficult to imagine?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Divide children into mixed-ability groups of three or four. Explain that they are going to create a short scene in which they are explorers. For inspiration, children can draw on their planning journals from previous lessons, or use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.57 (camping trip), pp.92–93 (sailing across the oceans), and p.126 (a mountain hike)

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Write a list of questions that children will need to ask themselves on the board: e.g. Who are you? Where are you? What are you doing? What equipment do you need? Ask: What is the main idea? Give children an opportunity to share exciting or dangerous things that might happen on an exploring trip. Write them on the board. Tell children that they are in the stretch zone. There are many things to consider when creating a scene. Write five categories on the board: ‘Story’, ‘Vocabulary and grammar’, ‘Performance’, ‘Clear speech’, ‘Confidence’. Remind them to think about each of these.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Invite each group to present their scene to the class. Evaluate the activity. Pair each group with another. Refer to the stretch zone categories on the board. Each group tells their partner group two things they did well, and one thing that they could improve.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Allow enough time for groups to plan their scene. Observe and support as they work. Invite each group to present their scene to the class. Remind children that if they feel worried while waiting to perform, they should focus on their breathing and try to let their thoughts go. The classroom is a supportive environment. Evaluate the activity. Pair each group with another. Refer to the stretch zone categories on the board. Each group tells their partner group two things they did well, and one thing that they could improve.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to include a strong story arc in their performance (referencing the story mountain structure from Week 17). Encourage less confident children to perform by reminding them that they are exploring their own abilities. Confidence is a skill they can build by practising over and over. Consider forming drama groups based on friendship groups where possible.

Oxford additional tasks: Practise and film the scenes to share with the school community. Watch and review a short film about explorers. Search online for ‘kids short films explorers’.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: In small groups, children have created and performed a scene about exploring.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Exploration’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by creating a short drama scene in a small group and performing it to the class.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
