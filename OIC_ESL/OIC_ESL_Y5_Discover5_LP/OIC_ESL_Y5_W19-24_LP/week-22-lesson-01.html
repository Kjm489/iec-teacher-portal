PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Show one or more photos to represent the unit topic of money. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.130–131, and/or your own photos. Divide children into pairs. Give them 2–3 minutes to discuss: What is money? What can you do or buy with money? What do we call money in our country? Do you know any money from other countries? Share answers as a class.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do a listening exercise to introduce money vocabulary. You can use the Big Question wrap-up video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.139. Ask children to listen for the following information and to make notes on their whiteboard: what we use money for,

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: different ways to pay, how people get money. On the first listening, see what children can understand. Elicit or explain the meaning of new vocabulary. Then listen again. See if children can add to their notes. Play a team bartering game.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Play a team bartering game. Divide the class into groups of three. Give each child two random flashcards. Ask them to compare what they have with their group members. Try to ensure each group has multiples of some items. Write all the available items on the board. Tell children that each group will ‘barter’ (exchange something for something) with the others for items they need. Ask them to predict whether this will be easy or difficult, and to explain their reasoning. Children then walk around the classroom and try to barter the items they have for something else they need. Give them a strict five-minute time limit. Then each team checks what they have against the list on the board.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Divide the class into groups of three. Give each child two random flashcards. Ask them to compare what they have with their group members. Try to ensure each group has multiples of some items. Write all the available items on the board. Tell children that each group will ‘barter’ (exchange something for something) with the others for items they need. Ask them to predict whether this will be easy or difficult, and to explain their reasoning. Children then walk around the classroom and try to barter the items they have for something else they need. Give them a strict five-minute time limit. Then each team checks what they have against the list on the board. Discuss children’s experience of the game. Ask: Was your prediction correct? Are you happy with the items you have? Is using money easier than bartering? Why? / Why not?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, discuss the meaning of key vocabulary before the listening exercise. To challenge more able children, personalize the game. Give each group six to twelve blank flashcards to create their own items. Give some groups the categories of food, clothing, and medicine. Give other groups the categories of animals, shelter, and tools. They then have 5 minutes to barter with other groups.

Oxford additional tasks: In pairs, discuss the three unit questions at the top of Oxford Discover WB 5 p.122. Make a mini vocabulary book for the unit. Start to add vocabulary to it and make sentences using new words.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned new vocabulary about money in a listening activity. They have thought about bartering and using money by playing a bartering game.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Why use money?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by listening for key words to expand their vocabulary related to the unit topic. They will have the opportunity to think further about the usefulness of money by playing a team game. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
