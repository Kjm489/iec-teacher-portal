PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Explain that today’s lesson will look at explorers’ diaries. Ask: Have you ever written a diary? Why do you think people write diaries? Help the class to understand that diaries can be a good tool to use when your mind begins to worry. Writing down how you feel about events that have happened (or will happen in the future) can help you to understand your feelings and be calm.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Give children two diaries to read and compare. Teachers can use ‘An explorer’s diary’. For the second text, search online for ‘explorers’ diaries reading text KS2’, or use Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.120–121, or choose one to two pages from another levelled reading book in diary format. Divide children into pairs and distribute the reading text handouts. Ask pairs to read them, and circle or highlight any features of each text which are common in diaries. Check comprehension. Ask: Who is the author of each diary? Why do you think they wrote their diary? Who is the intended audience? (usually, the author)

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Discuss features of diaries, referring to children’s highlighted examples. Features include: Date First person subject pronouns (I, we) Verbs in the past tense Description of events Description of feelings

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Would you change your own diary to make it less truthful, if you knew someone else was going to read it? Give children time to discuss the moral dilemma in small groups. They can then vote. Take feedback on the voting. Discuss any reasons why children may have voted differently.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Written in sections over a period of time (Optional) drawings and photos Discuss any other similarities or differences children find between the texts. Challenge children to think about when it is appropriate to publish someone’s diaries. Ask a question which poses a moral dilemma, such as: Would you publish someone’s diary if you knew it described exciting or important events, but the author talked about others in a hurtful way? Would you change your own diary to make it less truthful, if you knew someone else was going to read it? Give children time to discuss the moral dilemma in small groups. They can then vote. Take feedback on the voting. Discuss any reasons why children may have voted differently.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability pairs. Consider discussing the features of diaries before children read the text rather than afterwards. Alternatively, provide support in a small group led by an adult. I arrived at the airport early this morning, ready for an adventure. My backpack was very heavy, but I was worried that I had left something important at home! Today I met the other members of the expedition. I was nervous because I had never seen them before, but everyone was very kind and friendly. I think I will become good friends with James. He is the leader of the expedition, and he is very funny!

After two days of training (I didn’t have any time to write!) now we are starting our expedition to the summit of the mountain.
