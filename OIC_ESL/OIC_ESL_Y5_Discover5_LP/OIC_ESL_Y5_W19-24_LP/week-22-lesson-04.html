PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Write five to six short anagrams on the board, with clues. Use vocabulary learned in the unit so far. Examples: a story = BAFEL (fable); we use them to pay = OSNIC (coins). Give children 2 minutes to solve as many as they can. Share answers as a class. Remind children that regularly revising vocabulary helps their long-term memory.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: This lesson will focus on improving children’s writing in three steps: effective language, meeting activity requirements, and accuracy. Children should understand that these are standards they can apply to any piece of work. Step 1: Effective language. Revise ways to make creative writing more descriptive and engaging using adjectives, adverbs, and comparisons. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.137 (adjectives and adverbs), p.139 (adverbs of degree), and p.147 (similes). Ask children to find two or three sentences in their fable where they can put this learning into practice.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Step 2: Meeting activity requirements. In pairs, ask children to give each other constructive feedback. Remind them to give and receive ideas and opinions with respect. Children should check that the activity requirements have been met by their partner: The story has a clear moral. The characters’ attitudes towards money are clear and different to each other. The characters’ actions lead to consequences, which are directly linked to the moral.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Step 2: Meeting activity requirements. In pairs, ask children to give each other constructive feedback. Remind them to give and receive ideas and opinions with respect. Children should check that the activity requirements have been met by their partner:

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: The story is divided into logical paragraphs. Step 3: Accuracy. Remind children about the importance of checking grammar, spelling, and punctuation. Throughout the lesson, encourage children to identify their own mistakes and to think about how to improve. Remind them that the ability to independently improve their work is an important life skill. At the end of the lesson, allow time for children to read their improved fables aloud, in small groups or to the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them more specific guidance about places they can make improvements. Use a highlighter to mark frequently made errors, repetitive language, or a sentence where an adverb could add extra detail. Challenge more able children to compare the style of their writing to that of the fable used in Week 22, Lesson 2, or any other fables available (such as levelled readers). See if they can use those fables to improve their own.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.126–127. Write and illustrate a final draft of the fable for display.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed the use of adjectives, adverbs, and comparisons for effect. They have checked, edited, and improved their fables from Week 22, Lesson 4.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Why use money?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by improving their fables from Week 22, Lesson 3, in terms of effective language, activity requirements, and accuracy. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
