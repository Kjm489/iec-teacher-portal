PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Give children around 10 minutes to complete their planning journal pages, if required. Alternatively, repeat the wellbeing survey from Week 2, Lesson 1. Ask: Are you feeling good? Are you tired? Are you hungry? Are you ready to learn? Do you feel calm? As you ask each question, perform an action which children must copy if they answer ‘Yes’ (such as tap your head, pat your stomach, close one eye). Then invite children to take a minute to close their eyes and breathe slowly. Remind children that they learn best when they have slept well, eaten well, and come with a positive attitude to learning.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Review the use of the present perfect tense using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.13. Model and elicit examples from children related to experiences of exploring. For example: I have (never) explored rainforests in South America.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Have you canoed on the Amazon River? Explain that by practising the present perfect tense regularly, children are building their long-term memory of this learning. Play a game. Divide children into pairs. Ask each child to list four things they have done on their whiteboard. Three of these must be false, and only one must be true. Each child then tells their partner their four facts. Their partner guesses: e.g. Have you climbed a mountain? – Yes, I have! Then swap roles. Play again in new pairs if time allows. Then teach the use of similes to compare two nouns, using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.147. Model and elicit examples from children related to experiences of exploring. For example:

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Play a game. Divide children into pairs. Ask each child to list four things they have done on their whiteboard. Three of these must be false, and only one must be true. Each child then tells their partner their four facts. Their partner guesses: e.g. Have you climbed a mountain? – Yes, I have! Then swap roles. Play again in new pairs if time allows. Play another game. Divide children into groups of three to four. Give each group 12 simile template cards. Ask each group to complete the cards with suitable adjectives in the first space. They should leave the second space blank.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: The river was as cold as ice. My auntie was as brave as a lion. Play another game. Divide children into groups of three to four. Give each group 12 simile template cards. Ask each group to complete the cards with suitable adjectives in the first space. They should leave the second space blank. In their groups, children take turns to collect a card until all the cards have been taken. To collect a card, children expand on their sentences from the first game. For example, if a child said ‘I have trekked across the desert’, they can now repeat their sentence and add ‘It was as hot as the sun’ to collect the card ‘as hot as _____’.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create a list of suitable adjectives and possible nouns for comparison together on the board, before they complete the simile template cards. Challenge more confident children by encouraging them to think of more unusual similes.

Oxford additional tasks: Create an imaginary list of achievements for ‘the Greatest Explorer in the World’ using the present perfect tense and similes. Complete the similes practice in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.137.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed the present perfect tense and used it to describe experiences as explorers. They have then expanded on their sentences using similes.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

YEAR 5 Exploration
Week 19, Lesson 3: Talking about experiences 
Learning outcomes: 5.2b, 5.2d, 5.2f

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Exploration’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by refreshing their knowledge of the present perfect tense and applying it to the unit topic. They will have the opportunity to create and play games in pairs and small groups. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
