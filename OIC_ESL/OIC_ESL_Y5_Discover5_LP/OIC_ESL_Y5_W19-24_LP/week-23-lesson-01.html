PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Divide children into small groups. Give each pair/group eight to ten photos of cheap, mid-range, and expensive items. Suggestions: a mid-range home in your city, an expensive home in a global capital city, a carton of milk, a bag of oranges, a video game, a mid-range car, a beach holiday, a computer, two cinema tickets, and a pair of trainers. On separate slips of paper, give them the prices of each item in your local currency. Give children a few minutes to match the photos to the prices. Then check answers. Ask: Which items are cheap? Which items are expensive? Did any of these prices surprise you?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Introduce the speaking activity. Tell children we can use ‘I want to …’ and ‘I need to …’ to discuss future money plans. Elicit the difference between ‘want’ and ‘need’. You can refer to Oxford Discover SB 5 p.138. For example: I want to save money for a guitar. What about you? I need to invest money for my future education.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Invite children to use these phrases to tell a partner one thing they want to save money for now, and one thing to invest in for the future. Then write two headings on the board: ‘short-term plans’ and ‘long-term plans’. Explain this means things we want to do or buy now or later in life. Add one or two examples to each column (e.g. a new video game, a new book, a holiday, a car). Give children a few minutes to make personal notes on their own whiteboard. Then take feedback. Add children’s ideas to the columns on the board. Divide children into groups of three or four. Ask them to research how much each of the items on the board costs, using websites, catalogues, and flyers. Tell them to consider a range of prices. They should agree a reasonable price for each item. Give each group a different colour whiteboard marker. Tell them to add the price they would pay to each item on the board.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Invite children to use these phrases to tell a partner one thing they want to save money for now, and one thing to invest in for the future. Divide children into groups of three or four. Ask them to research how much each of the items on the board costs, using websites, catalogues, and flyers. Tell them to consider a range of prices. They should agree a reasonable price for each item. Give each group a different colour whiteboard marker. Tell them to add the price they would pay to each item on the board.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Discuss the results. Talk about the time it might take to save or invest for these items. Tell children that spending priorities can be different for different people. Ask them to think about their top three priorities from the ‘short-term’ and ‘long-term’ columns. Then do the initial speaking activity again. See if children can expand on their previous sentences about their wants and needs. Finish the lesson with a quick ‘True or False?’ definitions quiz to check understanding of new vocabulary.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a picture dictionary of common items. Challenge more able children by giving them a realistic monthly budget. Ask them to divide it into amounts for food, clothes, housing, transport, and entertainment using real-life prices. Invite them to add any other categories of spending they can think of.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a spreadsheet to plan monthly savings for different items. Give children a list of statements: e.g. I want to buy a pair of trainers in six months. They cost $48. How much do I need to save every month? Ask them to add their own ideas to the spreadsheet. Review adverbs of degree using the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.129.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have talked about future financial plans using want to and need to. They have researched the cost of different items that the class wants or needs now and in the future.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
