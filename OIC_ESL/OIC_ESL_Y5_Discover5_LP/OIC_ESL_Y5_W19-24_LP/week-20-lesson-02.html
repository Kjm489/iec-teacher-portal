PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Divide children into pairs. Give them 2 minutes to write a list on their whiteboard of any vocabulary they know linked to the topic of exploration. Ask each pair to count the words they wrote. Then introduce a physical task: 1–5 words = 5 star jumps 5–15 words = 10 star jumps 15+ words = 15 star jumps Invite each pair to share some vocabulary. Write a selection on the board. Remind children of other vocabulary which could be useful in interviews, particularly linked to explorers’ thoughts and feelings (e.g. ‘confidence’, ‘dilemma’, ‘diary’, ‘goal’, ‘risk’).

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Play a short video about the daily activities of an explorer. Use a video which has a lot of visual interest, and clear speech and/or closed captions for support. On the first play, ask children to make notes on their whiteboard about the gist. Ask: Who is in this video? Where are they? What are they doing? Play the video again. Tell children to look for detail, such as the explorer’s goals, special equipment, or their thoughts and feelings. Draw out as much vocabulary as you can. Form groups of three or four. Ask them to make a list of questions they would like to ask the explorer(s) in the video. Challenge them to use the present perfect tense in some questions. Examples:

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: How many documentaries have you made? Have you ever seen an iceberg? Share answers as a class. Write some example questions on the board. Model an interview by inviting one or two volunteers to ask you questions. Use topic vocabulary and the present perfect tense, where appropriate. Model the use of short answers (taught in Week 14). Then build on them with further information.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Observe children as they talk. Praise those that you see actively trying to incorporate topic vocabulary, short answers, and/or the present perfect tense. As a class, give children the opportunity to share any questions or comments they have following the speaking activity.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: e.g. Yes, I have. I think I have seen more than fifty icebergs! Explain that by practising topic vocabulary, short answers, and the present perfect tense, children are building their long-term memory of this learning. Divide children into pairs. Give each child 5 minutes to play the role of interviewer or explorer. Then swap roles for another 5 minutes. Observe children as they talk. Praise those that you see actively trying to incorporate topic vocabulary, short answers, and/or the present perfect tense. As a class, give children the opportunity to share any questions or comments they have following the speaking activity.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, do the interviews in a small group, with an adult acting as the explorer. The adult then asks each child a question, and supports their response. Challenge more able children to give longer answers in response to open questions. Support less confident children in the interviews by encouraging them to make full use of their notes from the introductory and group activities.

Oxford additional tasks: Record the interviews. Children give and receive constructive feedback on their work.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used a video about (an) explorer(s) to inspire their own interviews. They have practised topic vocabulary, short answers, and the present perfect tense.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
