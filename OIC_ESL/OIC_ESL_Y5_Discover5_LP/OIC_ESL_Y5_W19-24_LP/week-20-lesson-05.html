PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Divide children into teams of four. See if they can recall the features of a diary, reviewing their learning from Week 20, Lesson 1. Give them several minutes to make a list. Then check answers as a class. Write the features of a diary on the board. The team with the most correct answers is the winner.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: The whole lesson will be used for writing the explorer’s diary. Give children the creative freedom to present their work in a personal way, while respecting the main features of a diary. Encourage them to illustrate their diary, add photos, or personalize it in any other way they wish. Set up a creative table with photos and other materials from Week 20, Lesson 4 which can be cut up and stuck into the diaries, as well as art materials such as colour pens and pencils, scissors, and glue sticks.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Remind children to include information on their (or their chosen character’s) thoughts and emotions. Ask: How do you react to the events that happen to you? What feelings do you want – or need – to share in your diary? Remind children of the importance of planning and managing their time effectively. Tell them how long they have for this task, then ask them to estimate how much time they need for drafting and illustrating each day’s entry. Remind them that it is their responsibility to monitor the time.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: During the lesson, observe and support children with their writing. Encourage them to use descriptive language, including similes, in their work. At the end of the lesson, invite children to share their diaries in small groups.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask: If you feel worried during the task, what can you do? (take a few deep breaths, stretch out, have a drink of water, ask someone for help) During the lesson, observe and support children with their writing. Encourage them to use descriptive language, including similes, in their work. At the end of the lesson, invite children to share their diaries in small groups.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide the resource packs from Week 20, Lesson 4. Encourage them to use the vocabulary banks and sentence starters. Support less confident children by providing a suggested timeline for drafting and illustrating each day’s entry. Observe them at regular checkpoints throughout the lesson. Encourage more able children to provide as much detail as possible. They should focus deeply on the thoughts and feelings of the explorer they are writing as. They may use a dictionary or thesaurus to find more interesting synonyms for basic emotions (such as ‘petrified’ instead of ‘scared’).

Oxford additional tasks: Read the diaries aloud in small groups, or to the class. Use expression and intonation to bring the diaries alive.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written and illustrated an explorer’s diary.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Exploration’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using prior learning about the features of a diary, as well as their own notes, to organize and write a diary.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
