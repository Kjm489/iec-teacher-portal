PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Why use money?’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: Money management (class/individual) Arrange a visit to a local bank, or invite a representative from a local bank or money management charity to visit the class. Ask them to make a presentation about how to manage money responsibly. Before the visit, provide information about the bank or charity to the class. Tell children to prepare any questions they have about: how bank accounts work saving and spending

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: budgeting. During the visit or presentation, encourage children to ask questions and to make notes. After the visit or presentation, discuss what children have learned. Tell them to write an illustrated leaflet to teach other children about managing money. A play about money (groups) Review the ethical themes discussed through fables in Week 22 (such as greed, and generosity). In groups of four to six, children write a new fable with a moral about money, then adapt it into a script for a play. Encourage children to organize this project themselves, thinking about allocating different roles fairly, and monitoring time available for writing and rehearsing.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: At the end of the week, each group performs their play. Other members of the school community can be invited to watch the plays. Prompt each group to explain the ethical value behind their play, and why the moral of the play is important to them. Divide children into pairs. Ask each pair to design and make a board game which uses money in any way they wish. Each pair then invents, makes, and tests their own game. Encourage them to think about incorporating vocabulary and life-skills they have learned in the unit.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Children may also use or make any relevant props, costumes, or scenery. At the end of the week, each group performs their play. Other members of the school community can be invited to watch the plays. Prompt each group to explain the ethical value behind their play, and why the moral of the play is important to them. Money board games (pairs) Divide children into pairs. Ask each pair to design and make a board game which uses money in any way they wish. First, encourage children to research and test out existing board games which use money for inspiration. Each pair then invents, makes, and tests their own game. Encourage them to think about incorporating vocabulary and life-skills they have learned in the unit. Other children can be asked to test it, to ensure it works as planned. Each game is then improved and finalized. At the end of the week, hold a board game session when all the games can be played by the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support in a small-group setting, to provide them with the tools to plan their work and make a confident start. Check on these children more frequently as they work. For the money management task, they may prefer to make a poster with less text, rather than a leaflet. Create mixed-ability groups and pairs for the play and the board game tasks. More able children can be asked to stretch themselves in writing tasks by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar in their work. At the end of each task, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures. For the board game task, these children should be encouraged to create a game with more text than others (such as making their own general knowledge or True/False questions).

Oxford additional tasks: Show children the Big Question wrap-up video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.139. See if they can identify things that they have learned during this unit. Allow them to share some of their thoughts and achievements from the Independent Reflection task with their classmates.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Complete Oxford source activity text — Year 5, Week 24

Independent reflection

Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Why use money?’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

Main activity

Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

Money management (class/individual)

Arrange a visit to a local bank, or invite a representative from a local bank or money management charity to visit the class. Ask them to make a presentation about how to manage money responsibly.

Before the visit, provide information about the bank or charity to the class. Tell children to prepare any questions they have about:

how bank accounts work

saving and spending

budgeting.

During the visit or presentation, encourage children to ask questions and to make notes.

After the visit or presentation, discuss what children have learned. Tell them to write an illustrated leaflet to teach other children about managing money.

A play about money (groups)

Review the ethical themes discussed through fables in Week 22 (such as greed, and generosity).

In groups of four to six, children write a new fable with a moral about money, then adapt it into a script for a play.

Encourage children to organize this project themselves, thinking about allocating different roles fairly, and monitoring time available for writing and rehearsing.

Children may also use or make any relevant props, costumes, or scenery.

At the end of the week, each group performs their play. Other members of the school community can be invited to watch the plays. Prompt each group to explain the ethical value behind their play, and why the moral of the play is important to them.

Money board games (pairs)

Divide children into pairs. Ask each pair to design and make a board game which uses money in any way they wish.

First, encourage children to research and test out existing board games which use money for inspiration.

Each pair then invents, makes, and tests their own game. Encourage them to think about incorporating vocabulary and life-skills they have learned in the unit.

Other children can be asked to test it, to ensure it works as planned. Each game is then improved and finalized.

At the end of the week, hold a board game session when all the games can be played by the class.

Additional task

Show children the Big Question wrap-up video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.139. See if they can identify things that they have learned during this unit. Allow them to share some of their thoughts and achievements from the Independent Reflection task with their classmates.
