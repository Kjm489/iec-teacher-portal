PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display photos of two to four different explorers, contemporary or historical, on the board. Aim for a mix of nationalities, ethnic backgrounds, and gender. Ask: What do you think these explorers’ stories might be? Tell children a brief biography of each explorer. See whether they were correct in their predictions.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Tell the class that they are going to write their own explorer diaries over two lessons. Today will cover research and planning, while next lesson will allow time for writing and illustration. Expectations for length and content can be matched to ability levels. Provide children with resources which they can use for inspiration. Place the materials at several ‘stations’ around the classroom. Ideas for suitable resources: The photos and biographies of the explorers from the introductory activity Their own planning journals from Week 19 Photos used in Week 19, Lesson 1 The diaries from Week 20, Lesson 1

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: The videos from Week 20, Lesson 2 Newspaper articles, magazine articles, and books about famous explorers Reference websites suitable for Year 5 Examples of creative diaries (search online for ‘creative diary layouts images’) Give children the whole lesson to explore the resources, to research explorers, and to make notes about their diaries. Children can work individually or in pairs. Provide dictionaries for support. Set questions for children to think about as they work.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Halfway through the lesson, pause for reflection. Encourage children to share their ideas with a partner and to give constructive feedback. Invite several volunteers to share their plans with the class. Discuss any common questions or problems.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: What information will you need to fill your diary? Are you writing as a real explorer, a fictional explorer, or yourself? Are you exploring a real or fictional place? How does this change your research? How will you set out your diary on the page? Observe children as they work. Praise those who manage their time well, stay focused, share resources, and assist their peers. Ensure that they are making notes. Halfway through the lesson, pause for reflection. Encourage children to share their ideas with a partner and to give constructive feedback. Invite several volunteers to share their plans with the class. Discuss any common questions or problems. At the end of the lesson, remind children to continue to think about their diaries before the next lesson.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create themed packs of resources to choose from, such as mountain climber, rainforest explorer, or Arctic explorer. Include levelled texts, a large number of photos, vocabulary lists, and sentence starters. Encourage them to write as themselves. Encourage more able children to plan their diary from the viewpoint of a person other than themselves (and perhaps a real-life explorer).

Oxford additional tasks: Recall the features of a diary. Make a list to compare with a partner.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used a range of resources to research and plan an explorer’s diary.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Exploration’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and writing skills by using a range of real-world texts and photos as inspiration to plan an explorer’s diary. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
