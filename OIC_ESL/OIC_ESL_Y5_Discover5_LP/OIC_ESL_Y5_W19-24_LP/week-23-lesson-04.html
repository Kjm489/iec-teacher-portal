PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: On the board, display photos of healthy foods including vegetables, fruits, meats, bread, pasta, rice, and other ingredients. Ask children to look at the food and think of a recipe they could make. They can list the ingredients on their whiteboard. Invite volunteers to share their ideas with the class. Explain the meaning of the words ‘waste’, ‘use up’, and ‘leftovers’. See if children can identify ‘use up’ as a phrasal verb. Ask: How can we use up leftovers to avoid wasting food? (e.g. we can store food in the fridge, we can make new recipes) Why do you think we are talking about food waste in a unit about money? (e.g. we can save money by not wasting food)

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Children are going to conduct a survey about reducing food waste to save money. They will collect data and present it to the class. As a class, create a list of possible survey questions related to food waste, and saving money. They can be Yes/No, or multiple choice questions. See suggested questions.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Explain how to use a tally chart to keep a record of answers and a pie chart (or bar chart) to show the results of a survey. Use your own subject knowledge, and/or your own examples of charts. Divide children into groups of three or four. Each group should allocate questions between themselves. Children then ask each other the questions, or if possible, ask adults in the school (including kitchen staff, if the school has these facilities). Each child keeps a tally chart of the responses.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into groups of three or four. Each group should allocate questions between themselves. Children then ask each other the questions, or if possible, ask adults in the school (including kitchen staff, if the school has these facilities). Each child keeps a tally chart of the responses. Each group then reviews their tally charts and creates pie charts of the results, using either spreadsheet software and a printer, or protractors and paper. (Bar charts may also be used if children have not yet completed the relevant learning in Maths.) Give children from each group the opportunity to present their results to the class.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Each group then reviews their tally charts and creates pie charts of the results, using either spreadsheet software and a printer, or protractors and paper. (Bar charts may also be used if children have not yet completed the relevant learning in Maths.) Give children from each group the opportunity to present their results to the class. Discuss: Do you think we can save money by reducing food waste? What ideas would you recommend to your family or friends?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, pair them with more able children to make their pie charts. As an extension activity for more able children, ask them to find out the actual savings from waste reduction (e.g. the cost of a bunch of over-ripe bananas in a banana cake). Encourage less confident children to present the results of their group by giving them sentence starters to use, such as ‘In this pie chart, we can see …’ or ‘The results of this question show that …’. Additional information: Week 23, Lesson 4 Suggested survey questions How often do you waste food? o every week o sometimes o never Which food do you waste? o fruit o meat o vegetables Do you make a shopping list? Do you check what food you already have before going shopping? Which food do you often buy too much of? o fruit o meat o milk Do you use leftovers? Do you think you can save money on food? Would you like to know more about saving money by reducing food waste?

Oxford additional tasks: Make a poster to highlight how reducing food waste can save money.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have conducted a survey about food waste and presented the results.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
