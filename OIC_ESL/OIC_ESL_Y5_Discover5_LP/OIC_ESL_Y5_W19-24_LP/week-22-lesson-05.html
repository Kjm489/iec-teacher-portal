PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play ‘spend or save?’. Display five or six logos of local, national, and international banks or shopping companies on the board. Remove the bank or company name if possible. In pairs, children decide whether the logo is for a shop (‘spend’) or a bank (‘save’). For bonus points, see if children know the name of each bank or company. Check answers as a class. Children say ‘spend’ or ‘save’ for each example. Then check bank and company names. The winner is the pair with the most correct answers. Tie-break question: What does [X company] sell?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do a listening activity about using money. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.138 exs.A–B. Check understanding of key vocabulary. Hold a short class discussion. For each question, allow children to discuss their answer in pairs. Then take feedback. Ask: Do you think it is easier for most people to spend money or to save it? Why?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: What things would you like to save money for? Is it important to donate money? What charity would you choose to donate to? Allow children to also talk about items they have already bought or saved for, or charities they have donated to, if they wish. However, ensure that no child feels left out because of their family circumstances.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Observe and talk to children as they make their money boxes. Check understanding of the topic and elicit their individual ideas about managing money. Encourage the class to show each other their money boxes. Ask: Would you (or will you) spend, save, invest, or donate money from your box? Reassure children that they do not need to share their answer, but take feedback from any who do wish to share.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Make a money box. Give each child a money box (net of a cube) template printed on card. Encourage them to decorate each side. They then cut a slit in the top to put coins and notes in. Finally, they cut out and stick the box together. (For inspiration, search online for ‘make a money box cube’.) Observe and talk to children as they make their money boxes. Check understanding of the topic and elicit their individual ideas about managing money. Encourage the class to show each other their money boxes. Ask: Would you (or will you) spend, save, invest, or donate money from your box? Reassure children that they do not need to share their answer, but take feedback from any who do wish to share.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Decorate shoeboxes as an alternative to cutting and sticking card templates.

Oxford additional tasks: Make an illustrated poster to explain basic money management terms with the headings ‘spend’, ‘save’, ‘invest’, and ‘donate’. Find out how to open a savings account for children where you live.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned the difference between spending, saving, investing, and donating. They have made a money box.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Why use money?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by identifying main information in a text about money. They will improve their personal finance knowledge by discussing different financial decisions.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
