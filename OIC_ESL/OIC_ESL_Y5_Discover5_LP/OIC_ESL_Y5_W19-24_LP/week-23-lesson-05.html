PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play a short video clip to introduce the lesson topic in a visual way, with pictures of different notes and coins. Search online for ‘Money from around the world kids video’ or ‘currencies of the world kids’. Explain the term ‘currency’. Ask: What currency do we use in our country? Have you ever used another currency? When and where did you use it?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Provide children with a wide range of input materials about world currencies (see ‘Materials’ for this lesson). If using the internet, direct children to use the search term ‘money around the world kids’ to find suitable websites. Hand out a world map template to each child.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Give children fifteen to twenty minutes to find out about different world currencies. Encourage them to make notes and sketches on their whiteboard or in their book. Tell them to mark at least two or three countries they want to write about on their map templates, then encourage them to find out an interesting fact about each currency. Allow 20–30 minutes for children to annotate their world map with information about currencies in at least two or three different countries. They can write, draw, and stick photos of coins and notes on and around the map. Remind children that when writing in this format, sentences should be very short and clear.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: During the planning and writing steps, encourage children to take mindful moments to pause and reflect on their work so far. At the end of the lesson, give children an opportunity to share their maps with each other and/or display them on the classroom wall.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: During the planning and writing steps, encourage children to take mindful moments to pause and reflect on their work so far. At the end of the lesson, give children an opportunity to share their maps with each other and/or display them on the classroom wall.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a map with countries already marked on it. Consider providing country-specific resource packs with facts and photos for that currency. Challenge more able children to find out the history of the euro and which national currencies preceded it. Encourage less confident children in their research by providing a list of recommended websites which clearly organize information by country/currency (such as in table formats).

Oxford additional tasks: Explain the term ‘exchange rate’. Challenge children to find out how much one local unit of currency is worth in other countries.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have researched world currencies based on their individual interests. They have created an annotated world map to illustrate their research.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Why use money?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using a range of input and their world knowledge to create a simple, annotated world map.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
