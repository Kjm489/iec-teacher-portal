PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display the picture in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.170–171 or a picture of the city/townscape where you live. Explain that the unit ‘Where we live’ will build on the previous unit ‘Extraordinary buildings’ by looking at homes in different locations, as well as how we get around in the place we live. Put children into pairs to discuss: Two advantages of homes or transport where they live (e.g. large houses, clean buses) Two disadvantages of homes or transport where they live (e.g. expensive houses, crowded trains)

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do the listening activity about traditional houses in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.186. Tell children they will hear information about homes in Japan. Find out what they already know about Japanese homes. Ask them to predict what they might hear. Then do exs.A–B. Play a matching game. Divide children into groups of five or six. Give each group a world map.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Ask them to locate the country or countries they heard about in the listening activity. Hand out sets of pictures of traditional houses and encourage groups to discuss which country these houses might be from. They can place them on the map. Discuss groups’ decisions. Elicit reasons for their choices. Then check answers. Hold a class discussion. Draw out deeper thinking about why traditional housing varies across the world. Questions to consider:

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into groups of five or six. Give each group a world map.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: What different materials are houses built from? Why might they be different? (e.g. availability, building skills, suitability for the climate) What natural weather or climate factors might affect the type of houses? (e.g. extreme heat or cold, situated next to water or forests, possibility of earthquakes) Why is housing an important part of culture? (e.g. self-expression, houses are beautiful as well as useful, shared community identity) Do you think housing design has become more similar across the modern world? Explain why or why not. (e.g. skyscrapers in most modern cities, but differences in design)

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, pause the audio after each sentence and repeat it slowly. Define any difficult vocabulary. Challenge more able children by asking them to think about the different types of housing in their own city, and compare them to the houses described in the lesson.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a sentence about each traditional house from the listening or matching activity. Find out what type of building design is best for earthquake zones.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened for information about traditional houses, then matched houses to countries on a map. They have discussed why traditional houses vary in different places.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
