PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Write five to six anagrams on the board, with clues. Use vocabulary learned in Weeks 31 and 32. Examples: water around a castle = TOMA (moat); a person who designs buildings = CATCHITER (architect). Give children two minutes to solve as many as they can. Share answers as a class. See how much vocabulary children can recall from the unit. Invite each child to share one or two new words they remember. Prompt them using pictures in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.170–180, or any other pictures used in the lessons so far. Through this revision exercise, children are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Read the story ‘The Mystery of Peril Castle’ in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.182–183. Read the story aloud as a class. Model the first few paragraphs. Then ask different children to read each subsequent paragraph.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Stop frequently to check understanding. Define key vocabulary. Ask children questions about the story so far, and what they predict might happen. Ask children to complete the activities in SB 5 p.184 in pairs. Check answers as a class.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: When asking children to read aloud, prompt them to notice if they feel worried about the task. They should take a deep breath; try to relax; and read slowly, carefully, and clearly. Remind the class to be supportive of their peers. If time allows after the main activity, encourage children to practise reading a section of the text aloud to each other in pairs. Focus on using correct pronunciation and adding expression.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: When asking children to read aloud, prompt them to notice if they feel worried about the task. They should take a deep breath; try to relax; and read slowly, carefully, and clearly. Remind the class to be supportive of their peers. If time allows after the main activity, encourage children to practise reading a section of the text aloud to each other in pairs. Focus on using correct pronunciation and adding expression.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, summarize the key points of the story or poem before you read it together. Encourage less confident children to read aloud by reading each sentence yourself and asking them to repeat it after you. Fast finishers can write their own short story or poem about a mysterious building.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.181 before moving on to the main activity. Challenge children to retell the story or poem(s) from the main activity in their own words. They can tell a partner and/or write a short recount.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read about a mysterious building.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Extraordinary buildings’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by reading aloud a longer text. They will read for pleasure as well as for understanding.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
