PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Write ‘the world’s most extraordinary buildings’ on the board. Review the meaning of this phrase. Ask: What do you think makes a building ‘extraordinary’? Note ideas on the board. In pairs, give children two minutes to write on their whiteboard any extraordinary buildings they know in 1. their locality, 2. their country, 3. other countries. Invite children to share their examples and any facts they know about them. Optionally, ask children to indicate to the location of their example on a world map.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do a reading activity. Children should use context, peer-support, and dictionaries to work out any unknown words. When they have finished, read aloud and summarize each paragraph to the class. Invite children to ask about any parts they are unsure of.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Use the text in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.174–175. In this lesson, direct children to read everything except the section on Zaha Hadid. This will be covered in Lesson 4. Put children into pairs to ask and answer questions about the text. Before they begin, elicit two or three example ‘What’, ‘Where’, ‘When’, ‘Who’, and ‘Why’ questions from the class to support less able children: e.g. What does the building in (Ohio) look like?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Remind children to support their partner with positive thoughts and words during the pair activity. At the end of the lesson, ask again: What do you think makes a building ‘extraordinary’? Ask children to review the notes on the board from the introductory activity. Encourage them to share any further thoughts with a partner. Then develop ideas through class discussion.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Remind children to support their partner with positive thoughts and words during the pair activity. At the end of the lesson, ask again: What do you think makes a building ‘extraordinary’? Ask children to review the notes on the board from the introductory activity. Encourage them to share any further thoughts with a partner. Then develop ideas through class discussion.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, read the text in a small group with adult support. Encourage less confident children to create and ask questions by writing the question words ‘What’, ‘Where’, ‘When’, ‘Who’, and ‘Why’ on the board. Tell them to choose one of these words and then to find a relevant fact in the text they can link to it (e.g. ‘Where’ can be used for questions about places). As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write a short paragraph about another extraordinary building, in the same style as the reading text.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 5 p.165 ex.C. Choose one of the buildings from the introductory or main activity. Find out more information about it using internet sources.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a non-fiction text about extraordinary buildings and architects. They have asked and answered about the text in pairs.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
