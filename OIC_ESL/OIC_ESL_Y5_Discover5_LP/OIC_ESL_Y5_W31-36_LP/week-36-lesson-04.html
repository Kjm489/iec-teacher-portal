PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Where we live’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to at the end of the school year to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: A city project board (small group) Support children to make a project display board to introduce their own city/town, or a capital city in another country, including information about how to get around. They must follow these steps: Research information about the city, interesting buildings and homes, transport options, and directions to famous landmarks. Decide how to organize the information and what photos or drawings to include. Draw and label a map. Put together the project board. Plan and give a presentation explaining the information on their city project board. Each member of the group should contribute.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Outdoor skills (class/individual) Organize a class trip to an outdoor centre where children can practise map-reading or orienteering skills. Alternatively, make a map of the school grounds and run a map-reading or treasure hunt activity within the grounds. Before the visit/activity, ask children to think about why map reading is important for outdoor activities. Give them time to research which sports and activities require map-reading skills. After the visit/activity, ask children to write an account of what they did. They should describe any map-reading skills they used, as well as the route they decided to take. They may draw a map to illustrate their account. Put children into pairs to talk about their experience, using their map to help them recount it. Display the written accounts and maps for other members of the school community to see. The Unusual Homes Agency (small group)

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: A city project board (small group) Plan and give a presentation explaining the information on their city project board. Each member of the group should contribute. The Unusual Homes Agency (small group) At the end of the week, support groups set up their ‘shop’ around the classroom. Explain that one child from each group will visit the other agencies, while the rest of the group will work in their ‘shop’ to try to sell an unusual home to their visitors. Divide the time available into parts to give each child an opportunity to play seller and buyer.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Briefly discuss the process of buying and selling homes in your country. Ask children to imagine an agency which only sells very unusual homes. Put children into groups of four or five to decide on a variety of unusual homes to sell in their agency. Encourage groups to make a ‘shop’ display by making posters and leaflets about their homes for sale. They should also make a grid map of the city, where they can add the location of each home. At the end of the week, support groups set up their ‘shop’ around the classroom. Explain that one child from each group will visit the other agencies, while the rest of the group will work in their ‘shop’ to try to sell an unusual home to their visitors. Divide the time available into parts to give each child an opportunity to play seller and buyer. To add further interest, ask each buyer to write down a list of requirements before they visit the agencies. Sellers will have to understand their buyers’ needs, read facial and body language, and use their persuasion skills to sell their homes. The buyer should also ask the seller for directions using the map: e.g. How do I get from the houseboat to the theatre?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support in a small-group setting by providing them with the tools to plan their work and make a confident start. Check in with these children more frequently as they work. Create mixed-ability groups for the city project board task. Allocate a member of staff to support children during any outdoor skills visits. Practise useful dialogue in advance for the final part of the unusual homes agency project. More able children can be asked to stretch themselves in writing tasks by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar in their work. At the end of each task, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures. These children should be encouraged to support their peers throughout the week, acting as mentors and guides for speaking and writing activities in particular.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a short class quiz to check understanding of key grammar and vocabulary.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
