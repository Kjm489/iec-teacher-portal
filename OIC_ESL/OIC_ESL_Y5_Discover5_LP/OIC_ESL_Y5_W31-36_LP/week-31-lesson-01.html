PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: To generate enthusiasm for the ‘Extraordinary buildings’ unit, play a video showing different types of buildings. Use the Big Question 9 video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.171 ex.A, or search online for ‘iconic world buildings video’ or ‘amazing buildings video’. Look at the picture in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.170–171, or display a picture of a cityscape with a variety of buildings. Ask children to describe what they see to a partner.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do the listening and sorting activity in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.172 exs.A–B to expand children’s topic vocabulary.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Ask children to listen for the words emphasized by the speaker to help them identify important information which supports their understanding of the new vocabulary (e.g. ‘sports’ / ‘stadium’; ‘ships’/’rocks’ / ‘lighthouse’).

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Hold small-group discussions. Put children into groups of four or five. Tell children to discuss the questions in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.171 ex.C and p.172 ex.C. Ask them to make notes on their whiteboard. Then share responses as a class, grouping short answers to each question on the board.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Hold small-group discussions. Put children into groups of four or five. Tell children to discuss the questions in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.171 ex.C and p.172 ex.C. Ask them to make notes on their whiteboard. Then share responses as a class, grouping short answers to each question on the board.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide adult support for the small-group discussion. Challenge more able children to make real-world connections, such as identifying buildings in their city which correspond to each building type.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.162–163 (optionally, before the small-group discussions). Write sentences using new vocabulary for buildings. Use photos or online resources to compare children’s own town or city with another in a different part of the world. Ask the class: What similarities and differences in the buildings can you see?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned vocabulary for different types of building. They have discussed why buildings are important.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Extraordinary buildings’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by using the speaker’s speech patterns to support their understanding of new vocabulary. They will practise using new vocabulary by sharing their ideas about the unit in a small group. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
