PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Draw a line divided into seven equal parts on the board. Write ‘Sunday’ in the first part. Invite six children to come to the board and write the other days of the week. Circle today’s day. Ask children how they would describe this day, the day before, and the day after (today, yesterday, tomorrow). Ask: Which is your favourite day of the week? Why? What activities do you do on that day?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Review different tenses used for the past (simple and continuous), present (simple and continuous), and future forms (will and going to). Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.185. For more able classes, you may wish to review a longer list of tenses. Put children into pairs to ask and answer questions about what they did yesterday, what they are doing today, and what they are going to do tomorrow. Prompt them to use appropriate tenses and forms. Display three pictures of homes to represent past, present, and future building designs. Alternatively, ask children to look at the pictures in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.180 (past), 170 (present), and 176 (future).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: In their pairs, ask children to use each picture prompt to discuss homes in different time periods, using suitable tenses and forms. They do not need to describe the picture itself. They may begin each sentence with ‘In the past/present/future …’. For example: In the past, some people lived in castles. In the present, architects design attractive skyscrapers.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Elicit sentences from the discussion task. Write them on the board. Praise children for managing multiple tenses and forms, while also focusing on common errors. Invite children to think about where they have gone wrong and why. Ask children to reflect on their knowledge of tenses and forms. They should identify any areas to work on. Invite them to set themselves a challenge to achieve by the end of the review week (Week 36). Discuss possible examples and write them on the board – for instance, practise for five minutes at home every day or write 10 sentences using past tenses. Children should note their personal challenge and log their progress throughout the week.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: In the future, everyone is going to live in buildings shaped like bubbles! Elicit sentences from the discussion task. Write them on the board. Praise children for managing multiple tenses and forms, while also focusing on common errors. Invite children to think about where they have gone wrong and why. Ask children to reflect on their knowledge of tenses and forms. They should identify any areas to work on. Invite them to set themselves a challenge to achieve by the end of the review week (Week 36). Discuss possible examples and write them on the board – for instance, practise for five minutes at home every day or write 10 sentences using past tenses. Children should note their personal challenge and log their progress throughout the week.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work with a small group to review tenses in greater depth, while the rest of the class completes the discussion tasks. Challenge more able children by including a longer list of tenses.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete one or more of the activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.174–175.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed grammar used for the past, present, and future. They have used this to talk about yesterday, today, and tomorrow; and about homes of the past, present, and future.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Where we live’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and reading skills by consolidating their learning of past and present tenses and future forms to describe different times.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
