PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play one or two short video clips of Zaha Hadid’s structures. Search online for ‘Sheikh Zayed Bridge video’, ‘London Olympics Aquatics Centre video’, or any other Hadid design. Elicit children’s opinions of the bridge or building. Ask: What do we call someone who designs buildings and bridges? (an architect) Put children into pairs to recall facts about architects from Week 31, Lesson 3. Share answers as a class.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Read about or research the architect Zaha Hadid in the text ‘Zaha Hadid, Architect’ in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.175. Ask: Does Zaha Hadid inspire you? Would you like to be an architect? Why / Why not? Give opinions about architecture in a paired speaking task. Ensure each pair can see the pictures of buildings and bridges from Lessons 3 and 4.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: On the board, write ‘My (least) favourite design is … because …’. Look at the pictures as a class. Elicit one or two adjectives and descriptive phrases for each picture. Write them on the board. Ask children to discuss their preferences with their partner, using the sentence starters and descriptions on the board for support.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ensure each pair can see the pictures of buildings and bridges from Lessons 3 and 4. Ask children to discuss their preferences with their partner, using the sentence starters and descriptions on the board for support.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: They may also refer to Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.14 and 22 for useful phrases to support their conversation. Observe children as they talk. Support less able pairs. Extend more able children’s conversation skills by asking them to make connections with their wider learning. Encourage children to reflect on the speaking task. Draw a smiley face, a neutral face, and a sad face on the board. Ask them to assess their own performance for confidence, vocabulary, and accuracy. Discuss any difficulties they had. Suggest and elicit strategies for improvement.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, read the text in a small group with adult support, or create mixed-ability pairs for the research task. Challenge more confident children to use the word ‘least’ with other adjectives: e.g. The least attractive building is … because … Fast finishers can compare the buildings in Lessons 3 and 4 with other buildings they know, using phrases like ‘is similar to’ and ‘reminds me of’.

Oxford additional tasks: Make a poster or online presentation about Zaha Hadid. Discuss the skills you might need to become an architect. Find out the steps necessary to train to be an architect. Look at university or careers websites for information.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a biography of the architect Zaha Hadid. They have discussed their preferences of architecture in pairs.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Extraordinary buildings’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by expanding on their knowledge from Week 31, Lesson 3, then sharing opinions with their peers.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
