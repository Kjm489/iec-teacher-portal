PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display a simple map of your city or local area on the board. Ask: How do we get around? Give children a few minutes to make notes on their whiteboard of all the different ways to ‘get around’ (travel around) their local area. Take feedback as a class. Elicit information about the ways children usually get around in their daily lives.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Before the lesson, write 12 to 20 quiz questions on the topic of ‘How do we get around?’. This is a good opportunity to review relevant facts and vocabulary from previous learning about wheels and exploration. In addition, try to include questions that cover useful information for your class about getting around your local area. You may choose to include a picture round and/or an anagram round (i.e. four to five pictures or anagrams of types of transport for children to identify). Ideas for topics to cover: Types of transport and their locations (e.g. cable car – mountain) Daily transport routines Distances between places in your city

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Distances between cities or countries Cost of transport tickets in your city Which number of bus/train/tram to take from your school to another landmark Rules for walking safely in your city (e.g. crossing the road)

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Hold the quiz. Divide children into teams of four or five. Ask each team to nominate a team leader. This should be someone who can lead their peers towards agreement on a shared answer in a fair and respectful way. Then ask them to nominate another team member to write down their answers. Encourage all team members to participate fully. At the end of the quiz, check answers. Give one point for each correct answer. Ask each team leader to say their team’s score. The team with the most points is the winner!

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Environmental impact of different types of transport World Records in travel (e.g. shortest time to travel from London to New York) Hold the quiz. Divide children into teams of four or five. Ask each team to nominate a team leader. This should be someone who can lead their peers towards agreement on a shared answer in a fair and respectful way. Then ask them to nominate another team member to write down their answers. Encourage all team members to participate fully. Observe teams during the quiz and praise children for examples of effective and inclusive teamwork. At the end of the quiz, check answers. Give one point for each correct answer. Ask each team leader to say their team’s score. The team with the most points is the winner!

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, say each question twice, slowly. Additionally, create a presentation with each question written clearly on a slide. Encourage less confident children to take part in team discussions, and remind more confident children to include them. Award bonus points to teams who demonstrate full participation and inclusive behaviour.

Oxford additional tasks: Challenge children to create more quiz questions. They may use their local knowledge, non-fiction books, or the internet to find facts. Give them time to practise asking their partner questions using correct intonation.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have taken part in a team quiz on the topic ‘How do we get around?’.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
