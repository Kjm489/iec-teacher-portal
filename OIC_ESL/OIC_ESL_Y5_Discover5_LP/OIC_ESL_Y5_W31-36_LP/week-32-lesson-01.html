PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display pictures of two homes on the board, very different in style to each other. Ask children to move to one side of the classroom if they prefer one, and to the other side if they prefer the other. Elicit reasons for their choices. Repeat with two more sets. Suggestions for pictures: castle, mansion, houseboat, yurt, eco-home with a grass roof, traditional European cottage, small city apartment, large city loft, igloo, farmhouse.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Review the use of definite and indefinite articles using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.177. For example: I visited a castle yesterday. The castle was very old. It was an ancient building. It’s next to a village. / It’s next to the village where Misha lives. Identify and explain the difference between ‘a’ and ‘an’ using the above examples, the text in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.174–175.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Then discuss the differences in use between ‘a’ and ‘the’ using the above examples, the text in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.174–175. Hold a small-group discussion based on the main lesson topic. Put children into groups of three or four. Provide each group with a set of pictures to prompt discussion. Ask children to ask and answer based on the question: Where would you like to live?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Hold a small-group discussion based on the main lesson topic. Provide each group with a set of pictures to prompt discussion.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.178 for support if required. Remind children to pay attention to accuracy in the use of definite and indefinite articles. Display one set of pictures around the classroom. Instruct children to stand next to their favourite home, then to share the reasons for their choice with others who chose the same picture. Encourage them to find similarities and differences in their reasoning. Ask: Why do you think we are comparing our favourite homes? Thinking about the advantages and disadvantages of different homes, as well as identifying personal preferences, helps children to understand what is important to them in their own lives. They can begin to think about the characteristics of homes they might prefer as an adult. Give children time to draw their dream home.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, spend more time on the review of definite and indefinite articles instead of the creative task. Use Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.166–167 or alternative resources for support. Challenge more confident children to lead their small-group discussion. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to create a listing for their dream home for an estate agency website.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to describe their dream home to a partner. Look at an English language estate agency website. Explore the listings to find a favourite home for sale. Read the listing and identify familiar vocabulary. Complete the grammar exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.166–167.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed the use of definite and indefinite articles. They have discussed the merits of different homes, then drawn their dream home.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Extraordinary buildings’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by reviewing their understanding of definite and indefinite articles. They will develop their speaking skills by building on their discussion of preferences from Week 31.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
