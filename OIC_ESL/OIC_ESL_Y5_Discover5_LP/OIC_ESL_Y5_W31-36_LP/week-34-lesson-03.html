PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Put children into pairs. Ask them to sit back-to-back. Give one child in each pair a picture of a house. The other child has their whiteboard and pen. They must not look behind them. Tell the child with the picture to describe it to their partner, who will draw what they hear. Stop the task after a few minutes for pairs to compare the drawing to the picture.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Tell children that today they are going to write a descriptive text about several homes. First, remind them of the types of descriptive language they have learned in Year 5. Add examples of each to the board. You may wish to cover, for example: sense verb + adjective: The bricks on this house feel rough. adverbs of degree: This palace is extremely beautiful. similes: The skyscraper is built as straight as an arrow.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: giving examples: for example, for instance, such as ‘–ing’ adjectives: I think this treehouse is fascinating. order of adjectives: Here is a small, round, clay hut. Challenge children to set themselves a realistic target for the number of different types of descriptive language they will include in their text.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Allow enough time for checking and correction in pairs. Children should read through their own and their partner’s work, and identify whether there is a sufficient variety of descriptive language. They can underline or highlight examples they find. Remind them to also check capitalization, punctuation, and spelling.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Display all the pictures of local and traditional houses from Week 34, Lesson 1 around the classroom. Give children a few minutes to decide which houses they will describe. They should choose a familiar style of home from your area, and one or two unusual houses. Dedicate most of the lesson to planning and writing. Tell children to generate ideas and make notes about each house. Then encourage them to use their notes to write one or two paragraphs of detailed description, including their opinion of each house. Allow children to get out of their seats during the writing task to review the pictures on display and add to their ideas. Allow enough time for checking and correction in pairs. Children should read through their own and their partner’s work, and identify whether there is a sufficient variety of descriptive language. They can underline or highlight examples they find. Remind them to also check capitalization, punctuation, and spelling. If time allows, invite volunteers to read their descriptions to the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide extra copies of pictures which they can glue onto a piece of paper and then annotate. Work together to identify key words and phrases to describe their chosen pictures before they write their text independently. Challenge more able children to compare the houses they have chosen and support this with evidence: e.g. Our local homes are more beautiful than this log cabin because we have carved wooden shutters around the windows. Its windows are plain.

Oxford additional tasks: Write up a final copy of the descriptive text. Illustrate it with pictures of the buildings. Encourage children to look up pictures of their school building, and a school in a different country, then describe them to a partner. What is similar and different?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed descriptive language devices. They have written and checked a text describing two or more varied homes.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
