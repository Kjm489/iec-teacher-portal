PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Tell children that, this week, Lessons 1 to 3 will cover giving directions and using maps. Find out if children have ever asked for directions. Allow them to share their experiences. Ask: When might we need to ask for directions? (e.g. visiting a new city, losing our way, the usual route is blocked) Ask: When we listen for directions, what is it important to do? (e.g. listen carefully, ask the person to repeat the directions, check we have understood them correctly) Ask: How can we stay safe when asking for directions? (e.g. ask a trusted adult such as a police officer or a shop worker)

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Teach key language needed to give directions using your own subject knowledge. For example: Go/Walk along the road/straight on until you get to the bus stop.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Turn left/right at the crossroads/roundabout/traffic lights. Continue past the shops/over the bridge/towards the park. Use the classroom, school gym, or a large outdoor space. Ask children to help you set up a large model ‘city’. Arrange the prepared set of large, folded cards with various place names around the space. If you have an outdoor space with a hard surface, use chalk to draw roads, bridges, and roundabouts. If you have a hard indoor floor, use easily removable masking tape to lay out the road system. Set up the traffic light cards.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Put children into pairs. Give every child one playing card. In each pair, instruct one child to stand next to the place they have been given on their card (e.g. park). Their partner must direct them to the place they have on their own card (e.g. supermarket). Then swap roles. After, they can return their cards and receive new ones. Remind children to support their partner and to use positive language to guide them towards the shared goal. They should also respect other pairs as they navigate around the ‘city’. After several turns, make this into a game. Explain that the child giving directions must now keep their card secret. They give their partner directions, who must guess which place they will reach at the end of their journey. Then swap roles and play again.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Put children into pairs. Give every child one playing card. In each pair, instruct one child to stand next to the place they have been given on their card (e.g. park). Their partner must direct them to the place they have on their own card (e.g. supermarket). Then swap roles. After, they can return their cards and receive new ones. Remind children to support their partner and to use positive language to guide them towards the shared goal. They should also respect other pairs as they navigate around the ‘city’. After several turns, make this into a game. Explain that the child giving directions must now keep their card secret. They give their partner directions, who must guess which place they will reach at the end of their journey. Then swap roles and play again.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give each pair a list of useful vocabulary for the task. Challenge more able children to give their partner the complete string of instructions before they begin to move. Their partner must try to get to the right location without further help.

Oxford additional tasks: Have spare, blank cards available (one large and four small per landmark). For the final part of the lesson, ask children to add their own landmarks to the city. Write a set of directions to direct someone between two points in your local area.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned vocabulary for giving directions. They have worked in pairs to give and follow directions around a model ‘city’.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

YEAR 5 Where we live
Week 35, Lesson 1: Giving directions
Learning outcomes: 5.1a, 5.1c, 5.2b, 5.2f

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Where we live’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to and providing clear and concise directions. They will have the opportunity to help each other to navigate a space in the classroom or outdoors.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
