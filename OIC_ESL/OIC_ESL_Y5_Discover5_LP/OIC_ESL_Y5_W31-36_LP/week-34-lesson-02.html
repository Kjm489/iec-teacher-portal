PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Review homophones. Write these sentences about homes on the board with options: These are my cousins. This is _______ new houseboat. (there/their) _______ traditional house is made of snow? (Who’s/Whose) ______ the tallest skyscraper in the world. (It’s/Its) There are many small apartments _____ in Tokyo. (here/hear) I think _______ going to love our new treehouse! (your/you’re) Tell children to write the correct word for each sentence on their whiteboard. Then invite volunteers to fill in the gaps on the board. (Answers: their, Whose, It’s, here, you’re)

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Share the following quote from the British designer, artist, and writer William Morris: ‘Have nothing in your houses that you do not know to be useful or believe to be beautiful.’ In pairs, give children time to discuss what they think this quote means.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Explain that Morris was talking about the key design terms ‘function’ (usefulness) and ‘form’ (the way something looks or is made). He believed everything must be useful or beautiful. Ask: Do you agree? Tell children to consider function and form in the unusual houses they read about in today’s lesson. Ask children to read the magazine article in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.178. Check understanding of key concepts and vocabulary, as well as similar words (e.g. ‘designs’/‘designed’, ‘shape’/‘shaped’, ‘place’/‘palace’, ‘a round (house)’/‘(turns) around’).

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Is each home useful, beautiful, or both? Why? What creative shapes would you use in your own designs? What problem would they solve? Would your designs be useful, beautiful, or both?

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Put children into new pairs to discuss: Is each home useful, beautiful, or both? Why? What creative shapes would you use in your own designs? What problem would they solve? Would your designs be useful, beautiful, or both?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, read the text in a small group with adult support. Encourage less confident children to think about problems they could solve in their unusual designs by creating a list together on the board first (e.g. a hot climate, a place with little sunlight, an area which often floods).

Oxford additional tasks: Match the houses from Oxford Discover WB 5 p.178 to the world map. Draw an innovative house design. Explain its function and form in a short paragraph.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed homophones. They have read and responded to a non-fiction text about unusual homes.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
