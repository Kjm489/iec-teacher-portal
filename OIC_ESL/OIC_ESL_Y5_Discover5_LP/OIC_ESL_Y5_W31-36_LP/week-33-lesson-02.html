PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Extraordinary buildings’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: Explore careers in architecture and construction (individual/group/class) In small groups, encourage children to research careers in architecture and construction. Individually, tell them to create a list of interview questions to ask someone who works in this field. They can practise conducting interviews in pairs. Organize a class visit to a local building firm or building site. Arrange for children to interview one or more staff members about their job.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Alternatively, use local connections to organize for a suitable visitor to come to the classroom. Ask children to write individual reports about the visit/talk and the staff interview(s). A selection of these reports can be published in the school newspaper or website, if available. Photography project (individual) Display and discuss unusual or powerful photography of buildings. Include photographers who find unusual angles or perspectives. Take children outdoors in the local area (or in the school grounds), with cameras and clipboards and paper, to observe buildings. Encourage them to take photos and to note down any aspects of the buildings that interest them. In the classroom, ask children to create a slideshow or a display book of their photos, and to describe the buildings using any form of descriptive language they know.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Explore careers in architecture and construction (individual/group/class) As a class, decide what buildings to include in your model city. Allocate one or more buildings to each pair. Children must negotiate between themselves to ensure all buildings are included.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Model city project (pairs/class) Display pictures or show a video about a model city or village for inspiration. As a class, decide what buildings to include in your model city. Allocate one or more buildings to each pair. Children must negotiate between themselves to ensure all buildings are included. Tell pairs to use junk modelling materials to make their building(s), and to write a label to describe it. They may use photographs or books for reference. Put together the class model city. Allow children to add details such as roads, trees, parks, or a beach using more junk modelling materials. Display the model city for other members of the school community to see. Ask children to identify whether they experienced ‘flow’ in any activities this week.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support in a small-group setting, to provide them with the tools to plan their work and make a confident start. Check on these children more frequently as they work. For the careers research, provide appropriately levelled materials chosen in advance. Allocate a member of staff to support this group during any off-site activities. More able children can be asked to stretch themselves in writing tasks by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar in their work. At the end of each task, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures. These children should be encouraged to support their peers throughout the week, acting as mentors and guides for speaking and writing activities in particular.

Oxford additional tasks: Watch the Wrap Up video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.189. Complete the review section in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.180–181. Alternatively, create a short class quiz to check understanding of key vocabulary.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
