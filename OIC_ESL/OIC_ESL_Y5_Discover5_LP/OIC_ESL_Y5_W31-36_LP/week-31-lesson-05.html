PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display a picture of a lighthouse in a beautiful seascape. Play sound effects of the ocean (search online for ‘ocean waves sounds’). Ask children to close their eyes. Invite them to let their thoughts go, relax, and pay attention to their breath. After one or two minutes, ask children to open their eyes.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Hold an introductory group discussion about lighthouses. Display three to four pictures of different styles and locations of lighthouses from around the world. Put children into small groups. Give them five minutes to discuss what they know about lighthouses, any predictions they have, and whether they have ever seen one themselves.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Invite each group to share their thoughts with the class. Identify any misconceptions or gaps in knowledge. Provide corrections or further explanations. Do the listening activity related to lighthouses in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.178 exs.A–B.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Hold an introductory group discussion about lighthouses. Invite each group to share their thoughts with the class. Encourage groups to act out a boat in a storm avoiding the rocks thanks to the lighthouse. Some children will be the ‘boat’, others the ‘storm’. A ‘lighthouse keeper’ in each group can use a torch. Play the calm ocean sound effects from the introductory activity, then add storm sound effects. Challenge each group to adapt their acting to what they hear.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Devise a short drama scene: Put children into small groups. Encourage groups to act out a boat in a storm avoiding the rocks thanks to the lighthouse. Some children will be the ‘boat’, others the ‘storm’. A ‘lighthouse keeper’ in each group can use a torch. Play the calm ocean sound effects from the introductory activity, then add storm sound effects. Challenge each group to adapt their acting to what they hear.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a transcript for them to follow while listening. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to research the history of lighthouses.

Oxford additional tasks: Make a junk model lighthouse using available recycled materials.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about the function of lighthouses. They have extracted key information from an interview. They have used their learning to devise a short drama scene.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Extraordinary buildings’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by extracting key information from an interview. They will have the opportunity to apply performance art to their learning.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
