PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Draw a simple road map on the board. Invite children to suggest places to add to the map. Use the opportunity to revise buildings vocabulary (e.g. ‘lighthouse’, ‘skyscraper’). Choose two places on the map. Ask a volunteer to say how to get from one to the other. Repeat several times. Invite children to share any experiences of map reading. Ask: Have you ever seen or used a map? What did you use it for? Why was it useful?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Teach phrasal verbs using the verb ‘go’ using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.179. Teach phrasal verbs of direction as well as one or two with different meanings (e.g. ‘go on’ [move on or continue]; ‘go back’, ‘go up’ [direction or price]; ‘go down’, ‘go across’, ‘go in’, ‘go out’). Plan a map-reading activity using online resources or realia. Ideas for the lesson:

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Look at a map together as a class. Identify and explain features such as grid co-ordinates, symbols, contour lines, the scale, and the key. In pairs or small groups, ask children to find the grid co-ordinates of different landmarks.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ask questions to check understanding: e.g. What is the symbol for a school on this map? Does the bridge cross the river in Town A or Town B? Discuss the information that should be included in a map. Talk about the information needed for different purposes (e.g. a city walking map, a national road map).

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask questions to check understanding: e.g. What is the symbol for a school on this map? Does the bridge cross the river in Town A or Town B? Discuss the information that should be included in a map. Talk about the information needed for different purposes (e.g. a city walking map, a national road map).

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, display the map(s) you are using for the main activity on the board. Children who find the questions difficult can come to the board and work through them together as a small group. As an extension activity for more able children, hand out one or more real-world map examples. Use a map of your own city and, if possible, a city in another country for comparison. Ask them to identify some or all of: grid co-ordinates, contour lines, symbols, a scale, and a key.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.168. Instruct children to draw a simple grid map, adding symbols, contour lines, a scale, and a key. Encourage them to tell a partner about their map using vocabulary from the lesson.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to read information on maps. They have learned phrasal verbs with ‘go’.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
