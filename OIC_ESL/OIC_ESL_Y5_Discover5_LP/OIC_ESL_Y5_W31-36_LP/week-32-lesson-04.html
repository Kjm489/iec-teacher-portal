PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Put children into groups of four to play a game. Say an object or a person in the classroom (e.g. ‘table’ or ‘teacher’). Challenge groups to write three adjectives which could describe the object or person on their whiteboard, and then to hold it up. The first group to identify three relevant and correct adjectives wins two points. Give bonus points to each team for any unusual or well-thought-out adjectives. Repeat for three or four more items. The group with the most points wins!

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Ask children to reflect on how confident they feel using adjectives (1 = not confident; 10 = very confident). Draw out their thinking about what they need to improve – for instance, a wider range of adjectives, how to use adjectives correctly, the logical order of adjectives. Teach the class how to improve their use of adjectives in two stages: Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.187 exs.D–E to show children how to create ‘–ing’ adjectives from verbs. For example: surprise / surprising; confuse / confusing; bore / boring Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.187 ex.F to show children how to put adjectives into the correct order when using more than one. For example:

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: The beautiful, tiny, green milk jug. The huge, ancient, Scottish castle. Put children into pairs to choose a building to describe from their book. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.170–183. Ask them to write adjectives on their whiteboard to describe their building. They may use a dictionary or a thesaurus for support. Encourage them to use an ‘–ing’ adjective. Then instruct them to choose two or three of their best adjectives and to put them into the correct order to describe the building.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Put pairs together to make groups of four. Tell each group to share their work, checking it for accuracy. Ask them to write the best descriptions on pieces of paper.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Repeat with two or three different buildings. Understanding and applying the order of adjectives is challenging and will need practice. Remind children that they are in the stretch zone. Put pairs together to make groups of four. Tell each group to share their work, checking it for accuracy. Ask them to write the best descriptions on pieces of paper. Display the best descriptions around the room. Ask children to walk around and read them aloud. Ask: Which description did you enjoy the most? Why? At the end of the lesson, ask children to reflect on their personal confidence level from the beginning of the lesson, and to consider whether this has changed. Remind them to continue to practise what they have learned to improve their confidence further.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a blank table with categories in each column for the order of adjectives, which they can fill in to describe their building. Fast finishers can make a poster listing all the ‘–ing’ adjectives they know, or have found in a dictionary/thesaurus, to display in the classroom.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.176–177, or provide additional grammar exercises for children to practise the order of adjectives.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned some adjectives ending in ‘–ing’ and the correct order of adjectives. They have applied this learning to the description of buildings.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Extraordinary buildings’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by evaluating and extending their confidence in using adjectives correctly.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
