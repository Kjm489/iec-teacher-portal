PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display pictures of a variety of beautiful or unusual tree houses. Elicit from the class that ‘tree’ + ‘house’ = ‘tree house’. Lead children in a simple warm-up activity, using building names from the previous lesson (see Week 31, Lesson 1) as a cue for actions. Call out each name and ask children to copy you. Repeat several times. Suggestions for actions: Tree house: Pretend to climb a ladder. Stadium: Pretend to play a sport (e.g. football or tennis). Skyscraper: Stretch up as tall as possible. Lighthouse: Turn on the spot, opening and clenching one hand to ‘flash’ a light. Office: Pretend to be typing energetically.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do an instruction sequencing activity. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.173 ex.D, or the instructions provided. Read the text to the class. Draw children’s attention to key words such as ‘first’, ‘next’, ‘then’, and ‘finally’. Identify the first instruction together. Then ask children to complete the exercise individually or in pairs. Check answers. Tell children to create their own plan for a tree house. Read out the following numbered instructions for them to follow. Ask children to recall all five instructions with a partner. Then check understanding as a class.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Take a piece of paper, a pencil, a ruler, and coloured pencils. Draw a picture of your tree house. Add any useful words to the picture.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Tell children to create their own plan for a tree house. Read out the following numbered instructions for them to follow. Ask children to recall all five instructions with a partner. Then check understanding as a class. Show and tell a partner.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Write a short list of instructions for building your tree house. Show and tell a partner. At the end of the lesson, hold a class vote on the most interesting tree house.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, draw their attention to useful vocabulary in the text which they can use. Then provide a short template for writing their own instructions. Challenge more confident children to present their instructions as a paragraph rather than as a numbered list. Encourage fast finishers to think about issues such as the suitability of different trees or materials, waterproofing, or security.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to talk to a partner about what living in a tree house would be like. How would they do daily tasks like washing or cooking? What fun activities could they do? Go outside into the school grounds (if there are any trees) or to a local park or woodland area. Assess the trees for suitability for a tree house.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used key words to sequence instructions for building a tree house. They have designed their own tree house and written a list of instructions to build it.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Week 31, Lesson 2: Building a tree house
Learning outcomes: 5.1h, 5.3g, 5.4d

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Extraordinary buildings’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening, reading, and writing skills by understanding and creating numbered lists of instructions. They will have the opportunity to be creative by making their own tree house design.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
