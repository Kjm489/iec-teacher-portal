PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Do a quick quiz about maps. Ask children to move to one side of the room if they think the answer is true, and to the other side if they believe it is false. Suggestions: Contour lines are used to show changes in height. (T) Symbols on a map must always contain a letter. (F) Car parks are often represented by the letter P in English. (T) Grid co-ordinates refer to an exact point on the map. (T) We use a scale to measure how long it takes to travel between two points. (F)

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Put children into groups of three or four. Give each group a large map of their local area. Ask each group to find and mark your school on the map. Then display a picture of a helicopter on the board. Play helicopter sound effects. Tell children that they are being dropped into the city at a random point. They must find their way back to school! Mark a point on each group’s map for them to be ‘dropped’ into. (Identify these before the lesson to ensure that they will work well with the activity.)

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Direct children to find a route between their drop-off point and their destination. They can use any route they like, including using transport networks. Each group should make notes for a list of directions. Draw on vocabulary from Week 35, Lessons 1 and 2. Tell each group to nominate one member to visit another group. That group will explain to their visitor how to get from their drop-off point to school. The visitor should follow the directions with their finger on the map. As a class, take feedback from the visitors. Ask: Did you find the directions clear and easy to follow? What feedback would you give the group? Now give each group access to online mapping and navigation tools. Ask them to use the online tools to find a route between their two points, then compare this route with the one they chose. Ask: If they are different, which one do you think is better? Why?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Put children into groups of three or four. Give each group a large map of their local area. Ask each group to find and mark your school on the map. Then display a picture of a helicopter on the board. Play helicopter sound effects. Tell children that they are being dropped into the city at a random point. They must find their way back to school! Mark a point on each group’s map for them to be ‘dropped’ into. (Identify these before the lesson to ensure that they will work well with the activity.) Direct children to find a route between their drop-off point and their destination. They can use any route they like, including using transport networks. Each group should make notes for a list of directions. Draw on vocabulary from Week 35, Lessons 1 and 2. Tell each group to nominate one member to visit another group. That group will explain to their visitor how to get from their drop-off point to school. The visitor should follow the directions with their finger on the map. As a class, take feedback from the visitors. Ask: Did you find the directions clear and easy to follow? What feedback would you give the group? Now give each group access to online mapping and navigation tools. Ask them to use the online tools to find a route between their two points, then compare this route with the one they chose. Ask: If they are different, which one do you think is better? Why? Ask each group to use their paper map and online tools to identify two or three alternative routes between their two points (e.g. quickest, shortest, best for walking).

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: On the board, invite children to share factors to consider when deciding a route (e.g. safety, speed, length of time, distance, environmental impact, cost [transport], attractiveness, traffic levels). Ask each group to use their paper map and online tools to identify two or three alternative routes between their two points (e.g. quickest, shortest, best for walking). During the lesson, model how to see things from another person’s perspective: e.g. I understand your point of view, but in my opinion XXX is a faster route because … During the lesson, praise children for working systematically to find solutions.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work through a simple example on the board before starting the group activity. Provide a list of useful vocabulary (directions, city locations, features of a map). Remind more confident children to support their less able peers during the group activity.

Oxford additional tasks: Discuss in pairs: A quicker route is always better than a longer route. Do you agree? Give each group a challenge: e.g. I am at school. I have 30 minutes to buy some pencils for my next lesson. How should I travel, where to, and what route should I take?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used a local map to find a route between two points. They have analysed alternative routes to explore their advantages and disadvantages.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
