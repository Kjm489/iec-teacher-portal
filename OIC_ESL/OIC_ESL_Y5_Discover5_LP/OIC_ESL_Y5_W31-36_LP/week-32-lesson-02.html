PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play two video clips of different castles (ruined or intact). Search online for ‘aerial video castle’. Then display three to four pictures of castles from different time periods and continents. Include a local castle if appropriate. Ask: Have you ever visited a castle? What was it like? Invite children to share their experience. Alternatively, find out whether the class would like to visit a castle, and why.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Teach vocabulary related to castles. Do Oxford Discover SB 5 p.180 ex.A, or use your own subject knowledge to teach the lesson’s vocabulary. See if children can work out some or all of the meaning of the compound words independently by breaking them into parts (e.g. ‘court/yard’, ‘draw/bridge’, ‘gate/house’). Challenge children to match the vocabulary to a picture or diagram of a castle. Complete Oxford Discover SB 5 p.180 ex.B.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Give children an opportunity to experiment with building their own castle using sand, modelling clay, cardboard boxes, or a choice of the three materials. Try to allocate at least half of the time available for this activity. Encourage them to include the features of a castle which they have learned in the lesson. For reference, you can use the pictures in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.180, as well as the castle pictures displayed in the introductory activity. Encourage children to find ways to solve common problems such as creating a working drawbridge or forming strong ramparts.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ask children to present their castle to a partner using new vocabulary.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Remind them that struggle and challenge can help the brain to grow. They probably won’t build the perfect castle on their first attempt! Ask children to present their castle to a partner using new vocabulary.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability pairs for each activity. Challenge more able children to think about the suitability of different building materials for a real castle, such as stone or wood.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.170–171. In pairs, ask children to take turns describing a feature of a castle. Their partner must guess the correct word from the definition given. Find out five facts about a local or famous castle. Use the internet or tourist information leaflets.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned and applied new vocabulary related to castles. They have made their own castle from sand, clay, or cardboard, and described it.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
