PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Tell children that they will be sitting down and writing for most of today’s lesson, so they need to be warmed up and energized. Ask them to suggest exercises for a simple warm-up routine, such as star jumps, stretches, and jogging on the spot.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Put children into pairs to find three pictures of homes to represent the past, present, and future on the internet. Remind them of safe search engine practices. They are going to use these three pictures as writing prompts, and plan and write three paragraphs. One paragraph will use past tenses, one will use present tenses, and the final paragraph will use future forms. Ask questions to stimulate thinking before writing. Suggestions: How can you describe this home? (Think about form and function.) Who built/is building/will build this home, and how? Who lived/lives/will live in this home? How did/do/will they live?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Where is/was/will this home (be) located? How do/did/will you get there? Collaborate with the class to create a short list of up to five success criteria for this writing activity. Suggestions: Correctly use past tenses, present tenses, and future forms. Write three paragraphs. Write in full sentences with correct punctuation, capitalization, and spelling. Link sentences together in a logical way within paragraphs.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Encourage children to identify their own mistakes and to think about how to improve. Remind them that the ability to independently improve their work is an important life skill. Invite volunteers to show their picture prompts and read their text to the class.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Use vocabulary learned in the units ‘Extraordinary buildings’ and ‘Where we live’. Children may refer to Oxford Discover SB 5 p.185 for grammar support. Allow 20 to 30 minutes for children to plan and write their text. Encourage them to be as creative as possible in their writing. In pairs, allocate enough time for children to check their work against the agreed success criteria. Allow a further five minutes for them to revise their text accordingly. Encourage children to identify their own mistakes and to think about how to improve. Remind them that the ability to independently improve their work is an important life skill. Invite volunteers to show their picture prompts and read their text to the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability pairs. Their partner can guide them in finding and describing appropriate homes before the writing task. During the writing task, support these children more closely. Ask them to draft a sentence on their whiteboard first. Check it together before they write it down. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to research and present common styles of homes and domestic life in the 19th, 20th, and 21st centuries in your city or local area. For further challenge, compare this with a location in another part of the world.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.174–175.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Using picture prompts, children have written a text about homes in the past, present, and future. They have incorporated a variety of past and present tenses and future forms.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
