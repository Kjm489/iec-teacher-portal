PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play an introductory video, ideally without commentary. This should show types of travel and transport in different countries. Search online for ‘transport around the world video’. Ask children to make a note of all the types of transport they see. Confident children can also note the country. Share answers as a class. Ask: Which of these types of transport do we use in our community?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: During the listening activity and follow-up activities, encourage children to take a few breaths before they respond to the questions asked. This will help to develop their attention and executive function. Use the text ‘Bicycles: Clean Transportation’ in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.44 as a listening text. Read only paragraphs 1, 5, and 6.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Read aloud one paragraph at a time. Ask questions to check understanding. Reread the paragraph. Then move on to the next. When you say a country or city, invite a child to find it on the map. Mark it with a pin.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Discuss the experience of using a bicycle in your city or local area in comparison with these cities. Ask: Do we have bicycle sharing programmes or cycle lanes? Is our city safe to ride in? What percentage of our class cycles to school? (Have a show of hands and ask children to calculate the percentage.) Put children into pairs. Ask: How can citizens help to make cycling safer in our cities? Then discuss different options as a class. Ideas: public campaigns, vote for supportive political parties, respect cycle lanes, organize cycle clubs and training programmes.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Display pictures of people riding bicycles in Montreal, Hangzhou, and/or Copenhagen. Discuss the experience of using a bicycle in your city or local area in comparison with these cities. Ask: Do we have bicycle sharing programmes or cycle lanes? Is our city safe to ride in? What percentage of our class cycles to school? (Have a show of hands and ask children to calculate the percentage.) Put children into pairs. Ask: How can citizens help to make cycling safer in our cities? Then discuss different options as a class. Ideas: public campaigns, vote for supportive political parties, respect cycle lanes, organize cycle clubs and training programmes.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able or less confident children, put picture prompts on the board for the discussion about ethics. Use pictures to represent the ideas suggested above. Challenge more able children by giving them a news article to read and respond to. This should cover a clean transport issue being debated locally, nationally, or globally.

Oxford additional tasks: Design a poster encouraging citizens to take part in a public campaign for clean transport.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have done a listening exercise about transport. They have compared it to transport in their own city or country. They have discussed how citizens can support clean transport.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
