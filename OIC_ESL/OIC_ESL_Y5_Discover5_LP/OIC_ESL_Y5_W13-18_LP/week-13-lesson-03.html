PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Introduce food as the topic of the lesson. Display a range of food packaging (or images of food packaging) and, if appropriate, invite children to sample the foods from different cultures. When asking children to sample foods, be aware of any food allergies. In pairs or individually, ask children to each choose one piece of packaging. Tell them to find the country of origin. Provide dictionaries for children to check the name of the country in English. Children then line up across the room in alphabetical order of the country of origin. Encourage children to respond to this activity with their thoughts. Ask: Did you know how many countries your food came from? Are you surprised?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Use the reading activities for ‘Choco Choco Boom Boom!’ in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.99–102.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Pre-teach any vocabulary or grammar which is new to your class using the vocabulary and grammar boxes in the Student Book, before completing the reading activities.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: During the reading activity, encourage children to relate what they read to their own lives. Ask: Have you ever eaten [food] from [country]? Did you like it? Do you think it would be part of a healthy meal or snack? Why / Why not? Share children’s answers as a class and discuss.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: During the reading activity, encourage children to relate what they read to their own lives. Ask: Have you ever eaten [food] from [country]? Did you like it? Do you think it would be part of a healthy meal or snack? Why / Why not? Share children’s answers as a class and discuss.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work in a small group with adult support to break the text down and check understanding of the key points. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think about why different cultures might have developed different foods and drinks (e.g. availability of different plants and animals, different climates, ways of cooking). Ask them to find out more about one food, drink, or spice from another culture that interests them.

Oxford additional tasks: Find all the countries mentioned in today’s lesson on the world map. Mark them with a push pin. If space allows, you may also choose to pin pieces of food packaging around the map and link them to the country of origin. Complete Oxford Discover SB 5 p.98 ex.C in small groups. See how many foods children can find. Make notes on a whiteboard.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have thought about where foods come from. They have read a text about food as an aspect of culture and related it to their own experience.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Experiencing other cultures’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by thinking about how the context of the text is relevant to their own lives. They will have the opportunity to link theoretical understanding with practical understanding by reading food packaging. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
