PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask: What’s the best gift you have ever received? How do you feel when you receive a gift? Why? Encourage children to share with a partner. Then discuss answers as a class. Explain that today you will look at what ‘gifts’ different cultures have given to the world.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: The main activity consists of a listening exercise followed by class discussion. Play the Wrap Up video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.97 again. Before watching, remind children of the new vocabulary learned in Week 13, Lesson 1. This time, ask them to listen for details to support the main information they learned previously.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: What countries are mentioned in the video? (Greece, China, Chile) What are their contributions (gifts) to the world? (the first computer and the Olympics, the compass, chocolate) What details can you hear which support the main idea about each contribution?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ask children to think about the impact of one of the cultural contributions in their own life. Encourage them to share their thoughts and ideas with a partner.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask children to think about the impact of one of the cultural contributions in their own life. Encourage them to share their thoughts and ideas with a partner. Hold a class vote. Ask: Which, for you, is the best ‘gift’ mentioned in the listening exercise? Then discuss children’s ideas as a class. Highlight the fact that there are a range of possible opinions, and that other classes might vote differently.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them a small number of specific questions to answer, rather than asking them to find supporting information in general. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think about how cultural experiences or inventions have been able to spread across the world, both in historical times and today (e.g. explorers, trade, immigration, tourism, books, television, the internet).

Oxford additional tasks: Find all the countries mentioned in today’s lesson on the world map. Mark them with a push pin. Make a list of cultural experiences or inventions that have been contributed to the world by a) your country of residence, and/or b) your country of origin.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened for details about different cultures. They have discussed the way different inventions and experiences have positively contributed to their own lives.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
