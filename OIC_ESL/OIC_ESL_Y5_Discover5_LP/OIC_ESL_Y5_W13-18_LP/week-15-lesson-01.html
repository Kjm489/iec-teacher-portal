PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Experiencing other cultures’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: Plan a cultural festival (pairs) Divide the class into pairs. Ask children to imagine they are planning a two-day festival in your local area, to celebrate different cultures. For inspiration, show children videos of festivals from around the world. Provide a range of materials promoting cultural festivals which celebrate your local culture and culture from other countries, such as leaflets, brochures, and internet sites. Each pair then identifies between one and three different cultures to showcase at their festival. Ask children to plan a timetable of different events (such as music, dance, drama, fireworks) and to make a list of different stands that could exhibit or sell products during the festival (such as food, or clothes). Tell children to write and illustrate a brochure, timetable, and a map of their festival. Remind them to use everything they have learned about writing about culture during this unit.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Each pair should divide the work between them equally. At the end of the project, ask each pair to present their festival ideas to the class. A cultural film (group) Invite children to share aspects of culture which are important to them, which could become the basis for a film. Refer to ‘Hair Love’ from Week 14 as an example. Draw out and develop ideas as a class. Allow children to create their own groups of four to eight children based on the ideas they want to explore in their films. Within each group, encourage children to allocate roles such as director, actors, camera person, and sound technician. The total running time for the film should be around 2–3 minutes. Children then write scripts, plan any props, costumes, locations, or other items which might be needed. Allow plenty of time to rehearse and then record the films. Observe the groups as they work. Remind children that they all rely on each other to complete their tasks.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Each pair then identifies between one and three different cultures to showcase at their festival. Each pair should divide the work between them equally. At the end of the project, ask each pair to present their festival ideas to the class. A cultural film (group) Within each group, encourage children to allocate roles such as director, actors, camera person, and sound technician. The total running time for the film should be around 2–3 minutes. Hold a mini film festival to finish the week. You may choose to invite other members of the school community to watch the films. Allow each group to walk down a ‘red carpet’ before sharing and celebrating their achievements. Ask each group to explain the idea behind their film and why it is important to them.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Hold a mini film festival to finish the week. You may choose to invite other members of the school community to watch the films. Allow each group to walk down a ‘red carpet’ before sharing and celebrating their achievements. Ask each group to explain the idea behind their film and why it is important to them. Celebrating our local culture (class/individual) Arrange a visit to a local cultural site, shop, factory, activity, or festival. This might include, for example, a museum of local social history, a dance performance, or a shop which makes local food specialities. Before the visit, tell children that they will create a leaflet to advertise this local cultural asset to visitors from other countries. During the visit, encourage children to take photographs (if allowed) and make notes. Provide clipboards and cameras. Back in the classroom, spend some time allowing children to ask and answer questions in English in small groups about what they have learned. Provide dictionaries so they can translate new or difficult terms. Ask children to write an illustrated leaflet for tourists. Encourage them to identify the special elements of this local cultural tradition, object, or performance and to promote them. At the end of the project, invite the local cultural practitioners involved in the initial visit to see the leaflets produced by children.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support in a small-group setting, to provide them with the tools to plan their work and make a confident start.  Check on these children more frequently as they work. For the cultural festival, you may wish to create mixed ability pairs. For the film project, allocate adult support, if available, to groups you consider will need it most. For the leaflet task, provide less able children with a leaflet template, vocabulary bank, and list of sentence starters. More able children can be asked to make notes about new vocabulary and grammar they have learned in this unit. Encourage them to stretch themselves by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar into their work. At the end of each task, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures.

Oxford additional tasks: Show children the Big Question video they watched in Lesson 13, Week 1 (Oxford Discover SB 5 p.97). Encourage children to think about how far they have come since they first watched it. Ask: How much more can you understand? Do you feel more confident watching a video about culture now? Allow them to share some of their thoughts and achievements from the independent reflection task with their classmates.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
