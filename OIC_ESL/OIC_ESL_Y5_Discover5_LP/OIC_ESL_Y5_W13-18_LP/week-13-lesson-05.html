PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Hold a brief introductory discussion. Ask: Do we have any history museums in our local area? Have you ever visited them? Have you visited any museums in other parts of the world? Explain that today, children will ‘visit’ a museum online. They will find out which historical cultures are displayed in the museum.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Divide the available time into two equal parts: research and presentation. Provide reading sources for a choice of two to four museums. Sources can include museum websites, magazine or newspaper articles, travel blogs, or museum leaflets/brochures. Suggestions for English language museum websites include the British Museum (UK), the Ashmolean Museum (UK), The Smithsonian Institution (USA), the Australian Museum (Australia) or use search term ‘Museums you can visit online’ to find other examples. You may wish to guide children to one or two specific online galleries within each museum. Tell children to research one museum (or gallery). Ask them to find out: which different historical cultures are represented there, and what exhibitions or objects the museum has on display.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Extension: Find out any interesting facts about one historical culture, and its links to our lives today. Invite children to explore the museum and make notes. Provide dictionaries for support. As a class, draw up a list of questions to ask in conversation. Examples might include:

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: After several minutes, ask children to get up and walk or dance around the room. Play music. Stop the music and ask them to find a new partner. Again, this should be someone who ‘visited’ a different museum. Repeat the speaking task.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Which museum did you choose? Does it have lots of exhibitions or galleries? Can I see objects from ancient Egypt there? In pairs, ask children to share what they have learned with someone who ‘visited’ a different museum. Prompt them to use their own notes and the list of questions on the board. Encourage them to create their own questions. After several minutes, ask children to get up and walk or dance around the room. Play music. Stop the music and ask them to find a new partner. Again, this should be someone who ‘visited’ a different museum. Repeat the speaking task. Hold a class vote. Ask: If we could go on a school trip, which museum would you choose?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, pair them with more able children for the research task, before encouraging them to work on their own for the speaking task. Challenge more able children by encouraging them to choose a civilization which has not yet been covered (such as ancient Rome or the First Nations peoples of Australia). Encourage less confident children by suggesting they choose a museum linked to topics covered earlier in Week 13, such as the ancient Egyptians.

Oxford additional tasks: Choose an object from the online museums or other sources. Make an information poster about the object using drawing and writing. Role-play being a classroom museum director. Invite children to choose significant objects from their classroom ‘culture’ and give other children a tour of the classroom ‘museum’.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have researched a history museum, using a range of sources, and talked about what they have learned.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Experiencing other cultures’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by building on their work in Week 13, Lesson 4 to produce conversations based on a more varied, real-world range of sources. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
