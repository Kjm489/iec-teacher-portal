PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display several posters of family-friendly films to create excitement and enthusiasm for the topic of cinema. Try to represent a range of different cultures in the films. Ask children if they have seen the films in the posters and elicit brief opinions. Ask: Where do you watch films, who with, and when? See if children can tell you why it’s important not to watch films late at night, and how it can affect their sleep if they do.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Ask children to read the ‘Hair Love’ film review. In pairs or groups of three to four, ask children to make notes about three things: what should be included in a film review (such as the main characters, the plot, the type of film it is [comedy, drama, etc.], your opinion and evidence for this, a rating); adjectives you can use to describe a film (e.g. ‘amazing’, ‘beautiful’, ‘emotional’, ‘exciting’, ‘funny’, ‘interesting’, ‘scary’);

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: things that might stand out (such as acting, animation, characters, script, special effects). Take feedback as a class. Write key terms on the board. Search online for the short film ‘Hair Love’ and watch it with subtitles (this will take approximately 7 minutes). Invite children to respond critically to the film. In pairs, children should discuss their ideas using vocabulary learned from the film review analysis task. Ensure that they understand the difference between ‘objective’ and ‘subjective’ criteria.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Hold a class vote to rate the film from 1 to 5 stars. At the end of the lesson, encourage children to think about watching films from different cultures. You may wish to provide a list of recent short films that your class might enjoy watching at home.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Encourage children to think deeply about the themes in the film and to provide evidence to justify their opinions. This activity will stretch children and develop their understanding of how film can portray important cultural ideas. Take feedback as a class. Continue to stretch children by drawing out deeper thinking. Hold a class vote to rate the film from 1 to 5 stars. At the end of the lesson, encourage children to think about watching films from different cultures. You may wish to provide a list of recent short films that your class might enjoy watching at home.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work through the three questions in the initial analysis task as a class, putting key terms and definitions on the board. Challenge more confident children to use a dictionary to find synonyms of commonly used words such as ‘interesting’ or ‘exciting’. Additional information: Week 14, Lesson 4

‘Hair Love’ film review

‘Hair Love’ is an animated short film from the USA about seven-year-old Zuri. In the opening to the film, we see that Zuri has beautiful, thick hair and needs her father to help her to style it.

Unfortunately, her father finds it very difficult. In fact, it turns into a real battle between Dad and Zuri’s hair! However, with the help of a video which Zuri’s mum has made for her hair vlog, Dad tries again and he finally learns how to make a beautiful hairstyle for Zuri. At the end of the film, they visit Zuri’s mum in hospital. She has lost her hair because of her treatment for cancer. But, in the end credits, her hair does start to grow back.

This film had some very funny moments such as Zuri’s cat’s face and the way Dad has to battle with Zuri’s hair! It was very interesting to learn about how to style her hair because it’s very different to mine. I liked all the different styles on Zuri’s mum’s vlog.

This film was not just a comedy, however. It also had emotional moments. I really liked the way it showed the strong relationship between a father and his daughter, and showed that you can learn new things even when you are an adult. Finally, the ending was beautiful and showed that being bald is okay, too.

I think this is a great film for families because lots of people will recognize the way that fathers and children relate to each other – and you can learn a lot about hairstyles, too!

My rating: 5 stars

Emily, aged 10, Manchester, UK
