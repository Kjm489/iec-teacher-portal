PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Introduce inventions as the topic of the lesson. Remind children that they learned about inventions in the ‘Historical revolutions’ unit as well as in this unit – they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning. Play a short video clip or slideshow showing different examples of inventions from different cultures through time. For one example, search online for ‘Evolution of Technology (Time Lapse) video’. See which inventions children can recall from the video and from their prior learning (e.g. bicycles, cars, water wheels, rollercoasters, compasses, chocolate, or hieroglyphics).

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Divide the lesson into two roughly equal parts: a reading task and a speaking task. You can optionally do the reading activity about whiteboards in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.91 ex.D. See if children know which country whiteboards come from (USA). Then move on to the next reading task.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Briefly teach or remind children about homophones, using your own subject knowledge. In pairs, ask children to identify homophones in the text ‘Find the homophones’ and their different meanings. Provide dictionaries for support. Then check answers as a class. Introduce the speaking task. Divide children into groups of five. Explain that they are in a hot air balloon, and they have with them the last one of each of these items in the world: a computer, a bar of chocolate, an Egyptian sheet of papyrus with hieroglyphics, and a bicycle. The balloon is sinking fast, but if they throw just one of the items out, they can survive. Which item will they choose?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ask each group to identify one child to defend each item and one leader/questioner. Give them time to make brief notes on their whiteboards to prepare. The group leader leads the discussion by asking each person about their invention and why it is important. Allow time for each of them to present their ideas. Then, as a group, they must agree which item they will remove.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask each group to identify one child to defend each item and one leader/questioner. Give them time to make brief notes on their whiteboards to prepare. The group leader leads the discussion by asking each person about their invention and why it is important. Allow time for each of them to present their ideas. Then, as a group, they must agree which item they will remove. Come together as a class. See if groups agree on their decision.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able or confident children, create mixed ability groups for the balloon debate and ask the leader of each group to support others in preparing their answers. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write a few sentences about why it is important that many different cultures can contribute to globally important inventions. Additional information: Week 14, Lesson 3 Find the homophones I like to see huge, modern ships travelling across the sea. Did you hear that the inventor of the telephone, Alexander Graham Bell, was born here in Edinburgh? You’re not going to believe your eyes when you see the latest mobile phone! If you look over there, you’ll see scientists from the city university showing their newest robot. Writers of historical television shows must be careful when writing their scenes, to make sure that modern day inventions like watches and coffee cups aren’t seen. Do you think the inventor of the whiteboard, Martin Heit, decided to invent it when he was feeling bored? Answers see, sea hear, here you’re, your there, their scene, seen board, bored

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the listening exercise about umbrellas in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.104. Write a paragraph summarizing the arguments for keeping one of the inventions in the balloon.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have recalled their learning about inventions. They have identified homophones in sentences about inventions, before taking part in a balloon debate. In group work, they have displayed effective leadership and teamwork skills.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Experiencing other cultures’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by recalling their work on questions in Week 14, Lessons 1–2 as well as vocabulary learned in the ‘Historical revolutions’ unit to take part in a debate about inventions. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
