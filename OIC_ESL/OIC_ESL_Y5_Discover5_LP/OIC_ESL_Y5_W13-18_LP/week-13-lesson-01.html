PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Introduce the unit topic. Communicate excitement and enthusiasm for other cultures and the opportunity to share information about children’s cultures, too. Put a world map on display to refer to during the unit. Put a push pin (or sticker) on your location. Ask children to tell you countries that they have a connection with (e.g. the location of a family member or previous home). Use push pins to mark these locations. In the event that there are few or no personal connections, ask children which countries they would like to find out about. Use push pins to mark these locations.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Watch the video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.97 (Wrap Up video). Pause the video after each section. Ask children to tell you what they have understood.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Use the same listening exercise in Week 13, Lesson 2 for a deeper listening task. In today’s lesson, focus on the basic elements of the video (the countries mentioned and the items or events they have contributed). Highlight key vocabulary for the unit as it arises, such as ‘contribution’, ‘culture’, ‘festival’, or ‘tradition’. Refer to the vocabulary list in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.90. Write new words on the board. More words can be added in the next lesson. Think and answer in pairs. Ask: How do we experience other cultures, and our own? Direct children to answer questions in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.89 1–4 ex.C.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ask children what questions they have. Make a note for reference throughout the unit. Wrap up this introductory lesson by celebrating the different aspects of children’s own cultures.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Come together as a class and share ideas. Refer back to the world map when children mention different cultures and countries. Write different ways we experience culture on the board as they arise (such as festivals, food, music, clothes, film, history, inventions, and sport). Encourage children to value their classmates’ contributions and learn from them. Ask children what questions they have. Make a note for reference throughout the unit. Wrap up this introductory lesson by celebrating the different aspects of children’s own cultures.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, play the Big Question video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.89 instead of the Wrap Up video. Provide your own brief commentary as you watch together. Challenge more able children by encouraging them to think about how many different cultures there are in their current city or region. Ask: What are the benefits of having lots of different cultures in one place? Encourage less confident children to share their own cultural experiences by providing a prompt for everyone, such as ‘food’ or ‘music’.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.82–83. Write a few sentences about one important or personal aspect of your own culture.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have begun to explore different ways to access culture. They have learned some new vocabulary through a listening task and subsequent discussion.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Experiencing other cultures’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by identifying key information in a video presentation.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
