PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Majestic mountains’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: Mountain mini-book (pairs) Divide the class into pairs. Invite children to use Google Earth, or videos made with Google Earth, to explore mountains around the world in 3D. Each pair then chooses their favourite mountain or mountain range to focus on in their project. Ask children to research the physical and human geography of this mountain (range). Headings might include ‘Physical features’, ‘Climate’, ‘Plants’, ‘Animals’, ‘People’, ‘Tourism’, or ‘Transport’. Use the internet and/or non-fiction books, magazines, and articles. Each pair should divide the work between them equally. Tell each pair to make a non-fiction mini-book of four pages about mountains. This can be word-processed or handwritten, with photos or illustrations. If handwritten, fold an A3 sheet of paper in half to create four pages. Encourage children to include the passive voice in their writing where appropriate.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Each pair can present their mini-book to the class and read a section aloud. Mountain news and weather report (group) In groups of four to five children, plan and record a short television news programme. This should include several news items and a weather report for a mountain area. Children should allocate different items to different members of the group to research and prepare. Also allocate roles such as director, presenter, camera person, and sound technician. Encourage children to plan any props, costumes, or other items which might be needed. The main focus of the project is to write scripts reporting on mountain news and weather. Prompt children to include the passive voice in any reports about past events. Observe the groups as they work. Encourage children to work together to resolve any differences of opinion. Allow sufficient time for groups to record their reports. Hold a ‘Did you hear the news?’ lesson to finish the week. Play all the news reports to the class. Make your own mountain (individual)

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Each pair then chooses their favourite mountain or mountain range to focus on in their project. Each pair should divide the work between them equally. Tell each pair to make a non-fiction mini-book of four pages about mountains. This can be word-processed or handwritten, with photos or illustrations. If handwritten, fold an A3 sheet of paper in half to create four pages. Each pair can present their mini-book to the class and read a section aloud. Mountain news and weather report (group) Children should allocate different items to different members of the group to research and prepare. Also allocate roles such as director, presenter, camera person, and sound technician.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask children to use Google Earth, or videos made with Google Earth, to explore mountains around the world in 3D. Tell them to choose their favourite mountain. They will make a model of this mountain for their project. Provide clay or other modelling materials to use as the structure of the mountain. Encourage children to make the shape of their model as realistic as possible. It should be large enough to allow them to add embellishments to represent surface features and human activity. Provide a range of junk modelling materials, paint, arts and craft materials which children can use to decorate their mountain to show snow, rocks, vegetation, human habitation, transport, and so on. While the model is drying, ask children to research their mountain using the internet and/or non-fiction books and articles. Children can then create a fact file about their mountain. Encourage them to include the passive voice where appropriate. At the end of the project, make an exhibition of all the mountain models with their fact files. Encourage children to talk about their mountain in small groups or to the class. Hold a class vote in categories such as most realistic mountain, most impressive mountain, or hardest worker. Praise children for the effort they have shown throughout the week, focusing on their process and not on their achievements.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support in a small-group setting, to provide them with the tools to plan their work and make a confident start.  Check on these children more frequently as they work. For the mountain mini-book, you may wish to create mixed ability pairs. More able children can be asked to stretch themselves in writing tasks by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar in their work. At the end of each task, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures.

Oxford additional tasks: Show children the Big Question video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.127. See if they can identify things that they have learned during this unit. Allow them to share some of their thoughts and achievements from the independent reflection task with their classmates.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
