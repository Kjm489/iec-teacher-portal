PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Refer to the world map used in Week 13. Ask children what they remember from their learning in Week 13, using question words and modelling rising intonation. For example: What did you learn about [the history of chocolate]? Who invented [hieroglyphics]? Explain that children will practise asking and answering questions about culture in today’s lesson.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Review the use of key question words: Who, Where, When, Why, What, Which, and How. Ask children to use their own subject knowledge and information from Oxford Discover SB 5 p.95. Remind children that they covered these words in Week 2 – they have to practise certain things over and over in order to master them. Build on their knowledge by explaining the difference between using Who as a subject and as an object in a question: Who saw you? (subject) Who did you see? (object) In addition, remind children of the importance of rising intonation to help your interlocutor understand that you are asking a question.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Ask children what phrases they can use if they don’t understand a question, such as: I’m sorry, I don’t understand. Can you say that again, please? Divide children into pairs. Ask each child to think about either: an invention or a cultural experience (such as a meal) which is important to them, or a cultural event (such as a festival) that they have taken part in.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: If time allows, swap partners and run the activity again. Spend the last 5 minutes reviewing the activity. Explain common errors. Check understanding.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask them to write the seven question words on their whiteboard. They then take turns asking and answering questions about their chosen topic. Tell them to tick off the question words as they use them. Encourage children to ask at least one question with each question word. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.96 as a model. Encourage children to take a few breaths before they ask, or respond to, questions. Observe children as they practise and note common errors. If time allows, swap partners and run the activity again. Spend the last 5 minutes reviewing the activity. Explain common errors. Check understanding.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able or confident children, spend time designing questions as a class before dividing into pairs. Write model questions on the board. Prompt children to ask for clarification from their partner if they don’t understand a question the first time. Challenge more able children by ensuring they have mastered the use of Who as a subject and as an object.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.86–87 exercises. Watch a video clip of an interview with a famous person, aimed at children. Identify statements and questions by listening for the structure and intonation of each sentence.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have asked and answered questions about culture using appropriate intonation and the question words Who, Where, When, Why, What, Which, and How.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
