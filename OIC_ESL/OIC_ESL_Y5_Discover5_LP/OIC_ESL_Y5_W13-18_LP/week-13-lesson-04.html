PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Display photos of well-known cultural aspects of ancient Egypt on the class whiteboard. Suggested items: pyramids, the Sphinx of Giza, mummies, ancient Egyptian boats, hieroglyphics, and papyrus sheets. See if children can say which country the photos come from. Ask: Do you think it’s important to know about historical cultures? Can you think of a link between ancient Egypt and our lives today? (e.g. using paper for letters, books, magazines, and newspapers).

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Divide the time available into three equal parts: for reading, for making notes, and for sharing ideas as a class. First, tell children to read Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.92–93. Ask them to identify the main sections of the text, and then to see how many details they can understand. Provide dictionaries for support.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Then, ask children to make notes, based on the information in the text, to answer the following question: Which country would you go to, and why? (Children may choose to use the organizer in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.94 ex.A for support).

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Either in small groups or in front of the class, ask children to share their answer to the question. Remind them that their audience knows nothing about the topic. Prompt them to use and explain any specialized vocabulary. Praise children for their efforts in paraphrasing difficult words.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask children to imagine they are giving a brief explanation of their choice to someone who doesn’t know anything about the topic. Encourage them to think about how they can explain a difficult word (such as ‘hieroglyphics’) to someone using other, simpler words. Remind them this is called ‘paraphrasing’. Either in small groups or in front of the class, ask children to share their answer to the question. Remind them that their audience knows nothing about the topic. Prompt them to use and explain any specialized vocabulary. Praise children for their efforts in paraphrasing difficult words.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work together as a class between the note-taking and speaking tasks to think about simple ways to paraphrase specialized vocabulary. Write definitions on the board. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to record their talk and watch it back. They can use the recording to think about any improvements they could make to the content or delivery of their speech. Encourage less confident children to do their best in the speaking task by allocating time for practice in pairs. Pair less confident children together with more confident ones.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.94 exs.B–C and the vocabulary practice in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.82–83.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a text about the cultural contribution of (a) historical civilization(s). They have made a short oral explanation of one historical achievement, using paraphrasing.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
