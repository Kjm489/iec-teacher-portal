PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Divide children into pairs. Ask: What do animals use wheels for? Tell children to write their answers on a whiteboard. Share answers as a class (such as pulling a cart or carriage, exercise wheel, pet wheelchair). Discuss: Do animals choose to use wheels? Why do humans give animals wheels? (to work for us, exercise, fun, health) Show photos of animals using wheels in different countries or time periods. Search online using terms such as ‘horse and carriage’, ‘coal pit pony’, ‘hamster wheel’, ‘dog wheelchair’ ‘panda tyre swing’, or ‘summer dogsledding’ with ‘images’. Encourage children to respond to the photos. Ask: How do you think this animal feels? How does that make you feel? Discuss the positive/negative impact of wheels on animals. Teachers may wish to elicit some of the adjectives from Week 8, Lesson 2.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do the listening exercise in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.62. Ask children to listen for short words that join different parts of a sentence together. Ask them to write them on their whiteboard. Invite children to share their answers (and/but/or/so) and write them on the board. In turn, ask: What job does this word do?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Play a game. Divide children into pairs. Ask children to choose an object with wheels that an animal might use. Their partner must ask questions to guess what it is. Take turns. Allocate enough time at the end of the lesson to teach irregular verbs in the past tense using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.63 ex.F. become – became, dig – dug, feel – felt, draw – drew, light – lit, give – gave.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Play a game. Divide children into pairs. Ask children to choose an object with wheels that an animal might use. Their partner must ask questions to guess what it is. Take turns.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Invite children to recall irregular verbs they learned in Week 2 or other previous lessons. Remind children of the ‘fire together, wire together’ concept. By thinking again about irregular verbs, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning. Tell children to write five sentences using these irregular verbs on their whiteboard. Check answers as a class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work as a class to create questions for the game. To challenge more able children, ask them to combine irregular verbs and linking words in their sentences: e.g. I drew a picture of a horse and cart, and I gave it to my sister.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 5 p.57. Research an animal which uses wheels. Write a paragraph about it. Organize a class debate on the topic ‘Should humans give wheels to animals?’.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified linking words in a listening exercise. They have played a question and answer game. They have learned new irregular verbs in the past tense.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
