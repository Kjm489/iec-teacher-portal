PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Tell children that people communicate in different ways in different countries. Play a short video showing different ways of greeting people around the world. Search online for ‘traditional greetings around the world’ or ‘greetings different cultures’. Ask: How many different ways can people say hello? (e.g. handshake, kiss, hug, bow, wave) Tell children these are called ‘greetings’. Ask: How do you feel when someone smiles? Ask children to smile at someone today.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.79. Ask: What questions can we ask about a text before we read? Use Why, What, or How. Write children’s suggestions on the board. Highlight the first sentence of each paragraph.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Note that the first sentence usually tells us what the paragraph will be about. Ask: What questions can you ask about these sentences? In pairs, discuss how the people in the photos feel. Ask: How do you know? Play a game. Divide children into pairs. Give one child a list of five familiar animals, and give their partner a list of five different animals. Tell children to hide their list.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into pairs. Give one child a list of five familiar animals, and give their partner a list of five different animals. Tell children to hide their list. Ask children to communicate to their partner using only words, gestures, or their face. Use a dice. Tell children that 1, 4 = words; 2, 5 = gestures; and 3, 6 = face. A child rolls the dice. They must communicate the first animal on their list to their partner, using only the option on the dice. Remind children that this is difficult! It will help to extend their understanding of communication.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask children to communicate to their partner using only words, gestures, or their face. Use a dice. Tell children that 1, 4 = words; 2, 5 = gestures; and 3, 6 = face. A child rolls the dice. They must communicate the first animal on their list to their partner, using only the option on the dice. Remind children that this is difficult! It will help to extend their understanding of communication. When they guess correctly, swap roles. Continue until both lists are complete. Discuss: Is it easy to communicate using only one option? Why do you think we usually communicate in several different ways?

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support children who might find analysing body and facial language difficult, provide a list of adjectives to describe feelings and a visual list of gestures. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to research sign language or braille.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a paragraph about the different ways that humans communicate.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about different ways humans communicate. They have used Why, What, and How to understand written and visual information. They have practised communicating in different ways in a game.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Animal communication’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by asking questions to help them understand what they are reading. They will develop their speaking skills by focusing on using words, gestures, and facial expressions to convey a particular idea. They will think about appropriate ways to communicate with each other and with people from different cultures. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
