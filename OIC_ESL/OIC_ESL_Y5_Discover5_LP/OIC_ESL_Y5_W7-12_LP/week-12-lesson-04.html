PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Animal communication’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: Observing animals (mix of individual and class) Organize a class visit to a local park, nature reserve, animal or bird sanctuary, or other location where animals or birds can be observed. Before the trip, play a video of a naturalist observing and interacting with animals in the wild. Discuss how humans should observe and interact with animals. Remind children that patience is important. On the trip, encourage children to observe one or more animals or birds in their natural environment. Provide clipboards and cameras. Ask them to take notes and photographs (if allowed). If a visit is not possible, give children a choice of live webcams to observe online from the classroom. Search online for ‘animal webcam’. Then walk around the local area and ask children to observe any animals or birds that they see. In the classroom, invite children to share their observations with the class. Ask whether children experienced ‘flow’ while observing animals. Ask children to write individual reports about their observations of animal communication.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Share reports and photographs in class. A selection of these reports can be published in the school newspaper or website, if available. Recording an animal podcast (pairs) In pairs, plan and record a podcast about animal communication. The show should cover any items of children’s choice from the ‘Animal communication’ topic. Choose two or three different types of presentation to include in the show, such as a news report, a quiz, an interview, or an opinion piece. The total running time should be around 10 minutes. Write scripts for each section of the show. Ensure the sections are connected together well. Rehearse. Make any changes that are needed to the scripts. Record the podcast. Hold a ‘Listening lesson’ to finish the week. Play all the podcasts to the class. Hold a class vote on the best podcast.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Tell children to decide on lead roles within their group: reporter, editor, designer, and photographer. Everyone will contribute to writing the articles, but these people will lead on decisions in these areas. As a group, children should choose which stories to include in the newspaper and divide the work. Invite each group to share their newspaper with the class. working in a team on a newspaper.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Writing an edition of ‘Nature News’ (groups) In groups of four, plan and write an edition of an imaginary newspaper, ‘Nature News’. Tell children that this week’s edition should focus on animal communication stories. Tell children to decide on lead roles within their group: reporter, editor, designer, and photographer. Everyone will contribute to writing the articles, but these people will lead on decisions in these areas. Ask children to research news stories involving animal communication. Encourage children to look at local and global news. If possible, take children on a field visit to investigate a local story. As a group, children should choose which stories to include in the newspaper and divide the work. Ask children to write their articles and opinion pieces for the newspaper. Remind children to practise using reported speech. Encourage children to work together to create the newspaper. Remind them to think about the importance of each story, appropriate headlines, photos, and graphics. Invite each group to share their newspaper with the class. Take feedback. Discuss what children have learned about: animal communication, working in a team on a newspaper.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Children requiring extra support can create shorter versions of the podcast or newspaper articles, working with an adult to focus on the quality rather than the quantity of their work. More able children may be asked to take on a mentoring role. In each lesson, ask them to spend five to ten minutes observing and supporting children in other pairs or groups.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the review section in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.80–81. Research the names of different animal sounds in English and in other languages. Create an ‘animal sound dictionary’. This could be illustrated with photos or drawings, to share with younger members of the school community.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
