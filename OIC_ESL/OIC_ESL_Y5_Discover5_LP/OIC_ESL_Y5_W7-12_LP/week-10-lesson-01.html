PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Explain to children that this is the first lesson of a new unit about animal communication. Ask: How do you think animals communicate? Share the photo of the penguin in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.68–69. Ask: What do you think the penguin/bird is communicating? Which senses is it using? (penguin – sound, sight; bird – sight) How do you think it feels? Do an empathy exercise. Tell children to close their eyes. They think about a time they have made eye contact with or held an animal. Invite them to think about how they were communicating with that animal. Ask: How did you feel? How do you think the animal felt? How do you know?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Play the video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.77. Ask children to identify the animals in the video. Tell them to make notes on a whiteboard about how and why they communicate. Explain that children might not understand everything they hear this time, but should listen for the main ideas. They will learn more vocabulary throughout this unit. Invite children to say which animals they saw. Write the animals on the board.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Divide children into pairs or groups of three. Ask children to share their notes. Remind children to be respectful of each other’s ideas. Add children’s answers around the animal names on the board to make a mind map. Give each pair or small group photos of familiar animals. Search online for ‘animal communication pictures’ or ‘wildlife photographer of the year’. You may wish to add search terms, such as ‘hungry’ or ‘angry’. Use animals from your own and other countries.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Give each pair or small group photos of familiar animals. Search online for ‘animal communication pictures’ or ‘wildlife photographer of the year’. You may wish to add search terms, such as ‘hungry’ or ‘angry’. Use animals from your own and other countries. Ask children to take turns to tell their partner(s) about an animal in a photo: e.g. This is a lion. I think it’s angry. It’s showing its teeth to scare other animals.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask children to take turns to tell their partner(s) about an animal in a photo: e.g. This is a lion. I think it’s angry. It’s showing its teeth to scare other animals. Ask them to copy any sentences they are proud of on their whiteboard. Share children’s sentences as a class. Teach new vocabulary using Oxford Discover SB 5 p.70.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, use the video without commentary in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.77. Add your own level-appropriate commentary. Encourage less confident children to write at least one sentence on their whiteboard. Challenge more able and confident children by asking why each type of communication is effective: e.g. Why does showing its [the lion’s] teeth scare other animals?

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.62–63.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have begun to think about key aspects of animal communication. They have watched a video and discussed how animals communicate.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
