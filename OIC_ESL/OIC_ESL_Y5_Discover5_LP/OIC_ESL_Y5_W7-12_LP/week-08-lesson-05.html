PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Tell children that this is the final lesson on the topic ‘Historical revolutions’. Play the video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.65. Remind children that they watched this video in Week 7, Lesson 1. Invite children to respond to the video. Ask: What have you learned during this topic? Write facts and vocabulary on the board. Remind children that they have worked hard during this unit. Praise them for their efforts. Highlight any particular successes in terms of work ethic, progress, or attitudes to learning.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Remind children of the key points of an opinion essay. Tell them to write their opinion essay using the notes in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.59 (or a different plan), which they completed in the previous lesson. Ask them to:

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: write a first draft, check their work with a partner, write a final draft. Provide timings for each stage. Remind children that managing their time is an important skill. Encourage them to use it wisely. Provide a visible reminder such as a clock at the front of the room.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: check their work with a partner, Divide children into groups of three or four. Ask children to read their opinion essay to the group. Model how to use your tone of voice to make the essay interesting to the audience.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Between each stage of writing, invite children to jog on the spot for one minute. Divide children into groups of three or four. Ask children to read their opinion essay to the group. Model how to use your tone of voice to make the essay interesting to the audience. Ask children to make notes about each other’s essays on their whiteboards. They should identify one thing that went well and one thing that could be improved. Take feedback from the class about what went well and what could be improved. Talk about both writing and delivery.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide sentence starters for giving opinions. Work in a small group, as in Week 8, Lesson 4, with adult encouragement and guidance. Challenge more able children by asking them to include and refute counterarguments in their essay. Encourage less confident children to use their voice by singing a familiar song or chant as a class before the speaking activity. Invite children to use as much expression as they can.

Oxford additional tasks: Share essays with the class or with an invited audience. Look in more detail at real-world opinion essays, reviews, and blogs. Identify the language which shows that the writer is giving their opinion.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written and shared an opinion essay about a topic related to wheels.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
