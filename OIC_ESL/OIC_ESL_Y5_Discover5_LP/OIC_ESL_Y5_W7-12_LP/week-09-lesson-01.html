PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Historical revolutions’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: Building recycled model cars (pairs) Divide the class into pairs. Explain that children are going to use recycled materials to design and build a model car for the future. Give each pair a wide choice of clean, safe recycled materials such as cardboard, plastic straws, containers, lids, and boxes. Ask children to research different wheel designs, compare different methods, and decide how to make the model car. Each pair should plan what materials will be needed. Remind them to think about the different parts of the wheels, such as the axle or the rim. Observe pairs building their cars. Elicit predictions about the possible success of their plans. Encourage children to embrace and learn from their mistakes as they work.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: When they have finished, ask children to write a presentation about cars of the future, including information about their car design and different opinions about the future of cars (industry, environmentalist, government, general public). Share all the model cars and presentations with the class and the wider school community. Wheels are fun! (individual) Ask children to choose a topic to research: ‘Wheels in sport’ (including parasports), ‘Theme parks and funfairs’, ‘Cycling in my city’. They are going to produce two pieces of work: a poster (to present to the class) advertising a future event such as a sports competition, a special day at a theme park, or a cycling festival; an opinion essay on one aspect of the chosen topic. Display all the posters and essays in the classroom or share with others in the school.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Give each pair a wide choice of clean, safe recycled materials such as cardboard, plastic straws, containers, lids, and boxes. Each pair should plan what materials will be needed. Remind them to think about the different parts of the wheels, such as the axle or the rim.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Wheels in action (class/individual) Arrange a visit to a location which uses wheels, such as a paper mill, motorsports track, factory, farm, pottery workshop, or funfair. Before the visit, provide information about the location to the class (online or promotional materials). Tell children to prepare questions about: the way wheels are used at that location, the advantages and disadvantages (if any) for the local area, how the site will change in the future. Provide clipboards and cameras. During the visit, encourage children to take photographs (if allowed) and make notes. Ask children to write an illustrated report about their visit. Encourage them to include sections in their report talking about the future and giving their opinions. Invite children to present their reports to the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Children requiring extra support can work with an adult to plan out their work over the five lessons. This adult should check regularly to ensure that they are confident about their task, and provide any individual support necessary. Children with a particular aptitude or interest in engineering may wish to use motorized construction kits to build their car. They may wish to research how cars work in greater detail.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the review section in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.60–61. Write a short story in which wheels play a part in some way.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
