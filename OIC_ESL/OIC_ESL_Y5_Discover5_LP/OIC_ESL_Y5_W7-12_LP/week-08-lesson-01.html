PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play a warm-up game: ‘True or False’. Ask children to move to one side of the room if they think the statement is true (T), and the other side if the statement is false (F). A tricycle has three wheels. (T) Wheels are only used for transportation. (F) A mechanical watch has small wheels inside it. (T) Add several more statements relevant to your class. These could relate to what they discussed in Week 7, Lesson 1: e.g. 50% of our class cycles to school every day.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Do the listening exercise in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.54. Ask: Do you agree that the printing press is the most important machine that uses wheels? Why/Why not? Discuss the features of the text which make it informal (rhetorical questions, use of ‘you’ to relate directly to the audience, use of ‘Well …’).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Ask: Who is the audience? (children in a classroom) Do you think they did a good job of matching the text to the audience? Ask: What other things that you hear might use informal language? (such as adverts, radio programmes, podcasts) Why do you think they use informal language? (to connect with the audience, to persuade you to listen to or buy something, to entertain) You may wish to share one or two real-world examples with the class. This might be a short radio advert or podcast. Search online for ‘Fun Kids radio’ or ‘kids podcast’. Teach the construction ‘verb + infinitive’ using your subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.55.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: What did you decide to watch last night? Encourage children to check their sentences.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Complete the Writing Study in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.49 or ask questions for children to answer on their whiteboards. Use ‘decide’, ‘forget’, ‘hope’, ‘want’, or ‘plan’ with an infinitive: Where do you hope to travel one day? What did you decide to watch last night? Encourage children to check their sentences.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide the transcript for them to follow as they listen. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write and record a short advert or podcast using features of informal language.

Oxford additional tasks: Watch English language adverts aimed at children. Identify informal language. Work in pairs. Children tell their partner things they decided to or forgot to do yesterday and hope to, want to, or plan to do tomorrow. Swap partners and repeat with new verbs.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have discussed informal language in a listening text and applied it to real-world examples. They have learned the construction ‘verb + infinitive’.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Historical revolutions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by thinking about the formality of language in a text and how it relates to the audience. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
