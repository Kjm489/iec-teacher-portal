PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Search online for ‘random name wheel’. Add all children’s names to the random selector. Use the wheel to choose a child. Ask them to say one fact they have learned about wheels this week, or one new word and its meaning. Repeat with other children. By thinking again about wheels, children are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning. Ask: How can we use wheels to travel on a trip?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Teach the grammar used to talk about future events using will, going to, and the present continuous. Use your own subject knowledge and refer to Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.53 and 61, and Oxford Discover WB 5 p.54. Teach the difference between future facts and beliefs (will), future plans and predictions (going to), and future arrangements (present continuous). There will be a general election next year. I will never learn to ride a bicycle! I’m going to visit Chile next year.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Look outside – it’s going to be a beautiful day! We’re taking my cousins to a museum on Saturday. In pairs, children plan a trip to an exciting place. Show them photos of destinations for inspiration. You may wish to use Oxford Discover SB 5 p.54 ex.C for this activity. Ask children to talk to their partner about where they are going, how they will get there, and what they will do there. Children should use the grammar taught in this lesson. Write on the class whiteboard:

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Ask children to talk to their partner about where they are going, how they will get there, and what they will do there. Children should use the grammar taught in this lesson. Observe the conversation of each pair. Ask children to explain to you why they chose a particular tense. Remind them to refer to the class whiteboard as a guide. Create ‘pairs of pairs’ to share their ideas. Encourage each pair to evaluate the other pair’s sentences and suggest improvements. Remind children to use positive language.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: WILL – FACTS, BELIEFS GOING TO – PLANS, PREDICTIONS PRESENT CONTINUOUS – ARRANGEMENTS Observe the conversation of each pair. Ask children to explain to you why they chose a particular tense. Remind them to refer to the class whiteboard as a guide. Create ‘pairs of pairs’ to share their ideas. Encourage each pair to evaluate the other pair’s sentences and suggest improvements. Remind children to use positive language. Choose several pairs to present their plans to the class. Discuss common errors.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide one or two trip ideas with related vocabulary. Encourage less confident children to choose a trip they are familiar with, so that they can focus on using correct grammar. Challenge more confident children by asking them to use all three tenses in their plans. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write and perform a radio advertisement for a holiday company which is organizing a trip to another country.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.46–47 and pp.54–55. Use travel blogs, reviews, or magazines to research trips in another city or country.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned the future tense using will, going to, and the present continuous. They have applied this learning to talking about a future trip.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Historical revolutions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by learning new grammar skills used for talking about the future. They will work with others in the class to choose the correct grammar in a speaking exercise about travel.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
