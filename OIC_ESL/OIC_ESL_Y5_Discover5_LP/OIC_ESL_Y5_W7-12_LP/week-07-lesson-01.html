PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Explain that children are starting a new unit today on the importance of wheels. Watch the video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.55 to generate interest and introduce children to the variety of wheels around them. Explain that children will be learning new vocabulary throughout the unit. They might not understand everything they hear this time, but should listen for the main ideas. Invite responses to the ideas raised in the video. Ask: Do you ever think about how wheels have impacted your life? If not, why not? What do you know about them? Elicit responses to make children think about how wheels make their lives easier without always noticing.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: In pairs, tell children to talk about: wheels they use (or don’t use) in their lives, how they use them, and how often they use them.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Invite them to write notes about these points on their whiteboards. Display the four large sheets of paper around the classroom walls. Alternatively, place one on each table. Invite children to move around the classroom. Ask them to add a sticky note to (or write on) each sheet listing wheels they use NEVER, SOMETIMES, OFTEN, or EVERY DAY. As a class, discuss and reflect on the results. If sticky notes were used, they can be moved around the sheet to collate similar answers.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: In pairs, invite children to discuss: If there were no wheels in the world, what would you do differently? How would this change your life? As a class, discuss and reflect on the answers.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Ask: Do you think the results would be the same in [insert names of cities or countries which are very different to your own location]? Why? Ask: Do you think your answers will change throughout your life? Why? In pairs, invite children to discuss: If there were no wheels in the world, what would you do differently? How would this change your life? As a class, discuss and reflect on the answers.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, use the Big Question video without commentary in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.47. Add your own level-appropriate commentary. Challenge more able children by asking them to predict the results of the class survey and explain their predictions. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to research one of the following topics: How wheels help everybody to have equal opportunities Wheels in sport Big wheels around the world

Oxford additional tasks: Take a photo of, or draw, wheels in your daily life and write a paragraph about it. Research how wheels are used in daily life in a different part of the world.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have prepared for the ‘Historical revolutions’ unit by sharing their knowledge of wheels. They have thought about how they are used in daily life.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Historical revolutions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by using video input to prepare their own remarks about the unit topic. They will discuss how the unit topic applies to real-life contexts in pairs and as a class.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
