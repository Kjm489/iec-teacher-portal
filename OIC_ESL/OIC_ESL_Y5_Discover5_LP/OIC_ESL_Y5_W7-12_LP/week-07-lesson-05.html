PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Play short videos of children (or adults) doing fun things on wheels, to generate enthusiasm for weekend activities. Suggested searches include ‘kids motocross racing’, ‘wheelchair freestyle’, ‘kids skateboarding’, and ‘kids BMX championships’. Try to ensure gender balance in the selected clips. Invite responses. Ask: Have you tried any of these activities? Which ones would you like to do? Is there somewhere to try these things where we live? Ask: How do you think the children (or people) in these videos feel? What do you think they are thinking about? Explain that when you are enjoying an activity so much that you focus all of your attention on it, forgetting time, things, and people around you, you are in a state of ‘flow’. Ask: What activities give you ‘flow’? Discuss: What does your ‘flow’ activity tell you about yourself / your talents / your skills / things you enjoy? See if children can reflect on this. Encourage them to list three positive things they have learned about themselves (such as ‘I like taking risks.’, ‘I like being creative.’, ‘I enjoy reading stories.’). Ask them to write these things on a whiteboard, or tell a partner.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Briefly review the grammar from Week 7, Lesson 4.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Tell children to write about their plans for next weekend using this grammar. Refer to Oxford Discover WB 5 p.49. Children can write about their actual plans or fictional plans. Invite them to include personal activities that give them ‘flow’ or some of the activities shown in the videos.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Remind children to check their use of tenses and improve their work. Ask children to write and illustrate a final draft. If possible, display these in the classroom or on a school blog.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Remind children to check their use of tenses and improve their work. Ask children to write and illustrate a final draft. If possible, display these in the classroom or on a school blog.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, use prompts to inspire their writing. For example, use event brochures (such as sports events, concerts, or festivals). Invite more confident children to share their ideas about ‘flow’ with the class. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to interview each other and write about their partner’s plans.

Oxford additional tasks: Choose a famous person or fictional character, and write about their weekend plans. Make contact with another class in your school, or with a school in another city or country. Swap your weekend plans in person, by email, or in a video link. Discuss similarities and differences.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have talked about what ‘flow’ is and thought about what this means to them personally. They have reviewed the future tense using will, going to, and the present continuous. They have applied this to a piece of writing about weekend plans.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
