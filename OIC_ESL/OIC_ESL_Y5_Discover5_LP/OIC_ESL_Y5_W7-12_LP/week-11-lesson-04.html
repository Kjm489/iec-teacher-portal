PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Watch the Big Question video in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.87. Ask children to volunteer facts they have learned during Weeks 10 and 11 about animal communication. Write key words on the board. Remind children of the ‘fire together, wire together’ concept. By thinking again about animal communication, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning. Tell children that over the next two lessons they will have the opportunity to practise everything they have learned.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Teach summarizing skills using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 5 p.84 ex.C. Remind children to use phrases such as: What did you learn about? / I learned that … / What else did you learn? / I learned about … / I found out that … Ask children to move around the room. Encourage them to ask five children what they learned, and to respond to five others.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Divide children into pairs. Children should tell their partner about what they heard. Tell them to practise reported speech: e.g. Maria said that she learned about elephant communication. John told me that humpback whales sang different songs. Tell children that in the next lesson they are going to write a descriptive essay about an animal and how it communicates. Ask: Why do we need to summarize information? How can we then expand our ideas for our essay? Teach children how to structure a descriptive essay. Use Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.86–87 or a separate text which describes an animal. This should have an interesting starting sentence, introduction with adjectives, body (one/two more paragraphs), and conclusion.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Divide children into pairs. Children should tell their partner about what they heard. Tell them to practise reported speech: e.g. Maria said that she learned about elephant communication. John told me that humpback whales sang different songs.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Point out examples of pronoun agreement in the text. Children share other examples. Invite children to research an animal using their own experience, the internet, books, and other materials. Suggest suitable websites and offer a limited or free choice of animal. Observe children as they research. Provide individual support where needed. Pause halfway through the time available. Invite children to share any general questions or concerns. Remind children to focus on descriptive words and to use vocabulary they have learned during this unit.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work in a small group to share information and ideas. Provide a vocabulary bank of adjectives, grouped by theme (colour, personality, etc.). Encourage less confident children to choose an animal they are very familiar with. Challenge more confident children to choose an animal which is less familiar to them.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 p.78. Divide children into groups according to the animal they have chosen to research. Invite them to share information from their research.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have summarized information learned in this unit. They have researched information to use in a descriptive essay.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
