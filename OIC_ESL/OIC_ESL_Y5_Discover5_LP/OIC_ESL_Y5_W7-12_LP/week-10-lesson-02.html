PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Write these sentences on the board. Ask children to solve the anagrams. Listen to this hungry NOLI roar! (lion) A blue HELAW sings a song in the ocean. (whale) I can hear RDIBS singing in the trees. (birds) A POPHI shows its teeth to scare predators. (hippo) A ODG wags its LATI when it’s happy. (dog, tail) Check answers. Ask: What do you think ‘wags’ means?

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Use Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.71–74. Teach character analysis using character webs with the headings: ‘How (s)he feels’, ‘What (s)he looks like’, and ‘What (s)he says or does’. Ask: How do these questions help us to understand a character?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Divide children into groups of three. Ask children to read the story and complete a character web using the headings above. Tell children to distribute tasks between themselves to complete the character web. Ask: How will you divide the work? Children may choose to fill in one section each and then feed back to their team, or to work on each section together. Remind children to think carefully about the words the author uses to show character. Ask: Why have these words been chosen? What do they say about the character?

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: Tell children to distribute tasks between themselves to complete the character web. Ask: How will you divide the work? Children may choose to fill in one section each and then feed back to their team, or to work on each section together. Set a timer for regular intervals during the group task. When the timer sounds, ask children to stand up and shake out their bodies before continuing their work. Evaluate how well the group activity went. Talk about task distribution and completion. Support children to identify where they might do things differently next time.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: Set a timer for regular intervals during the group task. When the timer sounds, ask children to stand up and shake out their bodies before continuing their work. Share answers as a class. Ask: Did you find character webs useful? Why / Why not? Evaluate how well the group activity went. Talk about task distribution and completion. Support children to identify where they might do things differently next time.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, fill in the character web in a small group with adult support. Encourage less confident children to highlight the character’s name wherever it appears in a handout of the story. They can use these as starting points when looking for information. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to complete a character web for a higher level traditional folktale which includes human and animal communication.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.64–65. Use the internet and/or non-fiction books to research dolphins. Tell a partner what you have learned.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have organized and completed a team task. They have read a text about animal communication and used a character web to understand the main characters.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
