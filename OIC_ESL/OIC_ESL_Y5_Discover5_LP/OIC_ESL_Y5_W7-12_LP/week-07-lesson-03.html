PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Search online for ‘wheels throughout history pictures’. Choose five pictures to display on the class whiteboard (such as a Roman chariot wheel, a 17th-century water wheel, a 19th-century ‘Penny Farthing’ bicycle, a 20th-century car wheel, and a 21st-century racing wheelchair wheel). Number them 1–5. You may wish to include wheels from local history. Ask children to put the wheels in order on their whiteboard, from oldest to most modern. Discuss children’s answers. Ask: What information did you use to help you decide? Explain that today’s lesson will look at how wheels have been used in the past.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Read the text in Oxford Discover SB 5 pp.50–51.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover SB 5 p.52. If time is short, you may prefer to only do the Think activity.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: As a class, discuss why people in history were motivated to create new inventions using wheels. Relate the discussion to the text children have read, as well as their broader learning. Ask: What problems do you think people had to solve? How did wheels provide a solution? Discuss the double meaning of the word ‘revolution’ and why these inventions were truly ‘revolutionary’. Explain that struggle and challenge can help the brain to grow. When people in history have faced problems, they have used their brains – and stretched them – to find solutions. Many of these solutions have used wheels.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: As a class, discuss why people in history were motivated to create new inventions using wheels. Relate the discussion to the text children have read, as well as their broader learning. Ask: What problems do you think people had to solve? How did wheels provide a solution? Discuss the double meaning of the word ‘revolution’ and why these inventions were truly ‘revolutionary’. Explain that struggle and challenge can help the brain to grow. When people in history have faced problems, they have used their brains – and stretched them – to find solutions. Many of these solutions have used wheels.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work on the text in a small group with adult support. Challenge more able and confident children by asking them to think about different ways to find a solution to a difficult problem (such as research, experiments, collaboration, independent thought) and skills you might need (such as persistence, enthusiasm, hard work, patience, willingness to take risks). Ask them to share these ideas with the class. Use a dictionary for support.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 5 pp.44–45. Choose an object or mode of transport which has wheels. Research and share with the class in speech or writing: who invented it, when and where they invented it, what problem it solved, how it solved this problem.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a text about wheels in history. They have discussed the motivations of historical characters in inventing and using wheels.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Historical revolutions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by thinking about the motivations of characters in a reading text. They will have the opportunity to be inspired, through class discussion, by historical people who solved difficult problems using wheels. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
