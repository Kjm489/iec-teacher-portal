PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Remind children that they are planning and writing a descriptive essay today. Ask them to move to one side of the room or the other to indicate whether they think the answer is true (T) or false (F) to the following statements. Summarizing means telling someone everything you know about a topic. (F) You should use adjectives to describe people, animals, or things. (T) You should state your topic for the first time in the second paragraph. (F) Drawing and labelling pictures makes an essay more interesting for the reader. (T) Planning an essay well makes it easier to write your first draft. (T) Ask children to recall writing an email about an endangered species (Week 2), a report about teeth (Week 5), and an opinion essay about wheels (Week 8). Ask: Did you plan well? Did you use your time well? Children can identify where they might do things differently today. Encourage them in this process.

ICQ: What will you do first? Are you listening, speaking, reading, or writing?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford teacher demonstration: Tell children to plan and write a descriptive essay. Remind them of the five stages: mind map/graphic organizer, outline, first draft, check, final draft.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task and think aloud before learners attempt it.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford guided practice: Discuss how much time children think they need for each stage. Agree appropriate timings as a class, and write them on the board. Tell children that they will be responsible for managing their own time. Ask children to use their research from Week 11, Lesson 4 to summarize and organize their ideas. This will help them in the writing phase. Use Oxford Discover WB 5 p.79 or create a separate mind map. Remind them to focus on animal communication.

CCQ: Which words or evidence from the Oxford task support your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford collaborative practice: At the end of the lesson, invite volunteers to read their essay (or a section of it) to the whole class. Ask children if they have learned from their mistakes in previous weeks. Praise children who have improved their writing process. Identify particularly positive processes or attitudes from the whole class during this unit.

Scaffolding: Provide a short model or sentence frame, then gradually withdraw support.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford independent practice: During the checking stage, encourage children to check pronoun agreement. At the end of the lesson, invite volunteers to read their essay (or a section of it) to the whole class. Ask children if they have learned from their mistakes in previous weeks. Praise children who have improved their writing process. Identify particularly positive processes or attitudes from the whole class during this unit.

Independent evidence: Each learner demonstrates the original learning outcome independently.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work in a small group to share information and ideas. Provide a vocabulary bank and a list of sentence starters. An essay template with separate boxes for each section may also be used. Encourage less confident children by reminding them to think about question words to develop their descriptions. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to swap their essay with a partner. They should then make suggestions about how to improve the text. This might include using more descriptive vocabulary, or expanding or moving a paragraph. Each child can then discuss whether they agree with their partner and, if they do not, justify why not.

Oxford additional tasks: Record the essays to share with the wider school community.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have planned and written a descriptive essay.

Assessment: Check the stated OIC learning outcome using individual spoken, written, or practical evidence.
