TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Introduce a role-play activity between a shop assistant and customers in a greengrocer. Assign each ‘customer’ one or two food items to buy. Give each ‘shop assistant’ some pictures of fruits and vegetables (each shop should have different items) to give to their customers.
  2. Remind children of their learning in Week 10, Lesson 4, where they practised making polite offers. Elicit some of the phrases: ‘Would you like a/an …?’ and ‘Would you like to + infinitive?’. You can also teach them ‘What would you like …?’ and ‘I would like …’ to use in the role-play activity.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Ask children to remind you of what foods people grow in your local area. Have a quick discussion about the importance of locally grown food. See Oxford Discover SB 4 p.62 ex.C.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Elicit from children the health benefits of the locally grown food discussed. Then lead the discussion on to key nutrients.
  2. Write ‘vitamins’, then ‘A, B, C, D, E, K’ on the board. Help children to understand that fruits and vegetables are an important part of our diet because they contain lots of vitamins which help us to stay healthy. Give one or two examples under each vitamin (e.g. carrots – A). The exception is vitamin D, which our body makes from sunlight. Elicit from or explain to children how different vitamins/foods help different body parts (e.g. bones – C, D, K; brain – B, C; muscles – D, E).

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. In pairs, children talk about their favourite fruits and vegetables, and how they are good for them: e.g. I really like broccoli. It’s great for my skin, brain, and bones. Remind children to listen to their partner’s thoughts attentively, then share their own ideas.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Challenge children to design and label a nutritious plate of food with examples of each vitamin. If time, encourage them to talk about their design with their partner and explain why they have chosen particular food items.
  2. End the lesson with a whole-class discussion. Encourage children to reflect on what they’ve learned in the lesson and to consider if they get enough vitamins in their diet.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Adapt and improve the plate design, following feedback. Write a paragraph explaining their choices.

Oxford differentiation

  1. There is a lot of factual information to learn in this lesson. To make it less challenging, you can focus on fewer vitamins, like B, C, and D. Alternatively, focus on the health benefits of particular fruits and vegetables local to your area, instead of on the vitamin names.
  2. Tell more confident children to close their books and hide their notes. Challenge them to tell each other what they can remember about different vitamins, fruits, and vegetables.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned about the health benefits of fruits and vegetables, focusing on locally grown food and vitamin intake.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Getting food to the table’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills in whole-class discussion 
and pairwork.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
