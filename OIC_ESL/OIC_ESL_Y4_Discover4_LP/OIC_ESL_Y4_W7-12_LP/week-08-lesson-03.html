TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Elicit how we explain the reason for something. Write on the board: ‘I like strawberries. Strawberries are sweet.’ Ask children to add a word (because) in order to combine the two sentences into just one sentence.
  2. Play a game with the word because. Say: I’m going to the bakery tomorrow. Challenge a child to give a reason for going to the bakery: e.g. I’m going to the bakery because I want to buy some bread. Another child then explains why they want to buy some bread: e.g. I want to buy some bread because I want to make a sandwich. Keep going until all children have had a turn. Encourage them to be as imaginative as possible!

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children that they are going to talk about the foods that they prefer and explain why. Ensure that they understand the concept – to ‘prefer’ something is when you have a choice of things and you like one thing better than the other. Use classroom realia or the food items from the previous lesson to demonstrate, modelling the expression ‘I prefer … because …’: e.g. I prefer the green pencil because it’s my favourite colour and it reminds me of trees. I don’t like the colour black so much. See also Oxford Discover SB 4 p.54 ex.C.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Children return to their groups from the previous lesson (Week 8, Lesson 2). Hand out the group books they created. Tell children to look through their books together and pick two foods randomly. In turn, children tell each other which of the two foods they prefer and why. Encourage them to help each other to find the correct vocabulary to express their preferences if a group member is struggling. Remind them not to interrupt – first, they listen actively to what their classmate has to say, then they make a suggestion.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Children will likely express different preferences because they enjoy different types of food. Remind children to be respectful of each other’s opinions and try to understand each other’s reasons, even if they don’t agree. Reinforce: What makes us different is what makes us interesting!

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Bring the class back together. Discuss each other’s preferences and add a tally to the board to show the most and least popular foods among children.
  2. Ask children to write a paragraph about their preferences.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. In pairs, children can role-play shopping at a greengrocer. Encourage them to use the structures they learned to ask and answer about preferences, giving reasons for their choices.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Write ‘I prefer … because …’ on the board to support less confident children. Model a few times first to ensure their understanding.
  2. For more able children, model another expression using but: e.g. I like broccoli, but I prefer carrots because they are sweeter.
  3. Encourage more confident children to agree or disagree with their classmates’ preferences and explain why.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned to express their preferences on the topic of food.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Origins of food’.
In this lesson, children will continue to build on their speaking and writing skills by talking and writing about their food preferences.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
