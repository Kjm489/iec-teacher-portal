PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Play the ‘Shopping basket’ game. Say: I went to the market and bought some eggs. Ask a confident child to repeat your sentence and add another food item: e.g. I went to the market and bought some eggs and cheese. Explain that the aim of the game is to make a sentence with the fullest shopping basket.
  2. Divide the class into small groups. Each group plays the game and counts the number of items they have in their basket before making a mistake. The team with the most items at the end wins. Remind children about the power of words in making people feel comfortable and making friends – in the game, they should encourage each other and be empathetic when someone makes a mistake.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Present the food words from the Vocabulary box on the previous page. Use images to show what these are.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Tell children that they are going to read about a big, famous market. You may like to show children a picture of a market, such as the fish market in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.91.
  2. Ask: Have you ever been to a market? What can you buy there? Why do you think some people prefer to shop in markets, rather than in big supermarkets? (Elicit that produce is fresher in markets and grown locally by local people.) Discuss these questions as a class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Hand out the reading text ‘The Queen Victoria Market’. Ask children to scan the text and circle any words they do not know. In pairs, children ask for and give each other help to try to guess the meaning of the words from the context. Check that all children understand all the words and model pronunciation of any difficult sounds.
  2. Read through the text as a class.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. In small groups, children answer the comprehension questions. Check answers as a class.
  2. Tell children to imagine a visit to an interesting market and write a paragraph about it. Encourage them to use a range of adjectives to add interest, and to describe their feelings towards the new things they saw or bought. Tell them that they can use their imagination as much as they like: their story does not need to be realistic, but it should capture the reader’s interest and make them want to visit the market!

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. In pairs, children can read each other’s paragraphs and tell their partner one thing that they really liked, and one thing that they think their partner could improve. Focus on descriptive language, correct grammatical forms, spelling errors, and organization of ideas. Is there anything their partner could elaborate on?

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the reading task, less confident children can work in small groups and be guided by an adult.
  2. For more support with the writing task, allow children to organize their ideas into a graphic organizer first, or write useful topic vocabulary on the board.
  3. Challenge more confident children to add further details about their imaginary market visit. For example, they could describe the weather or the different sights, smells, and sounds to evoke a clear picture for the reader.
  4. Additional information: Week 7, Lesson 5
  5. The Queen Victoria Market
  6. Hi, I’m Akira. Yesterday, my sister and I spent the day at an amazing open-air market – the biggest in Australia! We’re on holiday with our family in Melbourne and the Queen Victoria Market is a national treasure. It’s more than 140 years old and has more than 600 small shops and businesses. We walked around for hours and enjoyed lots of food there, and brought even more home!
  7. First, we stopped at a famous bread shop. They sell so many delicious breads and pastries. I had a lemon tart and bought a loaf to have with our dinner. Later, we went to a colourful fruit and vegetables stall. Much of the produce isn’t sold in a supermarket, so I didn’t know what some of it was! That was definitely my favourite shop because I learned about different kinds of vegetable, like fennel, and tasted some unusual fruit. Finally, my sister bought a toy kangaroo at the souvenir shop to remember the trip.
  8. The market was fantastic and we took lots of photos with our camera. I can’t wait to show them to my friends when I get back home!
  9. Comprehension questions
  10. What is the name of the market that the children visited?
  11. Where is the market?
  12. How do you know that Akira liked the bread shop?
  13. What was Akira’s favourite shop and why?
  14. What did Akira’s sister buy as a souvenir?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have read a text about a famous market and answered comprehension questions. They have written about an imaginary visit to a market, using descriptive language and new topic vocabulary learned.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Origins of food’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by answering comprehension questions on a text. They will develop their writing skills in a creative writing task.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
