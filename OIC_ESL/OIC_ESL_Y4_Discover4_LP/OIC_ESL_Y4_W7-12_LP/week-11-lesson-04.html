PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Explain to children that in today’s lesson they are going to plan a research report.
  2. Ask: What is research? How can we conduct (do) good research? Discuss as a class. Write their ideas on the board. Aim to elicit ‘find information in books and other information texts’, ‘use the internet’ (reliable information websites, using key search terms to filter results), ‘visit places’ (e.g. look at a food label in a supermarket), ‘talk to experts’, and ‘make a survey’.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Show children a research report in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.64 or WB 4 p.56.
  2. Read the report as a class. Ask children what information is in each section.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Allow children to reread the report individually or in pairs.
  2. Give children a list of well-known dishes to choose from, or allow children to choose a dish. In pairs, they discuss the ingredients they would expect to find in the dish.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. On the board, write the headings: ‘Name of dish’, ‘List of ingredients’, ‘What I’ve learned from food labels’, ‘What I’ve learned from people’, and ‘What I’ve learned from books and the internet’.
  2. Ask children to use these headings in their plan. They can organize their ideas using their preferred planning method. This could be a graphic organizer in the form of a table or mind map (see also Oxford Discover WB 4 p.56). Remind them to think about the different steps they need to follow to find the information they need. Ask: What task do you think you need to prioritize? What will you do first? What will you do after?

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Encourage children to take a few deep breaths and reflect on their work so far.
  2. Children have the rest of the lesson to research the origin of the ingredients. Monitor them actively and ensure that they are using sensible sources to find the information they need. If you cannot provide internet access or reading material, advise children to spend the time planning how they will find the information before the next lesson.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Write a first draft of the title and introductory paragraph for the research report.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Given the time constraints, you can choose to pre-prepare and look at different information sources for one dish as a class. Support less able children in a small group during the planning stages. Allow more able children to make their plan individually.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have planned a research report about the origins of ingredients in a dish. They have used a variety of information sources to conduct their research within a time limit.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Getting food to the table’.
In this lesson, children will develop their research skills by planning a research report.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
