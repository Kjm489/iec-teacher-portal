TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write all the letters of the alphabet on the board or hold up alphabet flashcards. In teams, children try to think of something which you can grow or find in a garden for each letter. Some letters will be more difficult than others, so challenge children to ‘think outside of the box’ and be creative. They are in the stretch zone and it’s OK if they can’t think of an example for every single letter. As a class, write answers and suggestions on the board. Award a point for each correct answer – the team with the most points wins.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain to children that they are going to talk about gardens and vegetable plots. Ask: Can anyone tell me what a ‘vegetable plot’ is? Does anyone have one? What do you grow in it? Elicit answers and discuss as a class. Encourage children to think of as many vegetable words as they can.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Pre-teach or revise prepositional phrases of location, using the prepositions ‘behind’, ‘between’, ‘near’, ‘next to’, ‘on’, and ‘under’. Highlight that these phrases include a preposition and its object: e.g. The potatoes are next to (prep.) the cabbage (obj.).
  2. Turn to p.63 ex.E in Oxford Discover SB 4. Children work individually to complete the exercise. Check answers as a class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Tell children to close their books and look away from the board. In small groups, ask them to list as many prepositions as they can think of, including ones already looked at in the lesson, and any others they might know, such as ‘among’, ‘around’, ‘in front of’, or ‘across’. They can draw diagrams to explain their thinking to each other.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Show children a photo or illustration of a vegetable plot for their reference, then ask them to draw their own vegetable plot individually. Allow them enough time to do this (e.g. five to ten minutes – it doesn’t need to be a work of art!). Then put children into pairs. In turn, they describe their vegetable plots to their partner using prepositional phrases of location. Their partner listens carefully and draws a vegetable plot to match what they understand from the description.
  2. At the end of the activity, children show each other their drawings and see how close they were. Prompt them to discuss any errors (if something is positioned in the wrong place) and reflect on what they could say differently next time so their message is clearer.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.55.
  2. Write a paragraph describing the vegetable plot created in the main activity.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the drawing activity, provide a vegetable plot template for children to complete with the names of different vegetables.
  2. Pair up similar abilities in the speaking activity so more confident children can challenge each other, using a range of interesting prepositions. Allow less able children to work at their own pace, with fewer vegetables to describe in their plots.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have described where something is using prepositional phrases of location. They have applied their learning to the topic of locally grown food (vegetable plots).

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Getting food to the table’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills in a description activity.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
