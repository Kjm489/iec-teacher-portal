PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. In pairs, ask children to tell each other about their favourite meal that someone makes for them. It could be a parent, a grandparent, a carer, or just a friend. Ask one or two more confident children to tell you how to make the meal.
  2. Help children to understand that making food takes effort and that it is nice to show gratitude to the person who cooks for us. Elicit how they feel when they express gratitude.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Present the cooking verbs from the Vocabulary box on the previous page. Use mime to clearly explain the meaning of the words. You could also show images or online videos to indicate the different actions. If you use images, show children the pictures of lasagne and the fruit salad from Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.64–65. Discuss how the meals have been prepared and practise using some of the action verbs.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Write five of the most common meals discussed by children on the board. Ask the class to vote for one by a show of hands.
  2. Pretend to be a chef giving a tutorial on how to prepare that meal. Write a list of ingredients on the board. Mime some of the actions while you explain clearly all the different steps to follow to make the meal.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Ask children to note down the recipe and try to write any new words, sounding them out phonetically. Reassure them that it is OK if they are not sure about how to spell some of the words.
  2. In pairs, children compare their notes. Encourage them to ask and give each other help with any information that they are not sure about, or any word meanings or spellings that they find difficult.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Check that all children understand the main points of the recipe, and write new or difficult words on the board. Model the pronunciation of any difficult sounds.
  2. In small groups, children make a poster to illustrate the recipe using mixed media (pictures from the internet, food magazines, or their own drawings). Ask them to nominate a speaker for their group. The speaker will present the poster to the class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Write a paragraph about a favourite dish. Encourage children to write about who makes it for them, what’s in it, and what they like about it.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the introductory activity, begin by telling children about your favourite dish that someone makes for you. Go into slightly more detail than you expect children to.
  2. Challenge more confident children by asking them to spell the words that they do not know, so they should think about how the words sound.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have discussed their favourite meal and listened to a simple recipe. They have worked together in a team to produce a poster of the recipe.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Week 7, Lesson 4: My favourite meal
Learning outcomes: 4.1b, 4.1g, 4.2a

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Origins of food’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to a recipe and discussing their favourite meal.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
