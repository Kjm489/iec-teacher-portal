PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. In today’s lesson, children are going to learn about different ways of growing food. Ask: How do you think farmers grow most of the food we eat? Elicit from children what they already know about farming in their country. If they have some knowledge on the topic, challenge them to think about the advantages and disadvantages of this type of farming.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.62 ex.A. Play the audio (track 1.40) and ask children to listen for gist. Tell them to note down any words they don’t understand. Write them on the board and give simple explanations to help them to understand the three types of farming: ‘terraced’, ‘hydroponic’, and ‘rooftop’. In turn, hold up pictures of the three types of farm. Ask children to call out their names, carefully sounding out the words on the board. Mix up the pictures and drill a few times.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Play the audio twice more (tracks 1.40 and 1.41). Children complete exs.A–B in pairs.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Check children’s understanding: Which type of farming doesn’t need soil? Which type is good for plants that need lots of water? Where can we find a rooftop farm?

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. In pairs, children discuss which type of farming they think is the most interesting. Encourage them to expand on their answers and give reasons using because. Invite a few volunteers to share their ideas with the class.
  2. Then divide the class into small groups. The aim of their task is to discuss which type of farming would be most suitable for their village/town/city. Allow them to draw their ideas on A3 paper to illustrate their thinking. At the end of the lesson, groups nominate a speaker to present their ideas to the class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Expand on the lesson’s group work by asking children to create a final, illustrated plan of their preferred type of farming for their village/town/city. Encourage them to label their ideas with new vocabulary learned, and write a few sentences to explain why this type of farming would work best and why.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Use the pictures to guide children through the different types of farming. Less confident children don’t need to learn the words ‘terraced’, ‘hydroponic’, and ‘rooftop’. Give clear, simple explanations.
  2. Challenge more able children to think about possible positive and negative aspects of their chosen farming method and explain why.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned about different farming methods. They have listened to and understood the main points of each, and discussed their ideas in pairs and small groups.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Getting food to the table’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by understanding the main points in a listening text and sharing their opinions on the topic.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
