TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell the class that today they are going to talk about future plans.
  2. Remind children that they have already learned a few grammatical forms for talking about the future (see Week 1, Lesson 3: predictions with will). Elicit how we can use going to + infinitive (e.g. We’re going to swim in the sea next weekend.) and will + infinitive (e.g. I will go to my friend’s wedding. / I think there will be a spelling test tomorrow.).
  3. In pairs, children work together to make three sentences about the future using both forms. Invite some volunteers to share their sentences. Ask the class if the future tense has been used correctly. Why / Why not?

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain to the class that we can also use the present continuous to talk about future plans (which have already been arranged). It is often accompanied by a future time word. See Oxford Discover SB 4 p.53.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Write on the board: ‘We’re having a party next weekend. I’m buying some eggs tomorrow. My brother is baking a big cake on Friday evening.’ In turn, ask volunteers to go to the board, and circle or underline examples of the present continuous and future time words.
  2. Then write on the board: ‘We’re making a cake now.’ Ask: Is this a future plan? When is the action happening? Help children to understand that the sentence is still an example of the present continuous, but the time word is ‘now’ – the action is happening at the time of speaking, so it isn’t a future plan.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Now challenge children to write one example of the present continuous that describes what’s happening now, and one example that describes a future plan. Encourage them to use food and cooking words they’ve recently learned in the unit.
  2. When you’re confident that children have a firm grasp of the present continuous and its function, elicit the interrogative and negative forms: e.g. Are you baking a coffee cake? No, I’m not baking a coffee cake. I’m inventing a recipe for a carrot cake.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Explain that in short conversations, children might not understand every word, which is OK. Sometimes it is helpful to listen for rising and falling intonation to detect a question. For example, in ‘yes/no’ questions, the speaker’s voice rises at the end of the sentence: e.g. Are you baking a cake for me? Model this for the class.
  2. Ask children to write two to three questions in the present continuous (for future plans). A simple but useful real-life example would be: What are you doing at the weekend? They walk around the classroom, and ask and answer each other’s questions, practising using the present continuous orally. Monitor actively, without interrupting the flow of conversation. Go through any common errors at the end of the class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.44–45.
  2. List as many future time words and expressions as possible, then write a sentence in the present continuous for each.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the speaking activity, ask children questions using the interrogative form. They can practise saying affirmative sentences only until they grow in confidence.
  2. Encourage more able children to use a variety of question words and expand on their answers using because.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned to talk and ask about future plans using the present continuous.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Origins of food
Week 8, Lesson 1: What are we baking tomorrow?
Learning outcomes: 4.1d, 4.2b, 4.2c

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Origins of food’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by talking about future plans using the present continuous and future time words.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
