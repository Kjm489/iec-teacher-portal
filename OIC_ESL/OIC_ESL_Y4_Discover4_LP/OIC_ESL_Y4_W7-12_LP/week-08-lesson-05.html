TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Show children how to draw a five-column table with the headings ‘Monday’ to ‘Friday’ (one weekday for each column) on their whiteboards. Tell them to note down one of their favourite meals under each day.
  2. Demonstrate asking and answering questions about the meals using the present continuous for future plans: e.g. What are you having for dinner on Tuesday? I’m having pasta with courgettes. In pairs, children tell each other their plans.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children that they are going to write a paragraph about a delicious meal. Turn to ex.B in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.47 as a guide.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Read the text aloud and children follow. Ask the class if there are any words they don’t know. Write them on the board, sound them out, and discuss their meanings as a class.
  2. Ask children to identify the commas. Elicit what their function is in this context (to separate food items in a list; to remind you to pause when reading aloud).

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Demonstrate how to break down a favourite meal into a mind map layout. See the example provided – it should have the name of the meal, each ingredient, and adjective ideas to describe each ingredient. Ask children to create their own mind maps.
  2. In pairs, children look at each other’s mind maps and make suggestions about how they could be improved. They might think of a more interesting adjective, or a forgotten ingredient.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Allow children enough time to write their paragraph using their mind map to help them. Explain that the activity will stretch them to extend their understanding of food vocabulary, new grammatical forms, interesting adjectives, and punctuation. Challenge them to include an example of the present continuous for future plans in the final sentence: e.g. I’m taking my delicious salad to a family reunion tomorrow!
  2. Invite volunteers to read out their work to the class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. In pairs, children read each other’s paragraphs. Ask them to discuss one thing that they really liked, and one thing that could be improved. They revise their paragraphs based on the feedback received. Remind them to check their spelling and punctuation.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Vary the difficulty of the writing task by asking more confident children to write about a three-course meal, while less confident children can focus on a simple dish.
  2. For more support with the feedback task, provide children with a few useful expressions or areas to give feedback on: e.g. correct spelling, correct use of commas, repetition, or use of interesting adjectives.
  3. Challenge more confident children to use more than one adjective to describe their food: e.g. ‘a sweet, juicy orange segment’.
  4. Additional information: Week 8, Lesson 5
  5. Example mind map

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have planned a descriptive piece of writing using a mind map. They have written a paragraph about a delicious meal, and peer-reviewed each other’s work.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Origins of food’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by writing a descriptive paragraph about a delicious meal.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
