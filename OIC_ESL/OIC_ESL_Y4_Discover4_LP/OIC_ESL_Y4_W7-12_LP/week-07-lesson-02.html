Original Oxford lesson: Drawing conclusions

Original learning outcomes: 4.2c, 4.3f

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Divide the class into small groups. Give each group a set of pictures and a set of labels for different foods. Tell each group to work together to match the pictures to the correct labels. Check answers as a class and model the pronunciation of any difficult sounds.
  2. Stick a set of correctly labelled pictures on the board so that children can refer to the words during the main activity.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children that they are going to read a story and draw simple conclusions. Explain that ‘drawing a conclusion’ is when we make a decision about something, based on the information we have read and what we already know. Ask children to relax their bodies, close their eyes, and take a few deep breaths before starting the main activity.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. In pairs, children complete ex.D in SB 4 p.49. Check answers as a class and ensure that all children are clear about the meaning of drawing a conclusion.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Read the preview to the reading text ‘The Breakfast Quest’ on p.49. Ask children to make predictions about the content of the story.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Read the text aloud, with children following. Have a class discussion about the story and check if children’s predictions were correct. Reassure children that it doesn’t matter if they didn’t understand all the words.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.42–43.
  2. Write a simple conclusion in response to the text. Support it with information from the text and/or pre-existing knowledge.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the reading task, focus on one or two paragraphs only instead of the whole text.
  2. Challenge more confident children to summarize the text orally with their books closed.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have practised drawing conclusions and predicting the content of a reading text.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
