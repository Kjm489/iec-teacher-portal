TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Play a warm-up game of ‘Hot seat’. Demonstrate with a confident child. Ask the child to sit on a chair, facing away from the board. Write the name of a familiar dish on the board. The rest of the class calls out clues about the dish (without saying its name!) for the child on the hot seat to guess. Encourage them to think of different ingredients, where the dish comes from, and how much their classmate likes the dish. Allow a few children to have a turn in the hot seat.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children they are going to talk about what goes into different dishes and look again at quantities. Elicit what they can remember about expressing quantities. They learned about How much …? and How many …? (and countable and uncountable nouns) in Week 2, Lesson 4. By thinking again about this language point, children are building their long-term memory of this learning. See Oxford Discover SB 4 p.22 ex.C Learn box.
  2. Show children the ingredients list for a recipe. Draw their attention to the picture. Ask: Do you want to try this dish? Why / Why not?

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Now tell them to look at the list of ingredients. Ask: How can we ask questions about what is in this dish? Elicit: What is in it? How much …? How many …?
  2. Write examples of questions and answers on the board: e.g. How many apples do we need? We need five apples. (countable); How much flour do we need? We need 400 g of flour. (uncountable); Is there too much salt? Yes, I think there is too much. (uncountable).
  3. Write different foods (singular and plural) on the board for children to copy down. Individually or in pairs, they decide whether to use ‘many’ or ‘much’ for each. Check answers as a class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Introduce the role-play task. Divide the class into pairs and give each pair the ingredients list for a recipe. Children read the ingredients list and imagine that they are going food shopping for the ingredients, using How much …? and How many …?.
  2. Encourage them to think of actions or use props (if available) to make their role-plays more interesting (e.g. pushing a trolley, taking things from shelves).

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Invite a few pairs to perform their role-play in front of the class.
  2. After completing the role-plays, children discuss their performances. Ask them to note the strengths of their partner (e.g. varied use of vocabulary, correct use of grammatical forms, creativity, acting ability, good listening skills, etc.) and what they did well.
  3. Elicit how positive and constructive feedback makes them feel.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Form new pairs. Repeat the role-play activity using a different recipe.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the role-play activity, demonstrate with a confident child first. Spend more time reviewing the grammar with less confident children, if necessary.
  2. Challenge more able children by giving them longer, more complicated ingredients lists.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned how to ask and answer about food quantities. They have practised using How much …? and How many …? in a role-play.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
