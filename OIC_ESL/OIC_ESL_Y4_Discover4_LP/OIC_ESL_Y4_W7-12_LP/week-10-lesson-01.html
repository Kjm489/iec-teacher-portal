Original Oxford lesson: Food production

Original learning outcomes: 4.1a, 4.1b, 4.1c, 4.3b, 4.3e

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children that they are starting a new unit today called ‘Getting food to the table’. Ask: What do you think we are going to learn about in this unit? In pairs, children discuss their predictions. Invite volunteers to share their ideas with the class.
  2. Establish that ‘Getting food to the table’ will look at how food is produced, and the different things (‘processes’) that happen before it arrives on our plate.
  3. Elicit from children what they already know about food production. If they need prompting, ask them to consider what they ate for their dinner the night before and where the different ingredients came from.
  4. In the same pairs, ask children to write down three questions on the topic that they would like to find out the answers to.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Write the lesson’s vocabulary on the board in a random order, or create flashcards. Carefully sound out the words and ask children to listen and repeat. Drill a few times. Play the first part of the audio in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.56 ex.A (track 1.37).

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Explain to children that the words they are learning are difficult and it’s OK if they don’t remember them straightaway, or struggle with spelling. Remind them that they are in the ‘stretch zone’ and hearing these words frequently will help them to understand the topic. Encourage them to write the words down so they can refer back to them throughout the unit. It will also familiarize them with what the words look like.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Now children are going to look at the words in context. Play the second part of the audio in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.56 ex.A (track 1.37). Children follow the sentences in their book while they listen. After each sentence, pause the audio and read out the sentence with children. Then invite a few confident children to read out the sentence independently.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. In small groups, ask children to look at the sentences they’ve just read aloud. Challenge them to work out the meaning of the new vocabulary from the context the sentences provide. Review answers as a class and model the pronunciation of any difficult sounds.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.56 exs.B–C and WB 4 pp.48–49.
  2. Children can create their own gap-fill exercises to test their partner on the new vocabulary. Encourage them to write simple sentences.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the small-group activity, say a few synonyms or key words to guide children’s thinking.
  2. Challenge more confident children to lead the small-group activity, making sure that everyone understands the task, takes turns, and has the opportunity to share ideas.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have listened to new vocabulary on the topic of food production, first in isolation and then placed in context. They have made predictions about the meaning of the words.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
