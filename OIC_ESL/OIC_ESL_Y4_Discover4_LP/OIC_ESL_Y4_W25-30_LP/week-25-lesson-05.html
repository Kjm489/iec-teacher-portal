Original Oxford lesson: How do you catch a cold?

Original learning outcomes: 4.1d, 4.1g, 4.1h, 4.2b

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children to work with a partner to list all the objects they touch during a typical school day. Check answers as a class and write them on the board.
  2. In small groups, ask children to think about and discuss how germs and viruses might enter our body, and what we can do to keep colds away. Encourage them to consider how germs can spread easily in school because of the items listed on the board.
  3. Draw a mind map on the board with ‘Healthy habits to keep colds away’ written at the centre. Invite children to call out ideas from their group discussion. Add all sensible answers to the mind map.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children that they are going to learn more about how we catch common viruses, such as a cold or a stomach virus, by doing some listening and speaking activities.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask children to look at the picture in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.114 ex.A and discuss what the girl is doing and why. Confirm that she is washing her hands to keep them clean and free of germs. Play the audio once (track 2.33) so children can listen freely to the announcement. Ask the class what the purpose of the announcement might be, and who might want to broadcast such a message. Then ask children to try to match the sentence halves independently. Play the audio again to check answers. Elicit what the main idea of the text is. Move on to ex.B and play the audio (track 2.34) once. Play the audio again so children can answer the questions, again independently. Check answers as a class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Revisit the mind map from the introductory activity. Challenge small groups of children to suggest more healthy habits which they would like to add now that they have learned more about how we catch a virus. Add new nodes labelled ‘exercise’, ‘food and drink’, and ‘sleep’ to the mind map. Encourage children to copy it down and add their new ideas.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. To support children in generating ideas, tell groups to consider what the risks of taking no action to prevent catching a cold or a stomach virus might be. For example, ask what might happen if somebody does too little exercise, or has a poor diet or too little sleep.
  2. Ask children to make a fun exercise routine they can use at home in order to take tangible steps towards adopting healthy habits. Elicit that this may help to reduce their own risk of illness.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Ask children to interview a partner about their healthy habits, then report back to the class what their partner’s healthy habits are. Support children by telling them what healthy habits you observe and why. You could write questions 1, 3, and 4 in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.114 ex.B on the board to prompt children’s thinking.

Oxford differentiation

  1. To support less able children during the listening activity, pause the audio and ask them to mime the main actions mentioned in each section of the announcement.
  2. Challenge more able children to infer the theme of the announcement.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have listened to a public health announcement about avoiding catching a cold. They have talked about how to adopt healthy habits.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
