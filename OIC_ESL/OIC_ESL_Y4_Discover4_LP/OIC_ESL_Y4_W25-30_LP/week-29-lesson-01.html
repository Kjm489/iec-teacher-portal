PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Show two suitable blogs that will interest the class and ask children to describe the subjects. If you do not have internet access, you could show them a screenshot of a real blog, or use the blog in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.138–139. Next, ask children if they read blogs. Elicit some common blog features, such as ‘posts’, ‘comments’, ‘contact details’, ‘text’, ‘photos’, and ‘videos’. Put target words for children to repeat on the board. Extend by showing them more examples of different types of blog.
  2. Ask who is the publisher of a blog and how it reaches readers. Explain that anyone (with internet access) can publish blogs. Check understanding of ‘publish’. Write it on the board.
  3. Use a free, online blogging tool to show how to set up a blog. Prompt children to suggest a title and content ideas for a class blog, considering what is different about having and reading a blog. Read out the Think box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.134. Encourage the class to think about who they would like to read their blog.
  4. Depending on internet access and school timetables, set the publication of class blog updates as an ongoing activity. Groups can take turns overseeing a different element of the blog each day/week, such as writing, design, or artwork. Remind children to allocate tasks among themselves so that initially they can play to their strengths, but to challenge themselves later on by stepping outside their comfort zones.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Do the listening for gist activity in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.134 ex.A (track 3.05). Elicit from children what the speaker is teaching us. Then read the questions in ex.B before listening for details (track 3.06). Check answers as a class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Next, ask children if they know what job pronouns do. Review their existing knowledge and help them further with a quick grammar explanation. Read the Learn box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.135 ex.E.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Hand out the reading text ‘A summary of Tina’s blog’ (see ‘Additional information’ page). Challenge children to find pronouns in the text. Give an example answer and elicit which noun the pronoun replaces. Check answers and address any common mistakes. Show versions of the text with the pronouns and redundant nouns highlighted to aid understanding.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Ask children to write three sentences about how they use mass media at home. Tell them to swap their work with a partner, who will write follow-up sentences with pronouns.
  2. Complete Oxford Discover WB 4 p.123 exs.A–B.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Pause after each section of the audio to elicit main ideas and supporting details. This should help less confident children to answer the questions.
  2. Challenge more confident children to complete the listening for details activity after listening only once. They can check their answers on the second play.
  3. Additional information: Week 29, Lesson 1
  4. A summary of Tina’s blog
  5. Tina writes a blog about her city, but it is also about nature. She says that her blog entertains readers. It teaches people new things, too. Tina writes blog updates on Saturday afternoons. She tries to be professional when she publishes it. Tina checks for mistakes before publication, and if she finds any, she removes them. Tina reminds children to stay safe when they publish a blog. She says that we mustn’t publish photos, home addresses, or surnames because our safety is important.
