PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children some facts about your school or local region. Then recall their learning by eliciting that facts can be proven, while opinions are determined by individual preference. Invite volunteers to suggest some more facts. Write them on the board.
  2. Add some adjectives to transform one of the facts into a short sentence that gives an opinion. Next, challenge children in small groups to transform the rest of the facts into short sentences that give opinions. Before they begin, elicit what kind of word they can use to give opinions – an adjective. Monitor discreetly and note common errors. Check answers as a class and address any common errors you noted.
  3. Teach children that the use of adjectives is often a clue that an opinion is being given. Share ideas about types of news content that usually deal with facts (like weather and police incidents) and opinions (like editorials, music reviews, and fashion reports).

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.142 and play the audio once in ex.A (track 3.12). Instruct children to listen actively for clues about the news being fact or opinion. Then play the audio a second time and ask children to match the pieces of news to the people.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask children to decide whether the piece of news is a fact or an opinion. If they think it is a fact, they move to one side of the classroom; if they think it is an opinion, they move to the other side. Check answers together, reviewing the use of adjectives.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Move on to ex.B in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.142. Read the questions before playing the audio (track 3.13). Put children into pairs to compare answers, then review as a class.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Introduce natural ways to give opinions when speaking. Read the Learn box in ex.C together. Play the audio (track 3.14) and pause occasionally to let children mimic the pronunciation and intonation of the speakers. After children have modelled the conversation, give them time to practise it with a partner to share their opinions about mass media. Invite volunteers to present their conversation.
  2. Explain that synonyms are useful for giving a wide range of opinions. Read the Learn box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.143 and complete ex.D together.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Make a whole-class list of synonyms for common adjectives.
  2. Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.130.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Support less able children by pausing the audio occasionally to check comprehension.
  2. Challenge fast finishers to write five facts and five opinions on the topic of mass media.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. In this lesson, children have learned to distinguish between facts and opinions, and practised their intonation. They have thought about synonyms to give a personal opinion.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 4 Mass media
Week 29, Lesson 4: Facts and opinions
Learning outcomes: 4.1d, 4.1i, 4.1j, 4.2c

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Mass media’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by distinguishing between facts and opinions in speech. They will improve their speaking skills by practising their intonation when giving opinions. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
