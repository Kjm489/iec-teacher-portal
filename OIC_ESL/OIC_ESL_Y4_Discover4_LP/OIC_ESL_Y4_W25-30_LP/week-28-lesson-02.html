PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Hand out the text ‘Mass media on my smartphone’. In pairs, ask children to scan it and highlight any key words they learned in the first lesson (Week 28, Lesson 1). As a class, check answers, then chain read the text together. Invite a confident child to read aloud until they reach a word they learned in the first lesson. When they reach it, they stop. The child next to them continues to read until the next target word is reached.
  2. Remind children of the concept of a main idea from the previous unit. They must review this learning regularly to deepen their understanding of how to analyse and understand a text. Elicit the main idea in the lesson’s text – the author uses a smartphone to connect with mass media. Follow up by asking if each sentence has a main idea, or something else.
  3. Invite a volunteer to read aloud the Learn box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.129 ex.D, before discussing the questions in the Think section in small groups. Check that all children understand this before moving on.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Encourage groups to discuss what they would include in a paragraph about learning new information about the world. Tell them to share ideas and to make a plan in note form. Remind them to put their main idea first, with supporting details afterwards. Present this on the board as a graphic organizer to help them visualize their ideas.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask children to read the text in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.129 ex.D. Support them to fill in the organizer based on Derek’s news habits. A confident child can read out the text about Derek first. Invite volunteers to write an answer on the board. Check all answers.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Look at ex.E together. Read aloud each new word slowly to model any difficult pronunciations, and ask children to repeat. Encourage them to guess the meanings of the words based on existing knowledge.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Ask children to scan the text in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.130–131. Confirm the meanings of the new words as a class and compare them with children’s previous guesses.
  2. In small groups, ask children to discuss any good news they shared at school this week, saying where and how they heard it, and why it gave them positive emotions.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. In pairs, draw and label a timeline charting the development of mass media, starting with the printing press and moving towards the present day.

Oxford differentiation

  1. To support less able children, read the text together in an adult-led small group. Focus on the vocabulary they learned in Week 28, Lesson 1 to reinforce their learning, instead of introducing more new words.
  2. Challenge fast finishers to write their own sentences using new vocabulary in context.
  3. Additional information: Week 28, Lesson 2
  4. Mass media on my smartphone
  5. I use my smartphone to connect with mass media. It’s great because I can go on the internet to read blogs, watch live news broadcasts, and check social networks anywhere. Sometimes I use it on the school bus, or at home in the evenings. Many publishers also make apps. I use these to read their newspapers and magazines, often without advertising. Before I had a smartphone, I used to get my news from a printed newspaper. It seems so old-fashioned now because information travels faster than ever using smartphones! Can you imagine a world without devices?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have read about media habits, distinguishing the main idea from supporting details.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Mass media
Week 28, Lesson 2: Learning new information about the world
Learning outcomes: 4.2c, 4.3e, 4.3f, 4.3g

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Mass media’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by chain reading a short text about someone’s media habits and discussing how they learn new information about the world. They will practise pronunciation of key words and learn how to distinguish the main idea from supporting details in a text.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
