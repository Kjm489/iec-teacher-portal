TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Introduce the new unit and ask children to make predictions about what they think they are going to learn about in pairs. Take some suggestions and write them on the board.
  2. Ask: What is mass media? Put children into small groups to list as many forms of mass media as they can. Take some suggestions and write them on the board.
  3. Challenge children to identify a theme for all the forms of media they know, namely the sharing or transfer of information. Aid them by asking what links the items on the board.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Play the video in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.127. Then ask children to look at the picture on the double-page spread. What is the man/boy doing?

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask children to discuss in pairs what is happening in all the pictures, and what types of mass media they show (or what mass media could be accessed using these items). If they watched the video, encourage a discussion about what they saw. As a class, review answers and the predictions children made about the unit.
  2. Tell children that, in this unit, they are going to think about different types of mass media over time and how people engage with them. Show children items of realia to enhance their understanding. Let them briefly handle newspapers, magazines, and a smartphone (if possible).

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Add any outstanding vocabulary from the lesson to the list on the board, then say the words. Ask children to listen and repeat, then drill any difficult sounds. You can use target vocabulary flashcards to do this. Encourage children to write down new vocabulary.
  2. Ask pairs of children to discuss the questions in exs.B–C in the Big Question section in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.127, then check answers as a class. Next, play the audio in SB 4 p.128 ex.A (track 3.02). Tell children to read the words as they listen. Then replay the audio for them to repeat the words. Ask children to do ex.B independently, before comparing answers in pairs.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Hold a whole-class discussion by asking children how they get their news and where from. Ask them to consider how they feel when they hear important news, and how they feel when they share important news which they have read/heard about from mass media. Help them to understand that sometimes news items circulated may be an opinion rather than a fact.
  2. Ask children to complete Oxford Discover SB 4 p.128 ex.C independently.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Answer the questions in the Big Question section in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.116. Then complete exs.A–D in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.116–117.
  2. Encourage children to create their own games to test each other on the new vocabulary.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Support less able children by providing definitions for the target words as you introduce them: e.g. A publisher is a company that makes books, newspapers, or magazines.
  2. When introducing target vocabulary in the main activity, challenge more able children to write down sentences that use at least two of the words in context.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have predicted the content of the unit, activated their existing knowledge of mass media, and begun to learn the meanings of target vocabulary.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Mass media
Week 28, Lesson 1: What is mass media?
Learning outcomes: 4.1e, 4.1f, 4.2c

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Mass media’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by predicting the content of the unit, discussing what they already know about mass media, and practising the pronunciation of target vocabulary.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
