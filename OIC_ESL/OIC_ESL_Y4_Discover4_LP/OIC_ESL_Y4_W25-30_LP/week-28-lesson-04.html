TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Invite a volunteer to briefly summarize for the rest of the class the gist of the interview they studied in the last lesson (see Week 28, Lesson 3).
  2. Play a warm-up game of ‘Hot seat’. Demonstrate with a more confident child. Ask the child to sit on a chair, facing away from the board. Write the name of a form of mass media on the board, then ask the rest of the class to call out clues about it (without saying its name!) for the child in the hot seat to guess. Encourage children to describe senses, materials, size, shape, how old it is, and how much they like it. Allow a few different children to be in the hot seat.
  3. When enough target words are on the board, challenge small groups of children to put them into chronological order of their invention. Remind them that by reviewing their knowledge of this timeline, they are building their long-term memory of the information. Review answers as a class and organize a timeline on the board with input from children.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain to the class that they are going to spend the rest of the lesson completing comprehension activities on the interview text. Remind them that the answers can always be found somewhere in the text. They can return to it during the tasks as necessary.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. First, ask children to write down three questions about mass media that they would now like the answers to after reading the interview. Ask them to tell a partner. Use the Think box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.132 to support teaching.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Next, ask children to do ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.132 independently. Check answers as a class. For extra support, go through the example answer, reminding children to think about their learning around main ideas and supporting details from recent lessons.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Then ask children to try ex.B independently, working from memory first, before returning to the text to check their answers. Take the opportunity to add to, amend, or correct the timeline the class made during the opening activity.
  2. Encourage children to find the target words from ex.C in the text in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.132. They should use their existing knowledge, the context of the topic, and guesswork to try to say the meaning of each word before they complete the exercise in pairs. Review answers as a class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–E in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.119.
  2. In pairs, encourage children to think of three more questions the journalist could ask the interviewee. Then imagine answers the interviewee might give and write them down.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Help less able children during ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.132 by highlighting clues in the text that can help them match the main ideas and supporting details. Ask targeted questions to aid understanding.
  2. As an extension task for more able children during ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.132, ask them to identify the main ideas and supporting details about radio and television.
  3. Fast finishers can make their own comprehension questions to test a partner.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have improved their reading skills by tackling comprehension questions on an interview text. They have reinforced their understanding of mass media vocabulary.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
