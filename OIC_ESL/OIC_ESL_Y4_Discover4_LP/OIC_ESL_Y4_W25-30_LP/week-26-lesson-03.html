Original Oxford lesson: Body systems

Original learning outcomes: 4.1b, 4.2a, 4.3e, 4.3h

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write the nouns from the vocabulary list on the board, or use flashcards. Say a word then ask the class to indicate to the word or related flashcard on the board.
  2. Next, say the words again and get children to repeat them. Model any difficult sounds and drill a few times. Play the audio (track 2.37) in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.116 ex.A. Pause after each sentence. Challenge children to define the words. Help them to perfect their definitions by miming or indicating to your own body parts/systems.
  3. Complete exs.B–C in pairs. Isolate the target vocabulary that belongs to the respiratory and circulatory systems, then ask pairs to categorize the words.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children to forget the new vocabulary for a moment. Ask them to pause, close their eyes, and take a few deep breaths with their noses and mouths. Ask how both methods feel. Urge them to focus on their breathing and notice which part of their body moves in and out. Have a vote and a short class discussion on which method is healthier and why (nose breathing has the advantage of nasal hairs which help to keep germs out).
  2. Read the Learn box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.117 with the class about the sequence of actions in a non-fiction text. Alternatively, explain how writers use sequence words to indicate the order of actions (see the lesson’s vocabulary list).

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Write sentences on the board that give examples of sequence words in use:
  2. First, I do ten star jumps. Next, I do ten squats. After, I drink some water.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Before I sneeze, I get a tissue.
  2. I always wash my hands and, as a result, I don't get viruses often.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Challenge children to choose in which order the events in your sentences happened.
  2. Ask children to do ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.117 independently after reading the text as a class. They can then complete ex.E by scanning the longer text on pp.118–119. Remind children that the aim is to guess the meanings of the new words and that making mistakes is a natural part of the learning process. Encourage them to do the actions in the story as each line is read out.
  3. Tell children to discuss the picture of parts and systems of the human body in pairs and to do Oxford Discover SB 4 p.117 ex.F. Check answers as a class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–D in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.105–106.
  2. Encourage children to read more about the body systems online or in reference books.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Support less able children by using pictures to help them define the words. For example, draw a heart with arrows on the blood vessels to show their different roles.
  2. Fast finishers can write ‘True/False’ sentences about the body systems, then swap them with a partner for a quiz.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned words for body systems and understood their function. They have identified the sequence of actions in a non-fiction text.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
