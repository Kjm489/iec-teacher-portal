PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Draw a two-column table on the board with the headings ‘Symptoms’ and ‘Remedies’. Write ‘sore throat’ as a symptom and elicit from children what a remedy might be, like having a hot drink or taking medicine. Do actions to prompt their thinking.
  2. Check that children understand the meaning of ‘symptoms’ and ‘remedies’. In pairs, ask them to make a list of the symptoms of illnesses they might get, like a common cold or the flu. Next, ask them to note down the remedies they use to fight the various symptoms.
  3. Challenge pairs to also list the things they do normally to stay healthy. Tell them they can take inspiration from the mind map they created in Week 25, Lesson 5.
  4. Ask children how many of them said they drink plenty of water. Encourage them to think of reasons why in pairs. Confirm that hydration is vital for good health. Then elicit reasons why hydration is even more important when we are sick/ill. As necessary, point out that hydration reduces irritation from coughing and sneezing, and that it helps our body produce mucus and maintain good blood circulation.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Prepare children to complete ex.C in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.114 by playing the model dialogue audio (track 2.35). Play each line of the dialogue. Pause to repeat it. Drill difficult sounds as a class.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. In pairs, children practise the dialogue they have listened to with answers that are true for them. They can refer back to the lists they made in the introductory activity. Give them enough time to practise speaking both roles. Monitor to offer extra support as necessary.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Read out the Learn box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.115 ex.D. Focus attention to ‘dead’ and ‘live’. Explain that although the words have opposite meanings, they are being contrasted in the example sentence by the inclusion of the negative term ‘can’t’.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Invite children to read out the target antonyms. Play the audio (track 2.36) and ask children to do ex.D alone. Review answers as a class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.103.
  2. Divide the class into two teams. Tell children to write down more pairs of antonyms they already know. Then ask each team to say one of their words, giving the other team a chance to confer and respond with the correct antonym, if they know it.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Support less able children to personalize the lines of dialogue in a small group.
  2. Challenge more confident children to write three more sentences that feature pairs of antonyms in relation to sickness and health.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have practised speaking about staying healthy using a model dialogue. They have also learned about antonyms.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
