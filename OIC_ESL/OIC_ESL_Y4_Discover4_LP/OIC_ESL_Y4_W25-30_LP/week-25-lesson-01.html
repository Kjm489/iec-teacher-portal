TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Introduce the new unit. Ask: What do you think you will learn about in this unit? In pairs or small groups, let children discuss their predictions. Next, explain to them that they are going to learn about the parts and systems that make our body work.
  2. Ask children to stand up and shake their limbs. Then sing ‘Head, Shoulders, Knees, and Toes’ as a class (you can find this online if you are unfamiliar with this popular song). Guide them by indicating to the relevant body parts during the song.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Elicit further body parts or systems which children may know. If they need more support, ask them how the body stays well and fights disease, or use flashcards. Model the pronunciation of common terms children suggest. Drill, focusing on difficult sounds.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Play the video in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.107, or ask children to look at the picture across pp.106–107.
  2. Encourage children to discuss their original predictions and what they saw in the video and/or picture in pairs. Now ask them to write three questions on the topic that they hope to be answered in the unit. Invite volunteers to share these with the class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Pre-teach/Elicit the lesson vocabulary using the flashcards or pictures. Write the words on the board and slowly sound them out, asking children to listen and repeat. Drill a few times. Before children use the vocabulary in context in the next steps, encourage them to write down new words so they can refer to them later in the unit.
  2. Play the audio in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.108 ex.A (track 2.30). Pause after each sentence and read aloud. Invite more confident children to take turns reading aloud.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. In pairs or small groups, ask children to look at the sentences they have just read out, and to try to work out the meaning of the new vocabulary from the context given. Review answers as a class, pausing to model difficult pronunciations like ‘microscope’.
  2. Ask children to complete Oxford Discover SB 4 p.108 exs.B–C in pairs.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Answer the questions in the Big Question section in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.97. Then complete exs.A–B in WB 4 pp.97–98.
  2. Draw a picture of a human body and label familiar body parts, adding any new vocabulary.

Oxford differentiation

  1. When introducing the main activity, support less able children to name the body parts they may already know by indicating to your own eyes, ears, hair, feet.
  2. Leave out selected letters when first writing new vocabulary on the board and challenge more able children to fill in the gaps. Stretch them further by asking if they know what jobs the different body systems do? (e.g. The immune system fights disease.)

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have been introduced to the new unit, learning topic vocabulary through listening and speaking exercises. They have understood the meaning of key words in context.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 How bodies work
Week 25, Lesson 1: How do our bodies really work?
Learning outcomes: 4.1a, 4.1e, 4.2c

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘How bodies work’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by singing a song, discussing the human body, predicting the content of a video clip, and practising the pronunciation of key words.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
