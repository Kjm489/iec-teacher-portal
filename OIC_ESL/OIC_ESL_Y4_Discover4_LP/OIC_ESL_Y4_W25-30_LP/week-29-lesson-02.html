PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write the lesson’s vocabulary on the board. Invite a confident volunteer to the front and ask them to indicate to the correct words as you read them out. Model the pronunciation of any difficult sounds. Then practise repeating.
  2. Next, present the target vocabulary in sentences. Encourage volunteers to guess the definition of the words after hearing them in context.
  3. In pairs, ask children to categorize the words as nouns, verbs, or both. Alternatively, sort them into people, actions, and things. Check answers as a class and write them on the board. Use the materials in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.136 ex.A to support teaching.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Ask children to complete ex.B in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.136 independently. Remind them of the categorizations from the introductory activity. Check answers as a class. Then ask children to discuss the questions in ex.C in pairs. Discuss as a class.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask questions to personalize the vocabulary presented: Who is the editor of your writing? What makes a good headline? What do you do when you make a mistake? What mistakes might a reporter make?
  2. Teach children about bravery. Tell them about something you used to be afraid of, and how you overcame that fear. Invite children to share what things make them feel afraid.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Explain that bravery is often the theme of many stories, whether they are works of fiction, or hard news stories.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Introduce the concepts of ‘conflict’ and ‘resolution’ in a story.
  2. Read the Learn box in Oxford Discover CB 4 p.137 ex.D. Then do ex.D as a class. Draw the table on the board. Guide children through the short story, with a different child reading each line. Mime actions from the story to aid understanding of conflict, possible resolutions, and a final resolution. Fill in the table together. Refer to the Reading Preview bar on p.137 together as a class. Check children’s understanding of the type of text being previewed. Next, ask children to scan the story in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.138–139 to find the words in ex.E on p.137. Encourage them to guess the meanings as they read. Check answers as a class and tell children they will be studying the text in greater detail in the next lesson.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Add more words learned in the unit to the correct categories (see introductory activity).
  2. Complete Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.124–125 exs.A–D.

Oxford differentiation

  1. To help children make their final choices in ex.B, write sentences that offer a clue to each answer on the board.
  2. Challenge fast finishers to write their own sentences using at least two of the words from the target vocabulary.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned new mass media vocabulary in a short story. They have considered the concept of bravery.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Mass media
Week 29, Lesson 2: Working as a news reporter
Learning outcomes: 4.2c, 4.3b, 4.3e

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Mass media’.
In this lesson, children will improve their speaking and reading skills by studying the meanings of new words related to mass media. They will deploy a reading strategy to help them understand a short story.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
