PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Hand out a set of game cards to each group of children. They are going to be reporters in a game about where news comes from. Each card has a different news story topic.
  2. Instruct groups to turn over a card and discuss their ideas about where to get the relevant information for their news story. They can choose from five different sources (write these on the board). Some news story topics may have more than one correct answer. Remind children that making mistakes during the game is only natural because some of the words may be new ones.
  3. Groups take turns saying which news sources and news stories match together. Offer praise for their effort in coming up with logical reasons for their decisions. Tell children that you are the editor, and that you are far too busy to help them. However, each group has your permission to ask you for one clue during the game. Award points for correct answers.
  4. After the game (or as a tie-breaker), ask children to make a list of newsgathering methods, such as writing emails, making phone calls, going to meetings, and interviewing local people on the street. Remind them that they are in the stretch zone – the activity is designed to challenge them and to extend their understanding of mass media. Write all sensible ideas on the board.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Return to the story in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.138–139 from Week 29, Lesson 2. Focus children’s attention on the blog entries that make up the story, indicated by the dates preceding each paragraph. Then look at the title and pictures. Ask them to predict (or recall) in pairs what is going to happen based on this information. Check answers as a class, reminding children to think about the conflict and resolution reading strategy.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Ask children to follow the story as you play the audio (track 3.10). Pause at each Think box for pairs to discuss conflict and resolution at each stage.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Work together to complete ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.140. Draw the table on the board and elicit ideas. Use the opportunity to explain that mass media can influence the public and politicians. Then give children time to complete exs.B–C independently, reminding them to look for details in the text to find the answers. Review answers as a class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete Oxford Discover WB 4 p.126 exs.A–B and p.127 exs.A–D.

Oxford differentiation

  1. You may add visual clues to the game cards to aid children’s understanding of new words.
  2. Support less confident children by drawing a timeline of events in the text with their input.
  3. Challenge fast finishers to imagine the scenario described in the blog, and to predict what the writer’s blog posts might say one week, one month, and one year later.
  4. Additional information: Week 29, Lesson 3
  5. Where does the news come from?
  6. Sources of information: Write these on the board, adding visual clues as necessary.
  7. Game cards: Give one set to each team, adding visual clues if necessary.
  8. Answers

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned about news sources in a group speaking game. They have identified conflict and resolution in a blog.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
