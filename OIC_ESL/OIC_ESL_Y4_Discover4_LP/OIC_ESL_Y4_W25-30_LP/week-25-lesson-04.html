TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Draw a monster on the board. Show its hands, feet, hair, and teeth, and other body parts covered in the unit like its ears, eyes, head(s), and nose. Use a variety of shapes, sizes, and colours. Ask children to label the picture by naming the features you choose.
  2. Elicit simple adjectives from children to describe the labelled body parts. Make a list on the board. Ask questions to focus attention to any details of your monster that children miss.
  3. Finally, draw a two-column table on the board – one for facts, and one for opinions. Ask children to discuss in small groups what the difference is between facts and opinions. Challenge them to decide which adjectives belong in which column, and to write three sentences about the monster using at least two adjectives. Review answers as a class. Teach children that facts can be proven, whereas opinions cannot.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children that they are going to sing a song and study its lyrics to learn how to put multiple adjectives in the correct order.
  2. Complete ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.113 as a class. Briefly review the symptoms of a common cold from earlier in the unit. Ask children to say which ones they remember. Write their answers on the board. Listen to the song together once, using the audio (track 2.32). Then sing it together as a class. Check that children understand what the character wants to do, and why they cannot do it. Ask children to do ex.E independently and to first check answers in pairs, then as a class.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Find a suitable chant/song online that covers the order of adjectives, or create your own. Ask children to clap every time they hear an adjective. Present the chant once before performing it together as a class.
  2. Ask children to take a few deep breaths before they continue with their work.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Read the Learn Grammar box in ex.F in Oxford Discover SB 4.
  2. Children can do ex.F individually. Check answers as a class.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Focus attention to the first line of the song or chant. Rewrite it on the board with the adjectives in the incorrect order. Ask children to explain why it is wrong.
  2. Ask children to complete ex.G in Oxford Discover SB 4 by writing their lists of objects individually, before describing their chosen objects to a partner, who should practise active listening and take notes. Encourage children to describe their partner’s objects to the class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.101.
  2. Draw a monster and label the body parts with nouns and adjectives. Swap with a partner, who has to write three sentences about the monster using a minimum of two adjectives.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Ask more confident children to correct mistakes in the sentences from the introductory activity. Challenge them to think of antonyms for their chosen adjectives.
  2. Support less able children by helping them to identify what each adjective refers to.
