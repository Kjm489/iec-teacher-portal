TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask the class: What is the heart? Elicit answers: e.g. The heart is a body part. / The heart pumps blood. Model joining the sentences by using ‘that’ and deleting the repeated noun: e.g. The heart is a body part that pumps blood. (See Oxford Discover SB 4 p.122 ex.C.)
  2. Next, ask children to use ‘where’ to join two sentences: e.g. The heart is where blood goes.
  3. Put children into pairs to ask and answer questions about other body parts they know. To support, you could first make a chart as a class with names of body parts and information about them. Challenge children to consider which body systems the parts belong to.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Read aloud the title and first paragraph of the reading text in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.118–119.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Using this information only, ask children to predict what they are going to learn about in pairs. Check answers as a class and write sensible ideas on the board.
  2. Play the audio (track 2.38) once in full and encourage children to read along. Then ask them to read it again in small groups, taking turns to read sections of the text aloud. Tell them to look out for sequence words and words taught in the previous lesson.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Ask children to complete the Think questions on p.119 and then exs.A–B on p.120.
  2. Recap children’s predictions and correct as a class in light of the content of the text.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Give two balloons to each child. First, they are going to measure how powerful their lungs are at rest. Instruct them to blow once into a balloon and tie it up. Then ask them to place two fingers on their wrists and to feel the blood pumping through their arteries. Tell them to count their pulse for 15 seconds, then calculate their resting heartbeat per minute. Next, instruct them to do ten star jumps, before blowing once into the other balloon. Tell them to measure their pulse again. Observe and discuss together how the balloons were generally smaller after exercise, yet their pulses climbed.
  2. Use the questions in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.120 ex.B to spark a whole-class discussion. Make comparisons between human body systems and other systems in nature (e.g. river systems or the design of plants). Explain how good habits help (eco)systems in nature, like our bodies, to stay healthy. By respecting the natural environment, we are also looking after the planet and ourselves.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the listening task about bones in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.122 exs.A–B.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Challenge more confident children to do the reading comprehension tasks alone. Fast finishers could write one extra step for each process described in ex.A in SB 4 p.120.
  2. Support less able children by jointly reviewing the text for answers.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have talked about the human body using that or where. They have read a text about body systems, before exploring the effects of exercise on their own bodies.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
