PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Elicit the meaning of a ‘phrasal verb’ (i.e. a verb combined with an adverb or a preposition). In today’s lesson, children are going to learn some phrasal verbs with ‘turn’. You can revisit the use of ‘turn away’ in the story from Week 32, Lesson 2 (see Oxford Discover SB 4 p.159 – ‘… they turned us away at the supermarket’ = the shop stopped letting people in).
  2. Write some more examples on the board, such as ‘turn off’, ‘turn around’, and ‘turn into’. Say them for the class to repeat. Then ask children to stand up. Work together to create actions to represent each one, such as pretending to turn off a light or spinning on the spot.
  3. Add all the phrasal verbs from the vocabulary bubble on the board. Write a few sentences to place them in context, then encourage children to try to work out their meanings. You could include multiple-choice options and challenge children to choose the correct one.
  4. e.g. The rescue worker turned up at the storm shelter. (arrived)
  5. You can use the sentences in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.163 ex.D.

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. We haven’t learned about the present perfect with adverbs yet.
  2. Draw a timeline to situate already in the past, just before the present, and yet in the future, reinforcing that already and just indicate completed actions. Support children to fill in the gaps with the correct adverbs by referring to the timeline.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. You can refer to the dialogue in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.161 ex.D. Play the audio (track 3.25) once for children to listen, then invite two volunteers to read it aloud. Play the audio again, pausing for children to echo-read. Model the correct pronunciation and intonation. Next, do exs.E–F in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.161 as a class. Check that children understand that already indicates an action that finished first, and just indicates an action that finished last.
  2. Put children into pairs to think of life events they can sequence using already, just, and yet.
  3. Bring the class back together and invite pairs to the board to write their ideas on a timeline. Elicit the full sentence and encourage the class to identify and correct any errors.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete ex.G in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.161; and exs.A–E in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.147–148 and exs.A–B on p.149.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Encourage more able children to support their less confident peers with understanding where the three adverbs are positioned on a timeline.
  2. Fast finishers can relate their learning to the unit by writing sentences with already, just, and yet about extreme weather.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned phrasal verbs with ‘turn’. They have deepened their understanding of the present perfect tense.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
