TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Use open-ended questions to ask the class what naturally beautiful places there are in their local area, and what visitors can do there. Invite volunteers to share their ideas.
  2. Explain that the questions you just asked were ‘open-ended’. This means they can have many possible answers, and can’t be answered with just ‘Yes’ or ‘No’, unlike ‘closed’ ones.
  3. Model a few open-ended and closed questions that use target language. Next, give children time to form their own open-ended questions on a topic of choice. Elicit suitable ones with ‘How’, ‘What’, ‘Who’, and ‘Why’ openers. Write the best ones on the board.
  4. Use the Learn box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.177 to support teaching.
  5. Invite a confident child to read aloud the text and Oxford Discover example in ex.D while the rest of the class reads along. To check that children have grasped the concept, ask how they know the example question is open-ended.
  6. Challenge children to write one or two more open-ended questions about details in the paragraph that they would like to know more about. Review as a class.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children to read the preview in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.177. Ask them what type of text they are going to read (realistic fiction), and to predict what the story will be about by cross-referencing the illustrations with the vocabulary in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.176. Discuss predictions as a class.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Elicit that the story is set in an aquatic biome (coral reefs in the ocean).
  2. You may play the audio (track 3.38) while children read along on pp.178–179. Pause to discuss the meaning of any challenging vocabulary like ‘overfishing’ and ‘coral reef’.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Put children into small groups to re-read the text, taking turns to read a section each. Remind them to pause often to discuss what they have read so far, and to help each other to understand its gist and any key words. Then tell them to note down at least two open-ended questions on the topic. You may refer to the ‘Think bubbles’.
  2. Bring the class back together to review children’s predictions and share their questions.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Discuss more ecological causes that a petition might be able to help.
  2. Set exs.A–C in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.180 as independent work. Allow children to check their answers with a partner first before feeding back to the whole class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete Oxford Discover WB 4 p.164 exs.A–B and p.165 exs.A–D.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Support less able children by reading the text together in an adult-led small group.
  2. Encourage more confident children to read the text independently from the outset, noting down anything they don’t understand. They can then work through these issues in pairs.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned how to ask open-ended questions to elicit as much information as possible. They have read about a timely ecological issue.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

IEC supplemental game — 1. Earth protection action hunt

Purpose: Apply the learning focus: Protecting the Earth.

Materials: Textbook page or lesson visuals; word/picture cards; board and learner notebooks.

How to play: Show six picture, word or short-text clues drawn from the original lesson. Learners identify the target idea, then explain the evidence for their answer.

ICQ: What do you do first in this game? Do you work individually, with a partner or in a team?
