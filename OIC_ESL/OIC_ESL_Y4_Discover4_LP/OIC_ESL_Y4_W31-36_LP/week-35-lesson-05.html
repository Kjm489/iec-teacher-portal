Original Oxford lesson: Which biome would you like to live in?

Original learning outcomes: 4.1a, 4.1i, 4.2e, 4.4d

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children that in today’s lesson they are going to get creative by planning and writing their own fictional story! They will try to incorporate what they have learned about biomes in the ‘Ecosystems’ unit.
  2. Look at the fictional story in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.184. Read it together and discuss the labelled parts: Title, Setting, Dialogue (between interesting characters), Conflict, Solution, and Ending. Elicit the purpose of each. Then discuss the questions in ex.B below it.
  3. Give children 1 minute to search for the answers in the model text in pairs. Review as a class.
  4. After studying the input text, take a few deep breaths together as a class. Then give children a few minutes to sit in silence and reflect on the task ahead.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. As a class, come up with some simple success criteria for writing a fictional story, based on the features explored in the introductory activity. You can refer to the tips in the Learn box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.184.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Children can work independently, in pairs, or in small groups to plan and write their story. First, ask them to write down the headings ‘Title’, ‘Setting’, ‘Dialogue’, ‘Conflict’, ‘Solution’, and ‘Ending’ in their books or on a large piece of paper. Give them time to note down their story ideas next to each one. Remind them that they can be as creative as possible with their ideas to make their story fun and exciting for the reader!
  2. Advise children to choose a title right at the end, once they know the main events.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. As they work, remind them of the importance of a conflict and a resolution.
  2. Before children begin writing, you may want to elicit how dialogue is formatted. Look again at the input text.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Children should spend the rest of the lesson writing their story. Writing is frequently sidelined as a core skill. Therefore, urge them to be patient, and explain that challenge and struggle can help their brains to grow.
  2. Allow enough time at the end of the lesson for children to read each other’s work so far in pairs; check that their partner has met the agreed success criteria; and check for correct spelling, grammar, and punctuation. They may complete and/or add further detail to their story in the following ‘Reflection and review’ week.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Read another fictional story in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.170.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Encourage less confident children to write about a biome they know well. Consider putting them into mixed-ability pairs or small groups so they can better organize their ideas.
  2. Challenge more able children by reminding them of the open-ended questions strategy. Can they write their stories with hints to certain details without explicitly stating them?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have read and understood the organization and content of a fictional story set in a biome, then planned and written their own fictional story.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
