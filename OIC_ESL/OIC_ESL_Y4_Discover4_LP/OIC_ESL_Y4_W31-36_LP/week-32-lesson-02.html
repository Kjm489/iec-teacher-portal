TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Put children into pairs to talk about if they have ever been in an emergency, and what it was like. Prompt them to say how they felt, and what the mood or atmosphere was like.
  2. Invite volunteers to share their partner’s response with the class. Then work together to make a mind map with ‘How we feel in an emergency’ written in the centre. Add feelings and emotions words that children suggest.
  3. Reassure children that feeling worried or nervous in an emergency is normal because we want to stay safe and avoid danger.
  4. Widen the discussion by explaining the ‘fight, flight, freeze’ stress response that people often experience in these kinds of situation. Ask children if they have ever experienced this response. Follow up by asking: In an emergency, would you stay to fight, run away, or hide in a safe place?
  5. Finally, talk with the class about why they should follow directions in an emergency.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Turn to the story in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.158–159.
  2. Read the title aloud, then ask children to quietly read the preview in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.157. Elicit the genre (historical fiction) and details of its format (a made-up story, with fictional characters, based on a real-life event from the past in a real-life setting).

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Recall what it means to make an inference, which children looked at briefly last lesson (see the final bullet in the main activity in Week 32, Lesson 1). Put them into pairs to read the story together and to make inferences when prompted by the Think bubbles.
  2. Before reading, ask pairs to look again at the title and pictures, and to write a prediction about what will happen using vocabulary learned last lesson.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. You can choose to play the audio (track 3.24) for children to listen and follow, or ask them to read independently. If you go with the former, pause the audio at each ‘Think bubble’ for pairs to discuss and make inferences using clues in the text.
  2. After reading, review children’s initial predictions as a class.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Encourage pairs to infer how the characters felt before and during the hurricane, and to discuss if they would feel the same or different.
  2. Set exs.A–B in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.160 as independent work. Review answers as a class.
  3. Do ex.C in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.160 together. Invite volunteers to read out the sentences. Challenge children to use context and the pictures to help them work out the meaning of the words.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–B on p.145 and exs.A–D on p.146, all in Oxford Discover WB 4.
  2. Ask children to predict what happens next in the story using may, might, and could to discuss possibilities (see Week 31, Lesson 4).

Oxford differentiation

  1. To support less able children, read and discuss the text in an adult-led small group.
  2. Encourage fast finishers to think about how ‘staying calm before the storm’ can also be used as a metaphor. What could it mean?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have discussed emergency responses. They have read a story about preparing for an emergency and used clues from the text to make inferences.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Extreme weather
Week 32, Lesson 2: Staying calm before the storm
Learning outcomes: 4.2e, 4.3b, 4.3h, 4.3j

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Extreme weather’.
In this lesson, children will practise their speaking skills in a class discussion about our stress response, and how we respond to emergencies. They will build on their learning from Week 32, Lesson 1 by making inferences about a story.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
