PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Play the matching game provided. Put children into pairs to match the problems to the solutions. Review answers as a class.
  2. Next, follow the instructions provided to play a drama game. You can use sound effects (e.g. of warning sirens, a tornado, and an earthquake) to create atmosphere if you wish.
  3. Tell the class they are going to experience four events that demonstrate nature’s power – an earthquake, a tornado, a flood, and a tsunami. They just reviewed these in the matching activity.
  4. Urge children to pay attention to clues, including audio clues, about the event that is happening. You can display the relevant flashcard or picture on the board as a second clue, and after a short time write the name of the event on the board, too. Set a time limit and ask children to move to the best location for staying safe in the unfolding disaster.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Briefly role-play two tornado alerts at your school with volunteers. In the first, children struggle to follow safety directions. In the second, they stay calm and follow safety directions. Afterwards, support the class to describe the differences between the two scenarios. Teach the difference between staying calm and panicking. Elicit from the class the importance of being prepared to respond to the dangers of nature’s power.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Tell children that they are going to listen to an expert talk about a natural disaster (see Oxford Discover SB 4 p.154 ex.A). Play the audio (track 3.19) once. Elicit the purpose of the talk (to offer safety advice), and the speaker’s expertise (earthquakes). Play the audio again and ask children to match the problems to the solutions in ex.A. Give them time to check their answers in pairs, then challenge them to do ex.B without listening again. Replay the audio so children can check their work. Review answers as a class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Introduce talking about possibilities with might, may, and could (see Oxford Discover SB 4 p.154 ex.C): e.g. There might be an earthquake next week. / They may buy a house on higher ground. / We could stay inside until the rain stops. / There could be a warning.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Read aloud the examples. Encourage children to listen carefully and to echo your intonation, with the emphasis placed on the modal verb. Then demonstrate a short dialogue with another adult or confident child, using the question form ‘What will … be like?’ and answering with a modal verb. Allow children to practise this in pairs.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Choose an extreme weather event and make a safety poster for children at school.

Oxford differentiation

  1. To support less able children in the listening activity, pause the audio after each problem is outlined. Provide a list of key words to listen for. Explain their meaning first.
  2. Encourage more confident pairs to practise using modal verbs in a dialogue about extreme weather: e.g. Q: What will the town be like after the flood? A: There might be a lot of damage.
  3. Additional information: Week 31, Lesson 4
  4. Match the problems to the solutions.
  5. [Answers: 1D, 2E, 3F, 4A, 5C, 6B]
  6. Drama game instructions
  7. Make signs labelled ‘rescue boat’ and ‘higher ground’. Designate two areas of the classroom (or its environs) as the ‘rescue boat’ and the ‘higher ground’, and display the signs accordingly. During the game, children should move to the ‘rescue boat’ location to avoid the flood, and the ‘higher ground’ location to avoid the tsunami. As an alternative to designating an area as the ‘higher ground’, children could stand on their chairs to avoid the tsunami (if it is safe to do so). To avoid the tornado, children should move to the centre of the classroom. To avoid the earthquake, they should crouch down beneath desks or tables. Reveal the correct answers after each round, and eliminate children who go to the wrong locations.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have listened for problems and solutions. They have talked about possibilities with the modal verbs might, may, and could.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
