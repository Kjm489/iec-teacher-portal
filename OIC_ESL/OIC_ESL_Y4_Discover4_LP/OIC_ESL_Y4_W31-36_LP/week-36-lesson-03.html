PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Ecosystems’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
  2. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.
  3. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to at the end of the school year to track their progress and achievements. Explain how neurons in the brain form connections when we learn new things. Reviewing what we have learned will make these connections stronger and help us to retain information.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas:
  2. Children can continue to write their fictional story set in a biome from Week 35, Lesson 5, adding further detail and making revisions based on any feedback received. To extend, they may illustrate their final draft, and use online and print resources to check and find facts about their chosen biome. Remind them that a fictional story has a description of a setting; interesting characters who use natural dialogue; and an engaging plot with a conflict, solution, and ending. Encourage them to include examples of both the present perfect and simple past tenses, and to use complex sentences with until, since, and because. At the end of the week, children can swap their stories in a small-group reading session, or read their story aloud to the class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. In pairs, children make a poster comparing two biomes. Explain that one of the biomes should be a familiar one – for instance, if they live close to a temperate forest or a desert, or enjoy visiting freshwater biomes in their local area (i.e. rivers, streams, ponds, lakes, and wetlands) – and the other one should be a less familiar one. They could compare the location of these biomes around the world, weather and climate, the wildlife residing in each, and any plants and/or natural features that you can find in each. To extend, encourage children to research any threats these biomes face and how conservationists are trying to protect them. The poster can be a mix of text, illustrations, and labelled diagrams. At the end of the week, invite pairs to present their poster to the rest of the class, who may comment and ask questions.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. In small groups, children create and act out a short play about a family who move to a new biome (see also Oxford Discover SB 4 p.185). Encourage them to think about the different roles, such as director(s), set designer(s), fact checker(s), and lead scriptwriter(s), and to assign them fairly between themselves. However, all group members should contribute to the scriptwriting process, based on biome facts learned in the unit, and act out a role in the play. When scriptwriting, remind children that a fictional story has a description (or visual) of a setting; interesting characters who use natural dialogue; and an engaging plot with a conflict, solution, and ending. Give groups time to rehearse their short plays, focusing on clear speech, tone of voice, and key words and expressions. Invite groups to perform for the rest of the class at the end of the week.
  2. For all activities, ensure that children plan effectively and listen attentively to each other’s ideas to achieve a shared goal. They might consider assigning a leader for the planning and creative stages, who will delegate tasks in recognition of classmates’ strengths and interests.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Mix up abilities in pairwork and group work so peer-support can be provided. Monitor children actively to ensure that everybody is able to participate in line with their strengths and interests. Write key topic vocabulary from the unit on the board to remind children.
  2. Challenge more able children to think about how they will present their work effectively at the end of the week. What language can they use (including new grammatical forms learned)? What do they need to do to maintain the audience’s interest?
  3. In the drama activity, challenge more confident children to learn their speaking roles without a script, or to take the role of a narrator.
  4. Less confident children may use prompts or a full script.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have identified the core English language skills they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has developed and reinforced these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
