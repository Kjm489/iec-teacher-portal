TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Say: I’ve taught [English] since [2010 / I left university]. Then gesture to children and say: You’ve learned about biomes for [four days]. Then write these sentences on the board. Elicit what they tell us about time – the first sentence gives a specific moment in time in the past, while the second sentence gives an amount of time.
  2. Use these examples to teach the class how to use for and since with the present perfect. These constructions describe states or actions that began in the past and are still happening now.
  3. See also the Learn Grammar box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.173 to support teaching. Play the audio in ex.D (track 3.32) so children can read along with the cartoon to boost pronunciation and fluency. Then do ex.F together. Read out each sentence, first using for and then using since.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Play a grammar speaking game.
  2. Ask children to write two true present perfect sentences with for and since.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Divide the class into small groups.
  2. Explain the rules. In each group, one child should say which states or actions their sentences refer to. The rest of the group must guess the duration (for) or starting time (since) by saying sentences: e.g. I think you’ve had your tablet for ten days. / I think you’ve studied English since your first day at school. When they have made their guesses, the child shares their answers. Continue to play so everyone can reveal their answers.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Returning to the biomes topic, ask the class what they know about food chains – that is, a sequence we can follow to see how energy moves between plants and animals in nature.
  2. Do the listening activity in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.174 ex.A.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Play the audio (track 3.33) once for the class to listen to the sequences. Then play it again so children can complete the task and check their answers. Review as a class.
  2. Move on to ex.B. Tell children to read through the questions before playing the audio. Challenge them to listen and answer the questions independently. Review as a class.
  3. Ask children to discuss in pairs where humans fit in the Earth’s food chain, and how it may change without us in it. Share ideas as a whole class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–E in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.158–159.
  2. In pairs or small groups, challenge children to write down food chains for other biomes, such as the tundra (the Arctic) or the tropical rainforest. Encourage them to think about how a biome may change if a certain species were taken out of its food chain.

Oxford differentiation

  1. To support less able children during the listening activity, pause after each link in the food chain is mentioned. Ask comprehension questions to check their understanding.
  2. Increase the challenge for more confident children during ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.174. Ask them to note down the food chains after the first listen, then replay the audio to check their answers.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned how and when to use the present perfect tense with for and since. They have developed their understanding of food chains, and thought critically about the importance of each part of a food chain in various biomes.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
