PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write the lesson’s vocabulary on the board. Invite children to indicate to the words as you say them. Drill together.
  2. Then write some sentences to place the target language in context: e.g. It is important to stay calm in an emergency. Read out each one, with children echoing your pronunciation and intonation. Focus on any difficult sounds. Invite a few volunteers to demonstrate.
  3. Turn to the sentences in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.156 ex.A (track 3.23) and ask children to listen and follow. Encourage them to complete exs.B–C independently.
  4. Next, invite children to think of questions that correspond to the sentences: e.g. What is it important to do in an emergency? Work together as a class on the first couple of sentences, then ask children to continue making questions in pairs. Review as a class.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Divide the class into small groups. Assign a few of the target words to each group. They are going to study them more closely by working together to create ‘squares of meaning’.
  2. Instruct children to draw squares (big enough to write in) and to write a different word (e.g. ‘supplies’) at the centre of each. In the four corners of each square, they must work together in their groups to write the following:

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Definition – e.g. The essential equipment and food that people keep for an emergency.
  2. Example – e.g. tinned food (if not applicable, draw a picture of the vocabulary item)
  3. Sentence – e.g. We need to buy more supplies before the rain starts.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Part of speech – e.g. noun
  2. Ask groups to share their completed squares. If some groups had the same word(s), compare the squares, discuss the similarities and differences, and decide if all are correct.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Explain that groups are now going to prepare a survival kit of three suitable items from the new words learned. Assign an extreme weather event to each group. Give children time to discuss the contents of their kits, and to decide which items would help them the most. Share ideas as a class, and compare the survival kits of any groups who prepared for the same emergency. Elicit why children made certain decisions.
  2. Praise children’s ability to think clearly and rationally about their needs in an emergency situation. There isn’t a wrong answer, as long as they can give sensible reasons for their decisions!
  3. Do some preparation for next lesson. Explain the meaning of ‘inference’ – when a reader can ‘read between the lines’ and understand something that a text doesn’t explicitly say. The reader uses clues from the text and their own knowledge to make an informed guess. See Oxford Discover SB 4 p.157 ex.D to reinforce children’s understanding.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete ex.E in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.157 and exs.A–D in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.143–144.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Help less confident children to engage with the new vocabulary by asking questions that explore their existing knowledge and that relate the words to a familiar context.
  2. Challenge more able children to write a few sentences about an emergency without naming its cause. Their partner tries to infer the extreme weather event being described.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned vocabulary for talking about emergency situations and survival. They have begun to make inferences when reading.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
