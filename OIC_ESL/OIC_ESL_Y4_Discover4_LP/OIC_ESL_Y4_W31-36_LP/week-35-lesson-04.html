PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children to imagine that they are going on a school trip to the desert. They will need to think carefully about what they should take with them. Say: Remember that visiting a biome without being prepared could be dangerous!
  2. Draw a mind map on the board with ‘DESERT’ in the middle. Add items that children want to bring. Discuss whether their suggestions are sensible or not, and encourage those who have previously visited a desert (if applicable) to share what they took with them. Remind the class that sharing knowledge and experiences in this way helps us to learn.
  3. Repeat with another biome, such as the tropical rainforest (humid conditions) or the tundra (frozen conditions).
  4. Model using need with verbs and nouns to ask and answer questions about a visit to the biome(s) discussed. See also Oxford Discover SB 4 p.182 ex.C to support teaching. Ensure that children understand when need should be followed by to or for.
  5. What will I need to prepare? (Q: need + to + verb)
  6. You’ll need to take plenty of water to the desert. (A: need + to + verb)
  7. What will the explorers need for their hike across the tundra? (Q: need + for + noun)
  8. They’ll need walking boots and really thick socks! (A: need + noun)
  9. Either play the audio in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.182 ex.C (track 3.42), or read aloud the examples on the board with another adult in class or a confident child. Encourage the class to pay attention to and echo the pronunciation and intonation accurately.
  10. In pairs, children practise the dialogue, taking turns to ask and answer the questions.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Turn to the listening activity in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.182.
  2. Play the audio (track 3.40) once for children to listen for gist.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Replay the audio for children to complete ex.A. Check answers together, then invite volunteers to comment on what challenges the speakers might face when visiting the biomes they mention. They can use might, may, and could to talk about possibilities.
  2. Replay the audio for children to tackle ex.B independently. Give them time to check their answers in pairs first before reviewing as a class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Explain to the class that when giving a reason, we can create more interesting sentences using since or because. See Oxford Discover SB 4 p.183 ex.E or write some example sentences on the board. In this context, the words can be used interchangeably.
  2. Since/Because rhinos are in danger of extinction [reason], we must try to protect them.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. I want to visit the Arctic since/because polar bears are my favourite animal [reason].
  2. Ask children to write five sentences using since or because at the beginning or in the middle to connect two actions. Encourage peer-correction in pairs.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.169.

Oxford differentiation

  1. In the introductory activity, allow less confident children to practise using the model dialogue, focusing on their intonation and correct inclusion of to or for.
  2. more confident children should be encouraged to adapt and change their dialogues.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned how to talk about needs, identify reasons, and form complex sentences with since and because.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 4 Ecosystems
Week 35, Lesson 4: Which biome would you like to live in?
Learning outcomes: 4.1a, 4.1i, 4.2e, 4.4d

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Ecosystems’.
In this lesson, children will improve their listening skills by identifying speakers’ reasons for favouring a particular biome, and will develop their writing skills by learning how to explain their reasoning using since and because. They will also practise their speaking skills in a model dialogue about needs.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
