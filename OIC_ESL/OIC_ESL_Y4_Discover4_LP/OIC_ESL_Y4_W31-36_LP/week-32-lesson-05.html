PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children that in today’s lesson they are going to write a speech about how to stay safe in an emergency. Briefly recap what they have learned in the ‘Extreme weather’ unit, including topic vocabulary; the present perfect tense; discussing possibilities with may, might, and could; and adverbs of manner (if studied in Week 32, Lesson 4). Give children time to look through their notes to remind themselves of their learning.
  2. Look at the model how-to speech in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.164 ex.A.
  3. Next, ask questions to check understanding of the features and content of a speech:
  4. How can you grab the audience’s attention?
  5. Begin with a greeting, describe the emergency using powerful language (i.e. adjectives and adverbs), ask rhetorical questions, and address the audience directly with the ‘you’ form (e.g. ‘How can you prepare for this?’).
  6. How should you organize your ideas?
  7. Title
  8. Greeting and introduction
  9. Main body, organized into steps with order words (e.g. ‘First’, ‘Next’, ‘Finally’)
  10. Conclusion
  11. What should you include in the conclusion?
  12. Include a final thought that will leave the audience reflecting on your words, often with the help of a rhetorical question.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Give children most of the lesson to write their own how-to speeches. They can refer back to their notes from previous lessons to remind them of new vocabulary and grammar. Monitor actively as they work, providing support where required.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Remind children that they are in the ‘stretch zone’ – extended writing isn’t easy, but it will help to reinforce all their learning from the unit.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. At the end of the lesson, invite volunteers to perform their speeches to the class. (To give every child the opportunity to practise their public speaking, you can continue to do this in the following ‘Reflection and review’ week in Week 33 if you run out of time.)

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Before children’s performances, read aloud the how-to speech in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.151. Read it out deliberately badly – fail to greet your audience, fail to make eye contact with them, and speak too quietly without sufficient energy. Use this badly delivered speech as a discussion starter. Elicit from children what you did wrong and what you could have done better to deliver your speech in a more engaging way.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Before children perform their speeches, give them time to peer-correct each other’s work and to practise their delivery in front of a partner.

Oxford differentiation

  1. To support less able children in the writing task, work together to write an outline plan on the board which they can adapt for their own speech.
  2. Allow more confident children to use dictionaries and thesauruses to make strong and interesting language choices. If they finish before their classmates, challenge them to memorize their speech or to perform it using notecards.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have written and presented a how-to speech about staying safe in an emergency.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Extreme weather
Week 32, Lesson 5: How to stay safe in an emergency
Learning outcomes: 4.2c, 4.3h, 4.4b, 4.4e

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Extreme weather’.
In this lesson, children will strengthen their reading and writing skills by studying a model speech, and then applying their learning in their own how-to speech. They will also have the opportunity to present their speech to the class.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
