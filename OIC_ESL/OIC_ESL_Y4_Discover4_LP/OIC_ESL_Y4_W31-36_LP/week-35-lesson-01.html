TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Play a game in which children practise classifying and categorizing vocabulary in teams. See the ‘Additional information’ page for full game instructions.
  2. Monitor actively while children work together. Remind teams that they are in the stretch zone – they must think deeply to make connections between vocabulary items from recent units. If a team is struggling, you can offer clues by asking them to focus on where the items come from, what they are made of, and how old they are.
  3. When the time is up, invite groups to share their two categories. Encourage them to explain the reasoning behind their choices.
  4. Although other suggestions might be correct, confirm that the categories you are looking for are ‘Natural’ and ‘Man-made’. Ensure that children understand natural objects haven’t been made or changed by humans, while man-made objects are human creations.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Present and teach the lesson’s vocabulary. Play the audio (track 3.37) in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.176 ex.A. Ask children to indicate to the words as they listen. Replay the audio (or say the words again) and ask children to repeat. Model pronunciation and drill any difficult sounds with the class. You may use flashcards and/or realia to support understanding.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Help children to understand the meaning of the word ‘astonish’ by explaining what astonishes you in nature (e.g. the Fibonacci sequence). Then encourage them to discuss in pairs what in nature astonishes them.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Hold a class discussion about borders. Find out if children have ever experienced crossing a border. Consider whether borders are truly man-made or a hybrid (since they can be natural features like rivers and mountains, or lines drawn on maps by humans).

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Put children into pairs to sort the vocabulary into ‘Natural’ and ‘Man-made’ categories.
  2. Wrap up by previewing next lesson’s reading text (see Week 35, Lesson 2). Encourage children to scan the story for the target words.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–D in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.162–163.
  2. After categorizing the target words from the main activity, discuss where the animals fit in their aquatic food chains. Do they know which one surfaces to breathe? (sea turtle)

Oxford differentiation

  1. Help less able children to learn and understand the new vocabulary by focusing on the compound words. Encourage them to think about how the two words in each one can help them.
  2. Challenge more able children to write sentences that feature at least two of the target words.
  3. Additional information: Week 35, Lesson 1
  4. Classify and categorize!
  5. Instructions for children
  6. In this game, there are 18 cards with pictures on them. In your group, you have ten minutes to categorize the pictures into two sets of nine items, according to a particular rule. Your teacher knows what the rule is, but they aren’t going to tell you straightaway. In each set, the items must have something in common. You will already know most of the words. Share ideas for categories within your group and decide on the best one. Then give each category a name. If you want some help, you can ask your teacher for some clues.
  7. Teacher’s notes
  8. Answer: The two categories are ‘Natural’ and ‘Man-made’.
  9. Source pictures of the 18 items and make a game card for each one. Make enough sets for one per group. Most of the words suggested in the above table will be familiar to children, but you can adapt this list to suit your class as long as there is an equal amount of natural and man-made items for children to categorize. You may also adapt the game instructions – for instance, you could alter the time limit, the number of clues you will offer, and what information to include on the game cards (pictures, words, or both).

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned new vocabulary related to biomes. They have sorted natural and man-made items into two categories, understanding the difference between them.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Ecosystems
Week 35, Lesson 1: Natural and man-made
Learning outcomes: 4.1a, 4.1b, 4.2c, 4.3e

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Ecosystems’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by engaging with new biomes vocabulary, and by working together to categorize natural and 
man-made objects. They will preview a reading text by scanning it for unknown words.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
