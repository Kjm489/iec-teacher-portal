PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. If possible and appropriate, take the class outside. Encourage them to observe their surroundings and the weather. Ask: What season is it? (If relevant to your context, or talk about dry and wet seasons if there aren’t four distinct seasonal changes.) Focus attention to the temperature and the duration of sunlight in your location today. Ask children to observe any plants and/or animals they can see. Talk about whether there are leaves on some trees, and if any flowers or fruits are visible.
  2. Draw a mind map on the board. Write ‘SEASONS’ at the centre. Elicit children’s observations about the current season. Add to that section of the mind map first with children’s input.
  3. Challenge children to say what season it will be in three, six, and nine months’ time. Discuss more signs of the changing seasons. Add good answers to the mind map. Remind children about using words for discussing possibilities, like might, may, and could: e.g. It could snow here in six months because it will be winter.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children they are going to talk about changes through the seasons with a partner. Play the audio for ex.C in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.174 (track 3.35) while children read along. Replay it, pausing after each line for children to echo pronunciation and intonation. Highlight the use of sequencing words like ‘next’, ‘then’, and ‘finally’.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Model the dialogue with a confident child. Then give pairs time to practise and adapt it themselves, taking turns to speak both roles.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Introduce words with ‘–tch’ (e.g. ‘hatch’ – Many birds hatch their chicks in spring.) See also ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.175. Model the ‘–tch’ sound and note its similarity to the first syllable in ‘chair’. Explain that ‘tch’ never appears at the start of a word in English. Elicit as many ‘–tch’ words as children can think of (see also the lesson’s vocabulary). Drill them together.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Next, teach complex sentences with until to use in writing. You may use ex.E in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.175 to support. Start saying the alphabet. Stop suddenly and draw a picture of a sloth on the board. Then write: ‘I read the alphabet. & I drew a sloth.’ Delete the full stops and insert until between the sentences to make one complex sentence. Teach children that as soon as you stopped the first action (saying the alphabet), you began the second one (drawing a sloth).
  2. Tell children to write a paragraph about a visit to a biome of choice. Challenge them to include an example of the present perfect with for or since, and a complex sentence with until. Refer children to the example text in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.161 ex.B.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete Oxford Discover WB 4 p.160 exs.A–B and p.161 ex.A.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Support less able children during the speaking task by writing a gapped dialogue on the board. They can be guided during the final writing task by giving them specific verbs to use in the present perfect. For each verb, talk about whether it goes better with for or since.
  2. To extend more able children in the speaking task, encourage them to personalize their answers by describing an activity they do in each of the different seasons.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have practised a model dialogue about seasons. They have learned to write complex sentences with until, before consolidating their learning in a writing task.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
