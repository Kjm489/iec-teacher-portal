PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Extreme weather’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
  2. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.
  3. Children may keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements. Explain how neurons in the brain form connections when we learn new things. Reviewing what we have learned will make these connections stronger and help us to retain information.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:
  2. Children continue to perform their how-to speeches from Week 32, Lesson 5. Give them time to practise in pairs, and to think about audience engagement via tone of voice, eye contact, rhetorical questions, and attention-grabbing language.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. In groups, children make a poster about preparing for emergencies. They think about the forces of nature from the unit, and focus on one of them. Encourage them to think about a survival kit (see Week 32, Lesson 1), the steps they will need to follow to ensure their safety in their chosen scenario, and any other details particular to their local context (type of terrain, equipment, and the role of the emergency services – do they know any emergency telephone numbers)? The poster can be a mix of text, illustrations, and labelled diagrams. At the end of the week, invite groups to present their emergency plan to the class, who will provide constructive feedback. Are they sensible and realistic? Why / Why not? See also Oxford Discover SB 4 p.165.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Put children into pairs to interview each other about their (real or imaginary) experience of an extreme weather event. They can take on the role of a character or play themselves, depending on their chosen scenario. First, they will need to plan their questions and write their transcripts together. Remind them to use language and grammar they have learned in this unit, such as topic vocabulary; order words; phrasal verbs with ‘turn’; the present perfect tense with ever, never, already, just, and yet; and discussing possibilities with may, might, and could. Give children time to rehearse their interviews and to perform them for the class at the end of the week.
  2. For all activities, ensure children plan effectively and listen attentively to each other’s ideas to achieve a shared goal. They might consider assigning a leader for the planning and creative stages, who will delegate tasks in recognition of their classmates’ strengths and interests.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Mix up abilities in pairwork and group work so peer-support can be provided. Monitor children actively to ensure their participation is in line with their strengths and interests. Write key topic vocabulary from the unit on the board to remind children.
  2. Challenge more able children to think about effective presentation skills. What language can they use (including new grammatical forms learned)? How can they maintain the audience’s interest?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
