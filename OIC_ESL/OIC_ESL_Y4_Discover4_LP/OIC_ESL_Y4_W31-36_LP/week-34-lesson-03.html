PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Today, children are going to study a long informational text to learn more about biomes.
  2. Look at the pictures illustrating your chosen text. Ask: How are the biomes in the pictures similar and different? Put them into pairs to note down their ideas in a mind map or Venn diagram. If necessary, guide them with an example on the board.
  3. Bring the class back together to share and discuss ideas.
  4. Close the task by inviting children to classify and categorize some of the animals in the pictures. Draw a table on the board for them to complete. Elicit what they can remember about these processes, as well as the difference between them.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain that biomes can be understood as ‘natural communities’. Ask children what other communities they have studied, and if a city can be classified as a natural one.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Tell children to read the preview in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.169 independently. Check understanding by eliciting what type of text it is and its purpose.
  2. Play the audio (track 3.31) and encourage children to read along with the text in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.170–171. Pause at the ‘Think bubbles’ to discuss the answers to the questions.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Ask pairs of children to reread the text, alternating sections, and to write down one interesting fact about each biome. After re-reading, tell children to close their books. Invite volunteers to share one of their facts in a simple sentence without naming the biome. Challenge the class to listen carefully and name the correct biome.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Ask children to do ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.172 independently by referring back to the text. Next, challenge them to answer the questions in ex.B from memory, before returning to the text to check their answers with a partner. They can correct false sentences as they do this. Review answers as a class. Then do ex.C together.
  2. Encourage children to think critically about which biomes on Earth are the easiest and most difficult for humans to live in and why, and whether any biomes overlap. Continue by asking them to imagine what would happen in the various biomes if a key characteristic were to change – for instance, if it were to snow heavily in the desert.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete Oxford Discover WB 4 p.156 exs.A–B and p.157 exs.A–D.
  2. Extend the discussion by asking the class how we can better protect the Earth’s biomes and their unique characteristics. Explore how well biomes are protected in your location.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Help less able children to engage with the reading text by pairing them with more able children, who will be able to explain unfamiliar words and answer queries as they read. In doing so, this will help to consolidate all children’s understanding of biomes.
  2. Fast finishers can write their own ‘True/False’ sentences about the text for a partner.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have deepened their understanding of the Earth’s eight major biomes by reading and responding to comprehension questions on a long informational text.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Ecosystems
Week 34, Lesson 3: Natural communities of the Earth
Learning outcomes: 4.2b, 4.3e, 4.3f, 4.3h

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Ecosystems’.
In this lesson, children will enhance their speaking and reading skills by studying and discussing an informational text. They will recall their learning from Week 34, Lesson 2 about how to categorize and classify information.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
