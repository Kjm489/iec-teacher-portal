PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write the names of the eight major biomes on Earth on the board – ‘oceans’, ‘freshwater’, ‘tropical rainforest’, ‘temperate forest’, ‘taiga’, ‘tundra’, ‘grasslands’, and ‘desert’. Use flashcards or pictures of these vocabulary items to aid teaching. Slowly sound out the words for children to listen and repeat. Drill a few times. Encourage them to write these new words down for their future reference.
  2. Focus children’s attention on the compound nouns ‘freshwater’, ‘grassland’, and ‘rainforest’. See if they can work out their definitions by breaking the words into two parts.
  3. Play the audio in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.168 ex.A (track 3.30). Pause after each sentence to read it aloud with children. Invite those who are more confident to do this independently. Alternatively, write some sentences on the board that use the new vocabulary in context. For example:
  4. Leaves fall from trees in the temperate forest every autumn.
  5. There aren’t many plants and trees in the desert.
  6. Ask children to write down three more words that they associate with each target word. This will help them to remember the meanings.
  7. Ask children to do exs.B–C. Check answers as a class.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Distribute the reading activity handouts (with the teacher’s notes and suggested answers removed). Alternatively, draw the table on the board. Set a time limit for children to group the words together in logical ways (categorize), and to name each group (classify). Go through the answers as a class.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Afterwards, elicit the difference between classifying and categorizing information (see Oxford Discover SB 4 p.169 ex.D). Children have just used these strategies to complete the grouping task. Make sure the class understands that ‘to categorize’ is to group similar things together, and ‘to classify’ is to name a group of similar things.
  2. Work through exs.D–E on p.169. Invite a confident volunteer to read aloud the paragraph. Copy the chart onto the board. As you reread the text, invite children to write each fish in the correct category. Review and discuss any mistakes.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Next, put children into groups to find new ways to categorize and classify the lesson’s vocabulary. This is a challenging activity, requiring children to think ‘outside of the box’ to come up with interesting classifications. Remind them that they are in the stretch zone.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Invite groups to share their categories and classifications.
  2. Finally, do ex.F together. Tell children to scan the informational text on pp.170–171 to find the listed words. Encourage them to guess the meanings of the words by combining their existing knowledge with the theme of the unit, and by using the reading strategies they have practised previously.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.154–155.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Support less able children in the reading activity by giving them some classifications. They can then sort the words into categories.
  2. Fast finishers can practise classifying and categorizing vocabulary sets from earlier units.

  5. Work in pairs. Put the words in the table into groups. Then give each group a name. Think of similarities and themes to help categorize the words.

  7. Make copies of the table above and give one to each pair. Allow children 5–10 minutes to consider how they will classify and categorize the words. YEAR 4 Ecosystems
Week 34, Lesson 2: Classifying and categorizing
Learning outcomes: 4.1b, 4.2c, 4.3b, 4.3e, 4.3j

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Ecosystems’.
In this lesson, children will practise their listening and speaking skills across activities that introduce them to new words for biomes. They will build on their reading skills by learning how to classify and categorize vocabulary items.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
