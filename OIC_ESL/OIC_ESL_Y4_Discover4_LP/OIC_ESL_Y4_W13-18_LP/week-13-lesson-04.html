PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Explain to children that over the next two lessons they are going to read about different and exciting artists.
  2. Write on the board: ‘title’, ‘heading’, ‘visual’, and ‘caption’. See if children can remember what these words relate to (see Week 13, Lesson 3) and ask them to write a definition for each in their own words. By thinking again about the organization of an information text, children are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.
  3. Invite a few children to read their definitions to the class. Elicit corrections, if necessary.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Note: Don’t read the entire text in this lesson. It will be revisited in Week 13, Lesson 5.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.70–71.
  2. Instruct children to note down five predictions about what they are going to read about, based on what they have understood from the title and introduction. Challenge them to rewrite the title in their own words. Take ideas from volunteers.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Explain that when we learn something new, it forms connections in our brain. The more we practise, these connections become stronger. Sometimes rewriting things in our own words makes more connections, allowing us to remember things more clearly.
  2. Show children the first heading – ‘The Painting Prodigy’ or ‘Zero-waste art’. Discuss its meaning as a whole class. Now instruct children to note down three predictions about what they are going to read about under the heading. Repeat with the other headings.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Show children the different photos and captions separately. In small groups, children work together to match the captions to the photos. Remind them to listen to each other’s ideas and respond to what they suggest. When complete, check answers as a whole class.
  2. Then ask groups to match the photos and captions to the correct heading. When complete, check answers as a whole class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Assign children two photos and captions to rewrite in their own words. Then divide the class into pairs. Ask children to read their paraphrased captions to their partner. Their partner guesses which photo the caption corresponds to.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Allow less confident children to work in pairs or adult-led small groups for the prediction and paraphrasing activities.
  2. Encourage more able children to support less able children throughout the lesson by explaining how to complete the tasks in their own words.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have used the features of a reading text to make predictions about what they are going to read about. They have practised paraphrasing and using logic to solve problems.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 4 Why do we make art?
Week 13, Lesson 4: Art through new eyes (1)
Learning outcomes: 4.3f, 4.3i

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Why do we make art?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by using the features of a reading text to make sensible predictions.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
