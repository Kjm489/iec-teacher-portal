PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Hold up some pictures of different kinds of art, including an artistic piece of furniture, a sculpture, a painting, and a piece of jewellery. Which photos do children think show ‘art’?
  2. Children discuss their ideas in small groups, giving reasons for their opinions. Help them to understand that there is no ‘right’ answer.
  3. Take a poll of children’s answers. Write down their results and keep the answers safe. At the end of the unit, they will do a similar poll to see if they have the same opinions.
  4. Ask children: What do you want to learn or see in this unit? Ask them to consider what they are interested in, then write five things they hope to see or learn about.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. To spark children’s thinking, show them two different pieces of art that they will find impressive, funny, or be intrigued by. It can be any kind of art, traditional or contemporary. In pairs, allow children time to discuss which they like/dislike and why.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.66–67. Play the video introducing the unit. First, children are going to work in pairs and make predictions about what they might see and hear. Then play the video and hold a whole-class discussion about whether children’s predictions were correct. Write any unknown words on the board. Elicit their meanings, giving clues and/or explanations. Sound the words out carefully together and drill a few times.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Play the video again and complete exs.B–C on p.67.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Elicit from children how they think they will stay motivated during the unit. To prompt them, ask: What are you looking forward to most? What do you want to learn or improve?
  2. In pairs, tell children to talk about the last piece of art they created. See if they can answer the questions: What was it? Where did you make it? Why did you make it? Bring the class back together. Encourage children to think about why people want to make art, relating this to their own experiences.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the Big Question 4 activities in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.59.
  2. Start a unit diary. Encourage children to write about what they have learned in each lesson, and their opinion on each topic.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For less confident children, pre-teach key words before the listening exercise.
  2. Challenge more confident children by asking them to explain their predictions to the class. Ask: Why did you think you would hear about …?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have explored different kinds of art and discussed why people make art. They have predicted the content of a listening exercise and listened for main information.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Why do we make art?
Week 13, Lesson 1: Why do we make art?
Learning outcomes: 4.1a, 4.1e

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Why do we make art?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by predicting likely content and recognizing key words in a listening text.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
