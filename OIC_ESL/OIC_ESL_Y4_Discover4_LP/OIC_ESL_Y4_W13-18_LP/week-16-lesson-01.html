PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write on the board and ask the class: Where do we see art? In pairs, children make a mind map of all the different places where we might see art. Bring the class back together and take children’s suggestions. Using children’s learning in the previous unit, help them to understand that art isn’t limited to art galleries, but it is all around us! Sometimes it depends on our perspectives and what we view as ‘art’.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Show children a picture of a famous or peculiar work of art to spark their curiosity (e.g. The Persistence of Memory by Salvador Dalí, Red Canna by Georgia O’Keeffe, The Starry Night by Vincent Van Gogh, Composition 8 by Wassily Kandinsky, or Esther Mahlangu’s Art Car).
  2. In pairs, children work together to describe the work of art. Then invite volunteers to call out words, phrases, or full sentences. Write ideas on the board.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Look at the words in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.76.
  2. Read the words aloud and elicit their meaning. Use common strategies to encourage ideas by placing the words in context, or by writing synonyms/antonyms on the board (e.g. ‘pale’ is the opposite of ‘dark’ and ‘bright’; ‘contrast’ means ‘difference’).

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Sound out the difficult words, such as ‘texture’ (/ˈtekstʃə(r)/) and ‘exhibition’ (/ˌeksɪˈbɪʃ(ə)n/). Children might work out how to pronounce the latter by applying the sound and spelling of familiar ‘–tion’ words (e.g. ‘station’, ‘emotion’, ‘fiction’).
  2. Then follow the steps:

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Play the audio (track 2.09 in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.76) and follow the sentences in ex.A.
  2. Work together to complete exs.B–C.
  3. Bring the class back together and discuss different reading strategies that children applied in today’s lesson to understand and pronounce unknown and difficult words.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–E in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.67–68.
  2. Write a paragraph about one of works of art from the lesson, using the new vocabulary.
  3. Test each other on the new vocabulary by writing context sentences for a partner.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For less confident children, focus on a few new words instead of all of them.
  2. Challenge more able children to find synonyms and antonyms for each new word.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned new vocabulary to describe art in more detail. They have read the words in context.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Where do we see art?
Week 16, Lesson 1: Describing art
Learning outcomes: 4.3b, 4.3e

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Where do we see art?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by using a range of strategies to predict the meaning and pronunciation of unknown words.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
