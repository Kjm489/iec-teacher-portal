PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Introduce some tongue-twisters. They are designed to be difficult, so reassure children that they shouldn’t expect to say them perfectly! Demonstrate saying some to the class:
  2. Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers. A peck of pickled peppers Peter Piper picked.
  3. You know New York, you need New York, you know you need unique New York.
  4. I thought I thought of thinking of thanking you.
  5. Red lorry, yellow lorry (x3).
  6. Four fine fresh fish for you.
  7. Write them on the board. Challenge children to choose a few and practise saying them in pairs. Encourage children to have fun with this task and say them as quickly as they can!
  8. Discuss what sounds children struggled with the most and why. Note these down. Model the shape your mouth makes saying these sounds for children to copy.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain to children that they are going to talk about wishes and desires (see Oxford Discover SB 4 p.82). Ask: What is a ‘wish’? What is a ‘desire’? Elicit answers and establish that the words are more or less interchangeable, although a ‘desire’ is more of a goal, whereas a ‘wish’ might be less realistic. They highlight something you want to do or achieve, but cannot right now.
  2. Write examples on the board: e.g. I wish I could make beautiful sculptures. / I wish I were more creative. / I want to display my paintings in an art gallery!
  3. In pairs, children practise saying the sentences. Ask them to identify and note down the different forms. Bring the class back together and take answers.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Then ask pairs to make three of their own sentences. Invite volunteers to share their ideas.
  2. Introduce the response: Why do you want to [be a sculptor]? Using the sentences on the board, discuss as a class what reasons someone might have for expressing a wish.
  3. Model a short dialogue with a confident child, or another adult in class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. A: I wish I were a famous painter.
  2. B: Why do you want to be famous?
  3. A: I want people to see my paintings all around the world.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. B: That sounds great. I wish I could paint brilliant landscapes!
  2. Reform pairs. Using children’s own sentences from the previous task, encourage them to role-play a dialogue. They will need to listen actively to each other to be able to respond to their partner and ask follow-up questions.
  3. Invite a few volunteers to perform their role-play to the class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Write a paragraph expressing a wish or desire related to art. Remind children to state their wish, then give several reasons why they want to achieve it.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the speaking activity, write a model dialogue on the board with variable sections highlighted.
  2. To support less confident children, focus on the expressions ‘I wish I could …’ and ‘I want to …’ only. Challenge more confident children to practise using a variety of past tense forms: e.g. I wish I had [more patience when I paint]. / I wish I lived [next to an art gallery].

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned to express wishes and desires in a dialogue, and applied their learning to a role-play task.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Where do we see art?
Week 17, Lesson 1: Expressing wishes and desires
Learning outcomes: 4.2b, 4.2c

Context
This is the sixth lesson of unit ‘Where do we see art?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by learning how to express wishes and desires in conversation.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
