PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write different words in a jumbled order on the board. At least half of the words should be subject and object pronouns, while the other words can be from other word categories (e.g. ‘paint’, ‘jump’, ‘donkey’, ‘across’, ‘wonderful’, ‘sad’, ‘exercise’, ‘eating’).
  2. In turn, invite children to the board and ask them to circle an example of a ‘pronoun’. After each try, ask the rest of the class if they agree or disagree. Confirm children’s understanding of subject and object pronouns.
  3. Tell children that today they are going to learn about ‘indefinite pronouns’.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Help children to understand that indefinite pronouns are used to talk about unnamed people or things (see Oxford Discover SB 4 p.73 or use your own subject knowledge).
  2. Write a list of indefinite pronouns on the board. Take suggestions from children first, then add the remainder: ‘something’, ‘anything’, ‘everything’, ‘someone’, ‘everyone’, ‘anytime’, ‘no one’, ‘nobody’, and ‘nothing’. Read them aloud, sounding them out carefully.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. In pairs, challenge children to try to group the pronouns. Bring the class back together and discuss possible categories.
  2. They might suggest ‘Person’, ‘Thing’, and/or ‘Time’. Write these headings on the board and ask children to write them down. Instruct them to sort the indefinite pronouns into the correct categories. Take answers and elicit why they chose particular categories (e.g. by looking at the suffixes ‘one’, ‘body’, ‘thing’, and ‘time’).
  3. Person – someone, everyone, no one, nobody

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Thing – something, anything, everything, nothing
  2. Time – anytime

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Write gapped sentences on the board with missing indefinite pronouns. In pairs, ask children to complete them orally with a suitable word. Highlight that sometimes more than one word can be used: e.g. Is there [anything/something] on TV tonight? / You can come to my house [anytime]. / Did [everyone/someone] go?
  2. Instruct children to write three of their own sentences with a different indefinite pronoun in each. More confident children can try to relate the sentences to art: e.g. Anyone can make art. / The artist is sketching something.
  3. In pairs, children read aloud and peer-correct each other’s sentences. They should give feedback on 1. whether the pronoun is used correctly, and 2. if the sentence makes sense. Tell them to circle an error instead of explaining why it is wrong, so their partner can try to work it out themselves. Remind children to give and receive feedback constructively – be kind and helpful – and that making mistakes is positive. It helps us to learn and improve!

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–F in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.63–64.
  2. Ask children to write a list of different people and objects in the classroom. In pairs, they take turns guessing their partner’s words, giving clues where necessary: e.g. A: I’m thinking of something that is red. B: Is it Keiko’s pencil case?

Oxford differentiation

  1. Pair up more able children to peer-correct each other’s sentences. Less able children might benefit from an adult-led small-group session, so they can learn at their own pace.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have practised using indefinite pronouns in pairwork.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
