LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 4
Theme: Why do we make art?
Week: 13
Lesson: 2
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 4.1b, 4.1f, 4.2c

Original Oxford lesson: What kind of art do you make?

Original learning outcomes: 4.1b, 4.1f, 4.2c

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. In small groups, challenge children to think of as many different types of artist as they can: e.g. ‘painter’, ‘photographer’, or ‘sculptor’. Write their ideas on the board.
  2. Discuss each type of artist as a class and make simple sentences to say what they do: e.g. A photographer takes photos.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Ask children when they last did any of the activities discussed above, and what equipment they needed to make their art. Write any key words on the board.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.68 for ideas. Help children to guess the vocabulary (e.g. ‘paintbrushes’) by miming actions or drawing pictures. Explain or elicit the words, sound them out together, and drill.
  2. Read the true-or-false sentences in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.68 ex.B aloud to the class. Children listen and write ‘T’ or ‘F’ on their whiteboard, next to each number.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. If children are unsure about the meaning of any of the words in the sentences, encourage them to note them down. At the end of the activity, look at each difficult word in turn. Ask children to look at the context of the sentence first, then make a sensible guess based on the information they have. Check answers as a whole class.
  2. Set children the task of making sentences about the art that they make, or want to make, using the new topic vocabulary from today’s lesson. Give them enough time to plan a short oral presentation about it.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Children take turns giving their presentations in small groups. Remind them of the importance of listening to each other carefully and respectfully. Invite a few volunteers to present their sentences to the class.
  2. Encourage the class to discuss the main strengths of the presenters, focusing on what they did well (e.g. confidence, clear voice, good eye contact, interesting ideas, good explanations, varied use of topic vocabulary, good attempt at using a range of grammatical forms, stepping outside of their comfort zone/stretching themselves).

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.59–60.
  2. Write a final draft of the presentation.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the introductory activity, show children some pictures of different types of artist to help generate ideas.
  2. Reduce the number of new vocabulary items for less able children.
  3. Encourage more able children to give their oral presentation without relying too much on their pre-prepared sentences and notes. See if they can make eye contact with their peers and make gestures as they speak.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned new topic vocabulary to talk about art-related activities and equipment. They have practised using new language in an oral presentation.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
