Original Oxford lesson: Making offers

Original learning outcomes: 4.2a, 4.2b

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Show children a picture of someone struggling with something. They could be holding too many shopping bags, or trying to reach something on the top shelf. Ask: What do you think you should do if you see someone like this? Elicit ‘offer to help’ from children.
  2. Ask children about the last time they offered someone help, or someone offered to help them. Listen to children’s stories and respond appropriately (e.g. give praise if they did a kind thing).

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain to children that today they are going to focus on using the words shall and will to make offers of help. Say: We use shall to make offers in the form of a question. We use will to make offers in the form of a statement. (See Oxford Discover SB 4 p.81.)
  2. First, establish if children already know how to make offers of help using shall and will. If they’re not sure, encourage them to make suggestions. Reassure them that this is to stretch them, not to trick them! It is OK if they are unsure. The task should encourage them to think deeply about their knowledge of grammar and what would be logical.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Then write a few examples on the board: e.g. Shall I carry your bags across the road for you? / I will get that box of cereal for you. It’s very high!
  2. Ask children to write down two more offers in response to the picture in the introductory activity, or show them a new picture. They must give one example of shall and one of will.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Divide the class into small groups. Give each group another set of pictures of people that might need help. Challenge children to make as many different offers as they can think of.
  2. As a class, discuss possible responses to these offers: e.g. Yes, please. / No, thank you. Explain that sometimes people don’t want to accept offers of help and this is OK, too.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Introduce a game. One child stands up and mimes a problem. They need some help! Group members take turns making offers until somebody makes an offer that the child requires. For example, a child could pretend to be out of breath after doing exercise. Their classmate could offer: I will get you a glass of water.
  2. Practise a few times in small groups, then play the game as a whole class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–E in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.71–72.
  2. Imagine you are a security guard in an art gallery. Write five sentences offering to help visitors and artists, using both shall and will.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the stretch zone challenge, make offers to children and see if they can recognize the language that you are using.
  2. Encourage more able children to explain the grammar point to less confident children in their own words. This will not only benefit their classmate, but it will help to consolidate their own understanding of shall and will.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have practised making offers of help in different scenarios.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
