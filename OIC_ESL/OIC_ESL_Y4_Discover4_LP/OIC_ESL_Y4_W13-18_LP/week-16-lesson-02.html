PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children what they think a ‘value judgement’ is. You can first ask them what the meaning of each word is, and then connect them. Elicit answers. Help children to understand that it is an opinion of a character, based on what they do, what they don’t do, and whether they do the correct thing or not.
  2. Give an example, relating it to the art world:
  3. e.g. Betty wanted to win first prize in an art competition, but she couldn’t think of any good ideas. She practised and practised her painting but, in her opinion, it wasn’t good enough. One day, she found one of her mum’s brightly coloured paintings in a silver frame. It was fantastic. She sent it to the competition and won first prize – a trip to a famous gallery!
  4. Encourage children to make a value judgement. Ask: Did Betty do the right thing? Why do you think that? What kind of person is she?
  5. Now ask children to work in pairs to imagine a story in which the character makes the right decision. Challenge them to relate it to art in some way, and consider what positive qualities or ‘strengths’ they would like to attribute to this character.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Refer children to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.77.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Read paragraphs 1 and 2. In pairs, children circle the value judgement they agree with (a or b). Encourage them to explain their thinking.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Then ask: In the same situation, what would you do?

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. To end the lesson, write on the board several characters from popular stories that all children should know in your context. Hold a whole-class discussion about the actions of these characters. Allow children to give their opinions and debate with each other if they disagree.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Read the text and complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.69–70.
  2. Write a paragraph about one of the characters from today’s lesson, or choose a character from a favourite book, film, or comic. Make a value judgement about their actions, giving reasons why.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Pair up more able and less able children in the reading activity so the former can explain the task to the latter and help them to understand each scenario.
  2. Challenge more confident children to rewrite the endings to each scenario to change their value judgements.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned about value judgements and practised making their own.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Where do we see art?
Week 16, Lesson 2: Making value judgements
Learning outcomes: 4.3g

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Where do we see art?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by analysing and forming an opinion on short texts.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
