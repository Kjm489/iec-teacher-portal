Original learning outcomes: 4.3e, 4.3g

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children that they have a lot of reading to do in class today, so they are going to start the lesson by doing some stretches. Elicit that staying inactive for long periods of time can negatively affect their moods, and that doing exercise can help them to feel less worried and more motivated! Ask children to stand up. Perform a short stretching routine for them to copy. Briefly discuss how children feel after moving around.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Recall what children predicted about the reading text in Week 13, Lesson 4.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Look at the text in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.70–71. Read the first heading and paragraph as a whole class. Discuss whether children’s predictions were correct. Write any unknown words on the board and encourage children to guess their meaning using the strategies they know. Explain, sound out, and drill. Then ask children to read the paragraph again individually. Ask comprehension questions to check their understanding of the main points, highlighting common functional language. Repeat with the other paragraphs.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. In small groups, children discuss which of their predictions weren’t quite right. Prompt them to talk about their reasons for making the initial predictions, and what information they needed to give them the correct answer.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Reform groups. Now ask children to give opinions about the different artists and their styles. Remind them to give reasons why they like/dislike a particular artist, and why they prefer one artist’s work to another’s.
  2. Bring the class back together. Hold a whole-class discussion about the strengths of each artist children have read about. They should consider how these strengths could be used in everyday life.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Read the text and complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.61–62.
  2. Write a summary of one of the artists from the text. Paraphrase the information learned in the article. Include a title, one or two headings, a visual, and a caption.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the reading activity, focus on just one paragraph. Deconstruct it together with children, using strategies (word category, context, co-text, synonyms/antonyms) to guess the meaning of difficult words.
  2. Challenge more able children to compare the different artists and art styles using comparative and superlative adjectives: e.g. [This artist’s work] is more colourful than [this artist’s work]. In my opinion, it is the best artwork.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have checked their predictions against a reading text. They have read about interesting artists, identifying and understanding the main information.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
