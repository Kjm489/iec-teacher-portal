PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children whether they have visited an art gallery or a museum before. Allow them to discuss their experiences in small groups. Encourage them to say when it was, where it was, and what their experience was like. If a child hasn’t been to an art gallery or a museum before, ask them to think about what it would be like and what they would like to see most. Invite some volunteers to tell the class about their trip.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children that they are going to read a story about a visit to an art gallery.
  2. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.78–79.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Read the text aloud and ask children to follow in their books. Alternatively, break the text down into sections and assign different sections to different children to read aloud.
  2. Ask children to call out words they don’t recognize or understand. Write them on the board and sound them out together. Encourage them to use known reading strategies to predict their meaning. Correct children where necessary.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. As a class, write down the main points in the text.
  2. Then ask children to read the text a second time, individually or in pairs.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Divide the class into small groups and assign group leaders. In their groups, children take turns making value judgements about Theo and Zayan Kahn. The group leaders must ensure that everybody has the opportunity to share their ideas and isn’t interrupted. If there is debate, it must be respectful. They can also ask their less confident classmates questions to prompt and encourage them.
  2. Invite groups to feed back to the class.
  3. Calm the class down and ask for 5 minutes of quiet. Allow children time to reflect on their learning this lesson. What are the lessons in the story? How are they similar or different to the characters in the story? Have they had a similar experience?

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.80.
  2. Challenge children to close their books and retell the story orally, using topic vocabulary.
  3. Ask children to write a paragraph comparing themselves to one of the characters they read about. They should initially make a value judgement about the character, and then say what is similar or different about themselves.

Oxford differentiation

  1. In an adult-led small group, give extra support to less able children during the second reading of the text. Ensure that they understand the gist – they don’t need to understand every single word.
  2. Encourage less confident but able children to take on the group leader role. Explain that sometimes it is helpful to step outside of our comfort zone and the classroom is a safe environment to do so. You may wish to assign two group leaders per group to help build their confidence.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have identified the main points in a story. They have discussed it in small groups.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 4 Where do we see art?
Week 16, Lesson 3: Visiting an art gallery
Learning outcomes: 4.2e, 4.3e, 4.3g

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Where do we see art?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by reading a long text and identifying main information. They will practise their speaking skills by making value judgements and actively listening to each other’s ideas.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
