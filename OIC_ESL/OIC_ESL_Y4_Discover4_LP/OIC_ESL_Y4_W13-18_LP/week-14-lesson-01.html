Original Oxford lesson: Talking to an artist

Original learning outcomes: 4.2a, 4.2b, 4.2d, 4.2e

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children what they think an ‘interview’ is. Elicit answers and discuss as a whole class. If possible, ask them to give examples of a famous interview. You may wish to show them a short, child-friendly video clip.
  2. In today’s lesson, children are going to role-play an interview (between a reporter and an artist). In small groups, give children time to guess who they think the interview will be with between based on their knowledge of the unit. Give children the answer.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Complete the Think exercise in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.72. In the same small groups, instruct children to think of as many different questions that a reporter could ask an artist as they can. Take suggestions and write them on the board.
  2. If necessary, revise Wh– question words.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Model role-playing an interview in front of the class with another adult or confident child. You may like to look at the model role-play and the Learn summary in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.124. The model should contain an appropriate opening and closing, and correctly formed questions:
  2. Example opening: Hello, I’m [reporter’s name] from Art News. Today, I’m speaking to a fantastic new artist called [artist’s name]. She will tell me more about her work.
  3. Example closing: Thank you very much for speaking with me today, [artist’s name]. We can’t wait to see more of your art soon. Back to the studio!

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Example questions: Can you describe your art? What is your favourite artwork / thing about being an artist? Do you like your job? How do you think of new ideas? What is the message in your art? Who is your favourite artist? Where can we learn to be an artist?
  2. Elicit how the conversation started and ended, and what the interlocutor does while listening (looks at the other person directly; pays close attention to what they are saying and responds accordingly; doesn’t interrupt or get distracted; non-verbal cues, like nodding and smiling).

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Divide the class into pairs. Give children 15–20 minutes to plan their role-play, prioritizing tasks to make sure that they have everything ready in time (choosing an artist; thinking of good questions, answers, and expressions to use in conversation; writing down their ideas; adding gestures). The artist can be real or imaginary. As children plan and write their dialogues, monitor their progress and offer help where necessary. Remind children that this activity is meant to stretch them to extend their understanding of topic vocabulary, conversational skills, and asking and answering simple questions correctly.
  2. Children practise their role-plays.
  3. Invite them to perform their interviews to another pair, or to the rest of the class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Ask children to reflect on their role-play and think about what they did well, and what interview skills they need to practise more.
  2. Write a final draft of the interview script.
  3. Write a short biography of the artist, based on the information learned in the interview.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Write useful interview expressions on the board to support less confident children.
  2. Allow less confident children to use their notes during the role-play, while challenging more confident children to see how much they can remember and improvise.
  3. Encourage more confident children to take the role of the reporter.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have role-played an interview between a reporter and an artist.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
