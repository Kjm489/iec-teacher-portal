TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Elicit from children what they can remember about indefinite pronouns from Week 14, Lesson 2. Write examples on the board (e.g. ‘anything’, ‘anytime’, ‘everyone’, ‘everything’, ‘no one’, ‘nobody’, ‘nothing’, ‘someone’, ‘something’).
  2. Invite a volunteer to explain the function of indefinite pronouns – they are pronouns that don’t refer to something in particular (e.g. a person, an object, or an amount), but to something more general without being specific.
  3. In small groups, ask children to take turns saying sentences using indefinite pronouns.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Write ‘Person’, ‘Thing’, and ‘Place’ on the board. In pairs, challenge children to put the indefinite pronouns that they know into the correct category. ‘Place’ (e.g. ‘everywhere’ and ‘nowhere’) is new, so you may need to give these examples and elicit their meaning.
  2. Bring the class back together and take suggestions from children. Encourage peer-correction where necessary.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Focus children’s attention to the negative indefinite pronouns. If not suggested already, write them on the board for them to categorize. See also Oxford Discover SB 4 p.93.
  2. Ask children to complete ex.F in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.93. Children work in pairs to select the correct option and complete the sentences. Review as a class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. e.g. ‘There is nowhere / nothing to do in my city.’
  2. Then provide gapped sentences with the negative indefinite pronoun missing. Children work in pairs to complete the sentences with the correct word. Review as a class.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. e.g. ‘_______ was allowed to enter the museum before 9 a.m.’
  2. Then write the following sentence starters on the board: ‘There is nobody / no one …’, ‘There is nothing …’, and ‘There is nowhere …’. Children work in pairs to suggest possible sentence endings. Invite pairs to share their sentences with the class. Encourage peer-correction where necessary.
  3. Form new pairs. Instruct Child A to mime an action related to one of their sentences. Child B tries to guess the sentence, using the correct negative indefinite pronoun. After two or three turns, children swap roles.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–F in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.82–83.
  2. Hand out indefinite pronoun word cards to small groups of children. Set them the task of writing a silly story about anything they like. However, for every other sentence, they have to pick up a card and try to incorporate the indefinite pronoun written on it. Children can do this as a speaking or writing activity.

Oxford differentiation

  1. At the beginning of the lesson, allow children to read their notes from Week 14, Lesson 2 before eliciting what they can remember.
  2. Challenge more confident children to practise asking and answering questions using indefinite pronouns.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have revised indefinite pronouns and practised using negative indefinite pronouns in sentences.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
