Original Oxford lesson: What do big cities have that small towns don’t?

Original learning outcomes: 4.1e, 4.1i, 4.1j

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Draw a large Venn diagram on the board. Tell the class that they are going to think about what big cities have that small towns don’t, and vice versa, as well as what they both have. Give children time to discuss and note down their ideas in small groups first.
  2. In turn, invite a child to the board to add one of the group’s answers to the Venn diagram. Repeat until all suggestions have been added.
  3. Invite the class to discuss whether they agree or disagree with the final diagram, and why. Change suggestions accordingly and agree on a final categorization.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Children are going to listen to city dwellers talking about the differences between big cities and small towns. First, ask them to note down ten key words they think they will hear.
  2. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.102.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. On the first play of the audio (track 2.26), ask children to listen for gist and for their key word predictions. As a class, discuss whether children’s predictions were correct.
  2. On the second play, ask children to complete exs.A–B.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Hand out copies of the listening transcript. Play the audio a third time. Check children’s understanding of the vocabulary.
  2. Ask: What language did the speakers use to give a fact or an opinion? Look at the transcript again. Instruct children to underline the facts in one colour and the opinions in another. Elicit how the speakers used comparative and superlative adjectives to give their opinions: e.g. ‘Pandas are the most beautiful bears …’, ‘Nothing is better than sushi …’, ‘A park is the best place to read …’, ‘Squash is the most exciting sport …’.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Pair up children. Tell them to make sentences, giving facts and opinions about a big city and/or a small town they know. Encourage them to engage their partner in their conversation by including a few tag questions. Their partner will need to actively listen to what they are saying in order to respond successfully to the tag question(s).
  2. Praise children for their effort in including tag questions and comparative and superlative adjectives in their sentences. Don’t intervene, but correct common mistakes on the board at the end of the lesson.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Have a whole-class debate on the motion: ‘A big city is more exciting than a small town’.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Allow less confident children to follow the transcript on the first listen.
  2. Challenge more confident children to explain their opinions using because or but.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have listened for facts and opinions about big cities and small towns, and established the differences between them. They have practised giving opinions.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Working in a city
Week 23, Lesson 1: What do big cities have that small towns don’t?
Learning outcomes: 4.1e, 4.1i, 4.1j

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Working in a city’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by recognizing the difference between facts and opinions, and identifying main information in short presentations.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
