PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children they will be reading a lot in today’s lesson, so they are going to begin by doing some stretches. Ask them to stand up and spread out. Perform a short stretching routine and instruct children to copy you. Make it fun: e.g. Stretch as high as a skyscraper!
  2. Invite volunteers to suggest a few stretches.
  3. Briefly discuss how children feel after moving around. Explain that short bursts of exercise and stretching can help to boost our mood and reduce anxiety.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.104.
  2. Form small groups. Hand out a cut-up text to each group. Ask them to read the different sections and to work together to arrange them into the correct order.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Groups share their final order with the class. Discuss and peer-correct until the correct order is agreed. Ask: How do you know this paragraph goes here?
  2. Elicit the four sections: title, introduction, body paragraphs, and conclusion. Talk about the function of each in a persuasive essay (see the ‘Learn’ box on p.104).

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Ask children to find useful expressions for persuading the reader of your point, such as: ‘You may think that … / It is true that … However …’ and ‘Although …’.
  2. Children read the full text again individually and complete ex.B.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Ask the class: Are you now persuaded that cities are the best places to live? Encourage them to directly reference the essay when they give reasons to explain why / why not.
  2. Children are going to write their own persuasive essay about the best place to live in the next lesson. In pairs, give them time to discuss which place they would like to write about.
  3. Hand out graphic organizers with headings for each section. Tell children to begin planning their own essay, using their learning from the lesson and the model text to help them.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete ex.A in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.94.
  2. Encourage children to offer constructive feedback on each other’s plan. Has their partner considered how the reader might disagree with them and thought of a persuasive argument to make them change their mind?

Oxford differentiation

  1. For additional support, provide example completed plans for the input text.
  2. Challenge more confident children to include strong adjectives and coordinating conjunctions in their plans.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have read a persuasive essay and began to plan their own.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Working in a city’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by working together to reconstruct an input text and identifying the features of a persuasive essay.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
