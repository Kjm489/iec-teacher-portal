Original Oxford lesson: Which cities do you know about?

Original learning outcomes: 4.3a, 4.3c, 4.3i, 4.4e

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask: Have you ever travelled to a city in a different country? What did you do there? Children discuss their experiences in small groups. If a child hasn’t travelled to a city in another country, ask them to talk about another city within their country or a city they would like to visit. Encourage them to use vocabulary from the previous lesson (Week 19, Lesson 2) when talking about city-based activities.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Using the same photos from Week 19, Lesson 1 or a new selection, show children some well-known cities. Elicit their names and any famous landmarks.
  2. Explain that today, children are going to learn how to ‘paraphrase’. Write the word on the board, then elicit that it means using your own words to explain what you have read in a text. This is helpful for remembering main facts and ideas.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask children to complete ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.89. Review answers as a whole class.
  2. Provide five simple sentences about cities around the world, including facts that will stimulate children’s curiosity. Tell children to rewrite the sentences in their own words, using language and synonyms that they already know.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Children compare their sentences in pairs. Then go through each sentence as a class, inviting children to share their best ones. Encourage peer-correction if necessary.
  2. Now challenge children to write five of their own facts about cities, using their existing knowledge of these places. As the aim of the task is to paraphrase effectively and not to write factually accurate sentences, you don’t need to fact-check the sentences, but encourage children to think of information they already know.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Quickly go around the classroom and check children’s sentences before the next step.
  2. In pairs, children swap sentences and rewrite them in their own words. This is a challenging task, so remind them that they are in the stretch zone. When they have both finished, they work together to decide if the sentences convey the same information using different language choices. Tell them to think about suitable revisions together.
  3. As a class, discuss how children found paraphrasing. Was it difficult to think of different words to explain the same fact or idea? Did they follow a particular strategy?

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.80–81.
  2. Choose another text to paraphrase from the coursebook.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the writing activity, pre-prepare fact files about well-known cities around the world, or tell less confident children to write about their own city.
  2. Challenge more confident children to look at the sentences in the first reading task and write a third sentence paraphrasing the same information.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have read and paraphrased sentences about cities. In pairs, they have helped each other to make appropriate language choices to convey the same information.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
