TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Refer children to the photo in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.86–87 and/or show them large pictures of (well-known and less familiar) big cities around the world. Elicit the city names.
  2. Stand in a circle. Throw a ball to a confident child and ask them to name one thing (a place, a building, a type of person [e.g. office worker]) that we can find in a city. They return the ball. Repeat with others, eliciting as much vocabulary as possible and writing good ideas on the board. How many words can they think of without repeating themselves?
  3. Children return to their seats. Ask: What do you want to learn about in this unit? Allow them time to write five things they hope to see or learn about in pairs.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Write ‘village’, ‘town’, and ‘city’ on the board. Establish the difference between the three places – in particular, how they differ in size and in level of activity – by using local examples in your context. In pairs, children discuss whether they live in a village, town, or city, and what makes them think that: e.g. I live in a village because it is smaller than a town. It has houses, a primary school, and three shops. It isn’t very busy.
  2. Form groups of four. Encourage children to make a list of what we can find in each place, referring back to the vocabulary in the warm-up. Make it clear that we might see some things in all three places, whereas other things might only be found in one.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Take children’s suggestions and write them on the board underneath the three headings. Check understanding of any new vocabulary and drill as a class.
  2. Tell the class you are going to describe a city.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Ask: What key words that we’ve learned today do you think you will hear? Take suggestions, then read aloud your pre-prepared text.
  2. Ask: What words did you hear? Were your predictions correct? Share answers as a class. See if children can recall the sentence where each key word appears. Pay attention to their pronunciation of difficult sounds (e.g. station – /ˈsteɪʃ(ə)n/) and drill as a class again.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Read the description again. Ask some comprehension questions.
  2. Discuss as a class: Is the place described similar or different to where you live? Why?

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the Big Question 5 activities in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.78.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the warm-up, elicit/pre-teach a few words first, then test children’s memory of the new vocabulary in the ball game.
  2. Fast finishers can use dictionaries to find more vocabulary and write it on the board.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have practised listening for new topic words, focusing on their pronunciation.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
