PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children to get out their notes from Week 19, Lesson 1. In pairs, tell them to discuss the five things that they wanted to learn about cities. Were their questions answered in the unit? If yes, encourage them to talk about what they have learned. If not, see if their partner can help them out, or suggest a way of finding out the information.
  2. Then ask: What was the most interesting fact you have learned about cities?
  3. Discuss as a whole class. If there’s time, you may wish to refer children to the texts in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.90–91, p.95 ex.E, or p.93 ex.D to refresh their memory of descriptions of a city.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Hand out children’s plans from the previous lesson (Week 20, Lesson 4).
  2. Give children some ‘quiet time’ to read through their plan individually and to reflect on what they need to do in today’s lesson to achieve their goal. If they are feeling anxious about the writing task, remind them to take a few breaths.
  3. Elicit from the class some basic success criteria for writing a description and write them on the board. This might include:

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. accurate spelling, punctuation, and language use
  2. the capitalization of proper nouns
  3. the logical organization of ideas
  4. the use of descriptive language

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. the inclusion of simple opinions.
  2. Children should spend most of the lesson writing their descriptive paragraph(s).
  3. Encourage them to take short breaks to breathe and reflect on their work so far.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. When they have finished their first draft, remind them to check their work against the agreed success criteria.
  2. Children share their description with a partner. They read each other’s work and provide constructive feedback, mentioning two things they did well and one or two things that they need to correct or improve. Remind them to follow the success criteria to structure their feedback.
  3. Encourage children to value their partner's recommendations and to empathize if a mistake is made.
  4. Children revise their description accordingly.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Invite children to read out their description in small groups. Remind them to think about their tone of voice and intonation when reading aloud to an audience.
  2. Write a final copy of the city description, adding photos and illustrations.

Oxford differentiation

  1. To support less able children, provide a writing template or a list of useful sentence starters for them to use. They might also benefit from writing their description in pairs (of similar ability) rather than individually, so peer-support can be provided.
  2. Challenge more able children to use strong adjectives in their descriptions, to connect their ideas with linking words, and to outline why the city is important in a concluding paragraph.
  3. Pair up similar abilities for the peer-review activity so children can work at their own pace and stretch each other.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have used their plans and followed simple success criteria to write a description of a city they have learned about. They have peer-reviewed each other’s work.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
