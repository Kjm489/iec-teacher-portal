PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children to imagine that they went shopping in the city centre. In small groups, they are going to take turns adding items to a list of what they bought. Say, for example: I went shopping and I bought a candle. Ask a confident child to repeat your sentence and then add one more item, for example: I went shopping and I bought a candle and a cake.
  2. Challenge groups to try to make the longest list they can, without forgetting or repeating an item!
  3. Then ask: What do we use ‘and’ for? (to connect words, phrases, and sentences).

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Write ‘and’, ‘but’, and ‘or’ on the board in big letters. Ask children what we use these words for. Elicit answers and check their understanding – they all connect words, phrases, and sentences, but have distinct uses (see also Oxford Discover SB 4 p.103 ex.E).
  2. and – simply connects two words, phrases, or sentences (often of the same part of speech or grammatical function)
  3. but – introduces a contrasting phrase or clause

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. or – connects two options or alternatives
  2. Elicit or write on the board a couple of examples of each.
  3. Ask children to complete ex.E in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.103, or provide five gapped sentences for children to complete with the correct coordinating conjunction: e.g. I like that cities have lots of zoos and green parks, [but] they can be very crowded.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Check answers as a class. Encourage children to check each other’s answers for mistakes and to peer-correct where necessary. Invite the child with the correct answer to explain why they chose a particular coordinating conjunction over the others. Reinforce that making mistakes is a positive thing – we can learn from each other and improve!
  2. Add five more complete sentences on the board without commas. Pair up children. Ask them to decide together if any of these sentences need commas. If so, why?
  3. In turn, invite children to insert commas into the sentences that they believe require one.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Hold a quick peer-correction session until children arrive at the correct answers.
  2. Elicit and help children to understand that we often use commas when connecting two independent clauses (sentences).
  3. Tell children to imagine a city – it can be similar to a real-life city, or a futuristic city. They are going to write a short paragraph about this city and what they can/want to do there using and, but, and or to make connections. They should use each coordinating conjunction at least once, and mention what they like and don’t like about the city.
  4. If there is time at the end of the lesson, tell children to swap their paragraphs with a partner. They peer-correct each other’s work, focusing on the use of coordinating conjunctions and mentioning at least one thing they really liked about the paragraph.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.93.
  2. Encourage children to make a worksheet for their classmates to practise and, but, and or.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Encourage more able children to support less able children with grasping the differences between the three coordinating conjunctions.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have reviewed how to use and, but, and or to connect words, phrases, and sentences.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
