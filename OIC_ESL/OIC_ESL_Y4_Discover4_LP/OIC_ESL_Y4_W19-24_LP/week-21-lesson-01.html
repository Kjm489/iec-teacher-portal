TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

Source note: Oxford provides one shared weekly reflection/review plan. IEC has organized it into five teaching sessions; this is an IEC adaptation, not five separate Oxford-authored lessons.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘What is a city?’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
  2. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Praise children for their effort in this unit and let them know that it hasn’t gone unnoticed.
  3. Children may keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements. Explain how neurons in the brain form connections when we learn new things. Reviewing what we have learned will make these connections stronger and help us to retain information.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas:

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Children work in pairs to design a leaflet about a city and what you can do there. They should decide on at least three aspects to write about (e.g. transportation, food, entertainment). They should use reference books and the internet to do their research. Alternatively, they may already have existing knowledge of a city, or know somebody who’s visited it who they can interview. Children present their leaflet to the class at the end of the week.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Children work in small groups to design and make their own model city, using a range of junk modelling materials. Provide a selection of materials – such as cardboard boxes (for buildings), textured paper, old newspapers and magazines, toilet rolls, fabric, bubble wrap, silver foil, buttons, yoghurt pots, and plastic bottles – but also ask children to bring in their own materials. Allow them the freedom to decide on their design and to experiment with the different materials. Instruct them to clearly label each place in their city. They will present their model to the class at the end of the week, explaining why they chose certain materials to make particular features and why they think this city would be a great place to live.
  2. For both activities, ensure that children work together nicely, listen actively to each other, and plan effectively. They should make a clear plan, deciding on the different stages, and prioritizing the most important aspects to ensure they complete it on time. Praise them for their effort and creativity shown during the week, regardless of the success of the final product.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Mix up abilities in pairwork and group work so peer-support can be provided. Monitor children actively to ensure that everybody is able to participate in line with their strengths and interests. Write key topic vocabulary from the unit on the board to remind children.
  2. Challenge more able children to think about effective presentation. What language can they use (including new grammatical forms learned)? How can they maintain the audience’s interest?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-life context.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
