PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Divide the class into small groups. Instruct children to work together to try to remember the story they read in the previous lesson (Week 22, Lesson 3).
  2. Invite a volunteer group to retell the whole story to the class.
  3. Elicit if any events were missing from their classmates’ retelling.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Read the stories again independently or in pairs (see SB 4 pp.98–99 or CB 4 pp.66–67).
  2. Read the texts as a whole class. Ask children if there are any words that they do not know.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Write these words on the board, but don’t explain their meanings yet.
  2. Divide the class into small groups. Ask children to work together to try and approximate the meaning of the words on the board. Invite them to use whatever strategy they might find helpful.
  3. Where relevant, write synonyms or antonyms next to the words to prompt their thinking.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Take answers from children. If their answer isn’t quite right, invite another child from another group to make another suggestion. When the correct answer is offered, encourage the child to tell the class what strategy they used to work out the meaning.
  2. Elicit what part of speech each word represents. Ask: How do you know?
  3. Instruct children to write down the words and their definitions. Dictate simple definitions or make available dictionaries.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Where relevant, discuss the spelling patterns of the words. Can children think of other words they know that are spelled similarly? How are they pronounced? Drill the words together, sounding out the correct pronunciation.
  2. In their small groups, instruct children to make lists of words that have similar spellings to the key words in the text.
  3. Before the end of the lesson, discuss with children if they liked working in a team to understand new language. Ask: What went well? What could you have done a little differently? What did another classmate teach you? / How did another classmate help you?

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.88–89.
  2. Encourage children to make a worksheet for their classmates to test them on new vocabulary. They can choose the activity type (e.g. a wordsearch, anagrams, gap-fill).

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the introductory activity, give children 2 minutes to scan the story for main information.
  2. Challenge fast finishers to find more synonyms in a thesaurus.
  3. Challenge more confident children to think about how the story would change if one of the main or minor characters had done something differently.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have worked together to understand new language in a story. They have recalled and identified different strategies for doing so.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Learning outcomes: 4.3b, 4.3d, 4.3e

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Working in a city’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by using different strategies to work out the approximate meaning of unknown words and recognizing spelling analogies between new words and known words.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
