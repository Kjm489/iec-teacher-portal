Original Oxford lesson: Cities and water

Original learning outcomes: 4.1e, 4.1f, 4.1i

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask: What is a body of water? (e.g. sea, ocean, river, lake, canal). Write children’s suggestions on the board.
  2. Divide the class into small groups. Ask children to take turns saying what they think is important about water. They might suggest that it helps us to stay healthy and hydrated; that some people find water calming; and that it is used in agriculture, cooking, and sport.
  3. Discuss children’s ideas as a whole class.
  4. Invite everyone to have a drink of water before beginning the next part of the lesson.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Ask: Do you know any cities in your country that are next to a body of water? What are they called? What are they used for? Encourage children to discuss each question in pairs.
  2. Bring the class back together for a whole-class discussion. Help children to understand what each body of water is used for if they don’t get the answer quite right.
  3. Tell the class that they are going to listen for information about bodies of water near some major cities. Show photos of the cities they are going to learn about.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. First, ask children to predict why these cities grew as a result of nearby water.
  2. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.94.
  3. Play the audio (track 2.19) and ask children to complete ex.A. Let them check their answers in pairs first before reviewing as a class.
  4. Establish if their predictions were correct.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Tell children to read the questions in ex.B. Play the audio again and ask them to listen carefully for the answers to these questions, using the key words to help them.
  2. Review answers to the listening activity as a class.
  3. Ask children to describe the tone of voice of the speaker (neutral but engaging, to make children interested). Elicit why this tone was used for a factual listening text.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Divide the class into small groups. Tell children to discuss the following questions and listen carefully to each other’s thoughts and opinions:
  2. What was the most interesting fact you learned about cities and bodies of water?
  3. Did any of the information surprise you? Why?
  4. How similar or different is [city name from listening activity] to your city?

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Research a city that is by a body of water. Find out: where the city is on a world map, atlas, or globe, three interesting facts about the city, and how the body of water helped the city to grow.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the listening activity, provide a gapped text of the transcript.
  2. Encourage more confident children to use indefinite pronouns during the speaking activity.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have listened for information about bodies of water in cities and why they are important.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
