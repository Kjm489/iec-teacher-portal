TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children to think about the last time they visited a city centre – it might have been for a shopping trip, to meet family or friends at the cinema, or to eat at a restaurant. Ask: Where did you go? Who did you go with? What did you do? Write the questions on the board. Give children 2 minutes to think about what they are going to say.
  2. In pairs, children tell their partner about their trip to a city. Invite a few volunteers to share their experience with the rest of the class.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell the class that they are going to focus on what we can buy, eat, and ride in a city.
  2. Form small groups. On large sheets of paper, invite groups to write the headings ‘buy’, ‘eat’, and ‘ride’ in a three-column table, then to categorize their activity ideas accordingly.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Encourage children to nominate a group leader, who will decide on the best way to complete the activity and will ensure that each group member has the opportunity to speak. Give groups 5 minutes to complete the activity.
  2. In turn, invite groups to present their table to the class. Tell listening groups to raise their hands if they have the same answer.
  3. Invite group leaders to explain how they completed the activity – what worked well, and what they would like to improve. Discuss as a whole class and elicit other strategies for group work (e.g. playing to each other’s strengths, allocating different sub-tasks to different people, dividing the group into pairs then coming back together to share ideas).

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.88. Listen and read the words, sounding them out carefully. Invite children to guess what the words mean from the pictures, then explain each one. Give groups time to add these words to their table, then check answers as a class.
  2. Children can complete ex.C in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.8 independently or in pairs. Check answers as a class.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. In pairs, children are going to imagine a short story set in a city. It can be as silly as they like, but it must include two examples each of ‘buy’, ‘eat’, and ‘ride’.
  2. Pairs can refer to their tables for ideas and write down clear notes on note cards to help them to remember what to say in the following speaking activity. Elicit how to take notes effectively (e.g. don’t write everything down in full sentences, but use key words and bullet point main ideas in the order that they appear).
  3. Form small groups. Pairs take turns telling their stories in an engaging way, using their note cards to remind them of main ideas and useful language. Remind the class that public speaking is challenging, so it is important to show kindness and be patient if a classmate forgets part of their story or makes a mistake. Encourage listeners to show encouragement and interest by asking simple follow-up questions at the end.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.78–79.
  2. Write up the story as a final, illustrated text, adding further details.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Allow less able children to read out full sentences on their note cards, while more confident children should try to keep their notes concise and maintain eye contact with their listening classmates as much as possible.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. In pairwork and group work, children have categorized topic vocabulary and used new language in a storytelling activity.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Learning outcomes: 4.2a, 4.2d, 4.2e, 4.2f

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘What is a city?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills in group work and in a storytelling activity, using notes to prompt their thinking in a final presentation.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
