TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write vocabulary from last lesson’s reading text on the board (see Week 19, Lesson 4).
  2. Divide the class into teams. In a random order, read out a simple definition of each word. Instruct children to raise their hand if they know which word the definition corresponds to. Choose the quickest child (or if this becomes too repetitive, a new child each time) to give their answer. If they get it right, their team wins a point. If they get it wrong, ask another child from a different team. By thinking again about the meaning of difficult words, children are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Ask children what they can remember about the city studied in last lesson’s text (see Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.90–91). Discuss as a whole class.
  2. Tell them to read the text again independently to refresh their memory.
  3. Invite a volunteer to remind the class what is meant by ‘paraphrasing’ and what a successful attempt at paraphrasing looks like – the new text retains the same factual information and/or idea, but new language is used to explain the point.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Elicit the steps involved in the paraphrasing process.
  2. As a class, read the introduction again. Elicit the key words and main points from children and write them on the board.
  3. Pair up children. Using the notes on the board, challenge them to work together to rewrite the introduction in their own words.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Invite pairs to share their new introduction with the class. After listening to each attempt, hold a feedback session with the class. Ask: Did your classmates include all the main points? What did they miss? Was the information clear and concise? What did they do well? What could they improve?
  2. Now tell children that they are going to rewrite more of the text, in pairs or independently depending on their confidence levels.
  3. Divide the class into three groups. Give each group a different section to paraphrase (‘A visit to the old city’, ‘Modern culture’, or ‘Getting around’).

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Allocate enough time for children to work on their rewrite. Make available dictionaries and thesauruses to help them.
  2. Form groups of four children working on the same section. Tell them to share their rewrite with each other and to discuss what their classmates did well and what they could improve, making alternative language suggestions.
  3. Praise children for their effort in paraphrasing the text and for working together to improve each other’s written work.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Paraphrase more sections of the text or find an alternative text to paraphrase.

Oxford differentiation

  1. In the warm-up, challenge more able children to suggest synonyms for the words, where applicable, to win a bonus point for their team.
  2. For more support, highlight key words in each sentence that must stay the same.
  3. Challenge more able children by giving them words that they shouldn’t use in their paraphrased paragraph to increase the difficulty level. Encourage them to find synonyms.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have practised paraphrasing a text about a famous city.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘What is a city?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and writing skills in a 
paraphrasing activity.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
