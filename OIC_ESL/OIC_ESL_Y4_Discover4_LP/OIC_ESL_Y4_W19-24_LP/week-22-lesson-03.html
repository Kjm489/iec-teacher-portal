PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Show the class pictures of main and minor characters from popular fiction and television shows in your country. In pairs, children try to recognize and name the characters.
  2. Check answers as a class. In turn, ask: Do you like this character? Why? / Why not?
  3. Establish the difference between a ‘main character’ (the focus of the story) and a ‘minor character’ (characters that appear in the story around the main character).
  4. Elicit more examples of main and minor characters from children’s favourite books.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain to the class that when reading about a character, they can learn more about them by asking themselves the following questions:
  2. Are they the main character?

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. What is the character like? Do they have strengths? Do they have weaknesses?
  2. What does the character do and why?
  3. Complete ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.97.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Read the text together and prompt children to answer the above questions.
  2. Turn to the story ‘Mayor for a Day’ in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.98–99. Read it together as a class – either read aloud for children to follow, or invite volunteers to read a paragraph each.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Stop at regular intervals to ask the class what they think about the characters, using the above questions.
  2. In pairs, children work together to complete exs.A–B on p.100.
  3. Ask children who their favourite character is. Allow them time to share their opinions in small groups first, listening to each other’s perspectives, then invite them to share their ideas. Encourage them to give reasons for their decision.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. In small groups, encourage children to reinterpret the story in a role-play. Ask: What is your character like? How can you use body language and facial expressions to show this?

Oxford differentiation

  1. Support less able children with the reading activities in an adult-led small group.
  2. Challenge more confident children to compare the characters using comparative and superlative adjectives.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have read a story about an interesting job. They have identified the main and minor characters and assessed what they are like.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Working in a city

Learning outcomes: 4.3c, 4.3g, 4.3h

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Working in a city’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by analysing a text to understand a character and their actions.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
