PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write a sentence using have to on the board: e.g. I have to go to work at 7 a.m. In small groups, give children one minute to discuss what they think it means. Elicit that it refers to a requirement or ‘duty’. For example, at school, you have to follow the rules.
  2. Ask: What do you notice about the word that comes after ‘have to’? (It is the base form of another verb.)
  3. In their groups, give children 5 minutes to take turns saying sentences with have to.
  4. Invite volunteers to share their sentences with the class. Peer-correct if necessary.
  5. Then introduce the negative form – don’t have to – and the question form – e.g. Do you have to go to school every day? Does a teacher have to be fair?
  6. In pairs, ask children to write one example of a negative sentence and one example of a question using have to on their whiteboard. Walk around and check their answers.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children that they are going to talk about jobs and duties in today’s lesson. Ask: What do you think a ‘duty’ is? (It is a responsibility.)

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask children to make a list of as many jobs as they can think of in their groups. Give them 2 minutes to do this, then take answers and write correct ones on the board.
  2. Choose one of the jobs. Ask children to think about what this person has to do in their job. e.g. ‘police officer’ – they have to help people, they have to stop crime, they have to make people feel safer.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Hand out a slip of paper with the name of a job on it to each child. Each slip should be folded in half so they can’t read the job name. Instruct them to stick the piece of paper to themselves without looking. Give instructions: they are going to walk around the classroom and give clues to their classmates about their job using have to: e.g. ‘Nurse’ – You have to take care of people in hospital. If/When children guess their job, they can either sit down or request a new slip of paper.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Form groups of four. One child takes a new slip of paper and reads the word without showing the other group members. Group members then ask questions using have to to try to work out what the job is: e.g. Do you have to work in a school? The child answers accordingly: e.g. No, I don’t have to work in a school. Repeat until the group guesses the job correctly. They then swap roles.
  2. Reassure groups that if they forget the word for a particular job, they can use gestures and mime to elicit the word from their classmates.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Write sentences about what children and other family members have to do at home: e.g. My mum or dad have to mow the lawn once a month. / I have to learn my spellings before playing video games. / My sister has to pick me up from school.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the speaking activity, pre-teach or elicit different job words and write them on the board. Children can then refer to these when making their guesses.
  2. Refer children to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.102 ex.C for additional support.
  3. Reduce or increase the number of different jobs in line with children’s ability.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have practised talking about jobs and duties using have to.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
