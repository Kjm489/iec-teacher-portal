TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Stand in a circle. Throw a ball to a confident child and ask them to name a workplace or a type of shop that we can find in a city. They return the ball. Repeat with others, eliciting as much vocabulary as possible and writing good ideas on the board. How many words can they think of without repeating themselves?
  2. Children return to their seats. Ask: What do you want to learn about in this unit on working in a city? Allow them time to write five things they hope to see or learn about.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.96.
  2. Ask children to look at ex.A. Play the audio (track 2.23) and ask children to listen carefully and repeat. Elicit that all the words are nouns (which will help them with the next bullet point).

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. In pairs, ask children to read the 11 sentences and try to work out the meaning of the bold nouns using context. Check and correct answers as a class.
  2. Ask children to complete ex.B. They check their answers in pairs first, then as a class.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Ask: Did you mention any of these words in the warm-up activity?
  2. Write new vocabulary for shops and places around town on the board. Give children 2 minutes to learn the vocabulary, then cover up the words.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Explain that trying to remember all the words will be a challenge, but testing their memory in the following game will help to extend their understanding of new vocabulary.
  2. Play a game. Demonstrate first with another adult in class or a confident child. First, think of a place (e.g. a greengrocer’s). Say a one-word clue (e.g. ‘vegetables’) and invite a volunteer to say the name of the place. If they guess incorrectly, give another clue. Repeat until they have guessed correctly. They win a bonus point if they can name a type of job at that place (e.g. ‘shop assistant’, ‘greengrocer’, ‘baker’, ‘travel agent’).
  3. Children play the game in small groups, ensuring that each group member has the opportunity to think of a place, give a clue, and make guesses. Encourage guessing group members to support each other with new vocabulary.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete exs.A–D in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.86–87.
  2. Encourage children to make a worksheet for their classmates to test them on new vocabulary. They can choose the activity type (e.g. a wordsearch, anagrams, gap-fill).

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the speaking activity, pre-teach vocabulary for different jobs as well as workplaces. Reduce the number of new words for less able children.
  2. Challenge more confident children to write down as many workplace and job words as they can remember, paying close attention to correct spellings. Can they list common suffixes for different types of job (e.g. ‘–ist’ – florist, chemist; ‘–er’ – baker, teacher, greengrocer)?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned new words for workplaces in a city and practised using them in a team game. They have thought about what kinds of job people have in these workplaces.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
