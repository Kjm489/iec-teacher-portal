TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

Source note: Oxford provides one shared weekly reflection/review plan. IEC has organized it into five teaching sessions; this is an IEC adaptation, not five separate Oxford-authored lessons.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Working in a city’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
  2. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Praise children for their effort in this unit and let them know that it hasn’t gone unnoticed.
  3. Children may keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements. Explain how neurons in the brain form connections when we learn new things. Reviewing what we have learned will make these connections stronger and help us to retain information.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas:

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. With the necessary permissions, invite one or two professionals into the classroom to talk about their job in English. They may be a doctor, a nurse, an engineer, a pilot, a firefighter, a shop assistant, a scientist, and so on. Try to combat gender stereotypes by asking a female doctor or scientist to talk to children and answer their questions about their job. Children have fun in a small-group role-play activity by pretending to work in different jobs in the city. They should create at least one character each, deciding on their personality, their appearance, and their job. They can use props and costumes when performing their role-play to the class at the end of the week. Support them to use language that they have learned in the unit to talk about what they have to do in their jobs and why they like working in a big city.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Children work in pairs to create a poster about a job. Encourage them to use the internet to find pictures of people working in their chosen job and to explore what the job involves on a daily basis. On the poster, they should write a paragraph about what people have to do in this job and why it is interesting. While children work on their poster, prompt them to think about what skills they would need to do the job well, and whether it is a job they think they would enjoy in the future. Children will present their poster to the class at the end of the week.
  2. For both activities, ensure that children work together nicely, listen actively to each other, and plan effectively. They should begin by making a clear plan, deciding on the different stages, and prioritizing the most important aspects to ensure they complete it on time. Praise them for their effort and creativity shown during the week, regardless of the success of the final product.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Mix up abilities in pairwork and group work so peer support can be provided. Monitor children actively to ensure that everybody is able to participate in line with their strengths and interests. Write key topic vocabulary from the unit on the board to remind children.
  2. Challenge more able children to think about effective presentation. What language can they use (including new grammatical forms learned)? How can they maintain the audience’s interest?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have identified the core English language skills they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-life context.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
