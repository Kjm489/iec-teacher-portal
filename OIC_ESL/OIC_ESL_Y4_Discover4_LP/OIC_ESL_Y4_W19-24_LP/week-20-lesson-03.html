TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Say the word ‘city’ several times. Ask children if it is a hard or soft ‘c’ sound. Elicit that it is a soft ‘c’ and discuss other examples (e.g. ‘circle’, ‘cycle’, ‘pencil’, ‘ice’, ‘face’). Write them on the board in one column. Help children to understand that we pronounce the soft ‘c’ (/s/) when it comes before the letters ‘e’, ‘i’, and ‘y’.
  2. Then elicit some hard ‘c’ (/k/) sounds in words. Write correct suggestions on the board in a different column (e.g. ‘cat’, ‘castle’, ‘classroom’, ‘corner’, ‘cup’).
  3. Ask the class: What do you notice about the phrase ‘capital city’?
  4. Refer children to ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.95.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. In pairs, ask children to discuss why they think cities are situated where they are. Encourage them to think back to their learning about water in Week 20, Lesson 2.
  2. Invite volunteers to share their ideas and discuss as a whole class.
  3. Write ‘mountains’, ‘roads’, and ‘food’ on the board. Form groups of four and ask children to consider how these would help a city to increase in size.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Invite volunteers to share their ideas and discuss as a whole class.
  2. Give each group two city fact files, one for each pair within the group. Each fact file should contain key facts about the city and reasons why it grew. Try not to include full sentences.
  3. Instruct children to use the information on their fact files to ask and answer questions. (See Oxford Discover SB 4 p.94 ex.C for a simple conversation template.) For example:

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Pair A: My city is Paris. It’s the capital city of France. It has a population of around 2.1 million people. It grew next to the River Seine.
  2. Pair B: How did the river help it grow?
  3. Pair A: Transportation on the river provided food to eat and water to drink.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Pair B: That’s interesting. My city is …
  2. Invite a volunteer group to present their conversation to the class. Encourage listening children to say what they liked about the exchange (e.g. choice of language, clear explanations, active listening within the group, interesting questions).
  3. Now ask groups to discuss why a big city local to them grew (if it isn’t close to a body of water, perhaps there is a particular industry or university in your local area that encouraged people to move to the city). They can note down their ideas and plan a short presentation. You might need to prompt them with questions: e.g. What jobs can you do in the city? What is the city well known for? Is it close to a body of water or a railway?
  4. Invite one group to present to another group informally, then swap roles. Prompt groups to evaluate each other’s work by writing a checklist of success criteria on the board.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. As a follow-up to the presentation task, write a paragraph about a local city.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Vary the difficulty level of the fact file activity by increasing or limiting the amount of information included on each fact file. Provide less confident children with full sentences and more confident children with brief notes.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have studied hard ‘c’ and soft ‘c’ sounds in words. They have thought critically about why big cities grow, applying this understanding to their local context.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
