TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Give nine children a picture of a planet or the sun. As a class, put children into the correct order from the sun.
  2. Explain that planets move around the sun. Ask children holding pictures of the planets to move around the child holding the picture of the sun. Tell them that Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars move quite quickly; while Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune move much slower. Tell children to act out moving at different speeds.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Online search ‘NASA-solar system overview’ and show children the solar system map.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Turn to the text in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.18–19, or search ‘theschoolrun – solar system’ and look at the ‘Did you know?’ section. (You can print off the latter, edit in line with class ability, and provide as a handout.) Children scan the text and write any words they do not know.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Children work in small groups to try to guess the meaning of the words selected. Ask children to share their ideas with the class. If necessary, clarify the definitions.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Ask children to play a game with the words they have just learned, in small groups. Tell them to put the reading text to one side. In turn, one person gives the rest of the group a definition in their own words, and the rest of the group tries to guess the word. Monitor while children work and make sure that everyone has a go at giving a definition.
  2. Read the text again, then ask children to do the comprehension exercises in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.20.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Work through the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.10–11.
  2. Allow children to explore NASA’s interactive solar system map in more depth.
  3. Create a ‘Solar System’ poster using mixed media (e.g. photos from the internet or drawings). Children should focus on the size and surfaces of different planets and how they orbit the sun. They can use reference books and the internet to research information.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the introductory activity, give children five minutes to look through their notes from Week 1 to help them remember information about the planets.
  2. Challenge more able children to answer more difficult comprehension questions based on the text: e.g. What is the surface of Mars like? How many years on Mercury is one year on Earth?

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned how and why different planets travel around the sun. They have scanned a text for unknown key words and guessed their definition, before practising basic paraphrasing in small groups.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
