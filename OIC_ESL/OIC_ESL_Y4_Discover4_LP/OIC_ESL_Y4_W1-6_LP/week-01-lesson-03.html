PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Write ten sentences on the board – five facts about the universe (e.g. Earth is the third planet from the sun.) and five predictions using will (e.g. We will travel to Mars one day.). In pairs, children discuss which are facts and which are opinions. Check answers as a class.
  2. Ask: What do you notice about the prediction sentences? Are the predictions facts or opinions? Establish that we use ‘will + verb’ to make predictions and that predictions are people’s opinions.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Review making predictions using ‘will + verb’: e.g. I will become an astronaut when I grow up. / We will travel to another galaxy one day. (See Oxford Discover SB 4 p.13.)

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Children work through the activities in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.6. Alternatively, give children several sentences using will for predictions and ask them to circle will and the verb in each. You can either create a pre-prepared worksheet or write them on the board.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Bring the class back together. Write some prompts on the board with a verb and a noun: e.g. ‘become/astronaut’, ‘find/life’. Demonstrate making sentences using the prompts: e.g. Minh will become an astronaut when she grows up. / We will find life on other planets soon. In pairs, children make sentences orally using the prompts.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Ask children to write five predictions about the universe. Encourage them to try and think of things they haven’t already said in the lesson.
  2. In small groups, children say one of their predictions in turn. The rest of the group says if they agree or disagree with their opinion and why. Encourage children to listen to everyone’s point of view and politely say if they agree or disagree, giving reasons for their answers. Explain that engaging in a group debate in this way is designed to stretch children’s thinking and extend their understanding of predictions.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.7.
  2. In groups, make a final list of five predictions that all children agree with.
  3. Write a paragraph on predictions about the universe or the future in general.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the prompt speaking activity, provide children with time phrases, such as ‘soon’, ‘in the future’, ‘by 2050’.
  2. Challenge more able children to use interesting verbs when forming their predictions.
  3. Mix more confident and less confident children together in the small-group activity so peer-support can be provided.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned to make predictions using ‘will + verb’. They have listened to their classmates’ predictions and expressed their agreement or disagreement politely.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 The universe 
Week 1, Lesson 3: Making predictions 
Learning outcomes: 4.2b, 4.2e

Context
This is the third lesson in the unit ‘The universe’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by using prompts to make sentences. They will learn to make predictions and will discuss whether they agree with their peers’ predictions.  
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
