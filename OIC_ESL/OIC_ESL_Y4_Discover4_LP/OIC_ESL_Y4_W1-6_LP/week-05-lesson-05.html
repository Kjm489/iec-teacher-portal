TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children to place their old photos on their desk. If anyone has forgotten a photo, give them one. Show your photo and tell the class where you found it and what/who it shows.
  2. In pairs, children take turns talking about their photos. If it shows a person they know (e.g. a family member), ask children to talk about the positive qualities of that person and what they appreciate about them.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Tell children to look at the descriptive report in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.44 or WB 4 p.37, or show them an example descriptive report. It should include the following sections, labelled so children can see how to organize their report:
  2. Title – e.g. ‘A Photo of my Grandpa as a Young Man’

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Introduction – e.g. I found an old photo of my grandpa in my living room and it taught me about the past.
  2. Body – e.g. In the photo, my grandpa is driving a horse and cart. He looks young and strong. The horse is big and black, and the cart is quite small and made of wood. He loved working with horses and used to be a farmer. We don’t use horses and carts today, but I can almost smell the hay and hear the wheels of the cart on the road!

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Conclusion – e.g. Because of this picture, I learned that my grandpa worked hard.
  2. Ask children to complete ex.B in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.44. Ask comprehension questions: e.g. What is the photo of? Where did the writer find it? Check children’s understanding of any difficult words in the report.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Ask children about the four sections of the report. Do they notice any sensory words in the body section?
  2. Ask children to look at the table in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.37. Alternatively, write ‘Time’, ‘Place’, ‘I see’, ‘I hear’, ‘I smell’, ‘I taste’, ‘I touch’, and ‘I learned’ on the board, and ask children to copy these. Children organize their ideas about their photo under the headings. Encourage children to use a variety of descriptive language.
  3. Children discuss their notes in pairs and offer each other feedback. They can use the checklist in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.37 to help them. Encourage children to say 1–2 positive things and 1–2 ideas for improvement. Explain that they must be kind and helpful when making suggestions – treating each other with kindness may lead to a new friendship.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete ex.C in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.37.
  2. Write first and final drafts of the descriptions and display them around the classroom.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the introductory activity, give children 3–5 minutes to prepare what they want to say about their photo.
  2. For more support with the planning activity, encourage children to use their notes from Week 5, Lesson 4. Write example descriptive words under the headings.
  3. Challenge more able children to write their reports with examples of several different grammatical forms, including verbs followed by infinitives, verbs followed by gerunds, and ‘I’d like to …’.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned how to write a description using sensory language. They have arranged their written text into four sections: Title, Introduction, Body, Conclusion.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
