TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Say: Today we are going to look back at all the things we have learned about the Earth, stars, and the universe. Do you think you have learned a lot? How do you think I will see how much you’ve learned? Encourage children to answer the final question using will.
  2. Tell children that they are going to make their own quizzes for their classmates.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Give children a short quiz about the things they have learned so far in Week 1. Questions might include: How many planets are there? Which is the biggest planet? Which planet is red? Can you name three things we can see in the night sky? Which planets can we see in the night sky? Check answers as a whole class. Remind children of the concept of ‘fire together, wire together’ – recapping a topic in this way enables children to build their long-term memory of this learning.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask children to make a similar quiz about interesting things they have learned. They can use a range of different resources to remind them of their learning: e.g. their class notes, the internet, and visual prompts. They can also look back at Oxford Discover SB 4, pp.8–25, and Oxford Discover WB 4, pp.2–20.
  2. If necessary, review question words.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Allow children 15 minutes to write their questions. Walk around and monitor their work. Are their questions appropriate and answerable? Encourage children to read their quiz to themselves to check that they have used question forms correctly.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Children walk around the classroom with their quizzes and ask and answer each other’s quiz questions. Encourage children to use full sentences in their responses.
  2. While children are speaking, choose five of the best questions to test the whole class at the end of the lesson.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Research more information about the Earth and the universe using different resources. Ask children to write down what they would still like to know about the universe that they haven’t yet learned.
  2. Write five short diary entries about this week’s lessons: e.g. Lesson 1 – Today, I learned lots of information about planets. My favourite fact was … I found it really surprising and interesting!

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the writing activity, remind children of each lesson to help them remember what they have learned this week. Write a list of topic vocabulary on the board.
  2. Challenge more able children to use a variety of question words.
  3. Consider how you group children in this lesson: A mix of abilities will enable more able children to support and guide less able children through the task, in turn consolidating their understanding of topic language. However, grouping more able children together will help to stretch their thinking and allow the teacher to offer additional support to less able children.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have revised their learning this week by practising asking and answering questions about the universe.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
