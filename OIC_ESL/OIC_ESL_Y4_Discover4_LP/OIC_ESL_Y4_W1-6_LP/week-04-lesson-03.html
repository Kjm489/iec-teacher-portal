TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask: Why do we write things? Create a whole-class mind map on the board.
  2. Then write on the board: 1. ‘A paragraph arguing that tennis is the best sport’, 2. ‘A paragraph about the history of yoga’, and 3. ‘A story about an adventure’. Ask: What do you think are the purposes of these three texts? Elicit answers.
  3. Explain that we write to either persuade (1), inform (2), or entertain (3). Direct children to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.29.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain to children that they are going to read about a great discovery. Ask: What kind of discovery would you like to read about? Elicit answers and discuss.
  2. Look at the text in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.30–31. Write keywords on the board that children are unlikely to know (e.g. ‘terracotta’, ‘generals’, ‘precious’). Ask children to think about the meaning of these words while reading independently. Then read the text as a class. In pairs, children discuss what they think the unfamiliar words mean. Encourage more able children to help less able children understand the words by providing simple explanations. Check the meanings as a class.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Read the text again and ask: What is the author’s purpose? Elicit that it is to inform.
  2. Children read the text again and, in pairs, do the activities in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.32. Alternatively, ask questions about the main information in the text. For example:

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. What is the main idea of the text?
  2. Why did the emperor bury himself with the statues?

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Encourage pairs to discuss the answers and explain why they think certain answers are correct.
  2. Ask: Would you like to visit the emperor’s tomb/a temple in Egypt? Why (not)?

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.23–24.
  2. Make a poster about the emperor’s tomb or hieroglyphics using mixed media (e.g. pictures from the internet or drawings). Encourage children to use reference books and the internet to research information about the topic and write captions.
  3. Write an informative paragraph about a famous discovery.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the reading activity, provide a list of difficult words and a list of synonyms or definitions in children’s first language. Ask them to match the words to the definitions.
  2. Challenge more able children by asking them what the difficult words mean before reading the text.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have understood the purpose of different texts and identified the main information in a reading text about a great discovery. They have answered comprehension questions about what they read.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
