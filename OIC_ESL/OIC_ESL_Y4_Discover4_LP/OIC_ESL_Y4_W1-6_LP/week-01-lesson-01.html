TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Welcome children to their first Year 4 lesson. Explain that today they are going to learn about where they are in the universe. Ask: What is the ‘universe’?
  2. Now ask them where they are right now and elicit answers. Then ask: What is just outside of the classroom? (e.g. the school, the playground, the corridor)
  3. Children work in small groups to think of as many things a little further than the previous place. Award one point for each thing that is further than the last. Encourage children to take turns speaking and listening to each other’s ideas. How far can they go?
  4. Children share their ideas with the class and try to make the longest list they can.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. In turn, show children pictures of the eight planets in the solar system and ask if anyone knows their names. Repeat the names as a class, focusing on their pronunciation of difficult vowel and consonant sounds: e.g. Ju-pi-ter /ˈdʒuːpɪtə(r)/.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Discuss what children already know about the planets and how we can find out more about space science in museums. Turn to the activities in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.16.
  2. Stick pictures of the planets and the sun on the board, then ask children to describe each one. Ask: Which is the biggest? Which is closest to Earth? Which is your favourite and why? Encourage a sense of wonder by explaining how incredible our solar system is – it can be difficult to comprehend its size!

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. See if children can help you to label each picture. As you add each label, drill the name of the planet and correct children’s pronunciation if necessary. Praise children for their effort in pronouncing the difficult vowel and consonant sounds.
  2. In small groups, children discuss the order of the planets from closest to furthest away from the sun and create a final list. Ask each group to share their list and compare results, then go over the correct answers. Invite a volunteer to move the planets into the correct order.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. You can challenge more able classes by adding a list of distances between each planet and the sun to the board, or dictate each distance in turn (online search ‘Planet Facts – order of the planets’). You will need to support them with the large numbers in the millions. As a class, add the distances to the correct planets to complete the solar system map. Check answers and show the class a visual of the largest and smallest planets from the ‘Planet Facts’ article.
  2. Introduce a small-group guessing game. Children take turns describing a planet for the rest of the group to guess. If the group answers correctly, a new speaker is chosen. If the group answers incorrectly, the speaker says one more description. Continue until all children have had the opportunity to be the speaker. Remind children to listen to each other’s ideas respectfully.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.2–3.
  2. Make a fact file about a planet using mixed media (e.g. photos from the internet or drawings). Children can use reference books and the internet to find interesting facts.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the speaking activity, write model descriptive sentences on the board: e.g. This planet is red. This planet is the furthest away from the sun.
  2. More able children can practise pronouncing the planet names with their books closed.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have learned about their place in the universe. They have worked together to order the planets in our solar system, focusing on the pronunciation of difficult vowel/consonant sounds.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 4 The universe
Week 1, Lesson 1: Where are we in the universe? 
Learning outcomes: 4.2c, 4.2e

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘The universe’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills in class and small-group activities, practising difficult vowel and consonant sounds. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
