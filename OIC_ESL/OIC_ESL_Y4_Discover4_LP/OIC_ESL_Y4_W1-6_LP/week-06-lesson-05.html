Original learning outcomes: Unit summary

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

Source note: Oxford provides one shared weekly reflection/review plan. IEC has organized it into five teaching sessions; this is an IEC adaptation, not five separate Oxford-authored lessons.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Understanding the past’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
  2. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Praise children for trying hard in this unit and let them know that their efforts have not gone unnoticed.
  3. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements. Explain how neurons in the brain form connections when we learn new things, so reviewing what we learn will make these connections stronger and help us to remember things better.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Children work in small groups to create a time capsule. Groups choose a container (e.g. a decorated shoe box) and place two items each in the container. The items should be things that are important to them, or to science. Children write a short paragraph to explain why each item was chosen. They present their time capsule to the class at the end of the week.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. In pairs, children choose a real-life discovery (e.g. dinosaur bones, cave paintings, buried treasure, hieroglyphics, an Aztec or Mayan temple, the Terracotta Army) and create a poster or leaflet. First, they write questions they want to find answers to: e.g. When was it discovered? Who discovered it? How did people use to live in this time period? Where is it now? After, they research their answers using a variety of resources. Children can also consult Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.26–45. They orally present their poster or leaflet to the class at the end of the week. They need to be prepared with facts to answer questions at the end of their presentation.
  2. For both activities, ensure children work together nicely, listen to each other, and plan effectively. Remind groups to begin their activity by making a clear plan and prioritizing tasks to ensure that everything is completed in time.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Work through the activities in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.21–39 if not yet completed.

Oxford differentiation

  1. Challenge more able children by putting them in groups with less confident children. Tell them that they should help everyone in their group understand the project. They could also take the lead in the planning process.
  2. Allow less confident children to identify and work to their strengths – they might be an excellent illustrator, or they might have advanced IT skills which they can use for researching information. They don’t need to excel at English to participate in a meaningful way.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have identified the core English language skills they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
