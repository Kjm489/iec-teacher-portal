PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Remind children that they looked at countable and uncountable nouns when talking about quantities in Week 2, Lesson 4: How much …? (e.g. sunlight); How many …? (e.g. stars). What can they remember? Say: By thinking again about countable and uncountable nouns, you are helping to build your long-term memory of this learning.
  2. If necessary, teach the grammar using Oxford Discover SB 4 p.43.
  3. Show children a range of different items on your desk. Write ‘countable’ and ‘uncountable’ on two desks at the front of the classroom. In small groups, children look at the items and decide if they are countable or uncountable. Encourage children to take turns speaking and to listen actively to everyone’s ideas.
  4. Check answers as a class. After an item is confirmed to be countable or uncountable, a volunteer moves it to the correct desk at the front of the classroom.
  5. In pairs, children make sentences using the items: e.g. I can see some flour. There are four apples.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain to children that they are going to talk about their different senses.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Write ‘hear’, ‘feel’/‘touch’, ‘smell’, ‘taste’, and ‘see’/’look’ on the board. Check children’s understanding of the different sensory words through mime. Under ‘hear’, write ‘loud sound’. Ask the class what other adjectives could go with ‘sound’. Elicit answers and write them on the board. Repeat this with the other sensory words.
  2. Ask children to work in pairs to describe some of the items on your desk using sensory words: e.g. Apples taste sweet. The marble feels smooth.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Then challenge children to apply the sensory words to the unit ‘Understanding the past’ using new topic vocabulary learned. They can make silly or sensible sentences: e.g. A dinosaur probably smelled terrible. / The buried treasure feels dusty. / You shouldn’t taste an archaeologist’s sandy boot!
  2. Now practise asking questions using How do…? and How does…? (e.g. How does it smell?).

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Introduce a guessing game. Say: I am thinking about an object. You have to ask me questions using the sensory words we learned today to find out what I’m thinking about. Children ask questions and then guess what the object is.
  2. In small groups, children continue to play the guessing game. Encourage them to use different objects that they have learned about in the unit. Remind them to listen carefully to each other so they don’t repeat a question, and to take turns.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Work through the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.36.
  2. Write a paragraph describing an object using the sensory words from the lesson.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the speaking activity, you can write a range of different vocabulary and structures on the board which children can use to ask and answer questions.
  2. You can vary the difficulty level of the guessing game by choosing a variety of different items. Make it easier by giving parameters: e.g. It must be a word from the unit or a word beginning with ‘S’. Make it more difficult by using any word children know in English.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have revised countable and uncountable nouns. They have learned to describe things using different sensory words and adjectives.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Week 5, Lesson 4: Using your senses 
Learning outcomes: 4.2b, 4.2e

Context
This is the ninth lesson in the unit ‘Understanding the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by sorting countable and uncountable items and talking about different senses.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
