Original Oxford lesson: Why is the sun important?

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children that they are going to play a game. When the lights are on, it is daytime; when the lights are off, it is night-time. In the daytime, children need to write as much information about the sun as they can. They can also list words that they associate with the sun (e.g. ‘bright’, ‘hot’, ‘sunny’, ‘sunburn’, ‘boiling’, ‘yellow’). At night, they have to pretend to sleep. Begin the activity with the lights turned off, then turn them on and instruct children to start writing. Make the game fun by turning the lights on and off in short bursts!
  2. Encourage children to share their facts and words with each other in small groups.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Show children the picture in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.18, or a sourced picture of the solar system. Ask: What is in the middle of our solar system? What planets can you see? Can you remember their names? Why do you think the planets move around the sun?
  2. Ask: Why do you think the sun is so important? Elicit answers and write them on the board (e.g. it is a source of heat and light, it keeps us warm, it helps plants to grow, provides vitamin D).

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Tell the class they are going to read about the sun. Search online ‘sciencetrek – sun facts’ and invite a volunteer to read the title and first two sentences clearly: ‘What would happen if there was no Sun? Nothing! The sun is the source of heat, light, and life on Earth.’ If you would prefer to use the text in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.18–19, you can copy the format of this exercise using language from ‘Traveling Together around the Sun’.
  2. In pairs, children discuss and write down what other information they think they will read about, and what questions they think will be answered.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Write ‘source’, ‘climate’, ‘ancient’, ‘centre’ (‘center’), ‘scientist’, ‘telescope’, and ‘revolve’ on the board. Ask children to try and work out what these words mean while they read.
  2. Read the text as a class (up to ‘travel in paths around the Sun’). Whose initial predictions were correct? Discuss the words on the board and elicit their definitions, asking children to explain how they arrived at their answer (context, co-text, word category). Explain unknown words.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Write ‘climate’, ‘plants’, and ‘people’ on the board. Discuss how the sun affects each of these things. Ask children to think about the effect of the sun on our hydration. Ask: What happens if we stay out in the sun and don’t drink any water? Is it good or bad for us? Discuss answers.
  2. Ask: Is there anything else about the sun that you want to learn? Encourage children to research the answers using reference books and/or the internet.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Write five or more sentences about why the sun is important.
  2. Make a poster about the importance of the sun using mixed media (e.g. photos from the internet or drawings). Children can use reference books and the internet to research information.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the speaking activity, spend more time talking about the first two sentences in the text. Give hints as to what might appear later in the text.
  2. Encourage more able children to predict what they are going to read based on the title of the text, before they read the first two sentences. They can summarize what they have learned from the text in their own words.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have considered the importance of the sun and have predicted the content of a reading text. They have tried to define unknown topic vocabulary using simple strategies.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
