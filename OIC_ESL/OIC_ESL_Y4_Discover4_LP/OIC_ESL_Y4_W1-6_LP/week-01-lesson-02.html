TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Divide the class into small groups. Groups discuss what the word ‘home’ means to them. Elicit answers and write them on the board.
  2. Explain that ‘home’ can mean a lot of different things to different people. Introduce the idea that ‘home’ can mean more than just a house or where we sleep – it is where we feel that we belong (e.g. with family, friends, and other community members). Ask: What does home feel like to you? The aim is to elicit answers like ‘warm’, ‘happy’, ‘cosy’, ‘safe’.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Show children a photo of the Earth seen from space to spark their interest and excitement.
  2. Ask children how the photo makes them feel. What can they see? Can they see their country?

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Tell children they are going to read about someone looking at the Earth and stars from space. In pairs, children write down five things they think they will read about.
  2. Turn to the reading text ‘Bella’s Home’ in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.10–11.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Read the text first as a whole class, highlighting any unknown topic vocabulary and explaining their meaning. Discuss if children’s predictions were correct.
  2. Ask children to read the text again, independently or in pairs, and do the comprehension exercises in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.12.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. In pairs, children are going to pretend to be astronauts looking at the Earth from space. Show them the photo again. Ask children to take turns reporting to their partner what the Earth looks like. Encourage them to use the reading text and their own ideas to describe the photo as much as they can, including how it makes them feel. They can be as creative as they like!
  2. Invite a few pairs to perform their role-play in front of the class. Tell them to take a few deep breaths before they begin – this will help them to focus and ease their nerves.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.4–5.
  2. Create a ‘Life on a space station’ poster using mixed media (e.g. photos from magazines/the internet or drawings). Children can research how astronauts live on space stations and what the Earth looks like from space.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For the reading activity, less able children can work in small groups and be guided by an adult. You can choose to focus on just one or two paragraphs, or a few sentences.
  2. For more support with the role-play, write model sentences on the board: e.g. I can see swirls of clouds. It makes me feel very small.
  3. Challenge more able children to describe what else they can see from space, such as other planets, stars, and galaxies. They can write their own paragraph, similar to ‘An astronaut’s view’.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have discussed what ‘home’ means to them and read a text about space for gist and for specific information. They have participated in a role-play in pairs.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
