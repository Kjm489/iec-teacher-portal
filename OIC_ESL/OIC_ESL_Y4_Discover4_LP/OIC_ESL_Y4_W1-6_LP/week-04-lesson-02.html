PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask: If you found a wallet or purse on the ground, what would you do with it? Elicit answers and discuss what most of the class thinks is the right thing to do. Ask: What if you found a football? Would you do the same thing?
  2. Explain that things have different ‘values’ but we should always be kind and helpful. Ask: Why do you think people are kind to each other? Discuss as a class.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Children look at the pictures in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.28. Alternatively, show children some pictures to represent the key words ‘armour’, ‘army’, ‘clay’, ‘emperor’, ‘jade’, ‘peasant’, ‘treasure’, ‘uniforms’. Discuss what the pictures show, then practise pronouncing each word.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask questions about the different items to check understanding: e.g. Is clay worth a lot of money? What did an emperor do?
  2. Divide the class into small groups. Give each group a set of pictures and ask them to work together to put the pictures into different categories of their choosing. Discuss groups’ ideas as a class – did two or more groups have a similar category?

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Ask follow-up questions: e.g. When did people wear armour? What can you make from clay?
  2. Then ask: Where do you think people have found old armour or treasure? (tombs, ruins, battlefields, in the ground) Discuss the answers first in pairs, then as a class.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Ask: If somebody finds something important, something that we can all learn from, who should it belong to? Suggest a few options and see how children respond: the country of origin, in a museum, an archaeologist’s personal collection at home. This might spark another discussion about right and wrong.
  2. Write ‘I’d like to find …’ on the board. Tell children to pretend to be archaeologists and say what they would like to find using the structure on the board. They can also draw pictures to support their thinking. Invite some volunteers to share their ideas with the whole class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Work through the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.21–22.
  2. Write a short story about an interesting discovery. It can be either true or invented and it must explain what it was, how children felt when they found it, and what they did with it.
  3. Write a paragraph, from the perspective of an archaeologist, saying what they would like to find/discover and why.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with some of the questions, write key vocabulary on the board to help children think of ideas.
  2. Mix less confident and more confident children in the group activity so peer-support can be provided.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have explored different things discovered from the past and have talked about their value and to whom they belong.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Week 4, Lesson 2: Finding things from the past 
Learning outcomes: 4.2b, 4.2c

Context
This is the second lesson in the unit ‘Understanding the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by discussing the importance of different items from the past and who they belong to.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
