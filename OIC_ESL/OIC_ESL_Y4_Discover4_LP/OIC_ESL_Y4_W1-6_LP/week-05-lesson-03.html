TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Say: Cooks cook cupcakes quickly. Ask the class to try and repeat it. Repeat several times and increase the speed if they can say it easily. Write three more ‘tongue twisters’ on the board:
  2. Six sleek swans swam swiftly southwards.
  3. Give papa a cup of proper coffee in a proper coffee cup.
  4. I saw Susie sitting in a shoeshine shop, where she shines, she sits, and where she sits, she shines.
  5. In pairs, children practise saying them. Encourage children to try and say them as quickly as possible and to not worry about making mistakes. Invite a few children to demonstrate saying their favourite tongue twister in front of the class. Ask: How does it feel to say something so difficult?

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. On the board, write a sentence that has a verb followed by a gerund: e.g. I enjoy finding fossils. Ask: What is the verb in this sentence? What is the gerund in this sentence?

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Ask: Can you think of any other verbs that could replace ‘enjoy’? Elicit answers and write them on the board: e.g. I love finding fossils.
  2. Pre-teach or revise verbs followed by gerunds using your own examples or Oxford Discover SB 4 p.41.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Online search ‘yac-uk – best and worst archaeology’ and project the archaeologist interview on the board, or give each child a handout. Read the interview as a class.
  2. Write ‘likes/enjoys’ and ‘dislikes/doesn’t enjoy’ on the board. Ask children to read the article again and, in pairs, write the things Julian likes and doesn’t like about being an archaeologist, using the ‘verb + gerund’ construction: e.g. Julian likes digging up clues. He doesn’t enjoy dealing with the weather. Children take turns saying their sentences to each other, then share their sentences with the class.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Ask children to reflect on and discuss what they would like or not like about a different job. Encourage children to think about their strengths and weaknesses, and how this would have an impact on their enjoyment of different aspects of the job.
  2. In pairs, children discuss the jobs they have chosen and their likes and dislikes. Remind them to give reasons for their decisions.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.33–34, or provide a worksheet on gerunds.
  2. Make a poster about being an archaeologist using mixed media (e.g. photos from the internet or their own drawings). It should say what they like and dislike about the job. Children can use reference books, careers brochures, and the internet to learn more about archaeology.
  3. Write a paragraph about likes and dislikes using the ‘verb + gerund’ construction.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the tongue twister activity, practise saying all the words very slowly as a class, or begin with single words and then add one word at a time.
  2. Challenge more able children by asking them to create their own tongue twisters and test them on their friends.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have read a short interview with an archaeologist to lead into a discussion about their own likes and dislikes, and future jobs they would enjoy. They have learned and practised using the construction ‘verb + gerund’ to express their opinions.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Understanding the past 
Week 5, Lesson 3: What do archaeologists like doing?
Learning outcomes: 4.2b, 4.2c

Context
This is the eighth lesson in the unit ‘Understanding the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by expressing their likes and dislikes using the ‘verb + gerund’ construction.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
