PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Divide the class into teams. Ask them to think about what we can see in the sky in the daytime and at night. Encourage teams to work together to find the best way to complete the activity. For example, they could divide the team into two groups: a ‘daytime’ group and a ‘night-time’ group, or they could mind map their ideas altogether with a nominated scribe.
  2. Write the teams’ suggestions on the board and compare each other’s answers. This should lead into a wider discussion. Ask: What can we see in the daytime and at night?
  3. Ask children to comment on their teamwork – what worked well during the activity? What could they improve on for next time?

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Show children a photo or a short video clip of the night sky. Ask: What can you see in this photo/video? How does the photo/video make you feel?

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.14. Play track 1.05. Ask children to listen to the audio and note down what they hear. Then play the audio once more. Share the main information from the audio first in pairs, then as a class.
  2. Children listen again and do exs.A–B on p.14.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Ask children to look at the two photos in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.14, or show them two photos of the night sky to compare and contrast. Ask: What’s different? What’s the same? In pairs, children discuss what they can see in each picture: e.g. In the first picture, I can see … The second picture doesn’t have …, but …

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Remind children to help their partner if they are struggling to identify similarities and differences.
  2. Ask children to look at the night sky tonight and note down what they see to feed back to the class.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Create a ‘What I can see in the night sky’ poster using mixed media (e.g. photos from magazines/the internet or drawings).
  2. Ask children to close their books and cover up their notes and list as many words as they can remember related to the night sky and our solar system.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the speaking activity, write example sentences and a range of topic vocabulary on the board: e.g. I can see … I can’t see …
  2. Pair up more able and less able children in the speaking activity so peer-support can be provided.
  3. Challenge more able children to use their notes to summarize the information from the listening text in their own words.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have listened to a text about the night sky and noted down the main information.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
