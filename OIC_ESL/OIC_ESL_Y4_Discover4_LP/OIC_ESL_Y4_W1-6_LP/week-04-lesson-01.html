Original learning outcomes: 4.1e, 4.1f

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Tell children that in today’s lesson you want everyone to carry out a ‘random act of kindness’. This can be helping to tuck someone’s chair in, explaining a difficult word, or picking up someone’s pencil from the floor.
  2. Explain to children that they are going to talk about things that happened a long time ago.
  3. Ask: What do you think life was like 500 years ago? How did people travel from place to place? How did people learn new things? Did people have any of the hobbies you have now? Elicit answers and discuss them. Ask: How do we know what life was like?

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Ask children to look at the pictures in Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.26–27 to generate interest in the topic.
  2. Ask: What can you see in the pictures? Then tell children they are going to watch a video about the pictures. Ask: What do you think you will hear?

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Play the video in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.27. Children watch and complete ex.B.
  2. Ask: Why do we want to learn about the past? Write children’s suggestions on the board. See if children highlight that it helps us to improve things or teaches us to do something differently, and it helps us to appreciate things people have done for us.

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Pre-teach or revise the suffix ‘–ist’ using Oxford Discover SB 4 p.35, or write examples on the board: e.g. ‘dentist’, ‘florist’, ‘cyclist’. See if children can explain what these ‘–ist’ nouns indicate.
  2. Ask: Whose job is it to research and learn about the past? Write ‘archaeologist’, ‘anthropologist’, ‘astrologist’, ‘paleontologist’, and ‘scientist’ on the board and explain what these jobs are if children don’t volunteer answers. All of these jobs involve uncovering the past and learning from past events.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Ask: Why do you want to learn about the past? What do you want to learn more about? In pairs, children write their answers and questions they would like to know the answer to, then feed back to the class.
  2. To conclude today’s lesson, discuss what random acts of kindness children have done. Elicit how easy it was and how good it made everyone feel! Encourage children to do this in their daily lives to spread positive feeling.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Make a poster or fact file about one of the ‘–ist’ jobs introduced, using mixed media (photos from the internet or drawings). Children can use reference books, careers brochures, and the internet to research their chosen job, as well as interesting discoveries.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the listening activity, pre-teach the words ‘dinosaur’, ‘king’, ‘queen’, and ‘museum’.
  2. Encourage more able children to think about some of the great discoveries made by any of the people they learned about: e.g. scientist – They discovered a cure for …

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have considered how we know things that happened a long time ago and learned different job titles with the suffix ‘–ist’.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

YEAR 4 Understanding the past
Week 4, Lesson 1: How do we know what happened a long time ago? 
Learning outcomes: 4.1e, 4.1f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Understanding the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video to introduce the topic. They will develop an understanding of how we learn about the past.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
