PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Divide the class into small groups and give each group a set of pictures. Ask children to match the pictures: what people used to do/use in the past to what we do/use now. Check answers as a whole class. Remind children that if they don’t know the correct answer, they can always ask another member of their group to help them.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. On the board, write structures that children can use to compare the past and the present:
  2. People couldn’t … Now, we can …

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. People used to … They didn’t use to …
  2. Long ago people … Now, we …
  3. Demonstrate using the structures to make sentences about the pictures: e.g. People used to play traditional games like ‘conkers’. Now we can play video games. Highlight that ‘used to play’ is an example of a verb followed by an infinitive (see Week 4, Lesson 4).

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. In pairs, children take turns saying sentences to compare the past and the present.
  2. Tell the class they are going to listen to someone speaking about how people lived in the past and how people live now. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 4 p.34. Play the audio, then ask children to complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.34. Then challenge them to rewrite sentences 1, 2, and 3 in ex.A using used to.
  3. Check answers as a class. Explain to children that they shouldn’t dwell on the questions that they got wrong, but feel proud of the questions that they got right.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. In pairs, children discuss the questions: What changes do you think are the most important? Is there anything people did that you wish we still did today?
  2. Pretend you have a time machine. Say: Here is my time machine. It can go to any place and any time in the past! I will let you use it, but you need to give me a good reason for going. Give an example of where you would like to go and why: e.g. I would like to travel to England in the 1600s. I would watch a Shakespeare play.
  3. In pairs, children discuss where they would like to go and why. Ask volunteers to share their ideas with the class. They can pretend to travel back in time using your time machine!

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.28.
  2. Write a paragraph about travelling back in time in a time machine. Describe what the people from that time period are doing that is different to the present day.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the speaking activity, provide children with some historical context.
  2. Challenge more able children by asking them to rank the changes between the past and the present, and give reasons: e.g. I think getting water from taps (faucets) is the most important change because it is much healthier.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have compared how people lived in the past and how we live now. They have listened to a text to extract information about the past, and discussed important changes.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.

Context
This is the fifth lesson in the unit ‘Understanding the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and listening skills by exploring how people used to live in the past and how it is different from the present day. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
