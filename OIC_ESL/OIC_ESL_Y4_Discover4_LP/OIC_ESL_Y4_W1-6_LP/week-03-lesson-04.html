Original learning outcomes: Unit summary

TESOL learning goal: Students will demonstrate the specified OIC outcomes through the original Oxford activity sequence, first with modeling and support, then independently.

Source note: Oxford provides one shared weekly reflection/review plan. IEC has organized it into five teaching sessions; this is an IEC adaptation, not five separate Oxford-authored lessons.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford introductory activity (original order)

  1. Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘The universe’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
  2. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.
  3. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

Teacher ICQ: Are you discussing the opening question with a partner/group before reporting back?

Scaffolding: Elicit prior knowledge, provide relevant visuals or word cues, and model one short response.

Oxford main activity — teacher demonstration and input

  1. Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

TESOL teacher modeling: Demonstrate the language or skill in the first Oxford step with a think-aloud, accurate pronunciation, and one worked response.

Oxford main activity — guided practice

  1. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

TESOL ICQ: Will we complete this part together before trying it ourselves?

TESOL CCQ: Which word, picture, example, or detail tells us that our answer fits?

Oxford main activity — collaborative practice

  1. Children work in small groups to create a model of our solar system. They need to decide how to make each part (e.g. paper, papier-mâché, foam balls for planets). They write a short presentation, focusing on one of the areas they learned about during the unit, such as names of planets, the distance between each planet, traveling between each planet, and the length of days. Children can consult Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.6–25. They can also research additional information to make more accurate models and gather interesting and awe-inspiring facts. Groups present to the class at the end of the week.

TESOL scaffold: Provide a vocabulary bank or sentence starter; check both partners take turns, then gradually remove prompts.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Oxford main activity — increasing independence

  1. Children work in small groups to create a ‘My solar system’ poster. They share creative ideas for their own imaginary solar system and its name, write descriptions about each planet, discuss how it compares to our solar system, and illustrate it with brightly coloured photos and drawings. Children can consult Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.6–25. They can also research additional information about other solar systems to inspire their own. They write a short presentation to give to the class at the end of the week.
  2. For both activities, ensure children work together nicely, actively listen to each other, and plan effectively. Each group should assign a leader who gives roles and delegates responsibilities in line with their group members’ strengths.

Independent evidence: Each student completes an individual oral, written, reading, or practical response linked to the original outcome; record whether the response was independent or prompted.

Oxford additional tasks

  1. Work through the activities in Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.2–18 if not yet completed.
  2. Write a diary entry talking about how well their project went.

Oxford differentiation

  1. For more support with the projects, give groups lists of different topic areas which they can discuss in their presentation: e.g. descriptions of the planets’ surfaces and their atmospheres.
  2. Challenge more able children to plan and perform longer sections within either presentation.
  3. Encourage less confident children to assume the role of project leader, explaining that stepping outside of their comfort zone will make them realize what they are capable of.

IEC TESOL adaptation: Provide targeted support to learners who need it and an optional deeper challenge for learners who are ready. Keep the original Oxford task and outcomes central.

Oxford learning review

  1. Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Exit check: Ask each learner to demonstrate one lesson-specific target independently; note secure / developing / needs reteaching.
