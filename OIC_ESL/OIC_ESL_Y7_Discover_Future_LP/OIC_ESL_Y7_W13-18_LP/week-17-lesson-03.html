Introductory activity

Ask the class: How do you remember past trips and what you did on them? Most students will probably mention taking photographs and videos, but others might volunteer writing a travel diary or a travel blog. Make the most of this opportunity to introduce the lesson’s main activity, or make the suggestion yourself.

Elicit from students the features of a diary entry – a date, possible heading and greeting (e.g. ‘Dear diary …’), written in the first person, a variety of tenses, a description of events, and a reflection on thoughts and feelings.

Main activity

Students are going to write three diary entries describing a real-life or imaginary trip.

Write on the board: ‘Day 1’, ‘Day 2’, ‘Day 3’, ‘Day 4’, and ‘Day 5’.

In pairs, ask students to think of a few ideas or activities that they could allocate to each day. Discuss as a class.

Model the use of descriptive language in a short diary entry. For example:

Day 1: We arrived late and were feeling exhausted. We were looking forward to going to bed. However, the hotel was terrible! There were too many noisy guests playing loud music and running through the hallway. Then there was no hot water. It was a disaster! I hope tomorrow will be better.

Ask students to identify the descriptive language and the different tenses. Compile a whole-class list of interesting adjectives that could be used in the context of travel.

Then write ‘memorable’ on the board. Elicit from students what can make a journey memorable. Explain that it can sometimes be negative. For example, say:

On our first night in Paris, it was pouring with rain and very windy. It was miserable, but the restaurant had delicious food. I ordered seafood and we enjoyed our night.

In pairs, students share with each other memorable experiences from their own trips.

Allow students enough time to write their three diary entries, covering a three-day trip. For each entry, tell them to write no more than 50 words.

In pairs, students exchange diary entries and read each other’s work. Encourage them to say one thing they really liked about their partner’s writing, and one thing they could do to make it better. Ask them to focus on:

Descriptive language

Accurate spelling and punctuation

At the feedback stage, tell pairs to discuss any mistakes and help their partner to understand why it is a mistake in a tactful way.

If time, invite a few volunteers to read one of their diary entries to the class.

Additional task

Based on their partner’s feedback and additional teacher recommendations, students can revise their work and write a final draft of their three diary entries.

Learning review

Students have written three diary entries about a real-life or imaginary travel experience. In pairs, they have peer-reviewed each other’s work in an encouraging way.

For less confident students, provide a handout of interesting adjectives.
