3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about travel planning. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to travel planning and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows destination, purpose and impact?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Challenge students to name the different types of pollution caused and its effects. In pairs, they copy down the table and complete it with their own ideas. In their discussions, encourage them to use phrases and fillers to help them to navigate difficult topic vocabulary and concepts, and to give them additional thinking time. For instance, ‘um’, ‘you know …’, ‘well …’, ‘actually, I think …’, ‘I mean to say …’, ‘… or something like that’.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: As students work, ask them to focus on how they feel when they think of the negative effects of pollution, and then how they feel when they think of positive ways to combat it. Explain that thinking positively about difficult situations has a very good effect on our body because it helps us to minimize stress and worry.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

Encourage students to consider how their actions to reduce pollution will affect their local area. Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of travel planning, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, ask them to make short lists and to use simple adjectives that describe their feelings.

Oxford differentiation: As well as ‘carbon footprint’, teach more scientific terminology to more confident students, like ‘greenhouse gases’, ‘climate change’, and ‘global warming’.

Oxford additional task: Research the effects a decrease in our carbon footprint would have on the planet.

Oxford additional task: Make a whole-class pledge to reduce your carbon footprint by agreeing on a few small and realistic actions within your context.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

YEAR 7 Travel
Week 16, Lesson 2: Our carbon footprint
Learning outcomes: 7.2a, 7.2d

Context
This is the seventh lesson in the unit ‘Travel’. 
In this lesson, students will develop their speaking skills by learning about what a carbon footprint is and by understanding how their actions can affect the world on a local and global scale. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
