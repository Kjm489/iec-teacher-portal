4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to travel planning and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows destination, purpose and impact?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Ask: What other phrasal verbs do you know? Write them on the board.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Form new pairs of students. Tell them to write a short travel blog post using the verbs from the board and a few other phrasal verbs, if possible. Remind them to think about their audience (e.g. other students, travel enthusiasts) and how they can adapt their writing style to capture their interest (e.g. informal, friendly tone yet informative; rhetorical questions; descriptive language).

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Make available travel magazines for students to source a suitable picture. Otherwise, they can illustrate their blog post themselves or look for pictures online.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining destination, purpose and impact in the context of travel planning. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Is an itinerary a list of planned activities or a description of a past trip?

Oxford main activity — independent application: This is a challenging activity – not only do students have to come up with an original idea and think carefully about their language choices and tone, but they have to incorporate recently learned phrasal verbs. Remind them that they are in the stretch zone. As students work, establish if they are finding the activity challenging. Explain that hard work and stretching their thinking isn’t always pleasurable, but it is meaningful. It will extend their understanding of how to make their writing engaging.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Invite pairs to read their blog post aloud to the class. Ask the rest of the class to listen carefully and to raise their hand every time they hear one of the phrasal verbs.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Hold a whole-class feedback session. Ask students if 1) the activity helped them to improve their understanding of grammar and vocabulary, 2) the activity made them think more carefully about their language choices and tone of voice.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of travel planning, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Allow less confident students to refer to a dictionary, a thesaurus, or their coursebooks.

Oxford differentiation: Ask fast finishers to research five to ten more phrasal verbs, ideally related to travel.

Oxford additional task: If your school has an online platform, encourage students to write up their blog post on the computer and upload it for other members of the school community to read, enjoy, and offer constructive feedback.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.3–5 in ODF WB 2 p.46.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about travel planning and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain destination, purpose and impact without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have written a travel blog post in pairs. In doing so, they have reviewed known grammar and vocabulary structures, understood their target audience and adapted their language to fit, and practised using travel-related phrasal verbs.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
