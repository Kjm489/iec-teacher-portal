Introductory activity

Help students to understand that learning never stops. Say: Although there were some problems when I was at school and I did find a few subjects difficult, I enjoyed learning. Even as an adult and as your teacher, I have continued to learn as I get older, have new experiences, and meet new people – including all of you! For me, learning every day helps me to grow as a person, understand how things work, and understand other people.

Elicit from students what education means to them. Prompt them with questions: e.g. What is the purpose of education? How does it make you feel when you learn something new? How does it feel when you finally understand something? How would your life be different if you had no education?

Main activity

Introduce the task: students are going to write a blog post about what education means to them. Encourage them to think back to the introductory activity.

Give students a graphic organizer handout, or write the following headings on the board:

Title – ‘What education means to me’

Introduction – Explain what the blog post will be about – a reflection on the importance of education in your life – and provide some context (e.g. where you go to school and what subjects you study)

Body paragraph 1 – What you have learned from education

Body paragraph 2 – How your education will help you in the future

Conclusion – A summary of the main points, including the most important thing that education has offered you

As a class, discuss what should be included in each section. Encourage students to share their ideas and write key vocabulary on the board.

Allow students time to plan their blog post and share their plans with a partner.

Students write the first draft of their blog post, following their plan and their partner’s feedback. As they work, remind them to use paragraphs, a variety of grammatical forms, and key topic vocabulary learned in the unit.

Remind them to put each new idea in a separate paragraph, and to give a reason and example(s) to support it.

Encourage them to check their work regularly to ensure coherence and correct grammar, punctuation, and sentence structure.

At the end of the lesson, gauge how students are feeling. Writing a piece of extended writing isn’t easy, so praise them for their perseverance in today’s lesson. They might not realize it right now, but even during more challenging lessons they are learning something new, stretching themselves, and giving their a brain the workout it needs!

Additional task

Learning review

Students have reflected on their personal experience of education in a blog post.

Differentiation

Instruct less able students to write one body paragraph only. Monitor them actively and prompt them when required.

Pair up mixed abilities at the feedback stage so peer-support can be provided.
