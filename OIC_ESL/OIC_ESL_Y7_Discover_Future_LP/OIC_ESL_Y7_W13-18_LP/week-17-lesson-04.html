Introductory activity

Independently or in pairs, ask students to read the text ‘Here comes the summer’ in ODF SB 2 p.52 for gist. This will warm up their thinking about travel plans and itineraries.

Bring the class back together. Ask students to focus on the highlighted phrases (different future forms). Write these sentences on the board and 1) elicit how each future form is constructed, and 2) what each future form expresses (e.g. a plan to do something – (be) going to + verb).

Look at ex.2 together to consolidate students’ understanding of the different forms and the nuances in meaning. This activity is designed to stretch their thinking.

Main activity

Tell students that, in pairs, they are going to prepare a three-day itinerary for a tourist in their country, following a similar format to the example on p.52.

On the board, draw a five-column table with the headings:

Where, when, and who with?

What are you going to do on Day 1?

What are you going to do on Day 2?

What are you going to do on Day 3?

What will you learn?

First, pairs work together to make a mind map to help them plan their daily activities.

As they plan, remind them to think about:

departure and arrival times

three different places and/or activities

what/where they can eat and shop

Remind students to be realistic when planning their itinerary. Encourage them to think about the distances involved in visiting different places, and what people can actually do there. Sometimes tourists end up doing things they don’t enjoy because the itinerary wasn’t clear enough. Say: I was once in Sydney. The itinerary said there was a visit to a valley with a group of other tourists. However, it didn’t explain that I would be gone all day and I would have to go trekking in the mountains. I got very ill and tired. If the itinerary had explained the trip more clearly, I wouldn’t have chosen it.

When pairs are happy with their plans, allow them time to write up their itineraries. They can copy the five-column table, or choose their own format. Ask them to use at least one example of each future form.

Display the completed itineraries around the classroom. Encourage students to move around the classroom and read the work of their peers. Provide sticky notes for them to note down one thing they really liked about each itinerary.

Additional task

Students can complete the listening activities exs.1–3 in ODF WB 2 p.49.

Learning review

Students have reviewed the use of four different future forms and applied their learning to a writing task. In pairs, they have planned and written a three-day travel itinerary.

Differentiation

Less able students can make an itinerary for a day trip, instead of a three-day trip. Pair up similar abilities so additional support can be provided to those who need it.

Challenge more able students to present their itinerary orally to the class, demonstrating a variety of future forms.
