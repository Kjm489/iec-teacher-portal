Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to Which place is best and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows a relevant example and supporting evidence?

Play some calming music. Invite students to close their eyes and take a few deep breaths. Say: You are in your favourite place on holiday. You don’t have any worries and you feel completely relaxed. Where are you picturing yourself? What can you do there? Take a few minutes to reflect on what you can hear, see, smell, and feel; the people you are with; and what you enjoy doing together. Main activity

Introduce the task: students are going to participate in a class debate on which is the best place to visit on holiday. Write on the board: ‘Places to see’, ‘Transport’, ‘Accommodation’, ‘Food’. Elicit that this is what people research before deciding on a destination.

Ask if there is anything else that they would add to the list (e.g. ‘Weather’, ‘Activities’).

Divide the class into groups of three or four. Make sure that there are an even number of groups. Allocate your home country/country of residence to one group, while other groups can choose their own. Ensure that each group has a different location.

Hand out five index cards to each group. This is where they can write their key discussion points. Remind them to write their ideas in bullet point form.

Each group must speak for five minutes, with each group member having a speaking role. The aim is to persuade the class that their chosen country is the best place to visit.

Elicit from students any key expressions, interesting adjectives, and rhetorical devices they can use to make their debate stronger. Write ideas on the board for reference. Students can also refer to ODF SB 2 p.56 to help them prepare and develop their argument.

Encourage students to research information online, look through travel magazines and holiday brochures, and talk to each other about their own experiences in their chosen country (if applicable – it would be useful if one group member is familiar with the destination). Help them to understand that in debates, it is important to use real facts and not make up information to be more persuasive.

Allow students approximately 20 minutes to conduct their research, note down their main points, and agree on the order of speakers.

Then invite two groups to the front. Ask them to present their country in turn. Allow group members from the opposite team to raise their hand if they object or have a counter argument, or wait until the end of each presentation.

Encourage the audience to note down interesting points and good uses of persuasive language. At the end of each mini debate, hold a whole-class vote on the most persuasive argument.

Additional task

Students can use their debate research to write a persuasive paragraph.

Learning review

Students have worked together in small groups to research a holiday destination of their choice and decide on main points to use in a mini debate. Using persuasive and descriptive language, they have presented their argument to the class.

Differentiation

Invite more confident students to guide their group members through the task and help them to plan their speeches. Depending on each group member’s ability, vary speaking times and choice of language.
