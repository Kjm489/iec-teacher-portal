Introductory activity

Ask students where they go to ‘book’ (‘reserve’) a journey if they want to travel by aeroplane, bus, or train. Do they look for online options, or do they go to a travel agent? Show them some examples of travel booking websites, such as an online travel agent, an airline website, and a website that provides bus/train timetables.

Elicit how they feel when they see that there are so many options available online. Do they feel comfortable or overwhelmed?

Main activity

Ask students to log on to the internet. Explain that you want them to help you find tickets to travel to Bucharest next weekend – by aeroplane, and then by bus to a hotel. First, elicit from students what they need to be mindful of when using the internet (to foster their digital resilience).

Give them three minutes to search. After the time is up, ask them what they found – they won’t have found much in such a short amount of time. This is intentional. Ask: What stopped you from finding the necessary information? What key words did you use in the search engine? Why is it important to be specific with key words in a search engine? (It saves time and leads you to the direct site.)

Explain the next task: students are going to practise using the internet to plan a journey overseas. On the board, write the following two options:

Travel from my town/city to Sydney, Australia

The tickets must be return tickets and cost less than 1000 US dollars (or the equivalent in your context) in total.

Accommodation must be central, but not over 150 Australian dollars a day.

It can be for any time of the year, for three weeks.

Travel from my town/city to the Greek island of Folegandros

The tickets must be open and cost less than 600 US dollars in total.

Accommodation must be in rented rooms – a maximum of 50 euros a day.

You can only visit the island in spring, for five days only.

Divide the class into small groups. Give each group one of the two trips. Explain that they have 30 minutes to plan the trip within budget. Remind them to use key words in their search engines and to not to get distracted in their search. Monitor them actively.

Ask students to think about how they will communicate to book their tickets and accommodation. Will it be by email, text message, or telephone?

When the time is up, ask students to stop work. Reflect on the activity as a whole class and establish if groups managed to complete the task within the time limit.

Ask: What problems did you face? Did you make any mistakes? Encourage students to reflect on how they can they use this experience to improve their teamwork, digital skills, and knowledge of key topic vocabulary. Ensure to reinforce that making mistakes helps us to learn and improve.

Additional task

Students can produce an example of an email they would send to a travel agent or hotel.

Learning review

Students have recognized the importance of being digitally resilient. They have found strategies for researching information efficiently online and planned a trip in small groups.

Differentiation

Form mixed-ability groups so peer-support can be provided in the research task.
