Original Oxford Week 13 source text — complete reference

Main activity

Explain that this week, students will work in small groups on a project of their choice. They can extend their learning into an area of particular interest to them, and practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way that they could apply to a real-world setting.

Students will focus on a project related to the topics ‘Language and communication’ or ‘Using pictures’. They should work in small groups with shared areas of interest.

Students can choose their own project, linked to either topic, or they can develop a project from one of the options below.

Encourage students to use the IDEAS to Action framework, which they will be familiar with from their Global Skills Projects lessons. Remind them that this is a smaller-scale project, so they may need to adapt and modify it to suit their needs.

Students will be responsible for the planning and management of their project. This should involve:

suggesting and allocating specific tasks

researching and drafting creative work

reviewing drafts and contributing constructive ideas and suggestions

joint presentation (the following week)

formative self-assessment and peer-assessment (the following week)

Students can present their work in a way that best suits their project, depending on resources and time available. See how creative they can be with their presentations. Here is a non-exhaustive list of different kinds of presentation:

A speech or oral retelling (perhaps supported by visual resources, such as a PowerPoint presentation, photographs, a campaign poster, a model, artefacts, graphs or charts)

A pitch (to sell a product or service)

A skills demonstration

A debate

An art/photography exhibition or display

An interview with an expert in the field of enquiry

A short film or documentary

A creative performance

Remind students that they have five days to complete the planning, research, and execution of their project. At the end of Day 1, teachers could look over each group’s plan to ensure that they have allocated tasks appropriately and know what to accomplish each day.

Topic 1 (Language and communication)

Language fair: Organize a language fair to celebrate languages in school. Possible ideas:

Conduct a survey of different languages in your school, followed by a report presented in graph form.

A compilation of interesting facts about different languages (e.g. least spoken and/or extinct languages, the number of languages in the world, the easiest and most difficult languages to learn, how learning a second language can help you (socially and cognitively), the longest words or place names in the world, the history of languages in your country).

More practical sessions, such as teaching people how to give personal information in different languages (including sign language) or offering a caligraphy session, where people practise writing their name in different scripts (e.g. Arabic, Japanese) depending on the languages known in class/school.

You will present your project as a plan for a language fair. Consider how you will do this. Outline all the elements that need to be considered e.g. the venue, the programme, the publicity, the participants.

Communication through the ages: Create a timeline showing how communication has evolved through the ages. From cave drawings, symbols (e.g. hieroglyphics), smoke signals, carrier pigeons, morse code, the postal system, radio broadcasts, newspapers, telegraphs, telephones, TV, the internet, texting, social media. Consider how you will present your findings. Will it be a physical timeline, in poster format, or a slideshow? Or will you use a range of different ideas, including a creative performance or demonstrations (physical/virtual)?

Topic 2 (Using pictures)

Picture story trail: Create a picture story trail through your school for another year group to follow. Select a series of pictures to build your story. Write the different parts of the story using the pictures to help you. At the end of each part, include clues for students following the trail on where to find the next part of the story.

For example, picture one sets the scene, introduces the characters, and includes a ‘hook’ to capture the reader’s attention. It is followed by instructions/clues on where to find the next part of the story: e.g. ‘Go to a place where you can paint a picture and ask the keeper of this room to provide you with the next part of the story.’

Students go to the Art room and ask the Art teacher for the next picture and part of the story.

Consider giving a prize to the person/group of people who get to the end of the story first!
