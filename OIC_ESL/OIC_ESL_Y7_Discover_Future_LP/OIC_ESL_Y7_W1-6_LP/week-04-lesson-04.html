PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining a relevant example and supporting evidence in the context of Can fiction and nonfiction tell us about the past. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does the evidence support the answer, or is it only a guess?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Discuss the answers as a class. Establish that the fiction text tells only a little about the immigration process (the health check and the interview), but provides new information about how the people are feeling – Karl nervously climbs the stairs; the father smiles to reassure Karl; Karl is calm on the outside, but shaking inside; the officials look serious; there is laughter at the end to show relief. It also describes the registry hall (huge, crowded, and people are talking loudly in many different languages). Elicit from students how this vivid description helps us to put ourselves in other people’s shoes (empathy).

Oxford main activity — independent application: Discuss as a class: Can we learn from both nonfiction and fiction about the past? Consider how both text types can increase our depth of understanding. Nonfiction gives useful and interesting facts, but fiction helps us to imagine the situation and the feelings experienced.

Context
This is the fourth lesson in the unit Learning from the past.

This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
