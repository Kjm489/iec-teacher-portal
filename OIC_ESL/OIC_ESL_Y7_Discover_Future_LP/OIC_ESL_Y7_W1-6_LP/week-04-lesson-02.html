Introductory activity

Elicit from students a list of modern inventions and write them on the board (e.g. tablet, self-driving car, LED light bulb, delivery drone, smart watch).

Then discuss the following:

How have these inventions changed our lives?

Have these inventions had the same impact as earlier inventions, such as the printing press, the wheel, or vaccines?

Invite students to share their ideas. Discuss as a class: Do modern inventions just improve our lives rather than completely transform them?

Main activity

List the following inventions on the board: wheel, microscope, telephone, internet, paper, fridge, light bulb, thermometer, camera, printing press.

Divide the class into groups of four. Students work together in their groups to list the items in the order they were invented – with approximate dates, if possible. Ask them to discuss the impact these inventions have had on our lives.

Rewrite the inventions in the correct order for students to compare (answers: Internet 1969; light bulb 1906; telephone 1876; fridge 1850; camera 1816; thermometer 1583; microsope 1590; printing press 1440; paper 100; wheel 3500 BC). You can add one item at a time as each group discusses their choice.

Divide the class into pairs and give an invention to each pair. (Possible additional inventions: compass, penicillin, x-ray, clock, morse code, automobile, aeroplane.) Students are going to prepare a two-minute presentation about their invention, including when it was invented, life before the invention, and the impact the invention has had on our lives today. Encourage students to use the internet and books to conduct their research.

Form groups of six (consisting of three pairs). Each pair presents their invention to the group, then the whole group discusses the impact of these inventions compared to the impact of modern inventions (discussed at the start of the lesson).

The whole class reviews their ideas on the impact of modern inventions compared to older ones. Repeat the question: Do modern inventions just improve our lives rather than completely transform them?

Additional task

Students can write up their oral presentation into a short text.

Learning review

Students have explored the impact of inventions on our lives, and discussed whether modern inventions improve rather than transform. In teams, they have made sensible predictions to create a timeline of inventions. They have used a variety of resources (internet, books, prior knowledge) to prepare a presentation on a particular invention.

Differentiation

Less able students can prepare presentations with more able students.
