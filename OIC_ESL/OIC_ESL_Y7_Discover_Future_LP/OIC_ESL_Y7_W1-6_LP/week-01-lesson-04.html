PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about education and learning; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford introductory activity: Divide the class into two teams. The teams go to opposite ends of the classroom with a whiteboard, and nominate a scribe. Set a timer to 1 or 2 minutes. Teams think of as many things as they can that identify us (e.g. what we are called, where we come from and where we live, the people we know, the clothes we wear, the things we like to do, etc.). The scribe writes their team’s list on the whiteboard. The team with the most correct answers at the end of the game wins. Encourage students to think of funny and unusual answers as well as more common ones!

Oxford introductory activity: Direct students to look at Asha’s profile on p.8 of ODF SB 2. Are there other items that can be included on their lists?

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “A study plan can include short daily revision sessions.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about evidence for a learning claim. 3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about education and learning. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: You can also remind students of the difference between defining and non-defining clauses before they begin the writing activity (see p.9 and p.113 of ODF SB 2).

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Divide the class into small groups of four, number each group, and appoint a group leader.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Ask the group leaders to put a list of students in their group on the wall beside their group number and give each student in their group a letter from A–D.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to education and learning and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows evidence for a learning claim?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Students write an anonymous profile of themselves, using the model on p.8 to help them. They should write the letter assigned to them (not their name) at the top. When students have finished the task, they stick their profiles on the wall under their group number.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Tell students to move around the room and read the profiles of another group. Can they decide who wrote each one? They write the name of the student on the profile paper. The list of names will help them.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining evidence for a learning claim in the context of education and learning. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Put the two groups that have read each other’s profiles together (e.g. groups 1 and 2; 3 and 4), forming groups of eight students.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Return the profiles to the groups. Students discuss their choices and explain why they made them. The correct authors can reveal themselves at the end of the activity.

Oxford main activity — independent application: As a class, discuss the questions: Were there any similarities/differences in the profiles? Were there any surprises? What did you learn from the activity about identity in your class?

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of education and learning, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Nominate a more confident student to be the scribe in the Introductory activity.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less able students to write short sentences about themselves. Organize the groups so that more able students can provide support.

Oxford additional task: See exs.5 and 6 in ODF WB 2 p.7; ODF SB 2 p.113 talking about the present.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.4 and 5 on relative clauses in ODF SB 2 p.9.

Oxford additional task: Peer-editing – students can correct each other’s work, focusing particularly on tenses.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about education and learning and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain evidence for a learning claim without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have written a personal profile using a variety of present tenses. They have read and compared each other’s profiles and continued to discuss the question of identity.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
