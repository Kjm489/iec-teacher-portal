3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about identity characteristics. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to identity characteristics and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows a characteristic that describes someone?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: As a class, go through some examples of ‘dos’ and ‘don’ts’.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Distribute flipchart paper, markers, and coloured pencils to each group. Introduce the task of producing an illustrated poster that gives advice on how to protect your online identity, based on students’ lists of ‘dos’ and ‘don’ts’. Encourage students to assign sub-tasks (organizing information, writing sentences, layout, and illustrations, etc.) in line with each other’s strengths and interests. As you walk between groups, ask them why they made certain decisions.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.
