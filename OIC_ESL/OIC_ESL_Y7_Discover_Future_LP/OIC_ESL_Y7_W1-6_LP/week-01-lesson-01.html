PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about identity characteristics; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Does identity mean only a person’s name, or several things that describe them?

Oxford introductory activity: Write the word ‘identity’ on the board. Ask: How do you define your identity? Elicit students’ ideas and write them on the board (e.g. gender, age, address, nationality, physical characteristics, family and friends, interests, religion or ethnic group).

Oxford introductory activity: Refer students to the photo and caption on p.4 of ODF SB 2. Ask: Do you agree with the caption? What are your reasons? Discuss as a class: Does the meaning of identity differ around the world?

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “An identity can include interests, culture and experiences.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about a characteristic that describes someone. Textbook anchor: 2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min Teacher model grounded in the original activity: Tell students to read the identities on p.5 of ODF SB 2. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Does identity mean only a person’s name, or several things that describe them?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Tell students to read the identities on p.5 of ODF SB 2. Ask: Do the descriptions match the list of characteristics on the board? Encourage them to find additional characteristics, such as job/profession, personality, dress sense/clothes, and experiences.

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Highlight and discuss the meaning of any new vocabulary (e.g. courageous, enthusiastic, well dressed, survived).

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about identity characteristics. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Does identity mean only a person’s name, or several things that describe them?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Ask students to do ex.2 on p.5 of ODF SB 2.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Watch the Factflix video. Ask students to list the different ways the presenters say that we identify people. Then compare this list to students’ list on the board. (Presenters talk about names, ID number, descriptions of people.)

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to identity characteristics and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows a characteristic that describes someone?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Watch again and answer the questions in ex.4 on p.5 of ODF SB 2.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Focus on the key phrases used for identifying people. Discuss the language that should follow each phrase. Ask students to find a partner, then do exs.5–6 on p.5 of ODF SB 2.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining a characteristic that describes someone in the context of identity characteristics. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does identity mean only a person’s name, or several things that describe them?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Students complete their own identity charts – a table with headings, under which students provide more information about this aspect of their identity. As a class, discuss which headings from the board should be included. (Project or hand out your own identity chart as a model.) Allow time for writing the charts, then display them around the classroom.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Divide the class into small groups and encourage them to walk around the room to read and discuss the identity charts. Do they share anything in common with other classmates? Recognizing shared interests can help to establish new friendships.

Oxford main activity — independent application: As a class, invite students to share their findings. Discuss as a class: What have I learned about my identity and that of others in my class?

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of identity characteristics, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: For pairwork, put less able students with more able students.

Oxford additional task: Tell students to identify what aspects of their identity are the most important to them from their chart. Invite them to select one and write a short explanation about why it is important part of defining who they are. (This could be a homework assignment for more able students. Less able students can write up their profiles into sentences.)

Oxford additional task: Students can share their writing with each other.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about identity characteristics and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain a characteristic that describes someone without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have discussed the different ways that we identify ourselves and others. They have compared their views to those of a presenter. They have written their own identity charts and compared them with those of their classmates to establish similarities.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.

YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
