Original Oxford Week 3 source text — complete reference

Introductory activity

Write ‘A descriptive profile’ on the board and elicit from students what categories they would include and some examples (e.g. age – teenage, elderly; physical appearance – well-dressed, tall, short; personality – shy, outgoing, chatty).

Divide the class into groups of four and ask students to list vocabulary ideas for each category. Encourage them to explore varied vocabulary.

Invite groups to share their ideas with the class. Note any new words on the board, and direct students to synonyms and opposites (e.g. well-dressed/stylish; blue eyes/blue-eyed; shy/outgoing).

Main activity

Tell students they are going to prepare an oral description of a person they know well.

Provide a model: do exs.1–2 in ODF SB 2 p.12. Focus on the highlighted adverbs of degree in the profile and discuss language point 5 on p.13.

Ask students to complete the mind map about Jimmy (ex.3, p.12) then compare their results in pairs.

Now students create their own mind map about a person they know well. They use this plan to write a short paragraph-length description about their chosen person, using adverbs of degree to modify their adjectives (to what extent is this person this way?).

Oral presentation: In groups of three, each student presents their description orally. The two students listening should listen for the following details and take simple notes.

Listen for: appearance, personality, interests, use of adverbs of degree, grammar errors.

Feedback: After each presentation, the listening students summarize the description they heard using the language they noted down. Tactfully discuss any errors and make sensible suggestions for improvements. Remind students that constructive criticism helps them to embrace and learn from their mistakes – making mistakes is normal and expected when learning and practising a language.

If students would like to know more about the person in each description, they can ask further questions.

Additional tasks

Complete exs.1–2 in ODF WB 2 p.6.

Complete exs.1–2 in the Discover Vocabulary section of ODF SB 2 p.112.

Learning review

Students have used a range of interesting adjectives and adverbs of degree to describe other people. They have planned an oral presentation to describe someone they know well using a mind map, then carried out an oral peer-correction and summary activity.

Differentiation

For the introductory activity, less able and more able students can work together.

For the oral presentations, put less able students together in groups. This will allow them to work at a slower pace.

* Note: While less able students will benefit from working with more able students for some activities, from time to time, working with students of a similar ability will allow them to go at a slower pace and have more time for reflection. This may provide an environment that helps to build their confidence.
