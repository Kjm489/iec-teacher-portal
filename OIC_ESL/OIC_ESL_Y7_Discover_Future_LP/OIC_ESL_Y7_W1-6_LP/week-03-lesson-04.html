Introductory activity

Write the title ‘Someone I look up to’ on the board. Discuss the meaning of ‘look up to’.

Ask students to think about someone they look up to and note down three or four words that they associate with this person (e.g. hardworking, joyful, dependable, loyal).

Tell students to explain to a partner who they have chosen and why.

Invite students to share their ideas with the whole class and discuss. Are there any similarities in the choices of people (e.g. parent, aunt/uncle, team leader, friend, public figure), the words used, or students’ reasons for looking up to these people?

Main activity

Explain to students that they are going to plan an article about a person they look up to.

Allow students time to reflect on a person they admire and why.

In pairs, students discuss what the article could include, before sharing their ideas with the class. Ideas could include: a description of the person (appearance and personality); how you know them; background information about their life; why you admire them.

Explain that the article will include an introduction, body, and conclusion.

Elicit from students what each part of the article might include. For example:

Introduction: introduce person, your relationship with them

Body: how you know the person, description of their personality and appearance, background information, why you admire them – support this with examples

Conclusion: explain again why you admire them using different language and write a concluding sentence explaining how this person makes you feel

Ask students to create a mind map (see ODF SB 2 p.12 for a model) with the headings: Personality, Appearance, Background information, Why I admire them.

Students use their mind map to describe their person to their partner. Encourage them to suggest how their partner can improve their work by recommending vocabulary and additional ideas. Invite them to ask questions to learn more about their partner’s person.

Allow time for students to revise their plans based on the discussion with their partner.

Additional tasks

Review what makes a good paragraph – topic sentence, supporting detail, concluding sentence. Use model paragraphs to demonstrate this. Students can write a draft paragraph using notes from their mind map.

Review the writing process – discuss the steps taken in today’s lesson and consider what the next steps are (writing a first draft, revising and editing, writing a final draft).

Learning review

Students have followed the first steps of the writing process: selected an idea, planned the contents using a mind map, shared ideas, and offered their partner constructive feedback.

Differentiation

To support all students, review tenses (present simple, present continous, and present perfect). Less able students will require additional support from the teacher, and might not be able to grasp the present perfect tense quite yet – work according to their level.
