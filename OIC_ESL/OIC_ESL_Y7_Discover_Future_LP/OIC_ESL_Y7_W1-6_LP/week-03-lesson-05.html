Main activity

Write a first draft: Allow students time to write a first draft of their article using their mind map. This activity is supposed to be difficult – remind students that their brains get the biggest ‘workout’ when they do challenging work. It is OK to feel a little nervous.

First peer-edit: In pairs (different from the previous lesson), students read each other’s articles. They should highlight any errors, but not make corrections. They can make notes in the margin with suggestions for improvement and clarifying questions.

EDITING CHECKLIST: 1. Is the article clearly organized into paragraphs with an introduction, body, and conclusion? 2. Does the writer use a range of interesting adjectives? 3. Are tenses used accurately?

Feedback: Students give oral feedback to their partner. They discuss the two articles together, and make corrections and changes where required.

Second draft: Students write their second draft based on the peer-feedback received.

Second peer-edit and feedback (if there is time): Students share their second draft with another partner and carry out the same process as before.

Final draft: Students write the final draft of their article.

Discussion: As a class, discuss how the peer-editing process went, including positive and more challenging aspects.
