1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about When I look in the mirror; collect two ideas. CCQ: Does the evidence support the answer, or is it only a guess?

Oxford introductory activity: Write on the board ‘slim’, ‘well-dressed’, and ‘patient’ and ask students to identify the category each word comes from (build, appearance, personality).

Oxford introductory activity: Select five new words from each of these categories that you think students will not know and write them down randomly on the board (e.g. stocky, plump, skinny, lanky, slender; messy, scruffy, unkempt, smart, attractive, elegant, shabby; trustworthy, open-minded, argumentative, good-humoured, extroverted, fickle, hilarious).

Oxford introductory activity: In pairs, students sort the adjectives into the three categories, using dictionaries to help them. Those who finish early can add other words and look for opposites.

Oxford introductory activity: Compare answers as a class. Then ask: What is the first thing you notice about a person when you meet them for the first time?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to When I look in the mirror and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows a relevant example and supporting evidence?

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining a relevant example and supporting evidence in the context of When I look in the mirror. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does the evidence support the answer, or is it only a guess?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Students walk around the room and read each other’s work. As they do this, ask them to note down any similarities they notice across the descriptions.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of When I look in the mirror, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: For the introductory activity, more able students can use an English dictionary to find definitions. Less able students can use a dictionary which gives definitions from English into their first language.

Oxford differentiation: For the main activity, less able students can write 1–2 sentences under each heading, instead of complete paragraphs. More able students can try to include more difficult words learned in the introductory activity.

Oxford additional task: Find descriptions of characters in fiction to develop students’ range of descriptive language. Students can draw a picture of what they think the character looks like.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about When I look in the mirror and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain a relevant example and supporting evidence without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have reviewed descriptive language and learned new vocabulary. They have used descriptive language to write a three-paragraph description of themselves with the help of their peers.
