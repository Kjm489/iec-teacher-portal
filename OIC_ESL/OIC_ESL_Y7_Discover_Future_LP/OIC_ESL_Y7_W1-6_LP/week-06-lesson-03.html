Original Oxford Week 6 source text — complete reference

Main activity

Explain that this week, students will work in small groups on a project of their choice. They can extend their learning into an area of particular interest to them, and practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way that they could apply to a real-world setting.

Students will focus on a project related to the topics ‘Identity’ or ‘Learning from the past’. They should work in small groups with shared areas of interest.

Students can choose their own project, linked to either topic, or they can develop a project from one of the options below.

Encourage students to use the IDEAS to Action framework, which they will be familiar with from their Global Skills Projects lessons. Remind them that this is a smaller-scale project, so they may need to adapt and modify it to suit their needs.

Students will be responsible for the planning and management of their project. This should involve:

suggesting and allocating specific tasks

researching and drafting creative work

reviewing drafts and contributing constructive ideas and suggestions

joint presentation (the following week)

formative self-assessment and peer-assessment (the following week)

Students can present their work in a way that best suits their project, depending on resources and time available. Encourage them to be creative with their presentations. Here are some examples of different kinds of presentation:

A speech or oral retelling (perhaps supported by visual resources, such as a PowerPoint presentation, photographs, a campaign poster, a model, artefacts, graphs or charts)

A pitch (to sell a product or service)

A skills demonstration

A debate

An art/photography exhibition or display

An interview with an expert in the field of enquiry

A short film or documentary

A creative performance

Remind students that they have five days to complete the planning, research, and execution of their project. At the end of Day 1, teachers could look over each group’s plan to ensure that they have allocated tasks appropriately and know what to achieve each day.

Topic 1 (Identity)

Project suggestions:

Safer Internet Day assembly: Plan a 15-minute school assembly which aims to raise awareness about the importance of online safety issues. Consider the issues raised in class, and the best way of presenting them to the school. This could be, for example, a PowerPoint presentation, stories of people who have been affected, or a short play.

Same or different: Show how you, and the other members of your group, identify yourselves as individuals and with your culture, exploring similarities and differences. You could share your findings in a variety of different ways, such as performing a short story, writing a poem, giving an oral presentation with a visual display, or showing a video clip.

Topic 2 (Learning from the past)

Project suggestions:

Time capsule: Prepare the contents of a time capsule to be opened in 50 years time, which will reflect life in your community. Consider items which show all aspects of your community life (e.g. school, current affairs, entertainment, free time, sports, food and drink, fashion, social media, technology). Include a short video tour and/or a poster collage. Give a short explanation for choosing each item.

A biography trailer of a person whose actions have had an impact: Prepare a trailer (short promotional video) for a biographical film or book which retells the life of a real person. If you prefer not to use technology, design a poster with an accompanying oral account. Include the important aspects of the person’s life and how they have had an impact.
