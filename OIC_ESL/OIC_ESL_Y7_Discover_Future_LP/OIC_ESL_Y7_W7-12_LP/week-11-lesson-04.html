Write on the board: ‘adjust’, ‘improve’, ‘reduce’, ‘whiten’, ‘enhance’, ‘change’, ‘alter’.

Elicit the lesson topic from these words – airbrushing/touching up or altering pictures.

In pairs, students discuss the following: Do you ever alter your pictures? If so, how do you do it and why? Who alters pictures and why? What do you think about airbrushing?

Invite students to share their ideas with the class and encourage them to be respectful of each other’s points of view. There is no ‘right’ or ‘wrong’ answer.

Main activity

Look at the pictures in ODF SB 2 pp.40–41. Ask: Where do you think these photos are from? Encourage them to use modals of deduction (could/might/may/must/can’t be).

Focus students on the use of question tags. Highlight that a positive statement needs a negative tag, and vice versa. Complete the intonation exercise (ex.2 in ODF SB 2 p.40).

Put students into groups of three. Hand out a set of cut-up statements and tags to each group. Arrange the statements in a pile and give three tags to each student. Complete exs.5–7 in ODF WB 2 pp.36–37.

Students have shared their opinions on altering pictures in different situations and explored vocabulary to discuss different ways of ‘touching up’ pictures. They have practised using question tags in conversation.
