PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about images and interpretation; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Can a photograph show every detail of what happened outside the frame?

Oxford introductory activity: Display a selection of photos taken from a newspaper or news website. Ask: Where would you find these photos? What else would you find with them? Why are they useful? (Answers: newspapers or news websites/apps; headlines and articles; they help us to visualize events happening around the world, they connect us to information we already know, they capture our attention and create interest in the story.)

Oxford introductory activity: In pairs, students choose a photo without letting other pairs know which one they have selected. Challenge them to write their own headline to accompany the photo.

Oxford introductory activity: Pairs take it in turns to read out their headlines. The rest of the class decides which photo it matches to. Allow some discussion about the different interpretations of the photos.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “The photograph suggests a busy street because several people are walking.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about evidence from an image. Textbook anchor: 2. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Can a photograph show every detail of what happened outside the frame?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Tell students that they are going to predict all three elements of a news story (the photo, the headline, and the story [article]) but they will only be given one element. (You will need the photo, headline, and article sets for this activity.)

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Divide students into groups of four. Allocate groups the letter A, B, or C. Give A groups a photo, B groups a headline, and C groups an article.

Oxford main activity — teacher model: A groups think of a headline and story to go with their photo.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about images and interpretation. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Can a photograph show every detail of what happened outside the frame?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: B groups think of a photo and story to go with their headline.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: C groups think of a photo and headline to go with their article.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Students write their ideas on flipchart paper. Tell As and Bs that the story only needs to be a very short summary.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to images and interpretation and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows evidence from an image?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: When groups are ready, ask them to display their information around the classroom.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Invite students to walk around the classroom and look at the different ideas. Encourage them to discuss whether they agree or not with the predictions.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: When students return to their seats, ask them to recall the different stories told using the photo and headline prompts. Note them on the board.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining evidence from an image in the context of images and interpretation. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Can a photograph show every detail of what happened outside the frame?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Then ask students to match the original photos, headlines, and articles together. Move them from the walls and put them on the board so that everyone can see them.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Encourage each group to explain how they made their predictions and reflect on their critical thinking skills.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Discuss as a class why photos are important in journalism (e.g. they reflect reality and are visual evidence of an event, they transport the reader to the event in their mind, they summarize what is being written and add clarity, they preserve important events.)

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of images and interpretation, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Give more able groups the articles in the group activity as this is more challenging.

Oxford differentiation: Include a mixture of news items that cater to all levels.

Oxford additional task: Students can find their own news photo and give a short oral summary of the story in their own words.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about images and interpretation and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain evidence from an image without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have discussed the importance of photos in journalism. They have used photos to make predictions about news stories, and have reconstructed a news story using headlines, photos, and articles.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
