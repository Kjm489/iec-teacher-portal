Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? 3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about images and interpretation. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Can a photograph show every detail of what happened outside the frame?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Introduction: a short summary that explains who, what, where, when, why, how

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Body: adds further detail to the question words and includes some quotations from people involved

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Conclusion: explains the outcome, perhaps including a quotation from someone to sum up the ending

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to images and interpretation and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows evidence from an image?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Hand out copies of the same graphic organizer from the previous lesson.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Tell students to take the photo and word list that they would like to use in their writing from the wall. (If several students choose the same photo, they can sit together.) The key words are to help them to construct their news story.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining evidence from an image in the context of images and interpretation. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Can a photograph show every detail of what happened outside the frame?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Students complete their graphic organizer with notes on their invented news story.

Oxford main activity — independent application: In pairs, they tell each other their stories, then offer each other feedback, make content and new vocabulary suggestions, and ask questions for clarification. Remind students of the importance of ‘constructive’ criticism and saying one or two things that they like about their partner’s plan. In this way, students will be motivated to revise their stories.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Allow students enough time to write their first draft of the news story, organizing it correctly.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of images and interpretation, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Provide less able students with writing frames to help them to write their first draft.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able students to ‘think outside the box’ and be as inventive as they like with their news stories. See if they can incorporate a variety of tenses and new vocabulary learned in their lessons to date.

Oxford additional task: Ask students to stick their first draft to the top of a large sheet of flipchart paper and leave it on their desk.

Oxford additional task: Encourage them to walk around the classroom and read each other’s news stories, leaving feedback on the flipchart paper.

Oxford additional task: Students reclaim their articles and read the feedback. Based on their peers’ comments, encourage them to think about what they might do differently next time. If there is time, they can write their final draft.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about images and interpretation and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain evidence from an image without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have planned a news article using photo and key word prompts, transferring their ideas into a graphic organizer. They have shared their stories orally and provided feedback, before writing their news story.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
