PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford introductory activity: Tell students how you felt (or make up how you felt) when you were a student on your first day at a new school.

Oxford introductory activity: How did you feel?

Oxford introductory activity: What facilities did the school have?

Oxford introductory activity: Elicit from the class what can make the first day at a new school more or less enjoyable.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “I use a formal greeting when writing to a school leader.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about audience and purpose. Textbook anchor: 2. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Explain that students are going to listen to two short conversations between a new student, who is being given a tour of their new school, and an existing student.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about communication. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Tell students to note down the names of the characters in each conversation, and to decide which guide they would prefer to have on a school tour.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Play the audio (see ODF WB 2 p.29, track 3.01). Check answers. Ask students why they chose a specific guide (e.g. the first guide is more empathetic, less distracted, helpful, gives more useful information).

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to communication and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows audience and purpose?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Turn to the grid in ODF WB 2 p.29 ex.2. Tell students to fill it in as they listen two more times. They check their answers in small groups, then review answers as a whole class.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Ask groups to think about what information they would want to know on a tour of a new school. Elicit some examples. Then ask what information parents of new students might want to know on a school tour. Establish that different audiences (e.g. different age groups) will likely find different kinds of information useful, and that language and register might need to be adjusted (e.g. more informal and chattier for students, more respectful for adults).

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining audience and purpose in the context of communication. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Students are going to imagine that they have been selected as ambassadors to conduct tours of the school for: a) a group of new students, b) a group of parents. Their task, in their groups, is to decide on a route for the tour and what information they would give during the tour. Encourage them to think about how language and information can be adapted to suit both their audiences.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Students perform their role-plays for both parents and new students in front of the class.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of communication, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Pair and group work encourages students to work collaboratively and allows linguistically more able students to support less able ones.

Oxford differentiation: All students should be able to participate actively in the design of the school tour.

Oxford additional task: Students can draw a sketch plan of the tour route and add speaking notes as prompts.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about communication and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain audience and purpose without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have listened to two different conversations and built on their knowledge of what makes a good conversation. They have considered what language, register, and information is appropriate for different audiences (students and parents) and applied this learning to a role-play activity.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
