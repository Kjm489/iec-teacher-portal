Previous lesson directions (reference)

Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to images and interpretation and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows evidence from an image?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Each group shares its information with the rest of the class to establish why each picture is an example of bad netiquette.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining evidence from an image in the context of images and interpretation. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Can a photograph show every detail of what happened outside the frame?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Initiate a short class discussion on netiquette and the damage that the thoughtless posting of pictures can cause to individuals like Emma (in picture A).

Oxford main activity — independent application: Set each group the task of producing a short list of netiquette Dos and Don’ts. Encourage students to think about their own experiences online and listen to each other’s experiences respectfully.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of images and interpretation, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Group work allows more able students to help less able students to complete the task.

Oxford differentiation: Less able students might benefit from working in pairs or small groups on the questionnaire activity. Monitor these students actively to ensure their understanding. It’s OK if they need to communicate their experiences online in their own language.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able students to role-play a new podcast episode based on their netiquette Dos and Don’ts.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.11–12 in ODF WB 2 p.38.

Oxford additional task: Create a whole-class poster of netiquette Dos and Don’ts to display in the classroom.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about images and interpretation and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain evidence from an image without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have learned about ‘netiquette’ and considered the ethics of posting certain types of pictures online and how this might affect others. They have extracted main information from a podcast and produced their own netiquette guidelines.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
