PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about communication; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford introductory activity: Read aloud the first paragraph of the Australia postcard in ODF SB 2 p.30. Ask students to write down what they hear. They then swap their dictations with a partner and check each other’s work for accuracy. Read the paragraph one more time, then allow them to turn to SB 2 p.30 to check their answers.

Oxford introductory activity: Ask: Can you identify the four tenses in the paragraph? (present perfect, present continuous, present perfect continuous, and simple past)

Oxford introductory activity: In pairs, students recall their previous learning and discuss the form and function of each tense –they might not be familiar with the present perfect continuous yet. Invite a few volunteers to share their ideas. Correct and give explanations where necessary.

Oxford introductory activity: Ask students to discover things that they have been doing longer or have done more of than their partner by asking questions: e.g. How long have you been wearing the shoes you have on now? How many countries have you visited? How long have you been studying English? Students write down their findings. Put some examples on the board.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about communication. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Do you write letters? Why/Why not?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Do you think people will write letters in the future?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: What’s the difference between emails and handwritten letters?

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to communication and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows audience and purpose?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: How has the way we communicate changed over the last 50 years?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Why do you think teachers might be concerned that people write fewer letters?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Invite students to share their views, then tell them to read the text ‘The Death of Letters’ on p.30. Ask: Are you surprised by any of the information given?

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining audience and purpose in the context of communication. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Students are going to write a letter to a friend they have not spoken to for a long time (see ODF SB 2 p.31 ex.6). It can be a real or fictional person. In the last paragraph, remind them to include two questions that they would like their friend to answer in their reply.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Give students writing paper and an envelope. The letter should be one draft only and must be written as neatly as possible, crossing out when necessary. Challenge them to include at least one example of the four tenses reviewed in the lesson.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Tell students to put the letter in an envelope and address it. (They can make up the address.) Collect in all the envelopes and keep for the following lesson. Look over the letters and make a list of points to discuss with the class in the next lesson (e.g. handwriting, tense errors, layout, greeting, signing off, address format).

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of communication, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: For less able students, spend more time studying the tenses (see ODF SB 2 p.117).

Oxford differentiation: During the initial dictation activity, speak more slowly or at a more natural pace depending on class ability. Be sure to emphasize the punctuation.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.5–7 in ODF WB 2 p.27.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about communication and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain audience and purpose without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have reviewed the present perfect, present continuous, present perfect continuous, and simple past tenses, and practised using them correctly in a handwritten letter to a friend. They have discussed the future of letter writing.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
