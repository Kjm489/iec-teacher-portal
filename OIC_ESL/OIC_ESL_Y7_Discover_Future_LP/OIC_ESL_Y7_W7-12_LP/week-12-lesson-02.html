4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to images and interpretation and explain a difference using evidence. 6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of images and interpretation, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Allow less able students to tell a shorter part of the story, in a few sentences. More able students can offer their support and make suggestions.

Oxford differentiation: The organization and management of the group gives students an opportunity to display leadership skills. Students who are less able linguistically or less confident in whole-class activities may prove themselves to be excellent organizers and leaders. Previous lesson directions (reference)

Original Oxford Week 12 source text — complete reference

Introductory activity

Before the lesson, prepare a story based on a set of pictures to serve as a model for today’s task. Alternatively, create a dynamic story on the board using simple (e.g. stick people) drawings. Your story can be as creative and imaginative as you like – this will help students to see the freedom they have to create their own picture story. It also helps if you can present the story with a degree of drama and suspense.

Main activity

Give each group of five to six students a set of five to six pictures (one per student in the group). You can either use the same set of pictures for every group in the class, or a different set for each group.

Set students the task of imagining and narrating a story using the pictures they have been given. Explain that this activity is designed to stretch them creatively and linguistically to extend their understanding of language. Encourage them to divide the story so that each student tells one part based on one of the pictures. They can be as creative and imaginative as they like!

Give a time limit (suggested 30 minutes maximum) for groups to create and practise telling their stories. Then bring the class back together so that each group can present their story. As one group tells its story, the others listen and can interject and ask questions as the stories unfold.

The activity might be more interesting if each group has the same set of pictures because they will have all created different stories. This should encourage interaction between the groups as they compare and contrast their stories.

When all the stories have been told, invite students to say which one they liked the best and why. Finish with an all-class vote to find the best story.

Additional task

Each group can write up their story.

Learning review

Students have practised their speaking skills in a way that encourages creativity and teamwork. They have been challenged to ‘think outside the box’ given that they have had no linguistic aids (e.g. notes, grammar input, or vocabulary lists) to help them.

Differentiation

Allow less able students to tell a shorter part of the story, in a few sentences. More able students can offer their support and make suggestions.

The organization and management of the group gives students an opportunity to display leadership skills. Students who are less able linguistically or less confident in whole-class activities may prove themselves to be excellent organizers and leaders. Remind these students that they are in the ‘stretch zone’ – stepping outside of their comfort zone can feel disconcerting but it will show them what they are capable of.
