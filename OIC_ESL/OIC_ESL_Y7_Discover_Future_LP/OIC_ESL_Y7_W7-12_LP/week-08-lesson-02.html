Introductory activity

To introduce the concept of body language, ask students: How do we communicate without words?

Demonstrate a couple of emotions (e.g. joy, sadness, anger) using only body language – a mix of facial expressions and gestures. Then invite students to come forward and do the same. Encourage the class to guess the different emotions.

Ask students what they think the following gestures mean (see ODF SB 2 p.29): 1) tapping one foot on the ground, holding your chin; 2) crossing your arms and looking around the class; 3) putting your hands on your hip and taking one step forward. Explain that they will find out the meanings when they read the story.

Main activity

Students read the first five lines of the text ‘The Spanish Lesson’ in ODF SB 2 p.29. In pairs, ask them to speculate on:

why Mary will never forget the Spanish lesson,

what ‘superpowers’ Mr. Fitzgerald has.

Write students’ predictions on the board. Then ask them to close their books.

Put students into groups of six. In a random order, give each group member a pre-prepared card with one of the following sentences from the text:

One of these was his ability to know what was happening behind his back.

Mr. Fitzgerald’s second superpower was something we called “The Look.”

Fitz’s final superpower was knowing exactly which students had not done the homework.

If you haven’t done your homework, you usually avoid the teacher’s eye.

“Mary Lambert, please.”

If I ever see a mouse now, I laugh and repeat Fitz’s sentence. In Spanish, of course.

Tell students to try to put the six sentences in the same order they occur in the text. They can read out their line of text in turn, but they cannot show it to the group. Once they have agreed on an order, ask them to line up in that order.

Check answers by asking different groups to read out the sentences in their chosen order.

Encourage the groups to speculate again on the content of the text based on these sentences. Then allow them time to read the full text.

Focus on the body language examples given in the text. Ask students to think up ‘conversations’ using only body language in their groups. Invite a few volunteer groups to present their conversations to the whole class.

Additional tasks

Write about a teacher who has ‘superpowers’ like Mr. Fitzgerald, or about a time when students did not do their homework and what happened as a result.

Complete exs.1–5 in ODF SB 2 p.28.

Students have predicted the content of a text and worked together to arrange sentences in the correct order, using logic and their knowledge of text structure. They have practised having a conversation using non-verbal communication.
