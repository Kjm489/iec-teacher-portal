PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining audience and purpose in the context of communication. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Para 2: Explain that you have been making plans and would like their opinion. Give your suggestions for each day and evening.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of communication, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Allow less able students to write fewer paragraphs. You may wish to group these students together and write a group email with an adult.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able students by asking them to include as many tenses as they can in their email. Can they remember how to construct the present perfect continuous? e.g. I have been looking forward to your visit for ages!

Oxford additional task: Pairs of students can respond to each other’s emails in the role of the friend.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about communication and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain audience and purpose without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have reviewed the features of an informal email. They have planned and written an informal email, making suggestions and using an appropriate register.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.

Introductory activity

Ask students to recall Week 10, Lesson 3. See how many features of an informal email they can remember. Remind them that by thinking again about informal email language, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

Write a few example sentences with errors from students’ emails from Week 10, Lesson 3 on the board. Keep students anonymous.

In pairs, ask students to identify and correct the errors. Review corrections as a class, reinforcing that it is good to share and learn from mistakes.

Main activity

Tell students to imagine that a good friend is coming to stay for a weekend. Their friend has never visited the local area / town / city.

In pairs, ask students to think of things to do and places to go with their friend. Invite volunteers to share their ideas with their class and write them on the board.

Discuss the possible content and language of the email: e.g. offering choices, making suggestions, and asking for preferences.

Elicit useful language for these functions: e.g. We could … + infinitive, How about … + verb + ‘–ing’, Would you like to … + infinitive.

Use the suggested activities on the board to practise the different functions. For example:

How about going to see a film?

Would you like to go to the skate park?

We could go to the bowling alley or we could just hang out at the nature reserve.

We could chill at home and listen to music.

In pairs, students discuss the organization of an informal email. Then bring the class together to share ideas and come to an agreement. Possible organization:

Greeting: Hi … / Dear … / How’s it going? / What have you been up to?

Para 1: Say how long it’s been since you last saw them and your feelings about the visit.

Para 2: Explain that you have been making plans and would like their opinion. Give your suggestions for each day and evening.

Para 3: Ask for a reply with some ideas so you can make arrangements.

Closing phrase: Take care / Chat soon / Speak later / Best wishes

Allow time for students to write their emails.

When they have finished, tell them to exchange their email with a partner. Invite students to read their partner’s email and say one thing they really liked about it.

Additional task

Pairs of students can respond to each other’s emails in the role of the friend.

Learning review

Students have reviewed the features of an informal email. They have planned and written an informal email, making suggestions and using an appropriate register.

Differentiation

Allow less able students to write fewer paragraphs. You may wish to group these students together and write a group email with an adult.

Challenge more able students by asking them to include as many tenses as they can in their email. Can they remember how to construct the present perfect continuous? e.g. I have been looking forward to your visit for ages!
