3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about technology and society. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Can the same technology have both benefits and drawbacks?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Divide the class into at least five or six small groups. Each group is allocated one invention from the board. They plan the arguments they will make in favour of their invention.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Ask: What are the qualities of a good persuasive speech? Elicit ideas to cover the content, speaker attitude, and language used when debating. CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to technology and society and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows benefit and drawback?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Students review their arguments with the success criteria in mind. Each student in the group must be prepared to speak in the balloon debate at least once. Encourage students to think about presenting their ideas in creative and inspiring ways.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Regroup students so that five or six inventions are represented in each debate. One student from each small group speaks in the first round; the other students listen and then vote which invention should be removed. A time limit can be set for each talk.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

Context
This is the second lesson in the unit ‘Technology and us’.
Students will develop their speaking skills by taking part in a balloon debate and by starting to develop their ability to self-evaluate through relevant success criteria. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
