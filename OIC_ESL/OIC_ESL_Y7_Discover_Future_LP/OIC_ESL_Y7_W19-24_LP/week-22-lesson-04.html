PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about technology and society; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Can the same technology have both benefits and drawbacks?

Oxford introductory activity: Choose a word which takes an affix (prefix or suffix) that your students are very familiar with, such as ‘help’. Write the root word on the board and elicit how the word can be changed by adding more letters (e.g. ‘helpful’, ‘unhelpful’, ‘helper’, ‘helpless’).

Oxford introductory activity: Elicit other words which use these affixes (e.g. ‘beautiful’, ‘happy’, ‘care’). Check students know the difference between prefixes and suffixes.

Oxford introductory activity: Explain that you’re going to play a game using prefixes and suffixes. You’ll say a word using an affix, then pass or throw the ball to the next student, who must give a word using the same affix before passing the ball on. If the word is correct, the student stays in the game. If it’s not correct, or the student can’t think of a word, they’re out.

Oxford introductory activity: Play a few rounds using different affixes. Allow students to choose new affixes if needed.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to technology and society and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows benefit and drawback?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Explain that students are going to create a poster to advertise their body technology. Elicit ideas for the advert (e.g. a diagram, exciting adjectives, explanations of its benefits). They can work in the same groups or in pairs. Set a time limit.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Display the adverts around the walls of the classroom. Give each student some sticky notes. Ask them to read each other’s adverts and to write a comment on a sticky note. Discuss what makes a useful comment, and the importance of respectful feedback.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.
