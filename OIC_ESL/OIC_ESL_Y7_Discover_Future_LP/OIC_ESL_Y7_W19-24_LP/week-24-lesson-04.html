Remind students what Dean Kamen said about failure in innovation (‘Inevitably when you try to innovate, most of the time you’re going to fail.’ – see Week 22, Lesson 5).

Show students three to five pictures of technologies that have been launched but have failed (search online for ‘technology failures’). Put them in small groups and invite them to guess what each picture shows.

Discuss responses as a whole class. Give a brief summary of each failed technology. Say: It is estimated that 90% or more of inventions are unsuccessful!

Main activity

Ask: Why do you think so many technological inventions fail?

Turn to ODF SB 2 p.76 ex.1. Put students in small groups to discuss whether they think each product succeeded or failed, and why. Remind students of the discussion strategies they learned from ODF SB 2 p.77.

Play the audio for ODF SB 2 p.76 ex.2 (track 2.02) for students to check their ideas. Were they correct? Play the audio again and ask students to make notes about why each product succeeded or failed. Check in pairs before going over answers as a class.

Show students some tech ideas of the future (search online for ‘future technology’). Put students in small groups. Explain that they are going to choose a future technology that they think will be successful and create a short presentation to ‘sell’ it to the class. Encourage them to choose technologies which are more unusual or strange.

Allow students about 20 minutes to work on their mini-presentations. Remind them of the success criteria they created in Week 22, Lesson 2 (balloon debate activity), which they can apply again now. The presentations:

should try to be persuasive,

can include facts as well as ideas from their own imagination,

should consider the reasons why some technology fails,

must involve two speakers.
