PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about education and learning; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford introductory activity: Write the following anagrams on the board: ‘GIESDN’, ‘BRETOPLA’, ‘YTRETBA ELIF’, ‘LEUVA’ (design, portable, battery life, value). If needed, give students a clue: they are standards to consider when choosing a product.

Oxford introductory activity: Put students in pairs to work out the anagrams, then check answers as a class.

Oxford introductory activity: Elicit what kinds of product you would apply these four criteria to if you were selecting one to buy (suggested answer: small electronic items).

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “A study plan can include short daily revision sessions.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about evidence for a learning claim. Textbook anchor: 2. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Explain to the class that they are going to listen to a discussion between three students about two different video cameras to buy for their school (see ODF WB 2 p.69). Elicit ideas about what they might discuss, and pre-teach/revise the lesson’s vocabulary.

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Write the name of the two cameras on the board: ‘Camera A – BX 405’ and ‘Camera B – EK 500’. Students write these down as headings for two columns.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about education and learning. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Instruct students to note the pros and cons under each camera name. Play the audio (track 7.01), then tell students to compare answers with a partner. Play the audio once more.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Check answers as a class. Then ask: Which camera do they choose and why? Do you agree with their choice?

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to education and learning and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows evidence for a learning claim?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Review ways of emphasizing comparisons using ‘much’, ‘far’, ‘a bit’, ‘not (nearly) as’, ‘There’s nothing more [important] than …’, ‘What could be more [important] than …’.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Give students handouts of the transcript. Play the audio again, asking them to identify examples of comparisons, in particular those which use adverbials for emphasis. Allow them time to check answers with a partner, then go over answers as a class.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining evidence for a learning claim in the context of education and learning. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Tell students they are now going to compare and evaluate two different models of a product themselves. Show them some information about two models of five to eight items which will interest them (e.g. smart phones, laptops, game consoles, solar chargers).

Oxford main activity — independent application: Put students in pairs. Ask them to compare the two different models of one of the items and to discuss which one they would choose for themselves, or for different members of their families, and why. Encourage students to use language from the lesson.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Invite each pair to report back their conclusions to the class. Ask students to bring the transcripts to the next lesson.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of education and learning, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: When completing the listening activity, provide less able students with key words to listen out for.

Oxford differentiation: Ask more able students to also identify which speaker makes each point.

Oxford differentiation: Less confident students can refer to the language used in the transcript when completing the speaking task.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able students to explain their choices in the speaking task in detail.

Oxford additional task: Complete ex.3 in ODF WB 2 p.69.

Oxford additional task: Students can write a recommendation explaining which product (between the two evaluated) is better and why.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about education and learning and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain evidence for a learning claim without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have listened to a discussion evaluating different video cameras. They have studied the language used and then practised it in their own evaluation activity.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
