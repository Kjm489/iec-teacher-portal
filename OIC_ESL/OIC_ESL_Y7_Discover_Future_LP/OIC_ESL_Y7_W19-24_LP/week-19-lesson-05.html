PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about Writing a coherent response; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Does the evidence support the answer, or is it only a guess?

Oxford introductory activity: In pairs, tell students to read through the discussion board in ODF SB 2 p.66, focusing in particular on Sai’s final response. Ask: What are the features of Sai’s response? Do you think he responds to his classmates effectively? Why / Why not?

Oxford introductory activity: Share ideas as a whole class. Elicit that Sai acknowledged and was able to respond successfully to his classmates’ comments, giving his own opinion.

Oxford introductory activity: Refer students to the writing strategy on p.66 to familiarize themselves with what is expected in a coherent written response.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “This example relates to Writing a coherent response; the evidence is in the text or image.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about a relevant example and supporting evidence. Textbook anchor: 2. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Does the evidence support the answer, or is it only a guess?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Tell students that in today’s lesson they are going to plan and write their own response to a discussion board. Divide the class in half. Give one half of the class (A) the same text in ODF SB 2 p.66, and give the other half (B) the incomplete text in ODF WB 2 p.60.

Oxford main activity — teacher model: B students will need to complete ex.3 on p.60 first.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about Writing a coherent response. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Does the evidence support the answer, or is it only a guess?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: In AA/BB pairs, students read through their text and plan a coherent response. Encourage them to note down the name of each person and their opinion, in the order that they appear, then think about if they agree or disagree with what they say.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Remind the class that they can use linkers of addition to make multiple points in response to different people. Elicit a few examples from the text (e.g. ‘as well as’ and ‘what’s more’).

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Give students enough time to write the first draft of their response independently.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to Writing a coherent response and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows a relevant example and supporting evidence?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Responding to every point made on the discussion board is challenging, so students will have to think deeply about how to best structure their response so it reads coherently. Reassure them that they are in the stretch zone – the difficulty level of the task will help to develop their writing skills.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: While students work, add the writing checklist in ODF WB 2 p.61 to the board for them to refer to when they have finished.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: In AB pairs, students exchange their first drafts. Tell them to read through and peer-edit each other’s work. They shouldn’t correct spelling and grammar mistakes, but underline them and indicate the error with a code.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining a relevant example and supporting evidence in the context of Writing a coherent response. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does the evidence support the answer, or is it only a guess?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Suggested code: Sp = spelling, T = tense, WO = word order is wrong, ᴧ = missing word, prep = preposition, WW = wrong word

Oxford main activity — independent application: Students discuss their mistakes and recommended revisions with their partner. Remind them that identifying and making mistakes is part of the learning process – they will continue improving with practice.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Students revise their work based on the feedback received.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of Writing a coherent response, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able students to peer-edit less able students’ work, to offer support, and to carefully explain their mistakes.

Oxford additional task: A students and B students can swap discussion boards and practise writing another coherent response.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about Writing a coherent response and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain a relevant example and supporting evidence without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have read a ‘thread’ about a school-related topic on a discussion board, then planned and written a coherent response. They have peer-edited each other’s work, providing helpful and constructive feedback, before revising their response accordingly.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
