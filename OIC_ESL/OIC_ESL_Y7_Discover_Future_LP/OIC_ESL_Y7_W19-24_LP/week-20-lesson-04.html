Original Oxford Week 20 source text — complete reference

Main activity

Explain that this week, students will work in small groups on a project of their choice. They can extend their learning into an area of particular interest to them, and practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way that they could apply to a real-world setting.

Students will focus on a project related to the topics ‘Travel’ or ‘Education’. They should work in small groups with shared areas of interest.

Students can choose their own project, linked to either topic, or they can develop a project from one of the options below.

Encourage students to use the IDEAS to Action framework, which they will be familiar with from their Global Skills Projects lessons. Remind them that this is a smaller-scale project, so they may need to adapt and modify it to suit their needs.

Students will be responsible for the planning and management of their project. This should involve:

suggesting and allocating specific tasks

researching and drafting creative work

reviewing drafts and contributing constructive ideas and suggestions

joint presentation (the following week)

formative self-assessment and peer-assessment (the following week)

Students can present their work in a way that best suits their project, depending on resources and time available. See how creative they can be with their presentations. Here is a non-exhaustive list of different kinds of presentation:

A speech or oral retelling (perhaps supported by visual resources, such as a PowerPoint presentation, photographs, a campaign poster, a model, artefacts, graphs or charts, etc.)

A pitch (to sell a product or service)

A skills demonstration

A debate

An art/photography exhibition or display

An interview with an expert in the field of enquiry

A short film or documentary

A creative performance

Remind students that they have five days to complete the planning, research, and execution of their project. At the end of Day 1, teachers could look over each group’s plan to ensure that they have allocated tasks appropriately and know what to accomplish each day.

Topic 1 (Travel)

Reducing our carbon footprint: Elicit from students that people travel by aeroplane for many different reasons – for work, family, and tourism – but unfortunately this produces a high amount of carbon emissions, which contribute to global warming. In small groups, set students the following task.

Research to what extent air travel is having a negative impact on the environment.

Consider what we can do to reduce our carbon footprint when travelling.

Calculate your personal carbon footprint (online search: ‘carbon footprint calculator’).

Design a more eco-friendly form of transport – this could be a new and innovative vehicle, or an adapted aeroplane design to make it more sustainable. Think about:

its size;

how many passengers it will take, and how their seating will be arranged (e.g. first-class seating often takes up a lot of space but accommodates few people, therefore wasting a lot of energy);

how far it will travel;

what fuel it will use;

(if applicable) the type of food and drink served, and its packaging.

Present your research and final design to the class in a visually interesting way.

Topic 2 (Education)

My dream timetable: In pairs or independently, design a school timetable which sets out your ideal fortnight of learning. You can be as creative as possible, but in order to complete the task in a meaningful way, first reflect on how you learn best, if learning should always be entirely pleasurable (e.g. working in the ‘stretch zone’ can sometimes feel challenging and uncomfortable, but what are its benefits?), and what skills you would like to develop. Also consider:

which school subjects (including more unusual subjects) you would like to study and how frequently;

which extra-curricular activities you would like to get involved in;

the breakdown of hours, including the duration of lessons, school opening and closing times, and breaks throughout the day;

the learning style(s) that would best suit the lessons you choose.

Present your dream timetable to the rest of the class in your chosen format, explaining your reasoning behind each decision you (and your partner) made.

Should education be free for everyone? In pairs or independently, reflect on whether education should be free and available to everyone, from early years (Nursery and Reception) through to higher education at university. Think about the facilities and/or opportunities that paid-for education provides and whether this should be a universal right. Complete the following tasks:

Design a survey for a group of classmates, analyse the results, and present them graphically.

Write a report in which you present both sides of the argument, then arrive to a final conclusion.
