Original Oxford Week 24 source text — complete reference

Introductory activity

Write the word ‘TECHNOLOGY’ vertically on the board. Put students in pairs and give them two minutes to find a topic-related word which starts with each letter (or as many as possible). Go over responses as a class.

Main activity

Review the five main themes around technology studied in this unit (see ODF TG 2 p.134). Say: We need to be aware of the positive and negative sides of technology so that we can make the most of progress without losing our humanity.

Explain that students are going to use the last part of the unit to think about how we can make sure that technological advances make our world better for everyone.

Show pictures of people who represent different sections of local or national society, for example: children, the elderly, people in rural communities, professionals, manual workers, city dwellers, and people with disabilities. Ask: How might different technologies make life better for these groups?

Put students in small groups. Each group chooses one section of society (or you could allocate them). Encourage students to imagine an invention that could help these people in some way. The inventions don’t need to be based on current technology – remind students of their creative ideas for using a potato, and their ideas about superheroes. Tell them that they will deliver a short presentation about their innovation and how it could benefit this group. Set a time limit for the presentation.

Remind students of the success criteria they created, then allow them time to devise their innovation and plan their talk. Encourage them to consider:

the method to present their invention – a diagram, a description, acting out a scenario, or their own idea;

the problem they want to address and how their invention will help;

language from the unit they could use;

useful phrases which will get the audience’s attention.

When students are ready, invite groups to present their innovation to the class. If time is short, divide the class in two and run two presentations simultaneously. Ensure all group members are involved. Listening students can write a short comment about what they enjoyed about the presentation on a sticky note for each group to read after.

After all the groups have presented, hold a class vote on which group had the best idea.

Additional task

Students can write a paragraph on how technology will benefit all members of society in the future, using ideas from their own or other students’ presentations.

Learning review

Students have reviewed their learning from the unit. In groups, they have prepared and given a presentation on a brand-new innovation that will benefit a particular section of society.

Differentiation

To support less able students, prepare some ideas to feed in to support them when planning their invention.

Give less confident students phrases and functional language items to use in their presentation.

Challenge more confident students to set themselves a personal goal for delivering the presentation.

This is the 15th lesson in the unit ‘Technology and us’.
