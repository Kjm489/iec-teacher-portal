1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about communication; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford introductory activity: Divide the class into pairs, A and B. Ask A students to close their eyes. Give B students their instructions to read in silence: Talk to your partner about your favourite show. Try to tell them as much information as you can. Don’t let them lead you off your topic.

Oxford introductory activity: Repeat for A students. Their instructions are: Tell your partner about your favourite artist. Find out who their favourite artist is. Get this information by the end of the conversation.

Oxford introductory activity: Tell students they have two minutes for the activity. Stop them when it’s clear that they have realized that they’re talking at cross-purposes. Ask them about their experience of the activity. Explain that today’s lesson is about how to have successful conversations!

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “I use a formal greeting when writing to a school leader.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about audience and purpose. Textbook anchor: 2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min Teacher model grounded in the original activity: Refer students to the transcript handouts from the previous lesson (Week 23, Lesson 3). ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Refer students to the transcript handouts from the previous lesson (Week 23, Lesson 3). Explain that this is a well-structured discussion that moves through different stages. Focus on the first three turns of the conversation. If needed, listen again to this section of the audio. Put students in pairs to identify the purpose of this section. Check responses as a class (the opening section sets the topic and aims of the discussion).

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Ask students to remain in their pairs to identify the different stages of the rest of the conversation. If appropriate, play the audio. Discuss ideas as a class, accepting all answers that students can justify/explain. List the different stages on the board.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about communication. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Elicit any other stages or functions that you might expect in a different type of discussion.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Refer students to ex.4 in ODF WB 2 p.69 for them to complete. Together, compare these phrases with students’ own ideas. In pairs, ask students to decide a good order for the different stages or functions. Check answers as a class.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to communication and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows audience and purpose?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Put students in pairs to find words and phrases in the transcript that Jessica, Isobel, and Rashid use to express the different functions. Fast finishers can also suggest their own ideas. In turn, invite students to come to the board and write phrases next to the relevant function. Discuss responses as a class, encouraging students to listen to each other’s reasons to reach a final, correct list.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Using the audio, help students to identify the pronunciation features of each phrase. Focus on the stressed and weak syllables, intonation patterns, and elisions between words.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining audience and purpose in the context of communication. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Give students product information for three to five models of a technological product to evaluate in groups of three or four. Allocate different roles for the discussion: Student A makes sure the discussion follows a logical order; while Students B, C (and D) give their opinions and make sure everyone has an opportunity to speak.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Allow students five minutes to review the product information and to think about the functional language they will try to use. They then hold the discussions.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Praise students for their effort to include the newly learned phrases in group work.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of communication, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, encourage them to prepare notes before the discussion.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to focus mainly on stress and intonation.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students by making them Student A in the discussion activity.

Oxford additional task: Complete ex.5 in ODF WB 2 p.69.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about communication and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain audience and purpose without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have studied the features and language of effective discussions. They have practised the pronunciation of this language, before using it in their own discussions.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
