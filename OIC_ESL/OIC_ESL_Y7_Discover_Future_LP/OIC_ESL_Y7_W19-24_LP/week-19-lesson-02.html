Introductory activity

Assign different parts of the classroom to ‘Rules’, ‘Recommendations’, and ‘Permission’.

Pre-prepare ten statements or questions with modals about school life. For example:

Students have to be kind and show respect.

Students should finish school at lunchtime on Fridays.

May I drink from my water bottle?

Before we couldn’t use tablets in the classroom.

Read out each one. Tell students to move to the part of the classroom that corresponds to the modal in the statement or question. Alternatively, they can assign different actions to the different categories.

Elicit reasons from one or two students after each statement.

Remind the class that they need to practise new grammar over and over again in order to master it.

Main activity

Explain to students that in today’s lesson they are going to create their own set of rules and recommendations for their school. There are three aims:

To retain the school rules that students think are important, and conducive to a safe and happy school environment.

To think of new rules or recommendations that will enhance their learning experience, will make them feel more motivated, and will promote positive wellbeing.

To use a range of modal verbs to express rules, recommendations, and ability/permission.

They can choose to do the writing task independently, in pairs, or in school groups.

Make clear that the rules shouldn’t be silly (e.g. We must eat crisps in every lesson.) but can be interesting and creative, based on students’ needs and interests (e.g. We can explore new and unusual sports in P.E. lessons. / We should be allowed to take 15-minute mindfulness breaks when we’re feeling anxious and can’t focus on work.).

Establish that students will have different ideas to suit their strengths, weaknesses, and personalities, and will therefore be motivated in different ways. This is OK!

Encourage students to present their final draft in a creative way. For example, they could create a slideshow, make a poster or scroll, or create illustrated cards (one card for each rule or recommendation).

Pair up or group together students for them to present their work. Listening students can vote on their favourite rule or recommendation at the end of each mini presentation.

Additional tasks

Compile the most popular rules and recommendations to make a whole-class version.

In small groups, students can make a list of unusual subjects that they would like to learn at school. Refer them to ex.4 in ODF WB 2 p.56.

Learning review

Students have created their own school rules and recommendations using modal verbs. They have presented them to their peers in a creative way.

Differentiation

Guide less confident students’ thinking by asking them to consider different aspects of school life: school subjects/lessons, exams, technology, clothes, activities, teaching style.

Challenge more able students to rank their rules and recommendations in order of importance, and to explain their reasoning behind each one.

Context
This is the seventh lesson in the unit ‘Education’.
