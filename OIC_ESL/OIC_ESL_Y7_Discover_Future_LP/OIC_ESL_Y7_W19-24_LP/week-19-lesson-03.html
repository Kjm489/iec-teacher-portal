Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford introductory activity: Read out the questionnaire statements one by one (A–G) in ODF SB 2 p.64.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “A study plan can include short daily revision sessions.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about evidence for a learning claim. Textbook anchor: 2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min Teacher model grounded in the original activity: Pair up students. Ask them to turn to the questionnaire in ODF SB 2 p.64. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

At the end, tell them to calculate their total score for each learning style to find out how they learn best.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about education and learning. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Allow students time to complete the questionnaire and discuss their findings with their partner. Ask: Are you surprised by your result? Did you know that this is how you learn effectively? How will you use this information to help you at school?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: On the board, write ‘Aural’, ‘Visual’, ‘Logical’, ‘Verbal’, ‘Physical’, ‘Social’, and ‘Solitary’. In a whole-class feedback session, review each learning method in turn and record students’ individual results in a tally next to each one.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to education and learning and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows evidence for a learning claim?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Have a short discussion. Ask: What is the most popular learning method? What is the least popular learning method? What do these results tell us about our class?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Form new pairs of students. Tell them to read through the questionnaire again and work together to learn the characteristics of each learning style. They can choose the best way of doing this (e.g. by taking notes, drawing pictures/visualizing concepts, using mnemonic devices, reading out loud, etc.).

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining evidence for a learning claim in the context of education and learning. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Tell pairs to close their books and test each other on each learning style. Student A writes a learning style on their whiteboard (e.g. ‘Logical’) and Student B says as much information as they can remember about this style: e.g. Logical learners are problem-solvers. They like to think of different ways to work out a problem. They are often organized and write lists to prioritize tasks.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Bring the class back together. Do a similar activity, but in reverse. Give a definition and students write on their whiteboard the type of learner you are referring to. Do a quick countdown and ask the class to hold up their whiteboards. After each one, invite a volunteer with the correct answer to explain how they knew it was a [verbal] learner.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of education and learning, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: In the introductory activity, allow less confident students to follow the text in their books.

Oxford differentiation: Pair up similar abilities in pairwork so students can stretch each other and work at their own pace.

Oxford additional task: Students can reflect on their questionnaire result. Ask them to write down three objectives for maximizing their potential and applying this learning method to their future studies.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.9–10 in ODF WB 2 p.58.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about education and learning and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain evidence for a learning claim without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have recognized their preferred learning style in a questionnaire and reflected on the result. In pairs, they have helped each other to memorize different learning methods.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.

Context
This is the eighth lesson in the unit ‘Education’.
Students will develop their listening, speaking, and reading skills by answering a questionnaire and analysing the results. They will reflect on how they learn best.
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
