PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about communication; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford introductory activity: Create a ‘story chain’ with students. Start the chain with an ‘if’ clause: e.g. If I had a holiday tomorrow, … Nominate a student to repeat your ‘if’ clause, and to complete the sentence with their own idea: e.g. If I had a holiday tomorrow, I’d go to the beach.

Oxford introductory activity: The next student then creates a new ‘if’ clause using the new idea, adding their own consequence: e.g. If I went to the beach, I’d swim in the sea.

Oxford introductory activity: Continue going around the class, supporting students with ideas or the form if necessary.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “I use a formal greeting when writing to a school leader.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about audience and purpose. Textbook anchor: 2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min Teacher model grounded in the original activity: Turn to ex.5 in ODF SB 2 p.85. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Turn to ex.5 in ODF SB 2 p.85. Individually, ask students to read the five situations and decide if each is probable or improbable.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about communication. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: In small groups, students discuss their opinions and talk about the possible consequences of each situation. Encourage them to think of as many consequences for each situation as possible.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: If possible, pick out some examples from students’ conversations when they needed some thinking time, and share them anonymously on the board. Alternatively, ask: When you’re having a conversation, do you sometimes need some thinking time? What can you say to buy some time? Use examples from L1 if necessary.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to communication and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows audience and purpose?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Elicit or teach some examples of fillers which are appropriate for your students, such as sounds like ‘er’, ‘hmm’, ‘um’; short phrases like ‘well’, ‘I mean’, ‘you know’; and words which add meaning like ‘actually’, ‘basically’, ‘seriously’. Ask each student to choose three to five fillers to use, and to write them on blank cards or slips of paper.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Give pairs of students time to discuss the questions in ODF SB 2 p.85 ex.4. Encourage them to use the fillers on their cards as many times as they can.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining audience and purpose in the context of communication. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Ask students to return to the same groups and focus on the same issue as in Lesson 2. Ask: If you were the leader of your country, what policies would you have to bring an end to this global problem? Groups discuss and agree on three to five policies which would help to improve the situation.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Regroup students so around four to five issues are represented in each group. Ask students to share their policies in turn and to agree on the top three policies they would choose to implement. Hold a whole-class feedback session.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of communication, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to refer to the grammar explanation in ODF SB 2 p.127.

Oxford differentiation: To challenge more able students, ask them to use as many different fillers as possible in the discussion tasks.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, they can prepare some notes to help them during the final discussion task.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able students, give them a role in the discussion task to ensure that all the group members are given an equal chance to speak.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.7–8 in ODF WB 2 p.77.

Oxford additional task: Groups can write their policy proposals as a message to their government.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about communication and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain audience and purpose without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have practised using conditional structures appropriately. They have taken part in a group discussion and negotiation.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
