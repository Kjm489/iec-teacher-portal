PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining strength, evidence and improvement in the context of project and reflection. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does constructive feedback include a useful suggestion for improvement?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Bring the class back together. Say a short sentence containing a word students don’t know, for instance: I’m going to talk to you about paraphrasing. Explain: Sometimes when we’re speaking, we want to use a word that we know the listeners don’t understand, or we can’t remember how to say something. In other words, we need to paraphrase. Teach/Elicit useful phrases to introduce a paraphrase, such as ‘What I mean is …’; ‘In other words …’; ‘It’s when/how/where …’.

Oxford main activity — independent application: In their groups, students identify words, phrases, or ideas that will be unfamiliar to their classmates and will need to be paraphrased. Give them time to prepare their paraphrases.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Put students in pairs with someone from a different group. They deliver their talks as a practice run, giving each other constructive feedback to help them improve.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

Previous lesson directions (reference)

Main activity

The presentations should contain attention-grabbing and factually accurate information about the problem, which will encourage listeners to take responsibility for the issue.

Give students a strict time limit for their research, by the end of which they must have around ten useful and motivating facts or pieces of information. They must plan their time and organize their searches. To support, supply groups with a list of useful and appropriate websites (and if possible, limit their searches to these sites), for example:

Give groups time to finish planning in note form, with the success criteria in mind.

Bring the class back together. Say a short sentence containing a word students don’t know, for instance: I’m going to talk to you about paraphrasing.
