PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about world problems and action; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Is a solution an action to address a problem or a description of its colour?

Oxford introductory activity: Prepare a list of different text genres that students might encounter in a normal day. As you call out each genre, students stand up if they have read a text like that today, or remain seated if not. For each genre, ask one or two students a short question about the text, for example: Where did you see it? Give me an adjective to describe the (poem/advert/text message, etc.).

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “Reducing food waste can help conserve resources.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about problem, cause and solution. Textbook anchor: 2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min Teacher model grounded in the original activity: Elicit different text genres that students are familiar with. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Is a solution an action to address a problem or a description of its colour?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Elicit different text genres that students are familiar with. Show a list of features of a range of text genres, with some features applying to more than one genre. In small groups, tell students to allocate each feature to one or more genre. Ask for one genre from each group, discussing any areas of uncertainty.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about world problems and action. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Is a solution an action to address a problem or a description of its colour?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Turn to ODF SB 2 p.83. Elicit quickly what genre each text is.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Review with students the aims of scan reading, and strategies for scanning, which they looked at in Unit 7. Refer to p.82 ex.1. Ask students to scan the two texts to complete the exercise, then give them time to check their answers in pairs.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to world problems and action and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows problem, cause and solution?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Allocate each pair either the blog post or the report. They read their allocated text and answer the questions on p.82 ex.3.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Reorganize the pairs, so each student works with someone who read a different text from them. Ask them to compare their answers, then discuss the questions on p.82 ex.2, finding examples in the texts to support their answers. This activity will stretch students’ thinking to extend their understanding of genre. Share feedback as a whole class.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining problem, cause and solution in the context of world problems and action. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Is a solution an action to address a problem or a description of its colour?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Ask: Which genre do you expect to be more reliable? Why? How can you recognize whether a text is being objective or subjective? Give some examples. How could this blog post and report be made more reliable?

Oxford main activity — independent application: In pairs, encourage students to note down how one or both texts could be made more reliable.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of world problems and action, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able students, give them a wider range of genres of which to identify the features.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, provide them with a set of strategies for scanning, encouraging them to focus on the most useful.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to focus on completing the reading tasks set, and not to worry about words or sections of the text that are difficult, if these are not relevant.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students by asking them to find data about volunteering among young people in their country.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.4–5 in ODF SB 2 p.82.

Oxford additional task: Students can also discuss the questions in ex.6 in ODF SB 2 p.82.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about world problems and action and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain problem, cause and solution without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have practised their scan and detailed reading skills. They have focused on the features of different genres to consolidate their understanding of their purpose and reliability.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
