3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about education and learning. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to education and learning and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows evidence for a learning claim?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Now ask pairs to complete ex.3 on p.96. They then swap their answers with another pair, to check for any differences and decide on the correct versions. Encourage them to think carefully about all the elements that change between direct and reported speech.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Challenge pairs to create their own reported speech sentences about facts William did and didn’t say in his talk.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining evidence for a learning claim in the context of education and learning. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does a learning strategy explain how you will study, or only where you live?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Form groups of four to five students. Each group is given, or chooses, a topic to research about their classmates – for example, how they keep healthy or their entertainment preferences. Instruct groups to devise five to ten questions to ask. One student from each group will then have one minute to ask as many classmates as possible a question. They can’t take notes, but will report back as many answers as they can using reported speech. Their group members write down the answers. Repeat with another student asking a different question, until all the questions have been asked.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Encourage groups to write a short summary of their findings.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of education and learning, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Advise less confident students to use only simple reporting verbs (e.g. ‘say’, ‘ask’) until they are more confident.

Oxford differentiation: Supply more confident students with a wider range of reporting verbs (e.g. ‘persuade’, ‘deny’, ‘promise’, ‘suggest’, ‘encourage’) to research and write into sentences.

Oxford differentiation: Refer less able students to the grammar explanation in ODF SB 2 p.129.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to write more complex questions to report back on.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.5–6 in ODF WB 2 p.87.

Oxford additional task: Complete some or all of exs.1–3 in ODF SB 2 p.129.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about education and learning and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain evidence for a learning claim without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have compared a written and a spoken text. They have practised using reported speech in writing and speaking activities.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
