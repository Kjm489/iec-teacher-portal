PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about goals and aspirations; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Is “I want to improve” more specific than a goal with an action and deadline?

Oxford introductory activity: Write some or all of the following anagrams on the board: ‘notum vrtseee’ (Mount Everest); ‘ahnoatmr sde beassl’ (Marathon des Sables); ‘blone ziper’ (Nobel Prize); ‘mcyeada draaw’ (Academy Award); ‘eveden bloge’ (Vendée Globe). You may choose to omit the goals that are not familiar to students.

Oxford introductory activity: In pairs, students work out the correct words (see ODF SB 2 p.93 ex.2). Talk through the answers as a class and refer them to the page in their student book.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

Previous lesson directions (reference)

Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “I will practise speaking twice a week to build confidence.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about specific goal and action. Oxford main activity — teacher model: Play the Factflix video, so students can check their answers. Discuss as a class.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about goals and aspirations. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Is “I want to improve” more specific than a goal with an action and deadline?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Ask: What kind of goals are Sofia and Zach discussing? (long-term goals, dreams, ambitions) What do these goals have in common? (they are very difficult/challenging, they take a long time to achieve, not many people achieve them)

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Ask: What is Sofia’s or Zach’s opinion about each of these goals? Play the video again, pausing after they discuss each goal. In pairs, students discuss each speaker’s opinion, referring to how they speak (intonation) as well as what they say. Discuss each goal as a class, listening again if necessary, before moving on to the next section of the video. Encourage students to repeat some of the more expressive phrases or sentences.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to goals and aspirations and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows specific goal and action?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Ask: Do you find any of these goals inspirational? What kind of goals are most important to you in your life – big, ambitious dreams or achievable goals? Divide students into two groups according to which they consider most important. Create a third group of students for those who think a mix of both goals is the best option.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: In these groups (or subgroups if appropriate), students discuss and list their arguments to justify their opinions. For each point, they should provide an example and explain how the example supports their point. Remind them that they are in the stretch zone.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining specific goal and action in the context of goals and aspirations. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Is “I want to improve” more specific than a goal with an action and deadline?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Regroup students, with at least one student representing each point of view. Review the work students did in Week 23 on well-structured discussions, and refer to the success criteria created then. The aim of today’s discussion is to listen to each other’s arguments and to identify any they agree with. If appropriate, review phrases for responding in a discussion (e.g. ‘That’s a good point!’; ‘I see what you mean’; ‘I take your point, but …’.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Encourage students to share their opinions and to note down any arguments from their peers that they like or agree with.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of goals and aspirations, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Refer less confident students to ODF SB 2 p.93 ex.2 while completing the anagram activity.

Oxford differentiation: When watching the video for the first time, remind less able students that they are only listening for the answers to the questions. Advise them to focus on the numbers they hear.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to use their language and intonation to express their opinions during the discussion activities.

Oxford additional task: Tell students to choose one of the awards/goals from the Factflix video to research. Alternatively, they could research another example of a difficult or challenging goal. They take notes to share their findings in groups.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about goals and aspirations and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain specific goal and action without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have listened for details and practised recognizing feeling in a dialogue. They have developed arguments to support their opinions in a discussion activity.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
