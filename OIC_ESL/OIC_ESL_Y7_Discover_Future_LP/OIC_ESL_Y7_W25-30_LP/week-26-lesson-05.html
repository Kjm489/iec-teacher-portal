PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about communication; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford introductory activity: Write ‘computer buddy’ on the board. In pairs or small groups, give students 30 seconds to guess what they think this could be. Encourage them to make as many guesses as they can.

Oxford introductory activity: Elicit responses one by one from groups. For each guess a group makes, they can ask you one Yes/No question. The group that guesses the answer wins. (A computer buddy is a person who helps others with their IT skills.)

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “I use a formal greeting when writing to a school leader.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about audience and purpose. Textbook anchor: 2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min Teacher model grounded in the original activity: Prepare an informal version of the email in ODF WB 2 p.80 ex.1. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Prepare an informal version of the email in ODF WB 2 p.80 ex.1. This should include both informal language and functional exponents, as well as being less clearly structured and containing fewer examples.

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Tell students you are going to read them an email applying to volunteer as a computer buddy. Read your informal email aloud.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about communication. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Elicit from students that the email is too informal.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Show the text of the informal email to the class. In pairs, ask students to identify aspects of the email which they think are in the wrong register.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Refer students to the email in ODF WB 2 p.80. Instruct pairs to compare the two versions of the email, and to identify the formal language and key features of formal emails.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to communication and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows audience and purpose?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Bring the class back together. Summarize the key features on the board.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Ask students to complete ex.3 in ODF WB 2 p.80.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining audience and purpose in the context of communication. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — independent application: In the same pairs as the previous lesson, tell students to revisit their draft emails. Give them time to write a final draft, focusing on making sure that the email is in a formal register. Remind them to substitute words and phrases as necessary.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Distribute the emails around the class, encouraging students to add a positive comment to each other’s work.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Praise pairs for supporting each other to complete the task (regardless of the success of the final product).

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of communication, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to provide formal alternatives to the language in the informal email before they look at the example in ODF WB 2 p.80.

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident students, supply them with a list of features of informal/formal texts to refer to while they compare the two versions of the email.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to find synonyms to replace key words or phrases from the sample emails, rather than copying language from the texts.

Oxford additional task: In groups, students can discuss the questions in ODF SB 2 p.82 ex.6.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about communication and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain audience and purpose without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have focused on features of formal language, before working together in pairs to produce a final draft of their email.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
