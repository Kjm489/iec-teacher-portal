Original Oxford Week 27 source text — complete reference

Main activity

Explain that this week, students will work in small groups on a project of their choice. They can extend their learning into an area of particular interest to them, and practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way that they could apply to a real-world setting.

Students will focus on a project related to the topics ‘Technology and us’ or ‘World problems’. They should work in small groups with shared areas of interest.

Students can choose their own project, linked to either topic, or they can develop a project from one of the options below.

Encourage students to use the IDEAS to Action framework, which they will be familiar with from their Global Skills Projects lessons. Remind them that this is a smaller-scale project, so they may need to adapt and modify it to suit their needs.

Students will be responsible for the planning and management of their project. This should involve:

suggesting and allocating specific tasks

researching and drafting creative work

reviewing drafts and contributing constructive ideas and suggestions

joint presentation (the following week)

formative self-assessment and peer-assessment (the following week)

Students can present their work in a way that best suits their project, depending on resources and time available. See how creative they can be with their presentations. Here is a non-exhaustive list of different kinds of presentation:

A speech or oral retelling (perhaps supported by visual resources, such as a PowerPoint presentation, photographs, a campaign poster, a model, artefacts, graphs or charts, etc.)

A pitch (to sell a product or service)

A skills demonstration

A debate

An art/photography exhibition or display

An interview with an expert in the field of enquiry

A short film or documentary

A creative performance

Remind students that they have five days to complete the planning, research, and execution of their project. At the end of Day 1, teachers could look over each group’s plan to ensure that they have allocated tasks appropriately and know what to accomplish each day.

Topic 1 (Technology and us)

Invent! Design and create an entry for an invention competition such as the Global Cardboard Challenge (search online for ‘cardboard challenge’). Possible ideas:

Research the competition website for the rules and tips or advice.

Evaluate previous entries and identify good ideas to adopt or modify.

Propose different designs, evaluating them for their advantages/disadvantages and identifying any potential problems and their solutions. Discuss your designs and choose the best one for your team.

Build your competition entry and be prepared to demonstrate it to others, and to explain its purpose and how you developed it.

The future human: Produce a piece of creative writing about what human beings might be like 100 years from now.

You could write a short story, play script, or film script; or produce a graphic short story or poem.

Think about what different body technologies people might be using, but also how our lives in general may be different. What will the natural world be like? How will we be travelling, living, and communicating?

There are lots of films and stories about the future. Do any of these have ideas that you could borrow or adapt for your creative writing?

Consider how you will present your creative writing. You could create a storyboard with all of your ideas and inspiration to form part of your presentation, as well as performing your piece.

Topic 2 (World problems)

A fundraising event: Organize a fundraising event for a charity that is important to you.

Choose a local organization for your fundraising. Who will you ask to participate in your event? The organization will need to appeal to them as well.

Think about what kinds of fundraising activities people in your age group like to do, and which could be organized within school – maybe the ones you have discussed in class, or you might have other ideas.

Consider who you need to contact about setting up this event, and communicate with them.

How will you publicize your event? How will you encourage people to participate, and to donate to the charity? Think about different means of communication and how you can use them most effectively.

You will present your project as a plan for a fundraising event. Consider how you will do this. Outline all the elements that need to be considered, such as the venue, the programme, the publicity, and the participants.

Activate! Carry out and present a survey on awareness of global issues within your school.

Think about what you want to find out – which global issues are people in your school aware of? How concerned are they about them? Are younger people more worried about some issues than older people, or vice versa?

What questions are you going to ask? Think about the wording of your questions, as you don’t want to influence anyone’s answers.

The kinds of question you can ask may also be affected by the way you are going to collect your data. Think about the different options – online survey, paper questionnaire, interviews. Which will work best in the time you have got?

While you are collecting your data, decide how you will present your results. Will you create graphs – if so, what kinds of graph? On paper or electronically? What materials will you need? You will also need to talk about your findings in an interesting way – think about what you have learned about delivering interesting talks.

You could also think about any follow-up activities; for example, you could have a class discussion about the results of your survey, or about how to make people in your school more aware of global issues.
