3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about project and reflection. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Does constructive feedback include a useful suggestion for improvement?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Refer to the writing strategy in ODF SB 2 p.101. Tell students to complete ex.6 in pairs.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Elicit how the writer introduces the conclusion (‘to sum up’), as well as any alternative phrases (e.g. ‘in conclusion’, ‘in summary’, ‘overall’).

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Display between two and four example conclusions for essays on the same topic. These should represent a range of more- and less-effective conclusions. In pairs, students discuss each conclusion and decide how effective it is, and why.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to project and reflection and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows strength, evidence and improvement?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Give students time to review their essay plans from the previous lesson. Individually, they plan their introduction and conclusion, checking ideas with a partner as necessary.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Allocate enough time in the lesson for students to write a first draft of their essay on a computer, focusing on the function and structure of each paragraph. Make the drafts available among the class (e.g. using a file-sharing program or via your school’s platform).

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Assign each draft to at least two other students, or pairs of students. Tell them to read each other’s essays and:

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining strength, evidence and improvement in the context of project and reflection. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Does constructive feedback include a useful suggestion for improvement?

Oxford main activity — independent application: identify and comment on a strength or positive point about the essay;

Oxford main activity — independent application: identify something that could be improved, and suggest how to do this.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Students review the comments on their drafts and reflect on how helpful they are. Invite volunteers to share their reactions to their peers’ comments. Remind them of the value of feedback – it is a positive tool to help us to improve, not to make us feel bad.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of project and reflection, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, encourage them to refer back to ODF SB 2 p.78 and the work they did on introductions.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able students to review their own essays and identify any repeated vocabulary or structures, and to find alternative ways to express these.

Oxford differentiation: Advise less confident students to use language and structures from the sample essay, but to adapt it to express their own ideas.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to write a longer essay by including more than one argument supporting their opinion.

Oxford additional task: Students can write a final draft of their essays, taking into account their peers’ comments, and using the writing checklist in ODF WB 2 p.91.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about project and reflection and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain strength, evidence and improvement without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have focused on the structure and language used in effective paragraphs. They have written their final essays and peer-reviewed their classmates’ work.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
