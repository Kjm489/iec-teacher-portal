Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about goals and aspirations. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Is “I want to improve” more specific than a goal with an action and deadline?

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to goals and aspirations and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows specific goal and action?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: In pairs, ask students to make short conversations by completing a question stem and supplying an appropriate response, completing it where necessary.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining specific goal and action in the context of goals and aspirations. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Is “I want to improve” more specific than a goal with an action and deadline?

Oxford main activity — independent application: In groups of around four, instruct students to pool their personal goals and shuffle them, placing them upside down on the table. Tell them to look at one goal at a time, asking the student who wrote it to expand and discuss: How realistic is this goal? Is it a short-term goal or a long-term goal? What would you need to do to achieve this goal?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Explain to students that they are now going to complete a free-writing task on the question: Why is it important to have goals in your life? Help them to understand that free-writing is an independent, reflective activity which isn’t read by anyone else. The aim is to write as fluently as you can without worrying about organization, grammar, or vocabulary; but it must be in English! Tell them to store their free-writing for later in the week.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of goals and aspirations, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students in the introductory activity, supply useful phrases for making suggestions and comparing and contrasting.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able students to organize the pictures into multiple types of category.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to create more complex sentences by writing more detailed personal goals, while still using only one sentence.

Oxford differentiation: In the group discussion activity, allocate one student the role of ensuring that everyone has an equal opportunity to speak, to support less confident students.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.1–2 in ODF WB 2 p.86.

Oxford additional task: Complete ex.2 in ODF SB 2 p.128, adapting the sentences to suit students’ own lives.

Previous lesson directions (reference)
