Previous lesson directions (reference)

Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? Oxford main activity — teacher model: Ask: How many of you have done any volunteering? Would you like to be a volunteer? Elicit some ideas for the type of volunteering students might be interested in.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about communication. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Turn to ex.1 in ODF SB 2 p.88. Ask students to read the advertisements and the email, then discuss their answers to question 2 in pairs. Share answers as a whole class.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Highlight that the email has four clear paragraphs. Ask pairs to complete ex.2 on p.88, noting down key phrases in the email which express the function of the paragraph.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to communication and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows audience and purpose?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Take answers as a whole class. Focus on the structures which follow any key or fixed phrases. Draw students’ attention to the fact that these phrases (e.g. ‘I believe that …’; ‘I would be grateful if …’) often come at the beginning of paragraphs (topic sentences).

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: If possible, show students some advertisements for local volunteering opportunities which might appeal to them. Alternatively, use the advertisements in ODF SB 2 p.88 and ODF WB 2 p.80, adapting these as necessary so they are of interest and relevance to the class. Give students time to read the advertisements and choose two they would be interested in applying for. Pair up students so both are interested in the same advertisement.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining audience and purpose in the context of communication. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — independent application: In pairs, students plan ideas for content and for language (phrases and vocabulary) for each paragraph of their email. Remind them to prepare a topic sentence for each one.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Give pairs time to write a first draft of their email. Encourage them to choose vocabulary they have learned during this unit.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of communication, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: When pairing up students to write the email, pair up students with different abilities so they can support each other.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to use the sample email in ODF SB 2 p.88 as a model, adapting it with their own ideas.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to find synonyms to replace key words or phrases from the sample email in ODF SB 2 p.88, rather than copying language from the text.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.12–13 in ODF WB 2 p.83.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.9–10 in ODF WB 2 p.78.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about communication and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain audience and purpose without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.
