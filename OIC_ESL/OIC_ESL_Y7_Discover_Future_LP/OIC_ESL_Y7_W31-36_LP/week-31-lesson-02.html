1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about communication; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford introductory activity: Play intonation bingo. Instruct students to draw a 4 x 2 grid and to write a phrase from a supplied list in each square. You will need 15 to 20 phrases (e.g. ‘How are you?’, ‘How was your day?’, ‘I don’t think so’, ‘I’m really sorry’, etc.).

Oxford introductory activity: Say the phrases in a random order, but only giving the intonation pattern of each. (It will help later if you number them as you say them.) Students must decide if the intonation pattern they hear matches to any of the phrases on their grid, and if so, they cross it off. The first student to cross off all the phrases shouts ‘Bingo!’.

Oxford introductory activity: Check that the phrases match to the intonation patterns you gave.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about communication. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: On the second listening, direct students to answer the questions in ex.5. Check answers.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to communication and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows audience and purpose?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Turn to the ‘Listening strategy’ on p.98. Give some examples of positive and negative intonation patterns using the following sentence: This sentence doesn’t mean anything.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Listen to the conversation again, pausing when appropriate to ask: How does (X) feel? Can you repeat the last phrase? Encourage students to copy their intonation patterns. Pause also when the speakers use a phrase from the key phrases in ex.6 on p.99, asking students to identify the phrase from the list. Practise saying these phrases together.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining audience and purpose in the context of communication. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Put students into pairs to complete ex.6 on p.99, then discuss as a class.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Give students a list of possible things to suggest, such as ‘join a sports team’ or ‘switch off your phone’. Choose ideas which will appeal to them. Then encourage them to take turns making suggestions to each other (they can adapt your ideas if they want) in their pairs. They should respond using a phrase from ex.6, focusing on appropriate intonation.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of communication, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, give them a copy of the transcript after completing ex.5.

Oxford differentiation: During the paused listening activity, ask more able students to also identify ways in which the speakers express sympathy or understanding.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to ‘overdo’ the intonation patterns when they practise by exaggerating them in an amusing way.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to listen for linking between words and to practise this.

Oxford additional task: Identify examples of informal language in the listening transcript.

Oxford additional task: Students can work together to write short dialogues and perform them as ‘intonation dialogues’. The rest of the class must guess the context, the characters, and what the dialogue is about. Ideas: complaining in a shop, apologizing for being late, asking for help.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about communication and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain audience and purpose without a model? Oxford learning review: Students have listened to an informal conversation between friends to develop their ability to understand feeling and attitude. They have applied their learning to a speaking activity.
