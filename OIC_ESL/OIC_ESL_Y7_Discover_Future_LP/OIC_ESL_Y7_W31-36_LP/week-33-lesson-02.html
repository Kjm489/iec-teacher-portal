PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about communication; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford introductory activity: Call out a list of verbs. Tell students to stand up if the second verb will be an infinitive, or to sit down if it will be a gerund. For each verb, ask one or two students a short question using the verb pattern: e.g. What time do you stop watching TV in the evening?

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “I use a formal greeting when writing to a school leader.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about audience and purpose. Textbook anchor: 2. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Put students into small groups to look at the pictures and discuss the question in ex.3 in ODF SB 2 p.108. Share ideas as a whole class.

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Play the audio in ODF SB 2 p.109 ex.4 (track 2.10). Tell students to listen to check whether their ideas were correct. Give them time to discuss their understanding of the conversation in their groups and to note down the negative consequences of each action.

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Remind students of the different features of more formal and less formal speech that they thought about in Week 32. Elicit what register this conversation is in (informal).

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about communication. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Hand out the listening transcript. On the second listen, encourage students to follow the text and identify any features of more informal language: 1. language choices (vocabulary and grammar), 2. sentence length, 3. use of fillers, and 4. how functions are expressed.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Focus on the ‘Speaking strategy’ in ODF SB 2 p.109, giving contrasting examples to help students recognize the difference in intonation.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Play the audio a final time for students to complete ex.6. Prompt them to repeat each phrase, including linking the words smoothly.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Put students into pairs to practise the phrases with different intonation, identifying whether their partner is using rising or falling intonation.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to communication and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows audience and purpose?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Give each student a blank sheet of paper. At each stage, they must write a creative answer to your prompt at the top of the page, then fold over what they have written and pass the paper to the next person. They must not read what has already been written until you give permission. The prompts are:

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Who is the first speaker?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Who is the second speaker?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: What’s the relationship between the two speakers?

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining audience and purpose in the context of communication. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Would you use the same greeting in a formal email and a message to a friend?

Oxford main activity — independent application: What are they discussing?

Oxford main activity — independent application: How does the first speaker feel?

Oxford main activity — independent application: How does the second speaker feel?

Oxford main activity — independent application: Tell students to read their completed papers in pairs and to choose one scenario on which to base a dialogue. They should use the key phrases from ODF SB 2 p.109, and choose language which is appropriate to the required register. Give them time to practise their dialogues, focusing on using appropriate rising or falling intonation.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of communication, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to move their hands up or down according to the intonation pattern when listening to and repeating the phrases.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to use features of connected speech when practising and performing their dialogues.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.4–5 in ODF WB 2 p.99.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about communication and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain audience and purpose without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have identified features of informal speech in a conversation. They have taken part in a speaking activity to practise intonation patterns.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
