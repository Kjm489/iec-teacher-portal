PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Activate: Display a lesson picture, quotation or question about goals and aspirations; collect two ideas. Textbook starting point: Use the original introductory task in the source reference. ICQ: Do you think first and then share one idea? CCQ: Is “I want to improve” more specific than a goal with an action and deadline?

Oxford introductory activity: Create a one-sentence story with students. Start by giving an opening phrase: e.g. It was a rainy day … Then ask the first student to add at least two words: e.g. It was a rainy day when I met them … Encourage the next student to build on this sentence by, again, adding at least two words: e.g. It was a cold and rainy day, with the wind howling in my ears, when I met them …

Oxford introductory activity: Continue going around the class, aiming to create as long a sentence as possible. Write the sentence on the board as it develops.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first step, and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min I DO — teacher demonstrates: Model: “I will practise speaking twice a week to build confidence.” Underline the evidence and think aloud about specific goal and action. Textbook anchor: 2. ICQ: Do you observe the model before making your own response? CCQ: Is “I want to improve” more specific than a goal with an action and deadline?

Oxford main activity — teacher model: As a class, elicit as many different types of personal goal as possible – for instance, long-term goals, short-term goals, sporting, and academic.

Oxford main activity — teacher model: Individually, ask students to choose three different types of goal and to write one goal in each that they would like to achieve. Note: students will continue to develop and refine these goals over the week’s lessons.

Teacher modeling (IEC): Think aloud through one example from the original Oxford task.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min WE DO — shared example: Choose a second example from the textbook about goals and aspirations. Elicit one answer and its evidence; build a response together on the board. Teacher prompts: What does the source say or show? Which detail supports the answer? How can we improve the sentence? ICQ: Do we complete the first example together? CCQ: Is “I want to improve” more specific than a goal with an action and deadline?

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Ask: What is a SMART goal? Elicit ideas from the class, then turn to ODF SB 2 p.98 to check and confirm their understanding. Explain that acronyms like this are often used in English.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Teach the lesson’s vocabulary, focusing on particular pronunciation features which may cause challenge.

Oxford main activity — guided practice: Ask students to complete ex.2 in ODF SB 2 p.98. Encourage them to discuss their rankings with a partner, before sharing them with the class. They should justify their answers.

CCQ (IEC): Which details in the example explain your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min Partner task: In pairs, compare two source examples related to goals and aspirations and explain a difference using evidence. Roles: A describes; B asks a follow-up; then swap. ICQ: Will both partners speak and switch roles? CCQ: What evidence shows specific goal and action?

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Ask students to decide how well the SMART criteria have been applied by completing ex.3 in pairs. If they think a goal is already SMART, they can leave it as it is. If they think it is not SMART, they should try to improve it by adding the missing information.

Oxford main activity — supported collaboration: Look at Nathan’s first goal in ex.3. Ask students to unpack the sentence in pairs – that is, to make as many short sentences as possible using the different pieces of information in the original. Focus on the different ways that information can be combined within a sentence.

Collaborative scaffold (IEC): Discuss one response with a partner, explain your reasoning, and take turns.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min YOU DO — independent task: Individually create a written, spoken or diagram response explaining specific goal and action in the context of goals and aspirations. Success check: Include an accurate example and one supporting detail. ICQ: Do you complete your answer independently before comparing? CCQ: Is “I want to improve” more specific than a goal with an action and deadline?

Oxford main activity — independent application: e.g. I’ll watch a TV show. / It’s my favourite TV show. / I’ll watch all the episodes. / I’ll watch it during the vacation. / It will be in the summer.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Now challenge pairs to do the same process in reverse. Hand out some sets of very short, simple sentences which contain all the information to make a single SMART goal. Direct students to create a single sentence for each set.

Oxford main activity — independent application: Bring the class back together to share ideas. Accept different answers as long as they are in correct English.

Independent check (IEC): Complete an individual response demonstrating the specified Oxford outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Support: Use a visual, short word bank and a claim–evidence sentence frame. Core: Give a complete explanation with one relevant detail. Challenge: Compare two examples of goals and aspirations, evaluating the strength of the evidence. Check: Ask learners to underline the evidence in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Offer some examples to illustrate each of the criteria in the SMART acronym.

Oxford differentiation: Fast finishers can research alternative words used in SMART. Encourage them to find out what SMARTER stands for.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students by giving them a list of questions to work through when combining information into one sentence: e.g. Can you combine an adjective and a noun? Can you make a phrase with a preposition (e.g. ‘in’, ‘of’, ‘during’)?

Oxford additional task: Complete some or all of exs.4–6 in ODF SB 2 p.128.

Oxford additional task: Complete exs.9–10 in ODF WB 2 p.88.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Previous lesson directions (reference)

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Review: Invite two different responses and compare their supporting evidence. Exit ticket: Independently write one claim about goals and aspirations and one supporting detail. Assessment: Can the learner explain specific goal and action without a model? Record: I = independent; P = prompted; N = not yet.

Oxford learning review: Students have studied SMART goals to extend their vocabulary. They have worked together to improve SMART goals using complex sentences.

Exit ticket (IEC): Show one independently completed response or explanation linked to the outcome.
