Oxford introductory activity: You can either:

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Explain to students that they are going make up false descriptions of unusual foods in a game. Their classmates will try to work out which description is true and which descriptions are false.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Focus students’ attention on the categories you used to describe food in the introductory activity. Write these on the board for them to copy: ‘Appearance’ – what it looks like, ‘Origin’ – where it is from, ‘Taste’ – what the flavour is like, ‘Smell’ – what it smells like, ‘Texture’ – what it feels like.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Put students into pairs. Give each pair a different picture of an unusual food with a brief description. (You might want to online search ‘pictures of unusual foods’ – there are many examples.) The information in the description must align with the above categories.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: First, pairs read the description of the food in their picture and make notes under the relevant categories. Then, challenge them to make up two more false descriptions using the same categories. They can be as imaginative as they like!

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Display each pair’s picture and three descriptions (one true and two false) around the classroom. Tell students to move around the room, look at each picture, read each description, and choose the description which they think is correct for each one.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Afterwards, invite each pair to stand next to their picture, reveal the correct answer, and describe the food using their notes under each category.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For less able students, use fewer descriptive categories.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able students to find interesting adjectives to describe appearance, taste, smell, and texture in a thesaurus: e.g. ‘wonky’, ‘sour’, ‘pungent’, ‘slippery’, ‘rubbery’.

Oxford differentiation: The activities require creative thought and the use of imagination which will give some students the opportunity to shine.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can use the true food descriptions to design an illustrated quiz on unusual food. This can be shared with other classes.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have read about unusual foods from around the world and learned new vocabulary to describe food in different ways. They have worked collaboratively in a true/false description activity.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
