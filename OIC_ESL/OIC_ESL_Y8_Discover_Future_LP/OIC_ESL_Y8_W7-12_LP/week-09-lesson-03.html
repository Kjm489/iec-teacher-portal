Original Oxford Week 9 — complete instructional text

Introductory activity

In small groups, ask students to discuss the following questions based on their existing knowledge and previous learning on the topic:

What are current food-related issues?

What will food-related issues look like in the future?

How could these problems be solved?

Have a whole-class discussion in which students share their thinking.

Based on this discussion (which probably focused on issues like climate change and our carbon footprint, consumption of meat, food miles, overproduction/consumption and food waste, overuse of water, obesity, starvation and malnutrition, and plastic packaging), can students predict what food will look like in the future?

Main activity

Students are going to read a social media discussion about new types of food which could solve some of the food production problems discussed.

Form groups of three numbered 1, 2, 3. Give each student within the group a letter A, B, C.

Give each group a handout. Use the text ‘The food of the future – today’ in ODF WB 3 p.25.

Cut up the text into three sections. Group 1 reads about algae (five parts), Group 2 reads about insects (five parts), and Group 3 reads about plant-based burgers (five parts).

Each group reads their part of the text and makes notes on: description of food, its nutritional value, its method of production, and its advantages.

Allow time for each group to read their part of the text, discuss it, and make notes.

Form new groups of A students, B students, and C students. Ask them to exchange information about what they have read about.

As a class, discuss the details of each future food:

Algae – easy to grow, doesn’t need fresh water, grows in the ocean, great source of protein

Insects/Crickets – easy to raise in the city, great source of protein, don’t need a lot of land, can be ground into flour

Plant-based burgers – created from meat molecules called heme, so we don’t need to rear cattle for beef which releases a lot of greenhouse gases

Ask: How would you feel about eating these foods – algae, crickets, or plant-based burgers? (Note that these foods might be familiar in your country already.)

Then ask: How will population growth and climate change affect future food production? What can we do about this?

Additional task

Complete exs.1–4 in ODF WB 3 p.24.

Learning review

Students have discussed current and future food issues and predicted the content of a reading text. They have read a part of a text and summarized the information to their peers, before discussing their reactions.

Differentiation

Create mixed-ability groups for the jigsaw reading. Students can use a graphic organizer to break down and better understand the information. For less able students, avoid using the text on plant-based burgers as it is more complex to summarize.

Ask more able students to research/think about other future food types and present it in the whole-class follow-up discussion.
