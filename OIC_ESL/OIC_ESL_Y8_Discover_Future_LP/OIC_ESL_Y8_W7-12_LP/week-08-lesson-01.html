PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Elicit what students understand by ‘carbon footprint’ and ‘greenhouse gas emissions’. They can turn to the definition of ‘carbon footprint’ in ODF SB 3 p.27.

Oxford introductory activity: Ask: Do you think about the impact that food production and transport has on the environment?

Oxford introductory activity: Show the picture of the avocado. Ask: Are avocados environmentally friendly?

Oxford introductory activity: Explain that if you eat one avocado a week, in a year that produces approximately 15 kg of greenhouse gas emissions. This is the same amount as a petrol car driving at 64 km, or heating a house for two days! Growing an avocado uses 3,519 litres of water, enough for 54 eight-minute showers. Comment on students’ reactions – are they surprised?

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Students are going to watch a Factflix video in which Bella and Josh discuss the environmental impact of food.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Ask students to listen for and list all the foods that Josh buys. Play the video once. Students check their answers in pairs.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Play the video a second time. Establish the final food list with the whole class.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Form groups of four. Ask groups to note down what Bella says about the impact that each food item and its packaging has on the environment. Play the video twice, pausing between plays so students can share their answers. Review answers as a whole class.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Either read out or write on the board a list of foods (e.g. chicken, beef, chocolate, pasta, rice, eggs). Ask students, first individually and then in their group, to rate them from the highest to the lowest in terms of annual greenhouse gas emissions produced if one person consumes each item once or twice a week (answers: beef, chicken, chocolate, eggs, rice, avocado, pasta). Note: there are many websites that provide this information if you want to use familiar foods from your country or culture.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Discuss the answers as a class.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Ask students to take a moment to reflect on their own diet and how it might affect the environment. Discuss as a class: What can we as individuals do to reduce the impact of food and packaging on the environment?

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Group work allows more able students to help less able students to complete the activities successfully.

Oxford differentiation: Students have the opportunity to display their own knowledge of the subject matter regardless of their language ability. Allow them to use their first language to describe more scientific terminology like ‘carbon’ and ‘greenhouse gases’.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can write a list of Dos and Don’ts that they can follow to reduce the impact of food and packaging on the environment.

Oxford additional tasks: Research different kinds of food and their environmental impact online.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have learned about the impact of food and packaging on the environment. They have extracted main information from a video and discussed what they can do, on a personal level, to reduce their carbon footprint.
