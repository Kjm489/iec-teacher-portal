Original Oxford Week 12 — complete instructional text

Introductory activity

In today’s lesson, students are going to write an essay on their personal hero.

Explain that last lesson you noted down of some of the errors that they made during the speaking activity. Give out a handout of the errors, keeping students anonymous.

In pairs, students try to identify and correct the errors.

Put an error correction code on the board: T= tense, prep = preposition, ww = wrong word, sp = spelling, ᴧ = missing word, wo = word order.

Review the corrections as a class and explain that checking and revising your work is an important part of the writing process.

Main activity

Give students enough time to write the first draft of their essay.

After a breather of three to five minutes, tell students to look again at their first draft:

Read through the essay once to get a general impression.

Study it more carefully and use the correction code to identify any errors.

Add any comments/suggestions in the margin.

When students have read and revised their own essay, tell them to exchange their essay with their neighbour and repeat the process. Remind them to communicate their opinions constructively, and with respect, kindness, and tact. They should also highlight one to two things that they liked about their partner’s essay.

Return the essays to their authors. Ask students to take a look at the corrections and comments. Remind them that it is a normal part of the learning process to make and learn from mistakes.

If time permits, ask students to write their final draft.

Before the end of the lesson, praise students for their efforts in class today. It is not easy to write an extended piece of writing in a different language, but they persevered and produced an essay (it doesn’t matter how short or long it was).

As a class, talk about each other’s personal heroes and draw a conclusion on their perception of a hero – for example, most of the class might have considered a family member to be their hero, whereas some students might have opted for a public figure like a sports star, novelist, or activist.

Additional task

Students display their essays around the classroom. Invite them to walk around the room and read each other’s work.

Learning review

Students have worked hard to write a first draft of their expository essay, peer-edited each other’s work, and revised their work accordingly based on the feedback received. They have thought again about what the word ‘hero’ means to them and the rest of the class.

Differentiation

Pair up more able and less able students for the peer-editing activity. This will allow less able students to receive the support they need, while more able students will consolidate their understanding of language by explaining errors and corrections to their classmate.

YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
