Original Oxford Week 6 — complete instructional text

Main activity

Explain that this week, students will work in small groups on a project of their choice. They can extend their learning into an area of particular interest to them, and practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way that they could apply to a real-world setting.

Students will focus on a project related to the topics ‘What connects us?’ or ‘What do places mean to us?’. They should work in small groups with shared areas of interest.

Students can choose their own project, linked to either topic, or they can develop a project from one of the options below.

Encourage students to use the IDEAS to Action framework, which they will be familiar with from their Global Skills Projects lessons. Remind them that this is a smaller-scale project, so they may need to adapt and modify it to suit their needs.

Students will be responsible for the planning and management of their project. This should involve:

suggesting and allocating specific tasks

researching and drafting creative work

reviewing drafts and contributing constructive ideas and suggestions

joint presentation (the following week)

formative self-assessment and peer-assessment (the following week)

Students can present their work in a way that best suits their project, depending on resources and time available. See how creative they can be with their presentations. Here is a non-exhaustive list of different kinds of presentation:

A speech or oral retelling (perhaps supported by visual resources, such as a PowerPoint presentation, photographs, a campaign poster, a model, artefacts, graphs or charts, etc.)

A pitch (to sell a product or service)

A skills demonstration

A debate

An art/photography exhibition or display

An interview with an expert in the field of enquiry

A short film or documentary

A creative performance

Remind students that they have five days to complete the planning, research, and execution of their project. At the end of Day 1, teachers could look over each group’s plan to ensure that they have allocated tasks appropriately and know what to accomplish each day.

Topic 1 (What connects us?)

Project suggestions:

Kindness Day: Organize a Kindness Day for your class/several classes in your school. This could include a presentation on the origins of World Kindness Day, why it is considered important, and why you have decided to introduce it into the school. Consider giving this presentation in assembly or to the classes you want to include in the event. Come up with different acts of kindness that students can try out. (Remember the ones that you discussed in class and research others.) Prepare flyers or posters to advertise the event. Think about follow-up activities after the event to report the results, such as interviews with students and staff members.

How do people manage without technology? Research communities that choose or don’t choose to live without technology. For example, the Amish – a community that doesn’t always reject technology, but has specific rules about how it is used; or a tiny West Virginia community that lives without modern technology because 11 large-scale telescopes were installed in the area by the National Radio Astronomy Observatory in the 1950s, so no technology is allowed. Consider how these communities feel and compare it to your own thoughts about technology. Could you live without it? You could look at the advantages and disadvantages and compare different views. How will you present your findings? It could be a PowerPoint presentation followed by a class debate.

Topic 2 (What do places mean to us?)

Project suggestions:

Places to see before they disappear: Did you know that there are places in the world that are disappearing? Venice in Italy is under threat from rising sea levels, Mount Kilimanjaro’s snow-capped summit is fast melting, and the Maldive Islands may also be lost to the oceans. You could research places that are disappearing, then select several that you would like to visit and make a travel brochure/guide for visitors to see these places before they disappear. Many of these places are threatened by global warming and climate change. You could make a documentary to raise awareness of climate change, informing people and making suggestions of how they could contribute to protecting the environment.

Places that are special to me (in my country): Do you have places that are special to you that you would like to share with others? How about creating a list, doing some research, and using your mapping skills to present them? Create a map with the places indicated on it and include useful information about each one. Consider doing this digitally and making it interactive. If several students come from the same country, then you can work together and combine your ideas, or you could use the country where you are currently living. There are various possibilities.

YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
