Introductory activity

Write the word ‘supermarket’ on the board. Ask: How can a supermarket be shown on a map? Elicit that it is drawn as a symbol.

Ask students to quickly draw what they think would be an appropriate symbol for a supermarket. Invite one or two students to the board to draw their symbol ideas.

Draw/Project a few more common symbols found on maps and see if students can guess what they represent (e.g. an airport, public toilets, a camp site, a car park, a hospital).

Main activity

Dictate the following words: ‘woods’, ‘houses’, ‘monument’, ‘library’, ‘café’, ‘bus stop’, ‘bridge’, ‘railway’. Ask students to draw symbols to represent them, then compare them in pairs.

Turn to the map in ODF SB 3 p.21 to check if students’ symbols are similar – whose picture is the closest representation? Ask: How are symbols helpful on a map?

Tell students they are going to listen to two people talking about moving around a city centre (see ex.2 ODF WB 3 p.19). During the first listening, ask them to:

1) decide what their relationship is (mother and exchange student);

2) tick any of the following phrases that they can hear (a, b, e, g):

a) what does this symbol mean? b) go east along this road c) according to the scale, it’s two kilometres d) you can take a shortcut e) look out for the supermarket f) check the key on the map g) go in this direction.

Play the audio up to the point when the girl says: ‘It sounds great. We have an area like that in my city, too.’ Then students check their answers in pairs.

Play the audio again and ask students to note down the instructions that the mother gives to the student. Review answers as a class and write the instructions on the board:

You go east along this road and look out for the supermarket, on your left.

If you go in this direction – and continue on a little past the supermarket – you can’t miss the school on your right.

Ask students to complete exs.4–5 in ODF WB 3 p.19.

Look at the sketch map on p.19 of ODF WB 3. You are going to give students directions from the bus stop to different places on the map. Tell them to follow the directions and tell you where they arrive. Do a couple of examples, using language from the listening exercise, then ask a confident student to take over your role.

Learning review

Students have deepened their understanding of maps. They have listened to a conversation about directions, then practised giving and receiving directions.
