PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: If possible, refer to a personal experience to talk to students about the challenges of moving and having to adapt to a new environment. If this isn’t possible, describe a (fictional) letter you have received from a friend who has recently moved to a new city/country. Talk about how they are coping with the change.

Oxford introductory activity: If applicable, students can talk about their own experiences of moving away from their city/country, including how it affected them (they might have felt lost or homesick), how it limited their ability to do something they could do previously, and how they feel now.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In pairs, students write a list of questions to ask someone about the challenges of moving and adapting to a new environment. Remind them of the interview with Li Yu in ODF SB 3 p.8, if they need some help.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Put students in new pairs. Ask them to compare their questions and produce a final list.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Put students in new pairs again. They are going to take turns to be an interviewer and an interviewee, using the questions they have prepared. The aim of the role-play is for students to practise putting themselves in somebody else’s shoes (i.e. empathize with somebody else’s experience) and think about things that people can do to cope with change. Encourage students to have fun and be as creative as possible!

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Ask the interviewer to note down their partner’s answers – they will need them later.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Using the interview notes, students write a short summary of the interview independently.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: The teacher can decide on the make-up of pairs to either ensure that less able students are paired together, that more able students are paired together, or that pairs are mixed abilities.

Oxford differentiation: The social media post activity can be completed in class by more able students. Less able students may require additional support. This extension activity allows students at different levels to produce a piece of oral work which can also be used for assessment purposes.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete ex.9 in ODF WB 3 p.8 about Aysun Demir.

Oxford additional tasks: Using Aysun Demir’s social media post as a model, students can produce their own post about their experience of adapting to change and present it orally.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have considered the challenges of adapting to change. They have prepared interview questions and practised speaking in pairs in a role-play, using the interviewee’s responses in a follow-up writing task.
