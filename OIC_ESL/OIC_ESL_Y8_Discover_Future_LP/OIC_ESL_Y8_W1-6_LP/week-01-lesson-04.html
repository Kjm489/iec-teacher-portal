Introductory activity

Tell students about Li Yu, a 15-year-old boy from China who moved to Canada with his family when his father got a job. Ask: What do you think were the challenges for Li Yu? (e.g. language barrier, new school and teachers, new home, food, culture, friends, life in general). Write students’ suggestions on the board.

Ask if anyone can relate to this change. If some students do, invite one or two students to share their experiences (of moving to a new house, city, or country).

Turn to ODF SB 3 p.8 and read the interview ‘Real Lives: Li Yu’. Ask students to compare their predictions on the board with how Li Yu felt.

Have a short discussion about the difficulties of adjusting to change.

Main activity

Draw three columns on the board with the following headings: Used to/Would, Be used to, Get used to.

Students reread the text and look for these expressions. Ask them to copy down what follows (e.g. We didn’t use to speak it very often; I used to know all of the teachers; My grandma would always make the best rice noodles; I’m used to it now; I’m slowly getting used to it, but I’m not used to the snow).

Ask students to discuss with a partner what each expression means, using the context to help them. Bring the class back together to share ideas.

Elicit from the class other situations where people have to adapt to changes (e.g. a friend moving away; a new sibling; moving from primary school to secondary school; a new teacher; losing a favourite pet; a sibling leaving home to go to college or get married).

Tell students to think of a change in their life that they have had to adapt to. Allow them time to breathe and reflect on their experience quietly.
