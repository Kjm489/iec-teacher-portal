Original Oxford Week 5 — complete instructional text

Introductory activity

Describe a favourite place of your own to students. Organize your description to mirror the organization of students’ essays: 1. Location and appearance; 2. Atmosphere and what creates this atmosphere; 3. Feelings – why the place is special to you.

Ask students to listen to your description with their eyes closed and try to visualize your favourite place.

When you have finished, ask individual students to share their image of the place you described. Elicit or bring to their attention any new language.

Invite students to ask you more questions about the place to get a better understanding of what it is like and why you like it.

Main activity

In pairs, students exchange the first drafts of their ‘My favourite place’ essays.

Tell students to peer-edit and revise each other’s work. They should already be familiar with this process (see Week 3, Lesson 5). Refer them to the following writing checklist:

Content

Do they explain what makes the place special?

Do they describe the appearance of the place?

Do they describe the atmosphere and what causes this?

Language

Do they use sensory language and varied adjectives?

Is their spelling correct?

Is their grammar correct?

Remind students that they shouldn’t correct spelling and grammar mistakes, but underline them and indicate the error with a code.

Suggested code: Sp = spelling, T = tense, WO = word order is wrong, ᴧ = missing word, prep = preposition, WW = wrong word

Allow time for students to discuss mistakes and recommended revisions with their partner. Remind students that identifying and making mistakes is part of the learning process – they will continue improving with practice.

Students revise their work based on the feedback received, then write a final draft.

Additional task

Students can form small groups and read their descriptions to each other. Encourage listeners to be respectful, close their eyes, and try to visualize the place. After each reading, listeners can ask questions to learn more about their classmate’s favourite place.

Learning review

Students have peer-edited and revised each other’s ‘My favourite place’ essays, providing helpful and constructive feedback. They have written a final draft.

Differentiation

Ask more able students to revise less able students’ work, offer support, and carefully explain their mistakes.
