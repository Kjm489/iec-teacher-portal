Introductory activity

Establish the role that YouTubers play as influencers and discuss as a class. Remind students of the listening exercise in Week 2, Lesson 3 in which Dr. Schmidt talked to a teenage podcaster about the generation gap.

Based on students’ suggestions or your own knowledge of cultural trends, play a short video clip of a YouTuber – ensure that the language and content is appropriate before showing to the class. Ask: How is this person influencing their followers?

Check if there are any YouTubers and/or influencers in the class and ask them about their experience – how and why do they do it? If not, ask the class: What type of content do you like to watch online? (e.g. skills tutorials, gaming or makeup videos, Q&A sessions and discussions, vlogs of an influencer’s ‘day in the life’)

Main activity

Turn to ex.3 p.10 in ODF SB 3. Students are going to listen to a reporter talk about the role of YouTubers as influencers.

Give each student the following list of words:

microphone, followers, movie stars, sports stars, popular, horror videos, social media, trust, makeup, jeans, products, audience, funny, Minecraft, webcam, listeners, comedy

Play the audio twice and ask students to tick the key words on the list that they hear, then arrange them in the order in which they are mentioned:

movie stars, comedy, products, popular, Minecraft, makeup, followers, trust, webcam

In groups of four, students check their answers. They bring together all the key words they heard and add any they may have missed.

Hand out a pre-prepared listening grid, or ask students to make their own:

Divide an A4 page into four squares and label the squares respectively: ‘What YouTubers often give advice about’, ‘Reasons why YouTubers give advice’, ‘How YouTubers influence and persuade’, ‘What it takes to be a YouTuber’.

Check students’ understanding of the categories, then tell them to listen again and make notes in each category. Explain to students that the listening activity is challenging because they have to process a lot of information quickly. They are in the stretch zone.

Allow students a few minutes to compare their answers in their groups. Then repeat the process a second time before bringing the class together to complete the listening grid.

Discuss as a class: Is it ethical for YouTubers to receive payment to advertise products through their channel?
