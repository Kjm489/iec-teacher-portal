Original Oxford Week 24 — complete instructional text

Introductory activity

Remind students that this lesson is step two in the job application process: writing a letter or email of application (a ‘cover email/letter’).

Put students in small groups to recall what they know about writing a formal letter or email, including any useful phrases.

Go over responses as a class.

Main activity

Give pairs or small groups a cover email/letter cut into sections (you could use the email in ODF SB 3 p.78). Students reconstruct the email and check their answers with the original.

In their pairs, ask students to discuss ex.2 in ODF SB 3 p.78. Then tell them to compare their answers with another pair. In these groups of four, students create a framework for a four- or five-paragraph cover email, deciding what goes into each paragraph.

Discuss ideas as a class, then compare the answers to ex.7 in ODF WB 3 p.71. Discuss any differences and agree on the framework students are going to use. Write this on the board for their reference.

Still in their pairs, ask students to discuss the following questions:

What is the tone of Liam’s email? Find examples to support your answer.

Look at paragraph 2. Why does Liam choose to give this information?

What examples does Liam give in paragraph 3? What language does he use, and how does it support his application?

Go through answers as a class. Direct students’ attention to useful phrases and content points that they could use in their own emails.

As a class, agree on a set of success criteria for writing a cover email. Aim for around five criteria to cover organization, structure, and content. Praise students for their work in being able to identify these success criteria.

Individually, students plan their cover emails for their chosen job using the agreed framework. Allow them enough time to do this.

When they have finished, ask them to swap their email plans with a partner for comment, before writing the first draft of their cover email.

Explain that students will continue to work on their emails in the next lesson.

Additional task

Complete ex.1 in ODF WB 3 p.70 for further practice with the language of cover emails.

Learning review

Students have analysed the key content features and format of a formal cover email/letter, developed success criteria, and planned their own emails.

Differentiation

To support less able students when devising the success criteria, refer them to the writing strategy in ODF SB 3 p.78.

As an extension task for more able students, ask them to research advice for cover emails/letters online to feed back to the class.

Challenge more confident students to add information to their sentences using noun phrases and more complex clauses.

Encourage less confident students to include useful phrases and language from the unit in their email plans.
