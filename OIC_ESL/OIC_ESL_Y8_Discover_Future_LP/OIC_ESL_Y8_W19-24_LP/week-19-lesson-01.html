PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Think of a sentence in the third conditional: e.g. If I’d tried harder at acting school, I would have been as successful as [Lupita Nyong'o]. Write it on the board in a jumbled order.

Oxford introductory activity: Challenge students to rearrange the words into the correct order to make a sentence. Check answers as a class and write the correct sentence on the board.

Oxford introductory activity: Elicit that the sentence is an example of the third conditional. Invite a volunteer to explain the function of the third conditional – to talk about hypothetical consequences in the past. Remind the class that they looked at this tense in Week 18, Lesson 2, but they need to regularly revise new tenses in order to use them confidently.

Oxford introductory activity: In pairs, students work together to write three sentences using the third conditional. Go around the class and check their work to ensure that they have grasped the grammar. Prompt them to think about where they have gone wrong if you notice a mistake.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Say that you have a secret identity. You are a famous writer whose books are now award-winning movies. Introduce the third conditional into the narrative. For example:

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: If my father hadn't read me adventure stories, I wouldn't have written my own.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: If I had worked at a music shop instead of a book shop, I wouldn't have met an agent.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: If I had been too afraid of failure, I wouldn't have published the book.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Refer students to the mini biography in ODF SB 3 p.63. Instruct them to read the five facts and write three third conditional sentences about the actor’s experience.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In pairs, students peer-correct each other’s work, helping each other to form the third conditional correctly. Invite them to share their partner’s best sentence with the class.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Form new pairs of students and give them one minute to each choose an actor or writer they admire. Explain the role-play activity. They are going to think about how these individuals achieved success. You could add some question prompts to the board to inspire ideas: e.g. What did they study at school? How did they overcome failure?

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Students prepare a short monologue in which they explain the sequence of events that led to their success as an actor or writer. (The aim of the activity is to practise the third conditional and not to be factually accurate, so they can make up a back-story.)

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In their pairs, students take turns to sit in the ‘hot seat’ and play the role of their chosen actor or writer, using the third conditional as much as possible. Encourage listening students to take notes – at the end of their partner’s presentation, they can ask questions to elicit more information (e.g. If you hadn’t achieved success, what would you have done?) and then summarize what they have learned. They then swap roles.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: While students work, put some success criteria for speaking activities on the board. At the end of the lesson, encourage pairs to talk about their partner’s performance using these criteria. They should include positive feedback and areas for improvement.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To challenge more confident students, mix together two sentences in the warm-up.

Oxford differentiation: Pair up similar abilities in the role-play task so students can stretch each other by incorporating the new grammar. For the initial peer-corrected writing activity, pair up more able with less able students to provide additional support.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can write up their role-play monologue as a mini biography.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have recalled and practised the third conditional in a role-play activity.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
