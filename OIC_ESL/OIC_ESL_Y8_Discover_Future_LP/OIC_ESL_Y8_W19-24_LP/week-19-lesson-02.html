Original Oxford Week 19 — complete instructional text

Introductory activity

Assign one action for ‘True’ and another for ‘False’ (e.g. a star jump and crossed arms) to suit your students’ abilities. Tell the class that you are going to say different statements about documentaries and they have to quickly decide whether they are true or false.

Statements could include:

Documentaries are made-up stories. F

They teach us about the world around us. T

They can cover real-life issues and are often hard-hitting. T

They are intended to entertain the audience. F

They usually have a presenter or a voice-over. T

People interviewed in documentaries are paid actors. F

Main activity

If possible, allow students to form their own groups of four or five. Alternatively, form these yourself. Explain that they are going to write a script for a documentary on a topic of their choice. They are going to produce it in the next lesson.

To begin with, elicit the audience and purpose of a documentary:

Audience – students or people interested in a particular topic or issue;

Purpose – to inform the audience about an aspect of culture or a real-life issue; to present information in an unbiased way, allowing interviewees to present their experience or ‘side’ of the story (the audience can then form their own opinion).

Give students five minutes to discuss and decide on a topic in their group. Encourage them to think about a topic that they are knowledgeable about, which is also relevant to their peers (e.g. young people’s viewing habits – if they prefer to watch movies on streaming sites or at the cinema; or the introduction of a new menu in the school café).

Tell them to nominate a team leader who will coordinate work, ensure that everybody has an active role in each stage of the process, and recognize strengths within the team.

As well as today’s script-writing task, they will need to decide on:

each other’s roles (presenter, one or two interviewees, videographer, director);

how they will bring together voice-over/presenting and interview(s);

which facts to include;

which interview questions will elicit interesting answers and engage the audience.

Elicit that the register will be neutral, as the presenter shares facts and educates the audience on a topic. This means that language shouldn’t be persuasive or colloquial, but engaging to maintain the audience’s interest. Quickly go over how intonation can make a monologue engaging.

Give groups most of the lesson to write their script. Provide support where required, but let their creative ideas flow.

Additional task

Complete the listening activity in ODF WB 3 p.59 exs.1–4.

Differentiation

Gently encourage able but less confident students to be a team leader. This will stretch them and help to develop their confidence in a supportive space.

Ensure that groups are composed of students with varying abilities. Not only will more able students be able to support their less confident peers with language choices and pronunciation, but students who are less able linguistically might excel in the more creative aspects of the task (e.g. in filming or directing).
