PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask students to sit in their groups from last lesson (Week 19, Lesson 2). Encourage them to think about their intonation in a quick warm-up. Give each group a set of emotion cards (e.g. with the words ‘excited’, ‘sad’, ‘disappointed’, ‘interested’, ‘angry’, ‘bored’). They take turns selecting a card, without showing the others, and trying to communicate the emotion in a sentence. Write some example sentences on the board, including a mix of questions, exclamations, and simple statements. Alternatively, allow students to create their own sentences. The listening group members try to guess the emotion.

Oxford introductory activity: Remind students that stressing different words in a sentence can change its meaning. Encourage them to focus on their intonation in today’s lesson to engage their audience.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Give groups five to ten minutes to talk through their plan and to practise reading through/memorizing their script and/or notes.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Make available audiovisual equipment for them to record their documentaries. They can do this in the classroom, or in an open space like the playground or school hall.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Elicit how students can work together effectively as a team to achieve their goal.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Groups should spend most of the lesson filming their documentary. It shouldn’t be longer than three to four minutes. Encourage them to have a few practice rounds first to test out different creative ideas.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: This is a challenging and possibly nerve-wracking activity for some students. Remind them that just like natural speech isn’t perfect, you don’t expect them to be word-perfect. Stretching themselves by stepping outside of their comfort zone will help to improve their speaking skills and to boost their confidence.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Write on the board a few strategies that they can use if they (or a group member) get(s) stuck or stumble(s) on their words, such as requesting repetition, self-correction, using fillers (e.g. ‘um’, ‘hmm’, ‘you know’) to give them extra thinking time, clarifying understanding (‘Do you mean …?’), and paraphrasing ideas using familiar words, etc.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Watch each group’s documentary in turn. Encourage the audience to share their favourite moments and to explain what their peers did well.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give targeted praise to students who are less able linguistically but have excelled in the more creative aspects of the task (e.g. in filming or directing).

Oxford differentiation: Allow less confident students to read their script or to follow their notes during the filming of the documentary.

Oxford differentiation: Remind more confident students to support their less confident team members by eliciting language and ideas.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able students in the warm-up by providing more difficult emotion words like ‘frustrated’, ‘motivated’, and ‘restless’.

Oxford additional tasks: Groups can watch their documentary together. Encourage them to have a discussion about what went well, and what they need to work on to improve their performance.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have worked as a team to produce a short documentary.
