PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask: Who has or has had a job? Do you know any teenagers who have a job? Establish the kinds of job that teenagers tend to have, jobs teenagers are allowed to do, and how much they usually work.

Oxford introductory activity: Say: We’ve looked at lots of different aspects of work and jobs. This week, you’re going to apply for a job for yourself. Elicit what students have learned over the last two weeks which will help them in the job application process (e.g. key job skills, the employment situation in their region, the career planning advice they have researched).

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Put students in pairs or small groups to share what they know about applying for a job. Discuss ideas as a class. Establish the relevant stages of a job application in the correct order. Explain the stages they are going to work through this week (finding a job, applying for the job by writing a cover letter, preparing for a job interview, having an interview).

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Ask: What information do you need to know when you’re deciding whether to apply for a job? Allow students time to discuss in pairs, then go over suggestions as a class.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In their pairs or small groups, ask students to rank these ideas, or sort them into two categories: ‘Essential’ and ‘Useful to know’. Invite each pair/group to write one idea into each category, then discuss as a class any differences of opinion.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Refer to the job adverts in ODF SB 3 pp.75, 77, and 78; and in ODF WB 3 pp.70–71. (Alternatively, find or devise five to eight job adverts that would appeal to your students.)

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Focus on phrases such as ‘Uniform provided’ and ‘Retail experience preferred’. Ask: What is missing from these phrases? Why? (auxiliary verbs and words which can be inferred are omitted to keep the adverts short). In their pairs, encourage students to identify examples of ellipsis in other adverts.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Now ask students to individually sort the adverts into three groups: ‘Very interested’, ‘Interested but not sure’, ‘Not interested’.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Put students in new groups to discuss their groupings. Remind them of the skills and qualities they identified in Week 23, Lesson 4. Ask them to help each other choose up to three jobs that they’d like to apply for. Encourage students to listen carefully to each other’s reasons for their choices and to help each other make the best decisions.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Identify how many students would like to apply for each job advert. Discard any adverts with fewer than five applicants. Make sure that there is an advert for every student.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In their groups again, students reach a final decision about the job they’re going to apply for. (Note: keep a record of these decisions.)

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able students, ask them to reduce the word counts of the adverts in ODF WB 3 pp.70–71 but keep the same information using more ellipsis.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to highlight key words in the adverts which match their skills and interests.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the skills survey in ex.2 in ODF SB 3 p.124 and then discuss ways in which students could improve their career skills.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can research local job adverts and identify the skills employers are looking for.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have discussed the jobs that teenagers can do and the process of applying for a job. They have reviewed job adverts and selected one to apply for.

YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
