Oxford introductory activity: Put students in groups of three. Call out five or six nationalities that students know. For each one, students note three words or phrases that they associate with this nationality.

Oxford introductory activity: Discuss responses as a class. Elicit that these are (probably) stereotypes, and don’t represent what the people from that country are actually like.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Tell students they are going to watch a TED Talk about cross-cultural communication. Ask: What do you think ‘cross-cultural communication’ means? What topics could the talk cover? Who has watched a TED Talk before? What do you think the talk will be like? Tell them the speaker will talk faster than they are used to and use more complex language.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Search online for ‘TedXBergen cross cultural communication’. Play from 0:01 to 2:44 (‘… which are accepted in our social group and which are familiar’). In pairs, students compare what they understood and whether any of their predictions were correct.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Play the video again. Ask students to note down information about the speaker (e.g. name, nationality, job, personality). Allow them time to discuss answers in pairs before checking answers as a class. Ask them to explain ideas about his personality by referring to the talk.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Write on the board: ‘pilgrim’, ‘fancy’, ‘perception’, ‘social experiment’, ‘take you seriously’. Explain that these are all words from the talk, but don’t give definitions (these should be words students don’t know, so remove any which are familiar and replace them if possible.) When you play the video again, students should listen out for these words and discuss the following questions in pairs:

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: How important is this word? Do you need to understand it to follow the talk?

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: If it is important, does the speaker paraphrase it? What words appear before and after it? Does the section give any clues to its meaning?

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Play the section again, pausing for students to discuss the unfamiliar vocabulary. Pause also at 1.17–1.19 and at 2.10 to establish that he is speaking English with an Italian accent at these points. Elicit reasons why he does this. Praise students for their perseverance.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Write on the board: ‘Culture: a system of behaviour that helps us act in an accepted or familiar way.’ In small groups, students discuss: What is your ‘culture’? Do you have a different ‘culture’ with your friends than with adults? How would you explain our culture to someone from outside of it?

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In their groups, ask students to choose one aspect of their daily lives which reflects their culture (e.g. shopping, using public transport, or driving). Invite them to write a set of ‘rules’ to explain how their culture works to a foreigner.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Display the groups’ rules on the walls. Give students sticky notes to write comments on each other’s rules.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to make notes on the whole video in the second watching, not just about the speaker.
