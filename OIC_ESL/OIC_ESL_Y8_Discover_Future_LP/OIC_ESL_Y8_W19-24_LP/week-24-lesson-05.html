PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: If possible, set up the classroom to resemble a number of interview rooms (one for each job) with desks and chairs.

Oxford introductory activity: Elicit what symptoms of stress are, and some ways we can help to calm ourselves. Encourage one or two students to demonstrate some techniques.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Organize students into groups of interviewers, with around four or five students in each group. There should always be at least two students carrying out each interview, and no more than three. Allocate each group one job to interview for. If there are a lot of students applying for the same job, two or more groups could interview for the same job. Aim for roughly the same number of interviewees for each group.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In their groups, ask students to study the job advert and to prepare a set of questions to ask. Encourage them to think about:

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: What skills are the most important? What question(s) would help you know if the applicant has these skills?

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: What personal qualities are the most important? What question(s) would help you know if the applicant has these qualities?

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Tell students they will now hold the job interviews. Give each group a list of interviewees, and tell the interviewees who they will be interviewed by and in what order. Students will switch between interviewer, observer, and interviewee during the activity.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Once the interviews are over, groups decide which applicant they will offer the job to, and why. Groups present their decisions and their reasons. Encourage them to use phrases such as ‘This was a very strong field …’; ‘It was an extremely difficult decision …’; ‘There were many excellent candidates, but by a narrow margin…’. Praise all students for teamwork in this task.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: To finish the lesson on a positive note, play the game ‘Definitions race’. In teams, students are given a set of vocabulary cards. One student from each team stands up facing the rest of the team. This is the ‘guesser’. When the teacher says ‘Go!’, the students sitting down take turns to select a card and to define the word on the card to the guesser. They must not use the word on the card. As soon as the guesser says the correct word, they select another word. Each round lasts one minute, then another student becomes the guesser. Repeat. The team with the most correctly guessed words at the end is the winner!

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, supply them with a copy of the transcript from ODF SB 3 p.77 (track 2.03) when preparing their interview questions.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able students, ask them to write a letter to the successful candidate, offering them the job.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to use their notes during the interview.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to think carefully about their pronunciation and intonation during the interview.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can do a free-writing activity reflecting on the unit and what they’ve learned.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete any of the self-assessment exercises in ODF WB 3 pp.72–73.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have prepared job interview questions and practised interviewing each other.
