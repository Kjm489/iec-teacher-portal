Original Oxford Week 17 — complete instructional text

Introductory activity

Show the class a picture of a charity shop. Ask: Do you ever shop in charity shops or donate items to them? Where do you think the money from a charity shop goes?

Then show the class pictures of a soup kitchen and a children’s hospital. Elicit from students what they are and what we can do to support these organizations.

Main activity

In pairs, encourage students to think of worthy causes they know about, either internationally or in their local context, and why it is important to support them. Also prompt them to talk about their own charitable efforts previously – perhaps they’ve participated in a sponsored walk, run, or silence.

Invite volunteers to share their ideas and generate enthusiasm for raising money to help those of us less fortunate. Write key vocabulary on the board.

Explain to students that, in small groups, they are going to create a leaflet in promotion of a worthy cause of their choice. You might want to provide a list of local charities, summarizing the aim and charitable efforts of each one.

On the board, write:

‘Name of charity’

‘Aim and goals’

‘How to raise money?’

‘Why should you donate to this cause?’

Tell students to address each bullet point in turn, and to think of three ways to raise money for their chosen charity.

Prompt them to think about what makes people donate money, and how they can present their cause as attractive and important.

Remind them to use persuasive language to encourage the school community to support their cause. This could be achieved through emotive language, strong adjectives, and the use of rhetorical questions. Elicit ideas from students and write them on the board. If they have a personal experience of a charity, they could even include a short anecdote on their leaflet, explaining why what they do is worth the community’s support.

Allow students enough time to research and create their leaflet, delegating tasks between them fairly.

Invite groups to present their leaflets to the class.

Hold a class vote on their favourite worthy cause.

Additional task

Encourage students to research charitable initiatives in their local area and see how they/the school could get involved.

Learning review

Students have worked collaboratively to produce a leaflet in promotion of a worthy cause.

Differentiation

Allow less able students to share their personal experiences of raising money for charity in their first language, where necessary. This will enable them to participate in a meaningful way. You or more able students can then support them to communicate their ideas in simple English.

Encourage more confident students, or students who have extensive volunteering or fundraising experience, to lead their group in the task.
