Original Oxford Week 13 — complete instructional text

Main activity

Explain that this week, students will work in small groups on a project of their choice. They can extend their learning into an area of particular interest to them, and practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way that they could apply to a real-world setting.

Students will focus on a project related to the topics ‘How do we choose our food?’ or ‘What makes a hero?’. They should work in small groups with shared areas of interest.

Students can choose their own project, linked to either topic, or they can develop a project from one of the options below.

Encourage students to use the IDEAS to Action framework, which they will be familiar with from their Global Skills Projects lessons. Remind them that this is a smaller-scale project, so they may need to adapt and modify it to suit their needs.

Students will be responsible for the planning and management of their project. This should involve:

suggesting and allocating specific tasks

researching and drafting creative work

reviewing drafts and contributing constructive ideas and suggestions

joint presentation (the following week)

formative self-assessment and peer-assessment (the following week)

Students can present their work in a way that best suits their project, depending on resources and time available. See how creative they can be with their presentations. Here is a non-exhaustive list of different kinds of presentation:

A speech or oral retelling (perhaps supported by visual resources, such as a PowerPoint presentation, photographs, a campaign poster, a model, artefacts, graphs or charts)

A pitch (to sell a product or service)

A skills demonstration

A debate

An art/photography exhibition or display

An interview with an expert in the field of enquiry

A short film or documentary

A creative performance

Remind students that they have five days to complete the planning, research, and execution of their project. At the end of Day 1, teachers could look over each group’s plan to ensure that they have allocated tasks appropriately and know what to accomplish each day.

Topic 1 (How do we choose our food?)

Our daily bread: Conduct a survey on what students’ families in your class have eaten over two days. Collect the packaging where possible, or photos of the packaging, to study the labels. Include in the survey how much food has been wasted. Complete an analysis of the results and work out the carbon footprint of the class and whether the evidence suggests a balanced diet. Things to think about:

How far did the food travel?

Who helped to grow or produce the food?

Are any unknown ingredients listed on the food labels?

What was the food packaged in? Recyclable or non-recyclable material?

How much food was wasted?

How balanced is the whole-class diet?

You will need to design your survey on Day 1 and circulate it on Day 2. Consider: What will you be doing while you are waiting for the survey results? How will you present your findings? Will you display the packaging in your presentation? – if so, you need to ask classmates to save them and bring them in. Refer back to the article ‘Daily Bread’ in ODF SB 3 p.29 for ideas.

Create a meal plan: Design a seven-day meal plan for breakfast, lunch, dinner, and snacks for a family of four. Things to include or think about:

Use four different types of vegetable. Ensure that there are at least two servings of fruit and three servings of vegetables daily.

Use an ingredient you have never tried before.

Make sure you are making enough to feed a family of four.

Decide on an acceptable budget. Consider the money you would spend on drinks. (You can research prices on online grocery stores in your country, or ask your teachers or parents for an estimate.)

Topic 2 (What makes a hero?)

What makes a hero? Write a superhero story. Remember what you have learned during this unit – the characteristics of a superhero, a superhero’s journey, different types of superhero (ones with special powers, or ordinary human beings or animals who do something heroic). Use the hero’s journey to help you to structure your story. Decide on the best presentation for your story – a comic strip, a play script, an oral retelling, a slideshow, a puppet show, or an animation or short film.

Animal heroes: Research and prepare a presentation about animal heroes. You could focus on specific types of animal or a mixture. They could be animals that have helped humans, or animals that have helped other animals. Think about how you will present the story/stories. Will you deliver it as a factual presentation, write a newspaper article, or turn it into a fictional account based on real-life events?

YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
