PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Ask the class: Do you prefer watching movies with a happy ending, a sad ending, or an unexpected ending? Invite a few volunteers to share their ideas and explain their reasons.

Oxford introductory activity: Tell students about a movie you recently saw and explain your reaction to its ending. For example: I recently saw ‘La La Land’ and the two main characters didn’t end up together, although they loved each other throughout the movie. Although it’s a fantastic story with lots of singing and dancing, I felt disappointed because I wanted them to live happily ever after. If I had written the ending, they would have stayed together, left Hollywood after a successful career, and bought a house on a farm!

Oxford introductory activity: In pairs, encourage students to think about their emotional reactions to various movie endings. Prompt them to think about why they felt that way.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Ask students to read the short text on happy endings in ODF SB 3 p.62. They can do this independently or in pairs.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Tell the class that they are going to write a happy ending for a movie they have recently seen whose ending they found disappointing.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Put students into small groups. Explain that, between themselves, they need to agree on a movie and then work together to produce a happy/happier ending. It is unlikely that all group members will want to use the same movie, so remind them that the key to effective teamwork is compromise – they might not get everything they want, but they can think of ways to keep everybody in the group happy. Tell them to mediate on differences of opinion in a respectful and friendly manner.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Tell groups to make notes on: what the movie is about, why the ending wasn’t happy or satisfying, and what their happy/happier ending would look like.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: If students prefer, they can choose a movie with a happy ending and give it a new, even happier ending.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Remind students to consider the time limit allocation. They need to make sure that they complete the planning and writing stages within this time limit. In order to do so, they should set a time limit for the completion of each stage and delegate tasks between group members.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: At the end of the lesson, invite groups to present their endings to the class.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Hold a class vote on whether the new endings were better than the original endings.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Allow less able students to discuss their happy ending ideas in their first language, if necessary. This will enable them to participate in a meaningful way during the planning stage. They should try to contribute a few sentences of their own in English during the writing stage, supported by a more able student.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more confident students to use a variety of strong adjectives in their happy endings to make it more engaging.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can produce a final, illustrated draft of their happy ending.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have strengthened their teamwork and time management skills in a small-group activity by working together to rewrite the ending of a chosen movie.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
