1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Tell the class that today’s lesson is about learning how to budget. Can they remember what the word ‘budget’ means from previous lessons?

Oxford introductory activity: Say: My niece receives pocket money from her parents once a month. She spends it on buying lunch at school if she doesn’t want to bring food from home, on going out with her friends at weekends, and on other personal items. She also pays for her mobile phone. She usually has enough money to ‘get by’ because she knows how to budget.

Oxford introductory activity: Can students remember what the phrasal verb ‘get by’ means from previous lessons?

Oxford introductory activity: Elicit from students what they think your ‘niece’ does to budget. They might suggest that she uses her phone only in emergencies, or she might only use the internet at home so she doesn’t waste her phone data. She could choose to prepare her lunch at home. She could go to free places with her friends, such as the park, and spend money in shops and at the cinema occasionally.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Tell students they are going to practise budgeting. Turn to the questionnaire in ex.1 in ODF SB 3 p.54, if they haven’t done it yet, and ask them to discuss each statement in pairs.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Discuss each other’s answers as a class and sort students into ‘Mostly True’ and ‘Mostly False’ groups. Check their understanding of the key vocabulary.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Form small groups of four, including a mix of students from ‘Mostly True’ and ‘Mostly False’ if possible.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Tell students that they have a budget of 25 [dollars/pounds/other denomination] for the month. With this money, they have to make sure they always have 5 [dollars/pounds/other denomination] in case of an emergency at the end of the month.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: You might need to adapt the amount and denomination depending on your context.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In their groups, students must decide 1) where they will spend their money, and 2) how much money they will spend on each item/activity. Reinforce that the money needs to last for the whole month and is for one person.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Allow groups enough time to debate their ideas, come to an agreement, and write down their budget clearly.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: As students work, ask them to discuss what difficulties people face when trying to budget and save money. Remind them to be mindful about and respectful of other people’s financial situations.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Invite one or two volunteer groups to share their budgets. Prompt them to explain their reasons behind their decisions and encourage the rest of the class to say why they agree or disagree. Hold a class vote on which group has the best budget.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?
