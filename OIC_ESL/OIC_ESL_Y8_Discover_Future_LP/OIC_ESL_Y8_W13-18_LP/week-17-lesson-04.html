Oxford introductory activity: What do these graphs show us? (the results of a survey on shopping)

Oxford introductory activity: How many people participated in the survey? (30 participants)

Oxford introductory activity: How do you know? (you add together the three totals represented by the bars)

Oxford introductory activity: Invite pairs to share their answers and check, correct, and discuss as a class.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Tell students that they are going to work collaboratively to prepare a survey on the spending habits of the class. In order to do so, they will need to decide on three or four suitable research questions, designed to elicit as much information as possible.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Elicit from students what type of questions are used in the examples on p.57 – multiple-choice questions. This research method will allow them to analyse and present their results clearly, as answers are already defined and represent a statistic.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Form groups of four to five students. Instruct them to discuss and decide on their research questions, different to those in the example. What would they like to know about their classmates’ spending habits? (e.g. if they receive pocket money, what percentage of money they save/spend, if they think they use their money smartly, if they budget, what they spend their money on regularly, what they spend their money on as a ‘treat’, etc.)

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Monitor groups. Ensure that their questions are appropriate and will elicit the information they need, and that their multiple-choice options accommodate all possibilities.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Groups make a final draft of their survey. Remind them to leave enough space next to each multiple-choice option so their classmates can add a tick or tally mark.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Tell groups to leave their survey on their table. They stand up and move around the classroom to answer the survey questions of other groups.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: When all students have answered all the survey questions, they return to their seats. In their groups, they analyse and discuss their results.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Gently remind students that the financial situations of their peers might be very different to their own. This is normal and isn’t a reflection on who they are as a person, but it means that they might have different experiences. Encourage them to put themselves in the shoes of their classmate(s) and to be respectful of each other’s answers.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Now instruct groups to display their results in mini bar charts, by hand or using computer software. Encourage them to divide results between group members so everybody has a bar chart to work on individually or in pairs.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: At the end of the lesson, encourage students to reflect on their own answers. Is there anything that they can do to change or improve their spending habits?

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.
