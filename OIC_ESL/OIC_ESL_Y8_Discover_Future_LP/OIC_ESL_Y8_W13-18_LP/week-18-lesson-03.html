Section 1: Original introductory activity: Write the word ‘identity’ on the board. Ask: How do you define your identity? Elicit students’ ideas and write them on the board (e.g. gender, age, address, nationality, physical characteristics, family and friends, interests, religion or ethnic group). Refer students to the photo and caption on p.4 of ODF SB 2. Ask: Do you agree with the caption? What are your reasons? Discuss as a class: Does the meaning of identity differ around the world? Scaffolding: Visuals, gesture, word bank and think–pair–share before whole-class responses. ICQs: “Are we thinking first?” “Does everyone share an idea?” CCQs: “What do you already know about what is identity??”

Section 4: In pairs, apply the language from What is identity? to the original reading, listening, picture, dialogue, investigation or writing task. Partners take alternating turns and must ask a follow-up question or justify a decision. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Is your response supported by the lesson example?”

Section 5: Original additional/extension task: Tell students to identify what aspects of their identity are the most important to them from their chart. Invite them to select one and write a short explanation about why it is important part of defining who they are. (This could be a homework assignment for more able students. Less able students can write up their profiles into sentences.) Students can share their writing with each other. Students produce an original written, spoken or visual response; assess against the stated Year 7 outcomes.

Section 6: Emerging: Provide pictures, keywords and an oral sentence frame. Developing: Complete a full response with one supporting detail. Section 7: Original learning review: Students have discussed the different ways that we identify ourselves and others. They have compared their views to those of a presenter. They have written their own identity charts and compared them with those of their classmates to establish similarities.
