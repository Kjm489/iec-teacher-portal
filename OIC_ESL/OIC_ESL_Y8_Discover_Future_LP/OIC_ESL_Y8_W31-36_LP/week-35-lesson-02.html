Original Oxford Week 35 — complete instructional text

Main activity

Explain that this week, students will present their small-group projects to the rest of the class, using their English language skills. Encourage them to practise using functional language, and new grammar and vocabulary learned over the course of the last two units.

After each group’s presentation, ask the rest of the class to peer-review the project, focusing on:

what they enjoyed and were impressed by;

what they think could be developed or improved for next time.

Ensure to explain or elicit the difference between constructive and non-constructive feedback before students offer their reviews.

To help students review each other’s projects, you could use a rubric similar to the one below where students can write their comments.

Independent reflection

Ask students to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last few weeks, while working through the units ‘Why are the seas and oceans important to us?’ and ‘How does age change us?’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

They can also consider any global skills they have developed or practised, which could be applied to a real-world setting in the future.

Students discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged.

Students may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements. Are their goals realistic and attainable? Have they thought about a time-frame? What can they do to achieve their goals?

Section 1: Original introductory activity: Write the word ‘identity’ on the board. Ask: How do you define your identity?
