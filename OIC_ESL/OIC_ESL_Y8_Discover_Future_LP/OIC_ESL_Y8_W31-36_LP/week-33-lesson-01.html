Oxford introductory activity: Identify two to three people who break age stereotypes. You could search online for ‘new start after 60’ for examples of older people; for teenagers or younger people, search for ‘teenager’ with the name of a hobby or activity that is usually associated with older people in your culture. Pre-prepare a short description for each person, as well as a large picture of each person to display. The descriptions shouldn’t indicate their age or gender.

Oxford introductory activity: Read the descriptions aloud one by one. After each description, hold a discussion, asking: Is this person male or female? How old is this person?

Oxford introductory activity: Share ideas, then read each description again. Show the picture of the person being described. Discuss students’ reactions, relating these to common stereotypes and how we should try to reframe our thoughts and biases.

YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
