1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Prepare some sticky notes with the names of famous sports stars on them.

Oxford introductory activity: Invite a volunteer to the front of class. Without letting the volunteer student see, put a sticky note on their forehead, or display the sports star’s name on the board behind them. The idea is that the volunteer cannot see the name, but the other students can. No-one should read the name aloud.

Oxford introductory activity: The volunteer asks Yes/No questions to identify who the sports star is.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Refer to the photos in ODF SB 3 p.83, or display alternative photos which will appeal to your students. Ask: What’s the connection between these photos? Elicit some opinions about sports stars’ salaries.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Check that students understand what ‘reading fluency’ is. Set a timer for two minutes. Ask students to read the text individually, stopping as soon as the timer sounds and marking where they reached. It doesn’t matter if they don’t finish the text. They should also indicate how well they have understood the text – for instance, by making a mark every time they need to re-read something, lose track, or don’t understand a word; or by drawing a happy/unhappy/neutral face when they finish.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In pairs, students discuss: How easy/difficult is the text? What have you understood so far?

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Repeat the process two or three more times. Each time, students should assess whether their understanding is improving and if they are managing to reach further in the text. If necessary, allow students to read to the end of the text untimed.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Bring the class back together. Discuss briefly students’ reactions to the activity, and praise them for their perseverance. Explain that this is a recognized technique for improving reading fluency.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Identify three to five unfamiliar vocabulary items in the text. Elicit what meaning they inferred for these words as they read the text again.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In pairs, ask students to discuss their answers to ex.3 on p.82. Discuss answers to the last question as a whole class.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Write four to six discussion statements about sport on the board: e.g. Sports stars shouldn’t earn more than the average salary of their country. Allow students time to rate each statement 1–6 (1 = ‘I strongly disagree’; 6 = ‘I strongly agree’).

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In groups or as a mingle activity, give students two minutes to talk about each statement, sharing their opinions and trying to persuade each other to agree.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to read as much as they can and not to be anxious about the time limit. Reassure them that you don’t expect them to read the whole text in two minutes, and that the purpose is for them to gradually read and understand more.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able students who finish reading the text more quickly, ask them to note down any new words and the meaning they infer, then to check the meaning in a dictionary.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can research a sports star from their country to find out how much they earn, and whether they try to be a positive influence.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have carried out a timed reading for fluency and taken part in a discussion activity.
