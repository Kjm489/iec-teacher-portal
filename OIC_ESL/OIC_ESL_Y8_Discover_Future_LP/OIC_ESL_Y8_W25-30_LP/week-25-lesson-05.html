3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Ask: Do you read articles about sport online, in magazines, or in newspapers? What makes a good article, in your opinion? Write students’ ideas on the board.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Add any other features of articles from ex.3 in ODF SB 3 p.88.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Then refer students back to the text in ODF SB 3 p.83. In pairs, ask them to check which of these features the article has, identifying examples.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Check answers as a class. Discuss any missing features, and why this might be the case.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Say: One of the most important things about an article is that it engages the reader, so they want to keep reading. How can a writer do this? (possible answers: questions, real-life examples, surprising statistics) Establish that powerful language is also very important.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Allocate one paragraph from the text to each pair of students. (Note: You may want to omit paragraphs 1 and 2 [up to ‘expensive sports merchandise’], as these sections don’t contain good examples of powerful language.) Students read the paragraph carefully and identify examples of powerful language. Prompt them to discuss the power of different words or phrases carefully. Share some examples in a whole-class feedback session.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Focus students’ attention on the final sentence in paragraph 3 (lines 22–25). Discuss how the use of ‘overpriced … underpaid and overworked’ is effective because of the repetition of the prefixes. Refer to ex.4 in ODF SB 3 p.82. Elicit the missing words, focusing on the meaning of the prefixes and how they combine with other words.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In pairs, challenge students to identify the missing words in ex.5 on p.82.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In groups of four or five, ask students to identify an issue that interests them, which they will write an article about for the school magazine/website/blog. This could be one of the issues from the warmer, or from ex.5 on p.82. They must agree on their chosen issue by the end of class. Explain that they are going to write their articles next week.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, help them to identify the powerful language in their paragraph quickly, then ask them to look up the words/phrases and identify how the idea could have been expressed less effectively.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to list other words which can be used with the prefixes.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able students, encourage them to consider how the sentence structures in their paragraph add to the text’s effectiveness.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete ex.3 in ODF SB 3 p.126.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can also read the article in ODF WB 3 p.75 and discuss the questions in ex.5.
