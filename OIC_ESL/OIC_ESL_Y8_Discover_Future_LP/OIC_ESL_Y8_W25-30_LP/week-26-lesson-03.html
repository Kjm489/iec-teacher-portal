PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Draw seven dashes on the board and a simple line drawing that will appeal to students, such as an ice cream cone, or slices of pizza. Students need to find the word ‘failure’ by suggesting letters. If a letter is correct, add it to the word; if a letter is wrong, remove one line from the drawing. If they guess the word before the drawing is erased, they win!

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Ask: How do you feel about failure? Have you ever ‘failed’ at something? Give students five minutes to write freely on their experiences of and feelings about failure. Make sure they understand that free writing is a reflective activity which isn’t read by anyone else. The aim is to write as fluently as you can without worrying about organization, grammar, and vocabulary, but it must be in English!

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Say: Raise your hand if you view failure as something negative. And raise your hand if you view failure as something positive. Don’t comment on students’ responses.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Turn to ex.2 in ODF SB 3 p.86. Tell students to read and complete the exercise individually, then compare their outcomes in pairs. Direct them to the ‘Life skills strategy’ at the top of p.86.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Elicit any unfamiliar vocabulary, writing words/phrases on the board. Instruct students to draw three columns with the headings ‘Positive’, ‘Negative’, and ‘Fixing Things’. In pairs, challenge them to guess the general meaning of the words/phrases and to categorize them accordingly. Check answers as a class, confirming meaning and pronunciation.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Give each pair of students five to ten strips of paper. Tell pairs to work together to write some discussion prompts or questions using the new vocabulary: e.g. Talk about a time when something went wrong in a sport or hobby. / When was the last time you messed up at school? Students review their prompts/questions for accuracy.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Collect in all the prompts/questions and shuffle them together. Form small groups of students and give each group an equal number of prompts, face down. Students take turns selecting a prompt and responding to it. Remind them of useful words and phrases to buy thinking time, and to reformulate when they need to express something in a different way.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Focus on interesting answers in a whole-class feedback session.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Provide less able students with a list of steps to work through when inferring meaning of unknown words and phrases.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able students, ask them to identify the phrasal verbs and work out what their grammar pattern is.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, provide them with some prompt and question stems to help them write their discussion prompts.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students by showing them any discussion prompts that are the same as other pairs’, and asking them to find a way to make theirs different.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete ex.5 in ODF SB 3 p.126.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can write a short description of a time they experienced failure and what they learned from it, using as many words/phrases from the lesson as they can.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have read a short text to expand their vocabulary around failure. They have taken part in a guided discussion activity.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
