1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Search online for ‘Daniel Webb plastic photo’. Choose an image of the artist Daniel Webb and his artwork made out of the plastic he used over one year. Display the photo to the class, but only show a very small part of the image to start.

Oxford introductory activity: Encourage students to make guesses about what the photo shows as you gradually reveal more and more. Before students become bored or frustrated, reveal the whole image and discuss what it shows. Elicit students’ reactions.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Say: A lot of people are trying to live plastic-free lives. What aspects of daily life would you need to consider if you did this? Give students time to discuss their ideas in pairs first, then discuss as a class. Write their suggestions on the board. Go through key vocabulary.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Refer students to ex.5 on p.98. Listen again to the second part, encouraging them to make notes and then discuss their answers in pairs. Listen again if necessary.

Give students time to discuss their ideas in pairs first, then discuss as a class. (She gave the interview to encourage other people to go plastic-free. Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In small groups, ask students to discuss the following ideas. Review the criteria for a successful discussion before they begin.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able students, ask them to research Daniel Webb’s project to share with the rest of the class.

Oxford differentiation: Give less able students a glossary of key words from the interview to refer to.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to note down the details of Anya’s decisions, including the reasons and arguments she gives.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to refer back to the phrases in ex.7 in ODF SB 3 p.99 and to use them during the discussion activity.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.10–11 in ODF WB 3 p.88.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage students to display their SMART goals around the classroom and to read each other’s goals, copying any that they like or would like to add to their own list.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have listened to an interview on the topic of plastic waste, then discussed their own plans for reducing their own waste.
