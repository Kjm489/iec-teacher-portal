1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Choose a word which takes affixes (prefix or suffix) that your students are familiar with, such as ‘help’. Write the root word on the board and elicit how the word can be changed by adding more letters: e.g. ‘helpful’, ‘unhelpful’, ‘helper’, ‘helpless’.

Oxford introductory activity: Elicit other words which use these affixes: e.g. ‘beautiful’, ‘unhappy’, ‘teacher’, ‘careless’.

Oxford introductory activity: Say a word using an affix (e.g. ‘overprice’), then pass or throw the ball to the next student, who will try to give a different word using the same affix (e.g. ‘overworked’). If the word is correct, the student stays in the game. If it is wrong, or if the student can’t think of a word, they are out. The student then throws the ball to another student. Allow them to choose new affixes when necessary.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Ask students to return to their groups from the previous lesson (Week 25, Lesson 5). Remind them of the topics they identified for their articles, and of the key techniques used in articles. In this lesson, students will write their articles together for publication on the school website, blog, or magazine.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Allocate, or ask students to agree on, the role that each group member will have. Encourage them to distribute roles fairly by taking into account each other’s strengths.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: fact checker and researcher – collects useful and interesting facts and anecdotes to exemplify points in the article;

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: language selector – creates a list or mind map of powerful or useful language (vocabulary, phrases, structures);

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: style monitor – decides what style/register the article will have and identifies language and features to achieve this;

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: coherence manager – decides on the line of argument for the article, and checks how well ideas follow on from each other.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Give students a fixed amount of time to research and plan their articles, taking responsibility for their own role and supporting other members of their group as needed. Encourage them to use a range of resources and reference materials.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Students agree on the content for their article, and decide how they will write it. All group members must be involved in the writing process as writers and editors. For example, one student could write the introduction and review paragraph 1; or the ‘language selector’ could write one paragraph and review the others for powerful language. Remind them that working in a team allows them to learn from each other!

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Make available computers for students to write and edit their articles. By the end of the lesson, they should aim to have a full draft which has been through a first edit.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able students, remind them of the P.E.E. paragraph structure.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident students to use other examples of articles in ODF SB 3 and ODF WB 3 for ideas about structure and language.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students by allocating them a role which will be more difficult for them, based on their strengths and weaknesses.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can research and identify photos to accompany their articles.

Oxford learning review: Students have worked as a team to plan and draft an article about an issue related to sports.

YEAR 7 Identity
Week 1, Lesson 1: What is identity?
Learning outcomes: 7.1d, 7.2f

Context
This is the first lesson in the unit ‘Identity’.
Students will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to how other people can be described and discussing the different ways we can identify ourselves. 
This lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, although the timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
