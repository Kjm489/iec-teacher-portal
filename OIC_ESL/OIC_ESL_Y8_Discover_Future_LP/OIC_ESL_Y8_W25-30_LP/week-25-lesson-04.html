PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Partner Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory activity: Divide the class into two halves. Invite a volunteer from each half to the front and instruct them to stand with their back to the board. Write a recently learned or topic-related word on the board. The seated students need to define/explain the word for their team member to guess. The first student to guess the word wins a point for their team.

Oxford introductory activity: Repeat, changing the volunteer student.

ICQ (IEC): What is the first task and what will you produce?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher model (IEC adaptation): Demonstrate one representative example from the Oxford main activity.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Give each pair of students one reporting verb. Ask them to write a short dialogue to illustrate the reporting verb, considering the relationship between the speakers, the context, and the topic. Tell them to practise their dialogues quietly, so that other students can’t hear them.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Invite one pair to the front of the class to perform their dialogue using only the intonation patterns, not the words. (Students can practise this beforehand.). The listening students try to report the conversation: e.g. He’s complaining that his dinner is too cold. Repeat with other pairs.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Display some photos of successful sports people from around the world, including their names. Include people with interesting stories, such as: Eric Moussambani, Derek Redmond, Jesse Owens, Yusra Mardini, Ade Adepitan, Ellie Simmonds, Anas Al Khalifa, Abbas Karimi, and Sky Brown. Ask students to choose one sports person to research. They should avoid sports people who are very well known by classmates.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Elicit some of the kinds of information students should find out – for example, background information, achievements, challenges. Set a time limit for them to research their chosen sports person.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: Form groups of students. Ask each group to prepare between 10 and 20 questions to interview students from other groups about their chosen sports person.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: One member from each group is interviewed in a ‘hot seat’ interview. The interviewee takes on the role of the sports person they researched (so answers in the first person). The interviewers try to ask as many of their questions as they can. Review with students ways to gain or fill thinking time.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: The interviews take place simultaneously and with a time limit. Repeat so most or all students have a chance to be the interviewees.

Oxford main activity — complete original sequence: In groups, encourage students to identify the most interesting/funniest/most moving/most surprising fact they heard in the interviews. Share as a class.

CCQ (IEC): What detail or example supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Supported collaboration (IEC adaptation): Use the pair/group discussion or shared response within the Oxford task; provide a sentence frame and gradually remove it.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application (IEC adaptation): Learners independently produce an answer, explanation, or product demonstrating the original OIC outcome.

Formative check (IEC): Show your own response, not a copied group answer.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give less able students a set of guided notes to complete when doing their research.

Oxford differentiation: In the ‘hot seat’ activity, encourage less confident students to revise ways of asking for clarification.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident students to check that the information they find is accurate by cross-checking with different websites and reports.

Oxford additional tasks: Students can write up the results of their research into a short biography.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Students have researched facts about a chosen sports person. They have asked and answered interview questions, before reporting back to their group.
