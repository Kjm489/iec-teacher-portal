Original Oxford Week 27 — complete instructional text

Main activity

Explain that this week, students will work in small groups on a project of their choice. They can extend their learning into an area of particular interest to them, and practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way that they could apply to a real-world setting.

Students will focus on a project related to the topics ‘What is the world of work like?’ or ‘What impact do sports have on our world?’. They should work in small groups with shared areas of interest.

Students can choose their own project, linked to either topic, or they can develop a project from one of the options below.

Encourage students to use the IDEAS to Action framework, which they will be familiar with from their Global Skills Projects lessons. Remind them that this is a smaller-scale project, so they may need to adapt and modify it to suit their needs.

Students will be responsible for the planning and management of their project. This should involve:

suggesting and allocating specific tasks

researching and drafting creative work

reviewing drafts and contributing constructive ideas and suggestions

joint presentation (the following week)

formative self-assessment and peer-assessment (the following week)

Students can present their work in a way that best suits their project, depending on resources and time available. See how creative they can be with their presentations. Here is a non-exhaustive list of different kinds of presentation:

A speech or oral retelling (perhaps supported by visual resources, such as a PowerPoint presentation, photographs, a campaign poster, a model, artefacts, graphs or charts, etc.)

A pitch (to sell a product or service)

A skills demonstration

A debate

An art/photography exhibition or display

An interview with an expert in the field of enquiry

A short film or documentary

A creative performance

Remind students that they have five days to complete the planning, research, and execution of their project. At the end of Day 1, teachers could look over each group’s plan to ensure that they have allocated tasks appropriately and know what to accomplish each day.

Topic 1 (What is the world of work like?)

Careers fair: Plan a careers fair to show fellow students a range of career opportunities that they could follow.

Research other careers fairs for ideas for what you could include, and decide on the aims of your fair.

Decide what year groups your careers fair will target, and survey students from those year groups about their interests.

Contact local businesses and employers, and local training and higher education organizations, to invite them to participate.

Plan the fair itself: Where will it take place? How long for? Will you ask anyone to give a talk? How will you advertise it?

Present your project as a plan for a careers fair.

My working life: Research and write an article about different people’s experiences of work.

Interview different adults about their careers and what they have experienced. Think about what kind of people would be interesting to interview, and how you will contact them. Plan your interview questions – what kinds of question will encourage the interviewee to give you interesting information?

Plan your article. How will you engage the reader? What is the theme of the article going to be? How can you link the interviews together? Research your interviewees: what background information will be useful for your article?

Think about the layout and look of your article. Will you use photos? Can you use publishing software to make the article look professional?

Topic 2 (What impact do sports have on our world?)

Get active! Plan a campaign to encourage people to be more active.

Decide who your campaign will target, and the best method to reach them. What messages will appeal to your target audience?

Research activities and sports which will appeal to your target audience. How many will you present?

Find a ‘face’ for your campaign – contact a local role model, or a young athlete at your school, who could present your campaign.

Prepare some campaign materials – a leaflet, a blog post, some social media posts, a podcast recording, and/or a poster. Think about pictures, layout, colours, and tone of voice. What language is most persuasive?

Debate: Plan, prepare for, and hold a debate on a sports-related issue.

Identify an issue which is important for your country or your local community. This could be a big issue, like inequality in sports, or a smaller issue, like building a new sports facility. Write the question or statement (the ‘motion’) that you are going to debate.

Research how debates are usually organized, and plan how you will structure yours, and who will take on the different roles.

Form two teams to research the arguments for each side of the debate. Remember to research your opponent’s arguments as well, and to prepare an argument against them.

Plan how you are going to hold your audience’s attention and give effective speeches.

Your presentation will be your debate.

Work out! Plan and create a blog post with workout routines for young people.

Decide who your blog post will be aimed at, and what kind of workout routine will appeal to them (e.g. cardio fitness, strength, flexibility, or a combination).

Research some exercises. How effective are they? Do they inspire you to want to work out? How could you improve them? What information could you give about healthy lifestyles?

Choose the exercises you want to include. Think about the benefits of each exercise, and how you will explain and demonstrate it.

If you have the necessary permissions, you could film yourselves performing the exercises to add to the blog post.
