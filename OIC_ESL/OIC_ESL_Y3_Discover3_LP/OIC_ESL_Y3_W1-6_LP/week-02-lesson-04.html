Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Receiving and responding to a letter from a friend. Original outcomes: 3.3e, 3.4d, 3.4e. Unit: Making friends.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Ask children how they feel when they receive a nice letter from a friend or family member. Talk about why it is important to have good relationships with friends and classmates.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Remind children that they are learning about giving and receiving letters.

Oxford opening: Divide the class into small groups and explain that they will work together.

Oxford opening: One child from each group writes the first section of a letter to a friend, then folds over the paper so their classmates can’t see. Then another child from each group writes the second section. Repeat until the letter is completed. Break down larger sections into one or two sentences each. Encourage children to be creative and interesting with their writing.

Oxford opening: Collect the letters and give them to a different group. Each group reads the letter they receive and discusses how they might reply. What information would they like to know?

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children how they feel when they receive a nice letter from a friend or family member. Talk about why it is important to have good relationships with friends and classmates.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children a model letter. You can return to one of the letters in Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.30–31 and p.44, or create one yourself, including a range of punctuation marks. See what they can remember about the structure of a letter – they learned about this in the previous lesson.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Choose one or two punctuation marks from the model letter. Children work in pairs to discuss what the punctuation is used for. Discuss children’s answers as a whole class. For example, elicit the purpose of an exclamation mark (!), then see if children can read an exclamation in a sentence by changing their intonation (e.g. It was so cold!). Have fun with this – explore how different punctuation marks make you read sentences in different ways.

Oxford task — guided: Write a few sentences with deliberate punctuation mistakes on the board. Ask children to rewrite the sentences using the punctuation mark correctly.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Choose one or two punctuation marks from the model letter. Children work in pairs to discuss what the punctuation is used for. Discuss children’s answers as a whole class. For example, elicit the purpose of an exclamation mark (!), then see if children can read an exclamation in a sentence by changing their intonation (e.g. It was so cold!). Have fun with this – explore how different punctuation marks make you read sentences in different ways.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Children choose one of the model letters to write a reply to. Ask them to plan their response and decide how to answer the questions – they can be as inventive as they like. Encourage children to thank the sender for the letter because it is important to express gratitude for nice things (e.g. friendships).

Oxford source task: Children may find writing challenging. Monitor actively and support. Remind children that they are in the stretch zone; finding a task difficult helps them to learn and persevere.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the punctuation input, read a letter aloud to children and accentuate what each punctuation mark does (e.g. take a long pause after each full stop, increase the pitch of your voice after a question mark, shout after an exclamation mark).

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by asking them to write a full reply based on their plan.

Oxford additional tasks: Draw the different punctuation marks and explain the purpose of each one in a sentence: e.g. We use a full stop to end a sentence.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.37.

Oxford additional tasks: Draft a response to the letter written as an additional task in Week 2, Lesson 3. Try to answer the questions and ask additional follow-up questions.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to extract information from and respond to letters from friends. They have explored the different uses of punctuation marks and how they ask the reader to do different things.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 2, LESSON 4

IEC supplemental game — Letter comprehension hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Receiving and responding to a letter from a friend.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Question answer match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Receiving and responding to a letter from a friend.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
