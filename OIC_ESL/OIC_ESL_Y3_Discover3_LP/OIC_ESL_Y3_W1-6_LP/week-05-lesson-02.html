PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children how long they spend doing chores each day, what time they wake up in the morning, and what time they go to bed. See if children remember to include routine vocabulary and adverbs of frequency in their responses. Remind them that by revisiting previous learning, they are helping to build their long-term memory.

Oxford opening: Ask what they think the answers would be for children in different parts of the world.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children your basic daily routine. Write notes with times on the board. You may wish to remind them of the table on p.133 of Oxford Discover SB 2 to get children thinking about more activities at different times of the day. Recap the question: What do you do in the morning/afternoon/evening? To assist children with the following post-discussion activity, where they will report back on what their partner does, you may wish to play them the song from ex.D of Oxford Discover SB 2 p.133.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Children work in pairs to discuss their own routines, comparing them with yours. Encourage children to use phrases such as ‘He/She gets up later than me’. Remind pairs to take turns and listen carefully to each other’s questions and answers.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Tell children they are going to read a text about someone’s routine in a different country. Search online ‘Journey North – A day in the life of a Mexican student’ and click through each slide about the young girl’s routine. Ask children to predict what they will read for each blog. Then read aloud for children to follow. Check if children’s predictions are correct.

Oxford task — guided: Ask: What is similar and different between the routine you’ve read about and your own?

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in pairs to discuss their own routines, comparing them with yours. Encourage children to use phrases such as ‘He/She gets up later than me’. Remind pairs to take turns and listen carefully to each other’s questions and answers.

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in pairs. They discuss what they like and dislike about each child’s routine. Which routine would they most like to have and why?

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Read the blog again. As a class, discuss the reasons why people’s routines are different. Why do they have to do more/fewer chores/classes? Why do they get up earlier/later?

Oxford source task: Children work in pairs. They discuss what they like and dislike about each child’s routine. Which routine would they most like to have and why?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the reading activity, you can read out the text to children slowly, drilling new language.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by asking them how they would feel if their routine was to change to the one they read about.

Oxford additional tasks: Write sentences using comparative and superlative adjectives about children’s own routines and the routines from the lesson: e.g. I get up earlier than Freda. Freda does more chores than I do.

Oxford additional tasks: Make a timeline for someone’s routine in a different country. Children can use mixed media, including drawings and photos from the internet, to make their timeline look more visually appealing. You can display children’s timelines around the classroom.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a blog post about a different routine to their own, and discussed similarities and differences.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Daily reading timeline

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A typical day (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Text evidence hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A typical day (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Main idea sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A typical day (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Partner reading questions

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A typical day (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Daily life compare

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A typical day (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

YEAR 3 Routines
Week 5, Lesson 2: A typical day (1)
Learning outcomes: 3.3f, 3.3g

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Routines’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills. They will understand how other people’s lives around the world are different to their own lives.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
