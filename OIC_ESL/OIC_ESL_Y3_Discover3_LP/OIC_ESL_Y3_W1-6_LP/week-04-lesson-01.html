Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: What are our routines?. Original outcomes: 3.1c, 3.1d, 3.1e, 3.2a, 3.2b. Unit: Routines.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Project pictures of people doing different routine activities, such as walking to school. Ask: What is a routine? Elicit answers and give the dictionary definition.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Tell children to work in teams. Ask them to think of as many things they do every day as they can. Go through answers as a class, writing the vocabulary as well as other ideas on the board. Award a point for each correct answer – the team with the most points wins.

Oxford opening: In the same teams, children take turns miming an activity for their group to guess. Let them have fun with this – they can do exaggerated movements.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Project pictures of people doing different routine activities, such as walking to school. Ask: What is a routine? Elicit answers and give the dictionary definition.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Divide the board into two sections with ‘Advantages’ in one section and ‘Disadvantages’ in the other. Ask the class to call out advantages and disadvantages of having a routine. Encourage children to think about how having a routine helps them sleep.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Record colleagues or unnamed children talking about their daily routines and play it to children. Otherwise, present your own routine to the class: e.g. On Monday morning, I usually get up at six o’clock. I always have breakfast and brush my teeth, then I have a shower. I go to school at half past seven. Play the audio and check whether children’s guesses are correct.

Oxford task — guided: Play the audio again and ask true or false questions about the routine: Does (he) eat breakfast in the morning? Does (he) watch TV in the evening? Then play it again and ask children to focus on the intonation patterns. Repeat some short sentences as a class.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children to write ‘Morning’, ‘Afternoon’, and ‘Evening’ in their notebooks. In pairs, they think of the different things they do every day and write them in the correct columns.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask children to write ‘Morning’, ‘Afternoon’, and ‘Evening’ in their notebooks. In pairs, they think of the different things they do every day and write them in the correct columns.

Oxford source task: Based on their columns, children take turns saying sentences about their own routines. Encourage them to give extra details, including adverbs of frequency, if they feel comfortable.

Oxford source task: Draw four clocks on the board showing different times, with a name below each. Read sentences out about routines (e.g. They go to sleep at nine o’clock.) and children guess who it is.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the speaking activity, write a model sentence on the board: e.g. I brush my teeth in the morning. I eat my lunch in the afternoon.

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the listening activity, pre-teach some words and phrases children may have difficulty with.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children. What times of the day do they do different routine activities?

Oxford additional tasks: Write a diary entry about a typical day. Show children an example diary entry if they need help with the format and writing style.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened to a dialogue about routines. Using this model, they have discussed their own routines and reflected on the importance of having a regular routine.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 4, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Daily routine mime

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What are our routines?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Time of day sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What are our routines?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Routine listening

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What are our routines?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Play or read a short lesson-related sound or utterance twice. Learners identify a key detail, compare answers in pairs, then explain which sound or word they heard.

ICQ: Do you listen twice? Do you compare with a partner before answering?

CCQ: Which word or sound helped you decide?

Assessment: Accurate listening response and evidence

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Routine sequence

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What are our routines?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Daily schedule report

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What are our routines?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
