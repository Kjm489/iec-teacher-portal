Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Guess my hobbies … (1). Original outcomes: 3.3d, 3.3g, 3.3h, 3.4c. Unit: Routines.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show the class pictures of people doing exciting hobbies to generate interest in the topic. You can display some of the pictures on p.28 of Oxford Discover SB 3. Ask the class if they have tried or would like to try any of the hobbies.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Write all the letters of the alphabet on the board. Tell children to work in teams and try to think of a hobby for each letter. Go through answers as a class, writing suggestions on the board. Award a point for each correct answer – the team with the most points wins.

Oxford opening: Children work in pairs and take turns acting out a hobby for their partner to guess. Let children have fun with this – they can do exaggerated movements and make sound effects. After each turn, ask children to discuss how the physical activity made them feel.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show the class pictures of people doing exciting hobbies to generate interest in the topic. You can display some of the pictures on p.28 of Oxford Discover SB 3. Ask the class if they have tried or would like to try any of the hobbies.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children to think about the most interesting or exciting hobby they know. They work in pairs and take turns telling their partner about the hobby. Encourage children to think about how different hobbies can affect their bodies and minds.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Tell children that they are now going to read about someone’s hobby. Search online ‘book dash – Grandpa Farouk’s garden’. What hobby do they think the chosen text is about by looking at the pictures and skimming the text for gist? Ask them what they would like to learn about the hobby before they start reading. Write their questions on the board.

Oxford task — guided: Read the text as a whole class and see if it answers children’s questions.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children to think about the most interesting or exciting hobby they know. They work in pairs and take turns telling their partner about the hobby. Encourage children to think about how different hobbies can affect their bodies and minds.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask children to read the text individually and answer comprehension questions focused on specific information: e.g. What items do the children take into the secret hobby room? / What is the secret hobby? / Why is Grandpa sad about his garden? / What happens at the beginning/end?

Oxford source task: While children are working, you can research the answers to children’s questions if the reading texts didn’t tell them everything they wanted to know about a hobby.

Oxford source task: Children write a paragraph about one of their hobbies. See if they can comment on the effect the hobby has on their body and mind. For example, if it’s a calm or energetic hobby, how does it make them feel? Ask a volunteer to share their work with the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the introductory activity, let children use collocations and adjectives for more difficult letters.

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the writing activity, provide a few model sentences that have highlighted variables for children to change.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to include more detail in their writing. Is their hobby difficult to learn? What is their favourite part about their hobby and why?

Oxford additional tasks: Compare children’s paragraphs and discuss how their hobbies are similar/different.

Oxford additional tasks: In groups, research and write about a new or peculiar hobby. Encourage them to use books, magazines, and the internet to research the hobby further.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have explored a variety of hobbies and how they can make us feel. They have read a text about a hobby, before writing about their own interests.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Hobby reading hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Guess my hobbies … (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

IEC supplemental game — Hobby charades

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Guess my hobbies … (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Profile writing

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Guess my hobbies … (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Hobby questions

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Guess my hobbies … (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
