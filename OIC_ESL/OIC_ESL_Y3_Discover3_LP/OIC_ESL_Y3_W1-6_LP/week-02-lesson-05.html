Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Writing to a penfriend. Original outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4b, 3.4c, 3.4d. Unit: Making friends.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Ask children if anyone has a penfriend: How did it start? What do you talk about?

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play the game ‘Guess who?’. Divide the class into small groups. One child pretends to be someone famous / a person they admire. The other children ask questions, in turn, to find out who their classmate is pretending to be. Encourage children to practise using the question words from earlier in the unit. Repeat until everyone has had a turn.

Oxford opening: Before starting, ask a child to explain the rules to the class.

Oxford opening: Ask a confident group to demonstrate the activity in front of the class. Discuss and write down the different questions that children ask.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children if anyone has a penfriend: How did it start? What do you talk about?

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Discuss what you say and ask when you talk to someone for the first time. Elicit some ideas from children and write them on the board.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: In pairs, children role-play a short conversation between new friends. Remind children that it is important to be kind, as this will lead to a stronger friendship. First, ask a child to explain the task to the class to ensure understanding.

Oxford task — guided: Read a letter from a penfriend to the class. You can invent your own or adapt one of the letters on pp.30–31 of Oxford Discover SB 3. Ask: What different things does (Juan) ask about? What does (Juan) tell (Liam) about? (age, appearance, family, hobbies, languages, weather)

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, children role-play a short conversation between new friends. Remind children that it is important to be kind, as this will lead to a stronger friendship. First, ask a child to explain the task to the class to ensure understanding.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Spend five minutes reviewing the sections of a letter that children have learned previously.

Oxford source task: Assign children made-up penfriends to write to from different countries around the world. They are going to write a letter to their penfriend for the first time. Encourage children to be polite, say nice things, and show interest in their penfriend’s life. Incite their curiosity about the world around them – for example, what do they want to know about their penfriend’s country that they don’t know already? Before starting, ask a child to explain the writing task to the class to ensure understanding. Allocate 20–30 minutes to this task. Before starting, children could create their own chart similar to the one in ex.B of Oxford Discover WB 3 p.37 to help them with their writing.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the writing activity, speak to children about their own neighbourhood and what they can do there. This should give them more ideas about what questions to ask.

Oxford differentiation: Write a list on the board of all the different topics children could write/ask about in their letters. Begin the list with easier topics and end with more difficult topics: Easy – age, name of town; Medium – hobbies, favourite food, likes and dislikes; Difficult – job they want in the future, facts about their country. Tell children to choose a range of different topics, and see if they can challenge themselves by choosing a more difficult topic.

Oxford additional tasks: Compare each other’s letters and discuss similarities and differences.

Oxford additional tasks: Swap letters with each other and write replies to them.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have revised question words and written a letter to a penfriend for the first time.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 2, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — Penfriend profile mystery

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing to a penfriend.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Question bank

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing to a penfriend.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

YEAR 3 Making friends
Week 2, Lesson 5: Writing to a penfriend
Learning outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4b, 3.4c, 3.4d

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Making friends’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by writing a letter to a penfriend for the first time. They will revise question words.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
