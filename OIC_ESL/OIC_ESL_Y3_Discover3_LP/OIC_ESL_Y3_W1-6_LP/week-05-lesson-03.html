LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3

Week: 5
Lesson: 3
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.1a, 3.1d, 3.2b, 3.2e

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: A typical day (2). Original outcomes: 3.1a, 3.1d, 3.2b, 3.2e. Unit: Routines.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show the class some photos of different schools from around the world. Elicit their initial thoughts and reactions.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Children work in teams to write a list of as many school subjects and school activities as they can. Award a point for each correct answer – the team with the most points wins. Encourage children to think of activities and subjects from other countries.

Oxford opening: You may also like to revisit the story about Layla’s day at school on p.104 of Oxford Discover SB 2 and ask children about the different places in and around school, e.g. crosswalk, computer room, cafeteria, school library, and the principal’s office. In order to prepare children for the main activity and thinking about similar and different school routines, you could compare Layla’s experience with the rest of the class, and ask: Do you have breakfast in the kitchen at home, or at school?

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show the class some photos of different schools from around the world. Elicit their initial thoughts and reactions.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Children work in pairs, taking turns to say what might be different about each school. Share answers with the class and compare everyone’s ideas. Encourage children’s curiosity – their ideas can be sensible, silly, and inventive!

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Show children a video or read out a script about a different school routine. Search online ‘A day in the life of a Costa Rican school pupil – Eat Happy Project’ or ‘typical primary school day around the world’. Finnish schools also have an interesting routine.

Oxford task — guided: Write a list of comprehension questions on the board and check children’s understanding of each one. Play the audio/video again and ask children to note down their answers as they listen. Check answers as a whole class. Explain any words that children may not know.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in pairs, taking turns to say what might be different about each school. Share answers with the class and compare everyone’s ideas. Encourage children’s curiosity – their ideas can be sensible, silly, and inventive!

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in pairs to discuss the similarities and differences between their own school day and the one they have listened to. What is the most interesting fact they’ve learned? Encourage them to think about the things they are grateful to learn about and have at their school.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Children work in pairs to discuss the similarities and differences between their own school day and the one they have listened to. What is the most interesting fact they’ve learned? Encourage them to think about the things they are grateful to learn about and have at their school.

Oxford source task: Ask: Who has the most interesting school day? Is there anything about their school day you would like to have in your day?

Oxford source task: As a class, discuss what children would change about their own school day and why. Write on the board ‘I would like to have …’ and ‘I would like to change …’ to support this discussion.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the speaking activities, write some model sentences on the board.

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the listening activity, pause the audio after a sentence or two. You may want to repeat the sentences to the class.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to paraphrase what they hear in the listening text.

Oxford additional tasks: Hand out blank school timetables. Ask children to fill in their perfect school timetable.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a poster about a perfect school using mixed media.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened to a text about a different school day and compared it to their own. They have reflected on what they like about their school day and what they would change.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 5, LESSON 3

IEC supplemental game — Daily listening notes

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A typical day (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Play or read a short lesson-related sound or utterance twice. Learners identify a key detail, compare answers in pairs, then explain which sound or word they heard.

ICQ: Do you listen twice? Do you compare with a partner before answering?

CCQ: Which word or sound helped you decide?

Assessment: Accurate listening response and evidence

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — A day interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A typical day (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — True false repair

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A typical day (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Time activity match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A typical day (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
