PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Divide the class into four teams. Team members discuss the different activities that friends can do together, taking turns to speak and write their ideas on the board.

Oxford opening: Introduce a challenge: each word must begin with the last letter of the previous word (e.g. soccer, read, dance). Award one point for each correct answer – the team with the most points wins. Stress the importance of teamwork, listening to each other, and taking turns.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children if they know what it means to feel ‘stressed’ (worried and anxious). Then ask them how they feel after doing different activities – some pleasant, some hard. Explain what stress is and the benefits of certain activities on stress: Stress is what you feel when you are worried or scared about something. It can give you a headache or make you feel like you have butterflies in your stomach. Being active and doing exercise can help you to feel better, more energized, and less anxious!

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Write ‘do’, ‘play’, ‘go’, and ‘have’ on the board, and review different collocations: do gymnastics, play tennis, go cycling, have picnics. See if children can write their activities from the ‘Introductory activity’ under the correct verb.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Take the opportunity to explain how some verbs (e.g. swim) can become a noun (e.g. swimming) by adding –ing (gerunds). Look at the grammar box in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.21.

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to say which activities on the board they do and don’t do every week: e.g. I skateboard, but I don’t cycle.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Write ‘like’, ‘don’t like’, ‘love’, and ‘hate’ on the board. Children work in pairs and take turns saying which activities they love, (don’t) like, and hate. Then ask pairs to order a short list of activities from most liked to least liked.

Oxford pair/group task: Encourage them to reflect on how doing these activities with friends might make them feel (less/more stressed, calm, excited). Invite some pairs to share their thoughts.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: They can also listen to and sing the songs on p.21 of Oxford Discover SB 3.

Oxford source task: Write ‘like’, ‘don’t like’, ‘love’, and ‘hate’ on the board. Children work in pairs and take turns saying which activities they love, (don’t) like, and hate. Then ask pairs to order a short list of activities from most liked to least liked.

Oxford source task: Encourage them to reflect on how doing these activities with friends might make them feel (less/more stressed, calm, excited). Invite some pairs to share their thoughts.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the speaking activity, write model sentences on the board: e.g. I love playing football, I hate doing gymnastics, I like skateboarding with my best friend.

Oxford differentiation: More able children can order a longer list of activities. When sharing their findings, encourage children to use comparative and superlative adjectives.

Oxford additional tasks: In small groups, work together to create an ‘Exciting Activity’ poster using mixed media (e.g. photos from magazines or the internet, or their own drawings). Use books and the internet to research activities and write captions.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete pp.14–15 of Oxford Discover WB 3.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have spoken about the activities friends do together using new collocations and gerunds. They have reflected on how different activities make them feel.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Friend activities survey

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Which activities do you do with friends?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
