Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Asking for help. Original outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2e. Unit: Making friends.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show children some photos of people who need help. Ask what they think they should do if they see people ‘struggling’ with things – this means finding something difficult.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Explain to children that they are going to talk about helping others and asking for help.

Oxford opening: Tell children a short anecdote about when someone asked you for help and what you did. Reassure children that everyone needs help sometimes – it isn’t a bad thing to ask for it.

Oxford opening: Ask children to tell the person next to them about the last time someone asked them for help or the last time they helped someone else.

Oxford opening: As a class, discuss how helping people makes you feel, and how you feel when someone helps you. Explain that understanding how other people feel in different situations and offering them help, if and when necessary, is called ‘empathy’.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children some photos of people who need help. Ask what they think they should do if they see people ‘struggling’ with things – this means finding something difficult.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask: How do I ask someone for help? What phrases can I use? Elicit their answers and write them on the board. Write more phrases if the class doesn’t think of enough: ‘Can/Could you help me, please?’ ‘Would you mind helping me?’ ‘Excuse me? I’m sorry to bother you …’.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Discuss why it is important to be polite when you need help. Ask: Which of the phrases on the board are the most polite? What do you say after someone helps you with something?

Oxford task — guided: You can also look at ex.C on p.34 of Oxford Discover SB 3 and teach any unknown language. Remind children that they can respond to questions by saying ‘Sure, I can help you’, ‘Yes, I can’, and ‘Of course’.

Oxford task — guided: Children work in pairs and practise asking each other for help, using the photos from the beginning of the lesson. Invite one confident pair to demonstrate their exchange.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in pairs and practise asking each other for help, using the photos from the beginning of the lesson. Invite one confident pair to demonstrate their exchange.

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in pairs to plan a dialogue between two people asking for help and giving directions.

Oxford pair/group task: Encourage pairs to perform their role-plays. See if they can use actions as well as words to make their role-plays more interesting.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Review asking for and giving directions (see Week 1, Lesson 3).

Oxford source task: Children work in pairs to plan a dialogue between two people asking for help and giving directions.

Oxford source task: Encourage pairs to perform their role-plays. See if they can use actions as well as words to make their role-plays more interesting.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the role-play activity, show a model dialogue to children with highlighted variables they can change.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by telling them to plan their dialogue around a different problem, such as asking for help with their homework or with making a meal.

Oxford additional tasks: Children write about when they couldn’t do something and someone helped them. This can be in the form of a diary entry or a simple paragraph.

Oxford additional tasks: Listen to the audio for ex.C on p.34 of Oxford Discover SB 3.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned that it is OK to need and ask for help. They have practised asking for help politely in short, structured conversations.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 3 Making friends
Week 2, Lesson 1: Asking for help
Learning outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2e

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Making friends’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by starting a conversation and asking for help politely. They will cultivate empathy by understanding that people can’t do everything by themselves, and that it is OK to ask for help and kind to offer help.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
