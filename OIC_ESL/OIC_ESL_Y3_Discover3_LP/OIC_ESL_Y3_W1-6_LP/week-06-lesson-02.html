Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Model and plan extended activity. Original outcomes: Unit summary. Unit: Routines.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Source alignment note: Oxford supplies one shared weekly review plan. The five IEC lesson divisions are adaptations, not separate Oxford lesson titles.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Routines’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford opening: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford opening: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas:

Oxford task — guided: Children work in a small groups to make a poster about an interesting routine from another country. Using multiple resources, children can research the daily lives of children from their chosen country, including daily activities (getting up, chores, etc.), hobbies and interests, school days and journeys to school (encourage children to research interesting journeys to school), and family lives. At the end of the week, children display their posters on the wall and other groups can ask them questions.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in a small groups to make a poster about an interesting routine from another country. Using multiple resources, children can research the daily lives of children from their chosen country, including daily activities (getting up, chores, etc.), hobbies and interests, school days and journeys to school (encourage children to research interesting journeys to school), and family lives. At the end of the week, children display their posters on the wall and other groups can ask them questions.

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in a small groups to plan a presentation about a fascinating school – one in a different country that is very different to their own. They write a presentation script and create a visual aid (slideshow, poster, etc.). They can include where the school is, what a normal school day looks like, and why the school is interesting and different to their own. Perhaps it is in a remote location, the schoolchildren study an unfamiliar subject, or they participate in an exciting-looking event or sport. They can look back at Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.30–31 for ideas in Sati’s letters about her new school.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Children work in a small groups to plan a presentation about a fascinating school – one in a different country that is very different to their own. They write a presentation script and create a visual aid (slideshow, poster, etc.). They can include where the school is, what a normal school day looks like, and why the school is interesting and different to their own. Perhaps it is in a remote location, the schoolchildren study an unfamiliar subject, or they participate in an exciting-looking event or sport. They can look back at Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.30–31 for ideas in Sati’s letters about her new school.

Oxford source task: When you divide the class into groups, make one child the group leader. This leader should delegate different parts of the project and give simple instructions to group members. It is also the role of the leader to ensure that everybody is listening to each other and working together respectfully.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the poster project, show children some example posters to demonstrate how pictures, diagrams, and writing can make a poster look appealing.

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the presentation project, provide a list of fascinating schools that children can research in more depth.

Oxford differentiation: Each group should include mixed abilities so more able children can help and encourage children who are unsure about the task or find writing hard.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete pp.23–32 of Oxford Discover WB 3 if not done previously.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Project idea pitch

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Model and plan extended activity.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
