Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Planning a blog post about your school day. Original outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4b, 3.4c. Unit: Routines.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show children the blog you presented in Lesson 2, or your school blog if it has one. There is also an example of a blog in Oxford Discover WB 3 on p.88. Discuss the different parts of a blog (including the title, links, pictures, main text, and comment box) and how ideas are presented logically.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask the class if anyone reads blogs. What are they about? Where do you read them?

Oxford opening: As a class, discuss what people can write about in blogs and why. Elicit some positive and negative reasons (to make readers laugh, to educate or inform, to criticize someone or something). Remind children how sharing positive emotions can make others feel better.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children the blog you presented in Lesson 2, or your school blog if it has one. There is also an example of a blog in Oxford Discover WB 3 on p.88. Discuss the different parts of a blog (including the title, links, pictures, main text, and comment box) and how ideas are presented logically.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children that they are going to plan their own blog post about their school routine.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Children begin by working in pairs to discuss what a typical day at school in their country is like. Write a series of questions on the board to prompt children’s thinking: e.g. ‘What time does school start?’, ‘When do you have lunch?’, ‘What subjects do you have?’, and ‘Do you have English every day?’.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Look at the structure of a blog post and the order of events. Draw a mind map on the board to help with planning, focus, and structure.

Oxford task — guided: Children complete their own mind map, individually or in pairs. Keep monitoring closely.

Oxford task — guided: Children start writing draft sentences, using their mind map as a guide. You can also write key words and phrases on the board:

Oxford task — guided: I go to school at … o’clock.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children begin by working in pairs to discuss what a typical day at school in their country is like. Write a series of questions on the board to prompt children’s thinking: e.g. ‘What time does school start?’, ‘When do you have lunch?’, ‘What subjects do you have?’, and ‘Do you have English every day?’.

Oxford pair/group task: Children complete their own mind map, individually or in pairs. Keep monitoring closely.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: My (favourite) teacher is …

Oxford source task: On Monday morning, I …

Oxford source task: I like … / I prefer …

Oxford source task: always, often, sometimes, usually

Oxford source task: Circle the class, checking that everyone understands the task and is working on their plan.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the planning activity, ask children more questions about their typical school day and write them on the board.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by using a range of planning tools, such as flow diagrams.

Oxford additional tasks: In pairs, compare their plans and discuss how they are similar and different.

Oxford additional tasks: In groups, children discuss what they like to read about in a blog and search online for blogs that are similar to their ideas. They can share these with their friends.

Oxford additional tasks: Label the different parts of the blog post on p.88 of Oxford Discover WB 3 (don’t focus on the content).

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about the format of blogs and planned their own blog post.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Blog structure hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Planning a blog post about your school day.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Audience purpose

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Planning a blog post about your school day.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Blog storyboard

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Planning a blog post about your school day.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Topic sentence

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Planning a blog post about your school day.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning
