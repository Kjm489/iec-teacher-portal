Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Time zones. Original outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2b, 3.3h. Unit: Routines.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Ask: How do we measure time? Elicit answers and discuss as a class.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask the class if it is the same time everywhere in the world, or even in their own country. Elicit what a time zone is and explain how time is different around the world. You can use a globe or a projected map to demonstrate these differences. Choose a few places on the globe/map, point, and say the city names and time of day/night in each place.

Oxford opening: Invite a confident volunteer to the front of the class to research different times around the world using a search engine.

Oxford opening: Demonstrate how to play the ‘time zone’ game. On the board, write a selection of time zones that relate to the cities on the time zone cards. Then pick up a card with a city name (e.g. Singapore) and roll two dice. This gives you the time in that city (e.g. 6 + 1 = 7, so the time is seven o’clock). Ask a child to pick up another card and show their city (e.g. Tokyo). The first child to say the correct time in that place gets a point (e.g. eight o’clock). Divide the class into groups of four and give each group a set of time zone cards. The child with the most points at the end wins. Encourage groups to be understanding, play slower, and help children that find the activity more challenging.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask: How do we measure time? Elicit answers and discuss as a class.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Talk about seconds, minutes, and hours, and that there are 24 hours in a day. You can also discuss how long it takes Earth to spin around on its axis (24 hours/one day), using a lamp and ball to illustrate. When it spins, the part of Earth facing away from the sun is night, and the part facing the sun is day. This is why there are different time zones. Drill key vocabulary.

Oxford task — guided: Tell children they are going to read about time and the Earth. Ask them to write five questions they want to know the answers to before looking at the text. Refer the class to Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.70–71.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children read in pairs. Encourage pairs to support each other’s learning, and explain words and concepts if their partner doesn’t understand. How many of their questions were answered in the text?

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Children read in pairs. Encourage pairs to support each other’s learning, and explain words and concepts if their partner doesn’t understand. How many of their questions were answered in the text?

Oxford source task: Children read the text again and answer the comprehension questions (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.72).

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the reading activity, pick out the most difficult and likely unknown words from the text, and teach the class the definitions before reading.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by putting them in groups with less able children. Ask them to help these children understand the topic and key words.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete pp.59–62 of Oxford Discover WB 3.

Oxford additional tasks: Hand out blank time zone maps. Children can colour and write the name of a country that falls within each time zone. Encourage children to use the internet to aid their research.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned new topic vocabulary and read a text about time zones.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Time zone clock match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Time zones.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — World clock research

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Time zones.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
