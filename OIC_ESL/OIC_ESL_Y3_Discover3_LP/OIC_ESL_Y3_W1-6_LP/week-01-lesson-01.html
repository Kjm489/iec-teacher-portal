Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: How do we make friends?. Original outcomes: 3.1g, 3.2a. Unit: Making friends.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Write one or two sentences asking for repetition on the board: ‘Sorry, could you repeat that, please?’ ‘Could you say that again, please?’ ‘Excuse me?’.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Welcome children to their first ESL lesson in Year 3. Explain that starting a new class can feel scary, but this is a friendly class. To begin the lesson, demonstrate ‘shaking out your worries’ by shaking your arms, legs, and body. Encourage children to join in.

Oxford opening: Ask if children feel more relaxed and energized to learn. Remind them that exercise and movement is good for us.

Oxford opening: Now tell children that today’s lesson is about making friends. Put children into teams of four and ask them to write a list of places where they can make new friends. After a couple of minutes, bring the class back together. Write their answers on the board. Award a point for each sensible answer – the team with the most points wins. Discuss the different activities that children can do at each place.

Oxford opening: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.16–17. Ask: Do any of your ideas match the pictures?

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Write one or two sentences asking for repetition on the board: ‘Sorry, could you repeat that, please?’ ‘Could you say that again, please?’ ‘Excuse me?’.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Encourage children to use one of these expressions in class if they don’t hear or understand something the first time.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children about your best friend, using a variety of adjectives. Children write down the adjectives they hear. Can they think of more words to describe their friends (e.g. kind, generous, helpful)? Write the words on the board, model saying them, and drill.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: In pairs, children take turns describing their friends in a sentence or two.

Oxford task — guided: Ask the class to discuss what friends do for each other. Ask: How do we make friends? What does your friend do to make you feel happy? What do you do to make your friend feel happy? Think of different situations. How can you be a good friend? Write suggestions on the board.

Oxford task — guided: Elicit sentences from children about their friends using ‘I like …’, ‘I love …’, and ‘I enjoy …’: e.g. I love that my friend Li walks home with me after school.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, children take turns describing their friends in a sentence or two.

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, children take turns talking about one of their friends – what their friend is like, what they appreciate about them, and what they do together. See if they can compare their experiences and identify how they are similar and different. Remind children to ask for repetition when necessary, using the sentences on the board.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask: Why do we say kind and positive things about our friends and the people we care about? (e.g. to make them happy, to show them that we appreciate their friendship, etc.).

Oxford source task: In pairs, children take turns talking about one of their friends – what their friend is like, what they appreciate about them, and what they do together. See if they can compare their experiences and identify how they are similar and different. Remind children to ask for repetition when necessary, using the sentences on the board.

Oxford source task: Invite one or two volunteers to tell the class about their best friend.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: You can change the difficulty level of the listening activity by speaking faster or slower.

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the speaking activity, write model sentences on the board: e.g. My best friend is Hannah. I like that she plays chess with me at lunchtime.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by asking them to write a paragraph about their best friend.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a mind map about where children can meet friends and what activities they can do at each place. Children can search for pictures online or draw their ideas.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have talked about making friends and described the positive qualities their friends have using adjectives. During speaking activities, they have practised asking for repetition.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Friendship qualities sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do we make friends?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Compliment exchange

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do we make friends?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Good friend scenario vote

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do we make friends?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Display two or three topic-related choices. Students vote individually, discuss evidence with a partner, and give a complete sentence explaining their choice.

ICQ: Do you vote before discussing? Must you give a reason?

CCQ: Which detail from the lesson supports your choice?

Assessment: A clear choice and a relevant explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Kindness role-play

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do we make friends?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
