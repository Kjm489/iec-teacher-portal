PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Explain to children that they are going to talk about moving house and how things change.

Oxford opening: Write five objects (toys and personal belongings) on the board. Tell children that they are moving house and there is only space to take three things with them. Children work in groups and decide which items they will take with them. Encourage children to give reasons why they are grateful for each item. Why are these objects of particular value? Ask one child in each group to choose five more items to repeat the task. Encourage children to think of funny items.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask the class if anyone has ever moved house. Where did they use to live and how was it different? If no child has moved house, tell them about a time you moved house. Explain how the environment changed and so did the people. Express how some things were hard to leave (friends), but some new things were positive (meeting new people).

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask: How would you feel if you had to move home and leave your friends behind?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Hand out the reading text ‘My moving diary’. Children look at the title and headings to predict what the text is about. As a class, read the text one paragraph at a time and discuss what happens in each. Write new vocabulary on the board and drill.

Oxford task — guided: Children read the text again independently or in pairs, then answer the comprehension questions.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children read the text again independently or in pairs, then answer the comprehension questions.

Oxford pair/group task: Now divide the class into pairs. Show children 1. a picture of a rural area with plenty of green spaces (e.g. Oxford Discover SB 3 p.38), and 2. a picture of a bustling city (e.g. Oxford Discover SB 3 p.39). Tell them to imagine that they have moved from one place to the other (Child A from 1 to 2; Child B from 2 to 1). They take turns telling their partner what they could do in 1 and what they can do now in 2, and vice versa.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Look at the third diary entry, at the sentence beginning with ‘Before I could …’. Introduce children to can, could, and but using the construction ‘Before I could …, but now I can …’. You can refer to p.33 and p.35 of Oxford Discover SB 3 to support teaching.

Oxford source task: Now divide the class into pairs. Show children 1. a picture of a rural area with plenty of green spaces (e.g. Oxford Discover SB 3 p.38), and 2. a picture of a bustling city (e.g. Oxford Discover SB 3 p.39). Tell them to imagine that they have moved from one place to the other (Child A from 1 to 2; Child B from 2 to 1). They take turns telling their partner what they could do in 1 and what they can do now in 2, and vice versa.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children with difficult topic vocabulary: e.g. abroad, countryside, homesick.

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the reading activity, focus on a few sentences or paragraphs instead of the entire text.

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the speaking activity, write model sentences on the board: e.g. Before I could go hiking with my friend, but now I can visit the cinema at weekends.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 2, Lesson 2

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.25–26 and 28.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a Venn diagram (two overlapping circles). Label the left and right sides ‘urban’ and ‘rural’ (or ‘city’ and ‘country’), and label the middle ‘both’. Sort different activities into the categories (what can be done where), then write a few sentences using could and can.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read and extracted information and topic vocabulary from a text. They have learned how to use could and can to discuss change and compare the past and present.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

YEAR 3 Making friends
Week 2, Lesson 2: Moving and losing friends
Learning outcomes: 3.3f, 3.3h

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Making friends’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by reading diary entries about moving to new places and completing simple comprehension exercises. They will discuss what they could do before and what they can do now.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
