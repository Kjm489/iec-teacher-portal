Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Where are you meeting your friends?. Original outcomes: 3.2b, 3.3f, 3.3g. Unit: Making friends.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Teach/Review the present continuous tense using a range of activities that children have learned (e.g. They are/aren’t ice skating.). Elicit what the people are/aren’t doing using the pictures on p.6 or pp.18–19 in Oxford Discover SB 3.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Write a sentence on the board that can be the beginning of an anecdote: e.g. Sara likes flying a kite at night. Do children notice the gerund in the sentence?

Oxford opening: Demonstrate with a small group of confident children how you can make a story by taking turns to say a single word.

Oxford opening: Divide the class into groups and see if children can make their own silly story. Encourage them to be creative – the funnier and more unusual their story, the better!

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Teach/Review the present continuous tense using a range of activities that children have learned (e.g. They are/aren’t ice skating.). Elicit what the people are/aren’t doing using the pictures on p.6 or pp.18–19 in Oxford Discover SB 3.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask: Are they skipping? Are they kicking a ball? Explain how you can answer these questions with ‘Yes, they are.’ / ‘No, they aren’t.’ Then ask children: What are you doing now? See if they can respond (e.g. I’m sitting on the carpet. / I’m not sleeping.). In pairs, children practise asking and answering questions using the present continuous.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: While children are working, write some directions on the board: ‘turn left/right’, ‘go up/down (the hill)’, ‘go over (the bridge)’, ‘go through (the park)’, ‘go along (the pavement)’, and ‘go past (the school)’. You can also add ‘walk’, ‘run’, ‘next to’, and ‘across’.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Bring the class back together. Use gestures to demonstrate the different directions and check children’s understanding. Use Oxford Discover SB 3 p.82 ex.C to support the teaching.

Oxford task — guided: Imagine that you are meeting a friend in a top-secret location. Draw a simple map on the board or on a piece of paper, or show children a real town map, with labels and start and end points. Reading the map, ask children to give you directions inside the classroom (or outside) using the imperative verbs on the board. After each instruction, model using the present continuous tense as you complete the action: e.g. I’m turning left. I’m going past the duck pond. I’m walking through the town centre. Elicit answers from children and correct them when necessary. Pretend to get tired if you are travelling a long way!

Oxford task — guided: Children work in pairs and take turns giving each other directions.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask: Are they skipping? Are they kicking a ball? Explain how you can answer these questions with ‘Yes, they are.’ / ‘No, they aren’t.’ Then ask children: What are you doing now? See if they can respond (e.g. I’m sitting on the carpet. / I’m not sleeping.). In pairs, children practise asking and answering questions using the present continuous.

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in pairs and take turns giving each other directions.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Bring the class back together. When children are settled, turn to the story in Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.18–19. Can they predict what they are going to read about just by reading the title and looking at the pictures?

Oxford source task: Read the text together. Then read again and give some children roles or sections to read aloud. Check if children’s predictions are correct.

Oxford source task: Ask the class if they can see: 1. an example of the present continuous tense, 2. an example of directions (focus more on the prepositions: e.g. ‘across’, ‘around’, ‘over’).

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more able children, provide a more complex map for them to read and give directions.

Oxford differentiation: For children who find reading a map difficult, support them by asking simple questions and miming different actions.

Oxford additional tasks: In small groups, design a map for an imaginary town on a large piece of paper.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete pp.12–13 of Oxford Discover WB 3.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned and practised using the present continuous tense, and have interpreted the visual features of a map to give directions. They have made sensible predictions before reading a text.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?
