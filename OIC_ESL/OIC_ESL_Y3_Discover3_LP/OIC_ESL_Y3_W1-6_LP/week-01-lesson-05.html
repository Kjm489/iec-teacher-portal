PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Show children photos of people enjoying different holiday activities to generate interest and excitement for the topic (making holiday plans and meeting friends). Ask: What are the people doing? Where do you think they are? Ask the class if anyone would like to try doing any of the activities (e.g. bungee jumping, visiting an animal sanctuary).

Oxford opening: Explain to the class that sometimes stepping outside ‘comfort zones’ can lead to fun and rewarding experiences. Can children think of a moment in the past when they stepped outside their comfort zone? How did they feel?

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Before introducing the lesson vocabulary, ask students if they can remember the meaning of ‘sequence’. You may wish to recap the Learn box in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.57. You can then tie this definition in with the lesson vocabulary by explaining that, in order to make the sequence of a story clearer, we can use sequencing words. Refer children to ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.29 and go through it as a class. Write the words ‘first’, ‘next’, ‘after that’, and ‘then’ on the board. Check children’s understanding and drill any unknown words. Choose several activities to write on the board, such as ‘go swimming’, ‘watch TV’, ‘eat dinner’, and ask children to make sentences using the sequencing words: e.g. First, I watch TV. Then, I go swimming. After that, we eat dinner.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Briefly speak about summer camps and explain to the class, if needed, what they are. Draw a weekend schedule for a summer camp on the board. Divide the grid up into Friday, Saturday, Sunday (far-left column) and Morning, Afternoon, Evening (top row), and include a range of activities (e.g. Take a horseriding class, Sing around the campfire). Talk about the different activities and discuss any unknown words.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask several comprehension questions: Can you go bowling on Friday afternoon? What do you do first on Sunday? After that?

Oxford task — guided: Demonstrate choosing some of the activities and making sentences in sequence.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: On Friday morning, I’m going to Red Rock Summer Camp. I’m very excited! First, I’m going skateboarding with my friend Maia because I love skateboarding. Then we’re climbing a mountain. After that, it’s lunch time!

Oxford pair/group task: Encourage a few children to read their paragraphs to the class.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Children write a short paragraph about their summer camp plans using sequencing words. See if they can use a mix of the present tense, present continuous tense (to express future plans), and gerunds. Remind children that thinking again about new language helps them to build their long-term memory of this learning. For example:

Oxford source task: On Friday morning, I’m going to Red Rock Summer Camp. I’m very excited! First, I’m going skateboarding with my friend Maia because I love skateboarding. Then we’re climbing a mountain. After that, it’s lunch time!

Oxford source task: Encourage a few children to read their paragraphs to the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the writing activity, show children a model paragraph they can base their own on.

Oxford differentiation: To challenge more able children, ask them to write a paragraph about their dream holiday or stepping outside of their comfort zone.

Oxford additional tasks: In pairs, compare summer camp plans, discussing similarities and differences.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have made holiday plans, and learned how to sequence activities and events in the correct order. They have used the present continuous tense in their written work.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 1, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — Holiday postcard

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Meeting friends on holiday.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Travel friend interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Meeting friends on holiday.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
