Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Comparing your daily activities. Original outcomes: 3.2b, 3.2e, 3.4c. Unit: Routines.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show children pictures of people doing different chores and daily activities. Elicit what the people are doing in the pictures. Ask children to categorise their chores and activities, using activity B on p.28 of Oxford Discover SB3. Ask them to compare their lists and add an extra activity per category.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to call out different chores and daily activities, and write them on the board. Give each of these an action. Make sure children know what the actions are.

Oxford opening: Play the game ‘Simon says’ using these actions. Instruct the whole class to stand up behind their chairs. Either say ‘Simon says’ followed by a chore or activity which children should act out, or just say the chore which the children shouldn’t act out. If children fail to do the correct action or do an action that they shouldn’t have, they sit down. The last child standing up wins. The winner of the game plays Simon next.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children pictures of people doing different chores and daily activities. Elicit what the people are doing in the pictures. Ask children to categorise their chores and activities, using activity B on p.28 of Oxford Discover SB3. Ask them to compare their lists and add an extra activity per category.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Review adverbs of frequency (never, sometimes, often, always) and questions with ‘Do you + infinitive’ and ‘How’: e.g. Do you walk the dog? How much TV do you watch? How often do you clean your bedroom?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Children work in small groups and discuss how often they do particular chores. Encourage children to think about how much they do compared to their parents and other children. Is it more or less? Why do they think people have different experiences?

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to write down their morning routine before school, including adverbs of frequency and who helps them with different activities: e.g. My mum always drives me to school. Help children to reflect on the help they receive, so they can learn to value it.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in small groups and discuss how often they do particular chores. Encourage children to think about how much they do compared to their parents and other children. Is it more or less? Why do they think people have different experiences?

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask the class several questions related to the order of activities: Who brushes their teeth before they have a shower? Who washes their face after they eat breakfast? Who sometimes wakes up after 10 a.m.?

Oxford source task: Now tell children to write five facts about their morning routine. After they have written their sentences, they walk around the classroom and ask ‘Do you + infinitive’ questions to see if other classmates have the same fact. Encourage them to record their results in a tally.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the group speaking activity, write some model sentences on the board: e.g. I never wash the car. I always do my homework before I play video games.

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the mingle activity, children can find people who answer ‘Yes’, instead of people with matching answers.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by encouraging them to ask more difficult questions during the mingle activity: e.g. Does your dad sometimes make you breakfast? Does your mum go to work before you go to school?

Oxford additional tasks: Write a paragraph about a true or imaginary daily routine, or a short interview with somebody about their routine using different question words and adverbs of frequency.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have talked about and compared the different activities and chores they do at home. They have practised using a variety of question words.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Routines Venn

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Comparing your daily activities.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
