PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Show the class several pictures of different types of house. If available, you can use the Oxford Reading Tree text The Right Home. Children work in small groups to rank the houses from their favourite to least favourite, explaining why. As part of their discussion, encourage them to think about what they could do in each house, where the houses might be, and how similar or different the houses are to where they live now.

Oxford opening: Ask someone from each group to report their results back to the class.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Pre-teach or revise different phrases to express (dis)agreement. You may like to look at some of the examples in the table in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.120 ex.A:

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Agree: Me too. / Me neither. / So do I. / That’s right. / I agree.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Disagree: I don’t (think the same). / I disagree. / I’m not so sure.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Write five random things on the board: e.g. ‘elephant’, ‘broccoli’, ‘badminton’, ‘cartoons’, ‘homework’. In pairs, children take turns making sentences about the things on the board. Their partner listens and expresses their (dis)agreement in response. For example:

Oxford task — guided: A: I love watching cartoons on Saturday morning. B: So do I!

Oxford task — guided: A: Broccoli is the most delicious vegetable. B: I disagree. My favourite vegetable is radish.

Oxford task — guided: A: I don’t enjoy playing badminton. B: Me neither. I prefer cricket!

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Write five random things on the board: e.g. ‘elephant’, ‘broccoli’, ‘badminton’, ‘cartoons’, ‘homework’. In pairs, children take turns making sentences about the things on the board. Their partner listens and expresses their (dis)agreement in response. For example:

Oxford pair/group task: Explain to children that they are going to look at different places around the world and decide on their favourite destination. Project the fact files on the board or provide handouts. You can create your own or use the ones provided. Divide the class into small groups and instruct each group to choose two or three fact files about different places. See if children can challenge themselves by choosing an unfamiliar place and use recently acquired topic vocabulary to talk about it.

Oxford pair/group task: In their groups, children read the fact files together. They could assign one fact file to each group member to read aloud. After a moment of reflection, they take turns saying which place they like the most and why, using the information offered to guide them. As each group member expresses their opinion, the others agree or disagree using one of the set phrases learned. Remind them to explain their decisions using because.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the speaking task, ask a more confident child to demonstrate the activity in front of the class before children practise in groups.

Oxford differentiation: Less able children can focus on just two fact files.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to compare the places in the fact files to their hometown: e.g. I would like to move to Ostuni because there are fewer cars than in Kuala Lumpur.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 8, Lesson 2

Oxford additional tasks: Allow children to research their favourite place in more depth.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a selection of fact files and chosen their favourite place, using because to explain the reasons behind their decision. They have expressed agreement and disagreement in response to their classmates’ preferences.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

IEC supplemental game — Favourite place photo ranking

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What place do you like most?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

IEC supplemental game — Preference reasons challenge

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What place do you like most?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
