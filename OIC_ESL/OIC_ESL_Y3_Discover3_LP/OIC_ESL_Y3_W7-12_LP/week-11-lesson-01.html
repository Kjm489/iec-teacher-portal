Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: A favourite game. Original outcomes: 3.1a, 3.1b, 3.2b. Unit: Having fun.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Ask children to think about their favourite game or activity and consider how it makes them feel. Highlight that doing something you enjoy is a natural way to relieve stress. Go to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.28. Alternatively, source a few pictures to inspire children.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Write on the board: ‘Racket’, ‘Ball’, and ‘Neither’. Children work in small groups to think of as many different games and activities that need a racket, a ball, or neither. Some games and activities can belong to more than one category.

Oxford opening: After a set time limit, ask groups to call out their lists. Award a point for each correct answer – the group with the most points wins.

Oxford opening: Choose five games and activities. Challenge groups to rank the activities from most liked to least liked. Children will need to collect each other’s preferences in an appropriate way and record the results. Ask a volunteer from each group to report their results back to the class. Discuss what the results reveal about the class.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children to think about their favourite game or activity and consider how it makes them feel. Highlight that doing something you enjoy is a natural way to relieve stress. Go to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.28. Alternatively, source a few pictures to inspire children.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: In pairs, children discuss why they like to do their favourite game or activity.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: In preparation for today’s lesson, record a few colleagues in your school talking about their favourite game or activity: e.g. A: What’s your favourite game or activity? B: I like tennis. A: Why? B: I like it because I can get together with a friend and do something active. It helps me feel better when I’m stressed and it’s fun to win!

Oxford task — guided: Pause the audio after each short conversation. On the first listen, children note down what they hear, tuning in to language that they recognize from the unit, as well as any words they don’t know.

Oxford task — guided: In a random order, write some of the games or activities mentioned on the board. Play the audio again. Ask children to number the words in the order they appear. Review as a class.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, children discuss why they like to do their favourite game or activity.

Oxford pair/group task: Ask: Did anyone have the same favourite game or activity as you? Why do they like it? Play the audio once more, then ask children to discuss their answers in pairs. If they didn’t say the same game or activity, they should talk about who they agree with most.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask: Did anyone have the same favourite game or activity as you? Why do they like it? Play the audio once more, then ask children to discuss their answers in pairs. If they didn’t say the same game or activity, they should talk about who they agree with most.

Oxford source task: Write sentences including phrasal verbs with get on the board: e.g. I really get along with my brother and sister. / My family get together for a meal every day. / I get off the bus at the plaza to go to the sports centre. (See Oxford Discover SB 3 p.23 ex.D.) Elicit the meanings of the verbs from the context, then practise saying a few sentences.

Oxford source task: Play the audio again and see if children can identify a phrasal verb with get.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the listening activity, pre-teach challenging vocabulary before playing the audio. In the ordering activity, write the activities and reasons on the board.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage a less confident but able child to demonstrate leadership in the introductory activity and suggest a suitable data-collecting method.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 p.16.

Oxford additional tasks: Plan a 30-second presentation about a favourite game or activity. Challenge children to give reasons using because and include at least one phrasal verb with get. In small groups or in front of the class, children take turns giving their oral presentation.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened to people talk about their favourite game or activity and discussed their own. They have been introduced to phrasal verbs with get.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Favourite game show-and-tell

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A favourite game.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Game rules ordering

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A favourite game.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Guess the game clues

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A favourite game.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Rules explanation partner test

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A favourite game.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Game equipment sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A favourite game.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

YEAR 3 Having fun
Week 11, Lesson 1: A favourite game
Learning outcomes: 3.1a, 3.1b, 3.2b

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Having fun’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by recognizing familiar words in a listening activity and reproducing them.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
