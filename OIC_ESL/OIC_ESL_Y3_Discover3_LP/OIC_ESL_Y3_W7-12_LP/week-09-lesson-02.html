Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Model and plan extended activity. Original outcomes: Unit summary. Unit: Moving house.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Source alignment note: Oxford supplies one shared weekly review plan. The five IEC lesson divisions are adaptations, not separate Oxford lesson titles.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Moving house’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford opening: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford opening: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: As positive emotions are contagious, discuss as a class how to share positive emotions.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas:

Oxford task — guided: In pairs, children imagine a friendship where one friend has moved house. Children work together to plan four letters, two from each friend. They write about and respond to where they moved to, why they moved, their feelings about moving, and their new neighbourhood. Encourage children to think about other things that friends say to each other: e.g. I miss you. I hope I can visit soon. Let’s play a video game next weekend. Let’s meet up next month. Children may look at the letters on pp.30–31 of Oxford Discover SB 3 to help them.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, children imagine a friendship where one friend has moved house. Children work together to plan four letters, two from each friend. They write about and respond to where they moved to, why they moved, their feelings about moving, and their new neighbourhood. Encourage children to think about other things that friends say to each other: e.g. I miss you. I hope I can visit soon. Let’s play a video game next weekend. Let’s meet up next month. Children may look at the letters on pp.30–31 of Oxford Discover SB 3 to help them.

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in small groups to design two posters about two different places – one urban area and one rural area. Encourage children to research different villages, towns, and cities around the world using the internet and/or reference books. Alternatively, allocate each group two places. They can mention what is the same and what is different from where they live now, as well as the main attractions of each place. At the end of the week, groups orally present their posters to the class. Children may also look at the reading text on pp.38–39 of Oxford Discover SB 3 to help them prepare their poster.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Children work in small groups to design two posters about two different places – one urban area and one rural area. Encourage children to research different villages, towns, and cities around the world using the internet and/or reference books. Alternatively, allocate each group two places. They can mention what is the same and what is different from where they live now, as well as the main attractions of each place. At the end of the week, groups orally present their posters to the class. Children may also look at the reading text on pp.38–39 of Oxford Discover SB 3 to help them prepare their poster.

Oxford source task: In pairs or small groups, children design a board game to practise the vocabulary they have learned in Weeks 8–9. They may use Oxford Discover SB 3 and WB 3 to help them put together their game.

Oxford source task: For all activities, ensure that children listen attentively to each other’s ideas and opinions to achieve a shared goal.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the projects, provide children with model letters, posters, or board game templates, or create a checklist of items that each project should have.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to think about how they will present their work effectively at the end of the week. What language can they use (including new grammatical forms learned)? What do they need to do to maintain the audience’s interest?

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 9, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — Moving-house project storyboard

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Model and plan extended activity.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — House model planning

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Model and plan extended activity.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.
