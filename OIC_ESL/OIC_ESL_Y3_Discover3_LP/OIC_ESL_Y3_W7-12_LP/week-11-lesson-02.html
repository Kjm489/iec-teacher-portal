Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Playing games. Original outcomes: 3.1e, 3.1h, 3.2e. Unit: Having fun.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show children the different board games. Ask: Do you know what these board games are? Have you played any of these before? Which is your favourite? Allow children to call out answers and share their enthusiasm for different games.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Teach the verbs in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.16 ex.A.

Oxford opening: Ask children how they are feeling today. Elicit a few answers. Then tell them to stand up and do each action you call out (e.g. kick, bounce). Make sure that they are well spaced out so they don’t hurt anyone. Change the pace of the activity and continue until children are noticeably out of breath. Ask: How do you feel now? Elicit that exercise is good for our body and our mind.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children the different board games. Ask: Do you know what these board games are? Have you played any of these before? Which is your favourite? Allow children to call out answers and share their enthusiasm for different games.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell the class that they are going to listen to someone talking about different board games. Refer children to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.22. Play tracks 1.12 and 1.13.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: On the first play, tell children to listen for gist.

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to copy the table in ex.A. On the second play, they listen for specific information and complete exs.A–B. Go through answers as a class.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: In small groups, children take turns telling each other about their favourite game. Remind them how to express (dis)agreement using different expressions: Me too. / That’s right. / So do I. / I don’t think the same (see Week 8). Encourage them to listen carefully and ask questions to elicit more information. They can also give opinions.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: If necessary, play the audio one more time. Ask: Who do you think likes their game the most? Why? Discuss as a class.

Oxford source task: In small groups, children take turns telling each other about their favourite game. Remind them how to express (dis)agreement using different expressions: Me too. / That’s right. / So do I. / I don’t think the same (see Week 8). Encourage them to listen carefully and ask questions to elicit more information. They can also give opinions.

Oxford source task: Invite one or two volunteers to tell the whole class about their game.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For the speaking activity, allow more able students to work together in a group so they can stretch each other by using a range of language and adding more detail to the rules of their games.

Oxford differentiation: Show a group of less able students a familiar board game. Set up the game together and elicit what they know about the game using question prompts: What do I do now? Do I move here? Whose turn is it? How do I win?

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.10–11.

Oxford additional tasks: In small groups, make up the rules of an invented game.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened to the rules of different games. They have participated in a small-group discussion about each other’s favourite games.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Play a classroom game

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Playing games.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

YEAR 3 Having fun
Week 11, Lesson 2: Playing games
Learning outcomes: 3.1e, 3.1h, 3.2e

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Having fun’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by understanding a set of game instructions and participating in a discussion about different games.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
