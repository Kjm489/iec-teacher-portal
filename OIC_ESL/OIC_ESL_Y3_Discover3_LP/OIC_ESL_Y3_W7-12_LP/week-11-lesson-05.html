PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Look at the play script in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.24. Alternatively, you could search online for a short play script such as ‘The Enormous Turnip’.

Oxford opening: Elicit the different parts of a play script: the title, the list of characters, the stage directions (the actions characters do), and the dialogue (what characters say).

Oxford opening: Explain to children that they are going to write a play script collaboratively about a fun event. The aim is to have fun and practise using new language, but working in a group to complete a creative writing task will also be challenging. Remind children that they are in the stretch zone.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Divide the class into small groups. Allocate a different story idea to each group, or allow children to create their own. Their aim is to explain a story plot clearly through dialogue. Give them enough time (20–40 minutes) to write their short play script. They need to:

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: decide how the fun event/conversation will start

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: work out what each character will say and in what order, considering different personalities

Oxford task — guided: describe the actions of each character

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Divide the class into small groups. Allocate a different story idea to each group, or allow children to create their own. Their aim is to explain a story plot clearly through dialogue. Give them enough time (20–40 minutes) to write their short play script. They need to:

Oxford pair/group task: Allow children to decide between themselves which character they are going to play. Encourage each child to take the lead on their character’s dialogue, share ideas, and support other group members.

Oxford pair/group task: Groups practise reading out their plays. Allow them to stand up and move around so they can get into character. If possible, let them go outside if others are still writing.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: decide how the fun event/conversation will end.

Oxford source task: Allow children to decide between themselves which character they are going to play. Encourage each child to take the lead on their character’s dialogue, share ideas, and support other group members.

Oxford source task: Groups practise reading out their plays. Allow them to stand up and move around so they can get into character. If possible, let them go outside if others are still writing.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Mix together ability levels so more able children can gently take the lead and elicit ideas from less able children.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to use a range of grammatical forms, including word/noun verb families, in their dialogue.

Oxford additional tasks: Perform the plays to the rest of the class.

Oxford additional tasks: Swap plays with other groups and give each other feedback. Focus on the organization of the play script, the language used, and the plot. On sticky notes, children can write one thing they really liked about the play script, and one thing that could be improved.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.24 and WB 3 p.18.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have looked at the organization of a play script and worked collaboratively to write their own play script about a fun event.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Fun event writing timeline

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing about a fun event.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
