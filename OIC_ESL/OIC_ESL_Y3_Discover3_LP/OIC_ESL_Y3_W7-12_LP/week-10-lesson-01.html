LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: Having fun

Lesson: 1
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.1d, 3.1e, 3.2c

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: How do people have fun?. Original outcomes: 3.1d, 3.1e, 3.2c. Unit: Having fun.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show children some pictures of fun events and festivals from around the world to spark their interest in the topic (e.g. La Tomatina festival in Spain, Songkran water festival in Thailand, Danza de los voladores in Mexico, a funfair, or carnivals and events for children in your region).

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Introduce children to the new unit: ‘Having fun’. They are going to learn about how people have fun around the world, relating activities and events to their own experiences.

Oxford opening: Elicit from children what they like to do to have fun, drawing upon their learning from Unit 1: ‘Making friends’. Make a list together as a class.

Oxford opening: In pairs, children take turns acting out a fun activity for their partner to guess. Let children have fun with this – they can create exaggerated movements and sound effects.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children some pictures of fun events and festivals from around the world to spark their interest in the topic (e.g. La Tomatina festival in Spain, Songkran water festival in Thailand, Danza de los voladores in Mexico, a funfair, or carnivals and events for children in your region).

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Refer children to the photos in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.8. Play track 1.02 and tell children to listen to the pronunciation of the words.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask questions to confirm children’s understanding of the vocabulary: Have you ever been to a family reunion? Did you have fun? What did you do there? What is your favourite festival? Are you in a sports team? What is your favourite ride at the fair?

Oxford task — guided: Write a few questions on the board and instruct children to ask and answer them in pairs. Walk around the classroom and monitor children actively. Model useful language: e.g. Yes, I did. / No I didn’t. / My favourite festival is … because [it’s when my entire family gets together to celebrate.]

Oxford task — guided: Show children a short video clip of a fun event, either from your country or globally.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask questions to confirm children’s understanding of the vocabulary: Have you ever been to a family reunion? Did you have fun? What did you do there? What is your favourite festival? Are you in a sports team? What is your favourite ride at the fair?

Oxford pair/group task: Write a few questions on the board and instruct children to ask and answer them in pairs. Walk around the classroom and monitor children actively. Model useful language: e.g. Yes, I did. / No I didn’t. / My favourite festival is … because [it’s when my entire family gets together to celebrate.]

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: First, give children a few visual clues and key words. Can they predict the content of the video with this information?

Oxford source task: Play the video. On the first play, allow children to watch for pleasure. On the second play, ask them to take notes on the type of event, when it takes place, what people are doing, and what the atmosphere is like.

Oxford source task: Ask: Would you like to go to [name of event]? Why? Elicit answers and discuss as a whole class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to think of as many verbs as they can related to having fun: e.g. ‘play’, ‘have fun’, ‘celebrate’, ‘enjoy’, ‘dance’, ‘sing’, ‘take part’, ‘get together’, ‘look forward to’, ‘laugh’. How many sentences can they create with these words?

Oxford differentiation: Allow less able children to focus only on festivals, events, and games that they are familiar with. Use picture and question prompts to elicit their thoughts.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.2–3.

Oxford additional tasks: Create fun vocabulary games so children can test each other on the new words learned in today’s lesson. Tell them that they are having fun by making games about having fun!

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have been introduced to a new topic about having fun. They have learned new topic vocabulary and discussed how people around the world like to have fun.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Indoor-outdoor activity sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do people have fun?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
