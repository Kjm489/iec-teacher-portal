PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask the class: What can you remember about last lesson? What did we read? What did the letter talk about? Elicit answers and discuss as a whole class.

Oxford opening: Choose five children. In turn, ask them to name one part of a letter (date, salutation, body text, closing, and signature). If they get stuck, encourage other children to call out the correct answer. Remind children that by thinking again about letter writing, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning. Being able to write a good letter is an important skill to have!

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain to children that they are going to write a letter to a friend or family member about moving to a different country, based on an imaginary experience. They shouldn’t choose a place that they know really well, but try to think creatively about an unfamiliar place or a place they’ve always wanted to visit, considering how it might be different to their present lives.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Draw a simple graphic organizer on the board, with the headings ‘Reason for moving house’, ‘Description of place’, ‘Things you can do there’, ‘How it is similar’, ‘How it is different’, ‘How I feel’, ‘Future plans’. Ask children to copy it down. Explain that this will help to organize their thoughts and ideas into a logical order. You may also like to remind children of the chart in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.37 which gives them further ideas for planning their letter and to ensure that they don’t miss any section out.

Oxford task — guided: Children should spend most of the lesson completing their plans.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, children read each other’s plans. Tell them to mention one or two good things about their partner’s plan, and one or two things that they could improve on (e.g. use of tenses, spelling, organization, describing words, any other information that they would like to know the answer to).

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: In pairs, children read each other’s plans. Tell them to mention one or two good things about their partner’s plan, and one or two things that they could improve on (e.g. use of tenses, spelling, organization, describing words, any other information that they would like to know the answer to).

Oxford source task: Invite a few volunteers to share their plans with the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For less able children, start them off by assigning them a place and a reason for moving. Reduce the number of headings in the graphic organizer and prompt them with questions to get them thinking: What do you think [place name] looks like? What is the weather like in that part of the world? Is it in a city or in the country? What can you do there? How would you feel about moving house?

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to connect their ideas using sequencing words like because. Encourage them to think of the positive and negative aspects of moving to a new place.

Oxford additional tasks: Allow children to revise their plans accordingly, in line with their partner’s feedback.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have thought creatively about moving to a different country. They have planned a letter about their experience, using a graphic organizer to organize their ideas, and worked with a partner to develop each other’s ideas.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — New home floor-plan mystery

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: My new home (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
