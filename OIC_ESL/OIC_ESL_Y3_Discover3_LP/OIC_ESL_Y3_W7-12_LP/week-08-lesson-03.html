Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Writing about moving to a new house. Original outcomes: 3.3g, 3.3h. Unit: Moving house.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Explain to children that they are going to review letter writing in today’s lesson. They last looked at letter writing in Week 2. Ask: Can you remember the different parts of a letter? Elicit answers.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play a vocabulary game: children have to say a word that is similar or related to the previous word on the topic of ‘Moving house’. Demonstrate with a confident child. Say: ‘house’. The child then says a similar or related word: e.g. ‘home’, ‘move’, ‘bedroom’, ‘apartment’. Continue until someone cannot think of a word or a word is repeated. Due to its quick-fire nature, this activity is designed to stretch children to extend their understanding of the topic.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain to children that they are going to review letter writing in today’s lesson. They last looked at letter writing in Week 2. Ask: Can you remember the different parts of a letter? Elicit answers.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: If necessary, remind children of the date, salutation, body text, closing, and signature. Invite volunteers to write examples of each on the board. You can turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.44 – a letter that children should already be familiar with. Additionally, you may also look at the letter in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.37 ex.A.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Children are going to read a letter about someone moving house. Ask them to write down three questions they think the letter will answer, then read the letter as a whole class. Discuss which of the children’s questions were answered. You can use the below letter text, or create your own.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: November 10th

Oxford task — guided: Hi Nadya,

Oxford task — guided: How are you? Sorry about my late reply. I’m very busy! Anyway, I have some news to tell you. We’re moving house tomorrow because my mum got a new job in Shanghai. She’s going to be a surgeon in a new hospital there. I feel excited and nervous! My new address is 15 Park Street. Here is a picture of my new house. It’s beautiful and there’s a café around the corner. I’m starting school in a couple of weeks, so I hope to tell you more next month.

Oxford task — guided: Speak soon,

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children discuss their answers in pairs, then feed back to the whole class.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Mei

Oxford source task: Ask: Who is the letter to? What do you think their relationship is? Why? Discuss as a class.

Oxford source task: Then ask the class some comprehension questions focused on specific information: e.g. Why is Mei moving to Shanghai? How is she feeling? Can you find two examples of the present continuous tense to talk about future plans?

Oxford source task: Children discuss their answers in pairs, then feed back to the whole class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Vary the difficulty of the introductory activity by using very simple starter words, or by narrowing or broadening the range of vocabulary that children are allowed to use.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, look at the letter together in small groups, guided by an adult.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to summarize the letter in their own words. Can they think of synonyms for ‘excited’, ‘nervous’, and ‘beautiful’?

Oxford additional tasks: Give children another model letter, cut up into sections. Ask them to read the text and rearrange the sections into a logical order.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have revised the structure and organization of a letter, predicted the content of a letter, and answered comprehension questions based on their understanding of the text.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Moving house’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by revising the structure and organization of a letter and answering comprehension questions.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
