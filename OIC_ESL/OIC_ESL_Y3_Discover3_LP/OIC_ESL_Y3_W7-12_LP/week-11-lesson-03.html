LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: Having fun
Week: 11
Lesson: 3
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2b, 3.2c

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Word families. Original outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2b, 3.2c. Unit: Having fun.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Explain that some words can be both nouns and verbs: e.g. ‘ride’, ‘laugh’, ‘answer’, ‘paint’ (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.15). Elicit a few more examples from children.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Children write down two things that they like doing in their free time (their hobbies). Tell them to walk around the classroom and ask their classmates about their hobbies. If they find someone with a hobby in common, they can cross it off. Children sit down after matching both of their hobbies. Continue until everyone has sat down or until there isn’t anyone left with a matching hobby.

Oxford opening: Encourage a child with an unmatched or unusual hobby to tell the class about it and why they enjoy doing it.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that some words can be both nouns and verbs: e.g. ‘ride’, ‘laugh’, ‘answer’, ‘paint’ (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.15). Elicit a few more examples from children.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Children work in pairs. One child says a sentence using one of the words as a noun: e.g. She has a really nice laugh. Their partner responds with a sentence using the same word as a verb: e.g. I like to laugh with friends. Swap roles and repeat with new words. Look out for children who are helping each other to distinguish between nouns and verbs, and making helpful suggestions. Praise them for their teamwork.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children work in pairs. One child says a sentence using one of the words as a noun: e.g. She has a really nice laugh. Their partner responds with a sentence using the same word as a verb: e.g. I like to laugh with friends. Swap roles and repeat with new words. Look out for children who are helping each other to distinguish between nouns and verbs, and making helpful suggestions. Praise them for their teamwork.

Oxford pair/group task: Then write on the board: ‘win’, ‘score’, ‘coach’, ‘colour’, ‘fish’, ‘pass’, ‘ride’, ‘hop’, ‘dance’, ‘show’, ‘cycle’, or other words of your choice that can be used as nouns and verbs to talk about having fun. Challenge pairs to take turns saying coupled sentences using these words: e.g. I dance in class every Saturday morning. On Sunday, I’m going dancing with my grandparents.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Remind the class that gerunds are also nouns (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.21). Elicit from children what a gerund is and how it is formed. Give a few example sentences: e.g. Fishing with my Dad is my favourite hobby. / I’m good at cycling.

Oxford source task: Then write on the board: ‘win’, ‘score’, ‘coach’, ‘colour’, ‘fish’, ‘pass’, ‘ride’, ‘hop’, ‘dance’, ‘show’, ‘cycle’, or other words of your choice that can be used as nouns and verbs to talk about having fun. Challenge pairs to take turns saying coupled sentences using these words: e.g. I dance in class every Saturday morning. On Sunday, I’m going dancing with my grandparents.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Support less able children by teaching them a limited selection of word families. Ensure that they understand the difference between a noun and a verb first, providing extra guidance if necessary.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to make sentences using all the words on the board. As an extension, they can research more words that work as nouns and verbs.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.14–15.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a paragraph about a favourite hobby, including at least one example of a word family. Explain what it is, when you do it, who you do it with, and why you like doing it.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have practised using word families in sentences about having fun.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 11, LESSON 3

IEC supplemental game — Word family sorting race

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Word families.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Root word detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Word families.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Build-a-word dominoes

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Word families.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.
