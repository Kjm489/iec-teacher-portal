Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Fun events. Original outcomes: 3.3f, 3.3g. Unit: Having fun.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Tell children that they are going to read about some fun events from around the world.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Divide the class into two or three teams. On the board, slowly draw a simple picture to illustrate one of the topic words learned in the previous lesson (e.g. a trophy to illustrate ‘winner’). The first team to guess the correct word nominates a team member to go to the board and draw a different picture. Repeat until most or all of the topic vocabulary from Week 10, Lesson 1 is covered. Explain to children that they have to practise things over and over in order to master them – this also applies to learning new vocabulary!

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children that they are going to read about some fun events from around the world.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Turn to ‘Having Fun’ in Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.10–11.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to look at the title of the text and the picture(s). In pairs, ask them to make predictions about what they are going to read about. Ask some children to share their predictions with the class.

Oxford task — guided: Complete the following activity:

Oxford task — guided: Divide the class into groups of four, then assign each group a number: 1 = Festivals, 2 = Sports, 3 = Family celebrations, 4 = Community events.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children to look at the title of the text and the picture(s). In pairs, ask them to make predictions about what they are going to read about. Ask some children to share their predictions with the class.

Oxford pair/group task: Divide the class into groups of four, then assign each group a number: 1 = Festivals, 2 = Sports, 3 = Family celebrations, 4 = Community events.

Oxford pair/group task: In their groups, children read the text that corresponds with their number. Tell them to divide the text into equal parts so they can take turns reading aloud. Give them enough time to talk about the main information in the text and the meanings of difficult words.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: In their groups, children read the text that corresponds with their number. Tell them to divide the text into equal parts so they can take turns reading aloud. Give them enough time to talk about the main information in the text and the meanings of difficult words.

Oxford source task: Create new groups made up of one child each from groups 1, 2, 3, 4. Encourage children to take turns telling each other what they learned about.

Oxford source task: Ask children if their initial predictions were correct. Then elicit their thoughts about the text. Ask: Does [celebration name] look fun? What is the most interesting thing you’ve learned? Can you think of a similar event in your country?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Mix together more able and less able children in the reading activity so peer support can be provided.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to close their books and summarize what they have read about in their own words.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises on p.12 of Oxford Discover SB 3. You can also look at the reading text and comprehension questions in WB 3 pp.4–5 for additional reading practice.

Oxford additional tasks: Create an illustrated poster about a favourite festival.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have predicted and identified the main information in a reading text.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 10, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — Fun event invitation match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Fun events.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Event timeline ordering

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Fun events.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Event poster detectives

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Fun events.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Plan an event role-play

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Fun events.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Fun-event ticket design

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Fun events.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Learners create a small labelled drawing, map or idea web representing one accurate example from the lesson, then explain it to a partner using a sentence frame.

ICQ: Do you draw and label before presenting? Will your partner ask a question?

CCQ: How does your drawing show the concept from today's lesson?

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Event announcement challenge

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Fun events.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
