Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Moving from here to there (2). Original outcomes: 3.3f, 3.3g, 3.4c. Unit: Moving house.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Tell children they are going to continue to look at moving to different places. Look at the text ‘Moving from Here to There!’ in Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.38–39. Before you read the text, allow children to look at the pictures and predict what it’s about.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask the class: What would you do if your parents told you that you are moving house tomorrow? How would you feel? Children respond to the questions in pairs. Ask some pairs to share their ideas with the class. Elicit feelings like ‘worried’, ‘excited’, ‘unsure’, ‘lonely’, and ‘happy’ and explain that all these feelings are normal when experiencing change.

Oxford opening: Ask: What would you miss the most about where you live now? What things could you take with you? Encourage children to reflect on the positive things that they can take with them that aren’t material (e.g. family, security, good health, communication with friends).

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children they are going to continue to look at moving to different places. Look at the text ‘Moving from Here to There!’ in Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.38–39. Before you read the text, allow children to look at the pictures and predict what it’s about.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Invite a few confident children to take turns reading aloud a paragraph, or a few sentences, from the text.

Oxford task — guided: Draw a two-column grid on the board for children to copy: Column 1 heading: ‘Main idea’; Column 2 heading: ‘Details’. In pairs, children work together to complete the table. Check answers as a whole class.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Draw a two-column grid on the board for children to copy: Column 1 heading: ‘Main idea’; Column 2 heading: ‘Details’. In pairs, children work together to complete the table. Check answers as a whole class.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask: Were any of your ideas from the introductory activity mentioned in the text? Did you learn any new reasons why people move home?

Oxford source task: Based on their learning over the course of today’s lesson and the previous lesson, ask children to write a short paragraph about one reason for moving, using their notes and their own ideas. Remind them to use because to explain their reasons and to consider the positive aspects of moving, in spite of the difficulty of leaving their friends behind.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, pre-teach any difficult words children may not know before reading the text. You might want to look at the text together in a small group.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to include original ideas in their paragraph about moving to a new place, instead of using an idea from the text.

Oxford additional tasks: Pair up children and ask them to read and peer-edit each other’s paragraphs, focusing on:

Oxford additional tasks: Main idea: Is the main idea clear?

Oxford additional tasks: Supporting details: Do they explain their main idea with supporting details?

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the main idea and supporting detail in a text. They have written a paragraph about why people move to a new place, using recently learned topic vocabulary and grammatical forms.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 7, LESSON 3

IEC supplemental game — First-then-next moving relay

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Moving from here to there (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Moving sequence story cards

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Moving from here to there (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Past and present sentence repair

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Moving from here to there (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — House-move diary dialogue

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Moving from here to there (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
