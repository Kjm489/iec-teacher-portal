Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Model and plan extended activity. Original outcomes: Unit summary. Unit: Having fun.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Source alignment note: Oxford supplies one shared weekly review plan. The five IEC lesson divisions are adaptations, not separate Oxford lesson titles.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Having fun’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford opening: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford opening: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas:

Oxford task — guided: In small groups, children produce a play script. It should be a brand-new idea – different from the play script they created in Week 11, Lesson 5. Remind them to think about the organization of a play script, as well as the feelings, actions, and behaviour of the characters. They can create their own props to make their performance more visual and entertaining. At the end of the week, children perform their plays to the rest of the class. Children may look back at the play scripts in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.24 and Oxford Discover WB 3 p.18 to use as references.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford pair/group task: In small groups, children produce a play script. It should be a brand-new idea – different from the play script they created in Week 11, Lesson 5. Remind them to think about the organization of a play script, as well as the feelings, actions, and behaviour of the characters. They can create their own props to make their performance more visual and entertaining. At the end of the week, children perform their plays to the rest of the class. Children may look back at the play scripts in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.24 and Oxford Discover WB 3 p.18 to use as references.

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs or small groups, children produce a podcast in which they talk about favourite games and activities around the world. Remind them that a podcast can be an informal conversation – it is an opportunity to give an opinion and/or express (dis)agreement with somebody else’s opinion. They can talk about a traditional game from their own country and why it is popular, and an unusual or exciting game from a different part of the world. Allow them to write a script to guide them through the conversation. At the end of the week, they can record themselves presenting the podcast and listen back to what they have produced.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: In pairs or small groups, children produce a podcast in which they talk about favourite games and activities around the world. Remind them that a podcast can be an informal conversation – it is an opportunity to give an opinion and/or express (dis)agreement with somebody else’s opinion. They can talk about a traditional game from their own country and why it is popular, and an unusual or exciting game from a different part of the world. Allow them to write a script to guide them through the conversation. At the end of the week, they can record themselves presenting the podcast and listen back to what they have produced.

Oxford source task: In pairs or small groups, children design a board game to practise the vocabulary they have learned in Weeks 10–11. Alternatively, they can bring in their favourite game and teach their classmates how to play it using new vocabulary learned.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children to support less confident children during the week’s work, ensuring that they feel involved and understand the task.

Oxford differentiation: Less confident children are likely to excel in different ways. Identify their strengths – they might enjoy creative tasks or be proficient in using computers or other digital devices – and allow them to build on these strengths.

Oxford differentiation: For the play script and podcast activities, challenge more able children to introduce new vocabulary and grammatical forms that they have learned in the unit. Remind them to explain their ideas using because.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Fun project selection pitch

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Model and plan extended activity.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.
