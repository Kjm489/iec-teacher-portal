LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: Having fun
Week: 11
Lesson: 4
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.2c, 3.2e

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Giving encouragement. Original outcomes: 3.2c, 3.2e. Unit: Having fun.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Teach a few expressions for giving encouragement orally (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.22 ex.C): e.g. Good job. / You can do it. I know you can! / Don’t give up! / Keep trying. / All you can do is your best. / You’re nearly there! / Well done.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Explain to children that they are going to play some fun revision games before doing a writing task in the next lesson. This will help to build their long-term memory of their learning in the unit ‘Having fun’.

Oxford opening: Ask: Does anyone know what it means to ‘encourage’ somebody? Elicit answers and/or demonstrate giving encouragement to another adult in the class or a volunteer child. Ask them how it made them feel (hopefully ‘pleased’, ‘motivated’, ‘positive’, ‘determined’). Help children to understand that encouraging others, in a game and in real-life, helps them to feel more positive and provides a good reason for them to persevere (keep on trying when something is hard).

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Teach a few expressions for giving encouragement orally (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.22 ex.C): e.g. Good job. / You can do it. I know you can! / Don’t give up! / Keep trying. / All you can do is your best. / You’re nearly there! / Well done.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Drill as a class. Try to be as animated and positive as possible.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Give children the task of giving encouragement to each other during today’s activities. Reinforce that recognizing a challenge and understanding that a classmate needs support to overcome it shows empathy.

Oxford task — guided: For each activity, encourage children to decide on a new team leader to set up the game, ensure that everybody is included, and give instructions. Suggested activities:

Oxford task — guided: Play a matching game. Divide the class into small groups and give each group a set of pictures and labels from the topic ‘Having fun’. Children evenly divide the pictures between themselves, then take turns matching them to the correct labels.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: For each activity, encourage children to decide on a new team leader to set up the game, ensure that everybody is included, and give instructions. Suggested activities:

Oxford pair/group task: Play a matching game. Divide the class into small groups and give each group a set of pictures and labels from the topic ‘Having fun’. Children evenly divide the pictures between themselves, then take turns matching them to the correct labels.

Oxford pair/group task: In the same groups, mix all the pictures and labels up and turn them face down. Play a memory game. Children take turns picking up one picture and one label to try to find a matching pair.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: In the same groups, mix all the pictures and labels up and turn them face down. Play a memory game. Children take turns picking up one picture and one label to try to find a matching pair.

Oxford source task: Play a long sentence game. Demonstrate the activity with a confident child by saying something you like doing: e.g. I like playing checkers. The child repeats your sentence, then adds one more thing they like doing.

Oxford source task: Play a three-sentence game. Write a word on the board from the unit (e.g. ‘wedding’), then challenge a confident child to say three short sentences about weddings. After, it is their turn to write a new topic word for another child. Ensure that all children have the opportunity to choose a word and say the sentences.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Mix up abilities in small groups so peer support can be provided. Encourage less confident children to practise their leadership skills – communicating to a small group of classmates instead of to the whole class will help to build up their confidence.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to try and recite the longest sentence they made.

Oxford differentiation: Allow less able children to suggest one sentence only in the three-sentence game. If they are struggling, give them a few minutes to look back at their notes.

Oxford additional tasks: Allow children to create their own revision games based on new topic vocabulary and grammatical forms learned.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have practised giving encouragement to each other in a variety of games.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 11, LESSON 4

IEC supplemental game — Encouragement phrase matching

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Giving encouragement.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Coach-and-player role-play

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Giving encouragement.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Positive feedback rewrite

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Giving encouragement.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
