PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play a guessing game. Stick the flashcards on the board and write a number under each. Give children ten seconds to look at the flashcards before turning them face down. Call out a number and children try to recall what the card is. Repeat with other numbers. Remind children that by thinking again about topic vocabulary, they are building their long-term memory of this learning, even if they don’t realize it!

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Remind children of their learning from Week 2, Lesson 2 when they discussed moving to new places and making new friends. If you have time, you might like to revisit Sati’s first letter about moving house in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.30 as an initial point of discussion. This week, they are going to build on their learning by looking at why people move.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask: Do you have family members who live in other places? Why do they live there? Children work in pairs to create a mind map. Elicit some answers and discuss as a class.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain what a ‘main idea’ is in a reading text. It answers the question: What does the author want me to know? ‘Supporting details’ help us to understand the main idea.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Do you think there are more jobs in towns/cities or in the countryside?

Oxford source task: What jobs can you do in the countryside that you can’t do in a city?

Oxford source task: Bring the class back together. Hold up a picture of a sunny beach town. Discuss why people may like to live there (e.g. relaxed atmosphere, warm weather, less crowded, less pollution from cars). Then hold up a picture of a busy city. Discuss why people may like to live there (e.g. job opportunities, lots of shops and restaurants, public transport, universities).

Oxford source task: Ask: Where would you prefer to live? Why? What activities and jobs can you do there? Engage in a whole-class discussion.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Vary the difficulty of the introductory activity by using more or fewer flashcards.

Oxford differentiation: Write ‘job’, ‘climate’, ‘school’, ‘activities’, and ‘cost’ on the board to prompt children’s thinking about why different people move to different places.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the comprehension questions in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.32.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a paragraph about where friends and family members live and why they live there. Encourage children to research the different places to learn more about the infrastructure and climate.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a text about moving home, identifying the main information and supporting details. They have considered why people move to new places.

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 7, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — Moving day suitcase challenge

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Moving from here to there (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Before-and-after map

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Moving from here to there (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Learners create a small labelled drawing, map or idea web representing one accurate example from the lesson, then explain it to a partner using a sentence frame.

ICQ: Do you draw and label before presenting? Will your partner ask a question?

CCQ: How does your drawing show the concept from today's lesson?

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Moving reasons interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Moving from here to there (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Transport route puzzle

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Moving from here to there (1).

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Moving house’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by identifying the main information and supporting details in a reading text.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
