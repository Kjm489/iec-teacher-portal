Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Why did you move? (2). Original outcomes: 3.3c, 3.4c. Unit: Moving house.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Remind children how to make full sentences with because using the ‘Learn’ box in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.43 ex.E. Read the text beginning with ‘My aunt moved to France …’. Invite children to take turns reading the sentences aloud. Then complete the activity.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Tell children something you did yesterday: e.g. I went to the park. Ask a confident child to explain why you did that thing using because: e.g. I went to the park because I wanted to go for a run. Ask another child to repeat the process beginning with the previous cause: e.g. I wanted to go for a run because exercise makes me feel calm. This activity is likely to be challenging, as children will need to think reasonably quickly to complete the sentence. Remind them that they are in the stretch zone.

Oxford opening: Children play the game in small groups. Encourage them to think of funny and creative reasons to make the activity more enjoyable.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Remind children how to make full sentences with because using the ‘Learn’ box in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.43 ex.E. Read the text beginning with ‘My aunt moved to France …’. Invite children to take turns reading the sentences aloud. Then complete the activity.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask: Which sentences are complete? Which sentences are incomplete? Help children to understand that it is OK to respond with an incomplete answer orally, but you cannot start a new sentence with because in writing.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Now write several coupled sentences on the board that can be combined using because. Some should be in the right order and some in the wrong order. Ask children to decide on the correct order and write them out as full sentences. Go through the answers as a class.

Oxford task — guided: She wants to move to India. / The food is delicious there.

Oxford task — guided: I’m saving my money to move abroad. / I don’t often go to the shopping centre.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Assign pairs a country/city that is different to their own country/city and provide information about each place (several different resources should be available to children). Ask them to write five complete sentences about moving there using because. Monitor actively and discreetly note down mistakes that children make as you go around the class.

Oxford pair/group task: Invite each pair to share one of their sentences.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Assign pairs a country/city that is different to their own country/city and provide information about each place (several different resources should be available to children). Ask them to write five complete sentences about moving there using because. Monitor actively and discreetly note down mistakes that children make as you go around the class.

Oxford source task: Invite each pair to share one of their sentences.

Oxford source task: Write a few common mistakes on the board and ask children to identify the issue. Correct them as a class. Explain that it is normal to make mistakes when learning a new language and the best thing that we can do is to share and learn from them.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For the introductory activity, less able children can respond using the present tense while more able children can use a range of tenses that they know.

Oxford differentiation: Pair up more able and less able children for the writing activity so peer-support can be provided.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 p.36.

Oxford additional tasks: Combine the five sentences from the final activity into a single paragraph. For further extension, children can elaborate on their ideas using If, When, and extra detail.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned to write complete sentences using because.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 8, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Moving reason follow-up interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Why did you move? (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Reason versus result sorting

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Why did you move? (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Because/so sentence builders

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Why did you move? (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — New-home case study

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Why did you move? (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
