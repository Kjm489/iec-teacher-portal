Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Town or country. Original outcomes: 3.2e, 3.3b. Unit: Moving house.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Tell children to look at the pictures and words in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.36.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Tell children that today they are going to talk about living in different places: in a town and in the country. You can also use the terminology ‘urban area’ and ‘rural area’.

Oxford opening: Show the class a photo of a busy city and one of a quiet countryside scene. In small groups, ask children to try to think of as many differences between the two places as they can. Go through the answers as a class, writing answers and suggestions on the board.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children to look at the pictures and words in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.36.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Drill the key vocabulary as a class and correct children’s pronunciation as necessary.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Tell children to close their books. Write ‘Where you live’ (country, suburb, rural/urban area) and ‘Where you work or visit’ (shopping mall, university, hospital, factory, clinic, zoo) on the board. Children work in pairs to categorize the vocabulary they have learned into the correct columns. Ask pairs to call out different words for you to write in each column. Encourage them to spell out the words, allowing them to check their books after a few attempts.

Oxford task — guided: Praise children for their effort in recalling the vocabulary and making logical attempts at spelling the words. Ask: How did you work out the different spellings? What other words do you know that are spelled similarly? Elicit their answers and discuss several different spelling and pronunciation patterns: e.g. the digraphs ‘pp’ in ‘shopping’ (hopping, happy, apple), ‘ee’ in ‘street’ (meet, tree, green), and the soft ‘th’ in ‘path’ (bath, Earth, Maths).

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Tell children to close their books. Write ‘Where you live’ (country, suburb, rural/urban area) and ‘Where you work or visit’ (shopping mall, university, hospital, factory, clinic, zoo) on the board. Children work in pairs to categorize the vocabulary they have learned into the correct columns. Ask pairs to call out different words for you to write in each column. Encourage them to spell out the words, allowing them to check their books after a few attempts.

Oxford pair/group task: In the same pairs, ask children to make sentences to describe where they live using some of the new words: e.g. There’s a hospital. There are lots of fields behind my house.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: In the same pairs, ask children to make sentences to describe where they live using some of the new words: e.g. There’s a hospital. There are lots of fields behind my house.

Oxford source task: Remind children that the question word Why? asks for what reason something happens and the word because explains why (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.42). Write examples on the board: e.g. Why do you like living in a town? I like living here because there are lots of shopping centres.

Oxford source task: Children practise saying what they like about where they live using Why? and because.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For less able children, sound out the more difficult words like ‘university’ one syllable at a time.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to use all the new words they have learned in today’s lesson in their sentences, using Why? and because.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.29–30.

Oxford additional tasks: Children can test each other on the new words by creating their own word search puzzles.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned new vocabulary to describe places in a town and in the country and talked about where they live using Why? and because.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 7, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Town-or-country photo detectives

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Town or country.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Rural/urban feature sorting

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Town or country.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — My ideal place interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Town or country.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Moving house’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and reading skills by taking part in short conversations about familiar topics and recognizing spelling patterns of new and previously learned words.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
