LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: Heroes
Week: 35
Lesson: 1
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.1c, 3.1g, 3.2c, 3.2d

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Heroes in films. Original outcomes: 3.1c, 3.1g, 3.2c, 3.2d. Unit: Heroes.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Elicit some heroes from films – not necessarily superheroes, but protagonists with hero-like qualities such as bravery, loyalty, and kindness.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play a game in an open space with children. Tell them to recall their favourite superhero from the previous week (Week 34). Invite them to stand in a horizontal line facing you.

Oxford opening: Say an activity from Week 34, Lesson 2. If children think their superhero is good at doing this activity, they can take two steps towards you; if they think their superhero needs to improve or hasn’t tried this activity, they must take one step back.

Oxford opening: The winner is the first child to reach you. Ask for the name of their superhero.

Oxford opening: Remind children that they don’t have to be good at everything, but everyone is good at something. Ensure you include an activity for everyone’s superhero.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit some heroes from films – not necessarily superheroes, but protagonists with hero-like qualities such as bravery, loyalty, and kindness.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: On the board, sketch a bar chart. Ask children what it is and encourage them to describe it to you. Remind them that they looked at this last week (in Week 34, Lesson 5). You can show children the example bar chart in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.38. Point to the y-axis and ask them if it is the x-axis or the y-axis? Check their understanding of what a bar chart can show. Ask if they now feel slightly more confident in reading and understanding bar chart data.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Then represent the same information in a sketch of a pie chart or a doughnut chart.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Talk about the similarities (same data) and the differences (the way the data is displayed).

Oxford task — guided: Show the class more examples of pie charts and/or doughnut charts. Each coloured part of these charts represents a different category of information (e.g. name of a superhero) and they all come together to make a whole. The parts are calculated as percentages.

Oxford task — guided: Children are going to create a survey based on film heroes. They will present the results in a pie chart or a doughnut chart. Explain that their survey question (e.g. ‘Who is your favourite film hero?’) should have multiple-choice answers (at least four or five). Put children into groups of four to design their survey question and answers. To make the activity more interesting, explain that the question can be based on any aspect of heroes in films, and doesn’t need to be about a favourite film hero. This will ensure that each group ends up with a different survey question and different results.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children are going to create a survey based on film heroes. They will present the results in a pie chart or a doughnut chart. Explain that their survey question (e.g. ‘Who is your favourite film hero?’) should have multiple-choice answers (at least four or five). Put children into groups of four to design their survey question and answers. To make the activity more interesting, explain that the question can be based on any aspect of heroes in films, and doesn’t need to be about a favourite film hero. This will ensure that each group ends up with a different survey question and different results.

Oxford pair/group task: Children then work together in their groups to analyse their data and present it in a chart. You will probably need to offer support when calculating simple percentages. Otherwise, use numbers instead of percentages and estimate what the chart will look like.

Oxford pair/group task: When everybody has finished, encourage groups to share their results.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Give children time to move around the classroom to collect their survey results.

Oxford source task: Encourage them to ask their survey question aloud using correct question intonation. Also write on the board phrases children can use if they don’t understand the question, such as ‘I don’t understand. What do you mean?’ or ‘Can you repeat the question, please?’

Oxford source task: Children then work together in their groups to analyse their data and present it in a chart. You will probably need to offer support when calculating simple percentages. Otherwise, use numbers instead of percentages and estimate what the chart will look like.

Oxford source task: When everybody has finished, encourage groups to share their results.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Form mixed-ability groups for the survey activity.

Oxford differentiation: Allow less able children to make a bar chart instead to build their confidence in this area.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to guide their group through the process of making a chart. If they are able, encourage them to use percentages. Show them how to do this first.

Oxford additional tasks: Ask children if they prefer one chart over another. Why? Which chart is easier to read?

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have carried out a survey and presented the results graphically.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?
