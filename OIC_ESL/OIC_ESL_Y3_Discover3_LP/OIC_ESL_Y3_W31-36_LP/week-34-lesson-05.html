Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Favourite heroes. Original outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4b. Unit: Heroes.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Talk with the class about what a bar chart is. You can show children the example bar chart in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.38.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: You may use this time at the beginning of the lesson for children to complete their comic strip stories from Week 34, Lesson 4 (or they can do this in Week 36).

Oxford opening: Using the Think-Pair-Share technique, ask children to think of as many heroes from stories (books and films) as they can.

Oxford opening: Take answers and make a list on the board. Then draw a Venn diagram showing the overlap between ‘heroes from books’ and ‘heroes from films’.

Oxford opening: Ask the class: Can you think of another way to show information? Hopefully children will suggest a ‘bar chart’ or ‘bar graph’.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Talk with the class about what a bar chart is. You can show children the example bar chart in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.38.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Draw a bar chart on the board.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Along the vertical axis, write the numbers 1 to 5. Along the horizontal axis, write four or five names of the fictional heroes discussed in the introductory activity.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Draw some bars so that children can really see what a bar chart is. You can either make up the data or hold a class vote to find out how many children like each hero best. If you choose the latter, you may need to extend the vertical axis accordingly.

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to tell you what the horizontal axis represents (heroes) and what the vertical axis represents (number of people who like each hero).

Oxford task — guided: Tell children that they are now going to write about the bar chart.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Bring the class back together. Invite a few children to read out their sentences and talk together about whether the numbers mentioned are correct. Then encourage children to tell you how they found the activity, if it was challenging, and what methods they used to make understanding the data slightly easier (e.g. by following the axes with their finger, through pairwork, by checking the numbers against the total number of people surveyed).

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: First, ask them to write one or two sentences about what a bar chart is and what it shows.

Oxford source task: Then ask them to look carefully at the data presented in the bar chart, following both axes, and to explain it in their own words. (e.g. How many children like each hero best, and which is the favourite and least favourite hero?)

Oxford source task: Some children will find this challenging, so give them enough time to make sense of the data, prompting their thinking where required (don’t just give them the answers). Remind them that they are in the stretch zone and, although the activity feels difficult, it is helping them to learn and deepen their understanding of bar charts!

Oxford source task: Bring the class back together. Invite a few children to read out their sentences and talk together about whether the numbers mentioned are correct. Then encourage children to tell you how they found the activity, if it was challenging, and what methods they used to make understanding the data slightly easier (e.g. by following the axes with their finger, through pairwork, by checking the numbers against the total number of people surveyed).

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Encourage children to copy the bar chart from the board, or provide handouts.

Oxford differentiation: Spend more time on how to interpret simple bar chart data for less confident children.

Oxford differentiation: Fast finishers can write a new survey question with multiple choice answers, carry out their survey around the classroom (using tally marks), and make a new bar chart.

Oxford additional tasks: Carry out a survey for another topic (e.g. a favourite superpower) and challenge children to show these results in a bar chart.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a bar chart and written about the information it shows.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 34, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — Favourite hero clues game

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Favourite heroes.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Hero comparison Venn

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Favourite heroes.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Favourite hero opinion ladder

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Favourite heroes.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Display two or three topic-related choices. Students vote individually, discuss evidence with a partner, and give a complete sentence explaining their choice.

ICQ: Do you vote before discussing? Must you give a reason?

CCQ: Which detail from the lesson supports your choice?

Assessment: A clear choice and a relevant explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Hero interview hot seat

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Favourite heroes.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

YEAR 3 Heroes
Week 34, Lesson 5: Favourite heroes
Learning outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4b

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Heroes’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by interpreting and explaining 
bar chart data, which is a useful real-life skill.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
