Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: A superhero story!. Original outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4c, 3.4d, 3.4e. Unit: Heroes.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show the class a picture of a familiar superhero. Elicit their name, appearance, clothes (costume), superpowers, the name of the planet where they live, and any other information they know.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play a vocabulary revision game using the new words and phrases taught so far this week.

Oxford opening: Give a simple definition and tell children to write the correct word on their whiteboard.

Oxford opening: On the count of three, ask everyone to hold up their whiteboard.

Oxford opening: Play this a few times, then ask a confident child to give their own definition.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show the class a picture of a familiar superhero. Elicit their name, appearance, clothes (costume), superpowers, the name of the planet where they live, and any other information they know.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children that today they are going to create their own superhero!

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Give them time to mind map some ideas on their whiteboard and discuss them in pairs.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Quickly talk about the features of a comic strip story as a class:

Oxford task — guided: It has a mix of text and illustrations.

Oxford task — guided: The story is told across panels – there is a new panel for each new event/scene.

Oxford task — guided: Dialogue is presented in speech bubbles.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Give them time to mind map some ideas on their whiteboard and discuss them in pairs.

Oxford pair/group task: Using the Think-Pair-Share technique, put children into pairs to tell each other what they have to do before beginning the writing task.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Use Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.98–99 as a reminder.

Oxford source task: Challenge children to use one gerund phrase with ‘good at …’ (Week 34, Lesson 2) and at least one superlative sentence (Week 34, Lesson 3).

Oxford source task: Using the Think-Pair-Share technique, put children into pairs to tell each other what they have to do before beginning the writing task.

Oxford source task: Children should spend most of the lesson creating their comic strip story. Encourage them to plan their caption and dialogue text on their whiteboard first. They can also ask a partner to check their spelling and punctuation. The last panel can end ‘To be continued …’ if appropriate.

Oxford source task: Monitor actively and praise children for their imagination in coming up with original and unique superpowers, and for their effort in producing their comic strip story. As you circulate the classroom, ask individual children what their favourite part of the process is, as well as the most challenging part.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with some story ideas. Encourage them to focus on just one or two panels. They may just write dialogue if they prefer.

Oxford differentiation: Remind more able children that a great story should have a beginning, a middle with a problem, and then an ending with a resolution (when the problem is solved).

Oxford additional tasks: Produce another version of the comic strip story from a different character’s point of view.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to think more deeply about their chosen superpower(s) and to try to define it more precisely – for instance, in what situations does it not work? Why?

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have invented a superhero. They have written a comic strip story about them.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Superhero story event cards

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A superhero story!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Comic dialogue studio

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A superhero story!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
