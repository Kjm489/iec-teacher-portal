PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: On the board, write ‘its’, ‘cant’, and ‘dont’ incorrectly, without the apostrophe. Ask children if anything is missing. Elicit the apostrophe (’).

Oxford opening: In these words, the apostrophe shows that a letter is missing. Sometimes this means turning two words into one.

Oxford opening: For each example, ask children to tell you which letter is missing, and if it should be broken down into two words (‘it’s’ = ‘it is’, ‘can’t’ = ‘cannot’, ‘don’t’ = ‘do not’).

Oxford opening: Read out the box on contractions on p.83 of Oxford Discover SB3. Ask children to follow in their book. Play the audio track. Children listen to and read the words in the orange box.

Oxford opening: Turn to p.123 of Oxford Discover SB3 and show children the difference between its and it’s. Explain that its is a possessive adjective, and it’s is the contraction for it is. Elicit some sentences with its and it’s and make sure that children understand the difference.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Spend the first half of the lesson on planning. Put children into pairs to mind map some ideas on the writing topic ‘My hero’. Ask: What things do you want to include when describing a hero? This can be a person they know well, or someone in public life they are inspired by. Stress that their hero must be a real person and not a superhero.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Bring the class back together. Discuss each part of a ‘My hero’ description. For example:

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: 1. Introduce your hero. Who are they? Give a brief summary of what they do. 2. Things your hero does every day or in a volunteer role. 3. Things your hero is good at. Do they like doing what they do? Is what they do challenging or difficult? 4. Your opinion of your hero – Why are they special/heroic? 5. What heroic qualities do they have? Are they brave/kind/helpful/inspiring?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Give children time to write notes next to each section or question prompt. Encourage them to do this independently, although they can share their ideas in pairs.

Oxford task — guided: Monitor children as they plan, asking questions where necessary to expand their thinking. Provide dictionaries if they want to look for specific adjectives.

Oxford task — guided: Spend the second half of the lesson on writing and reviewing. Now ask children to write their description of a real-life hero. Encourage them to be creative with their language choices – can they include interesting adjectives, superlative adjectives, or a gerund phrase? Can they use a mix of present and past tenses?

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Spend the first half of the lesson on planning. Put children into pairs to mind map some ideas on the writing topic ‘My hero’. Ask: What things do you want to include when describing a hero? This can be a person they know well, or someone in public life they are inspired by. Stress that their hero must be a real person and not a superhero.

Oxford pair/group task: Give children time to write notes next to each section or question prompt. Encourage them to do this independently, although they can share their ideas in pairs.

Oxford pair/group task: Check children’s work as well during the pairwork activity.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Commend their effort and perseverance during the challenging extended writing task.

Oxford source task: After a set time limit, ask children to swap their description with a partner, who will check it for correct spelling, punctuation (including use of apostrophes in contractions), and language use. Ask: Are the ideas clearly organized? Does the description make sense? Remind the class that it is good to make mistakes because this is how we learn.

Oxford source task: Check children’s work as well during the pairwork activity.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less able children to focus on writing a few accurate sentences – sometimes ‘less is more’.

Oxford differentiation: Give more confident children a writing checklist of all the newly acquired language and tenses they should try to include at least once in their description.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to make a final, illustrated draft of their description.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written an interesting description of a real-life hero using newly acquired language and vocabulary. They have practised using apostrophes accurately.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 35, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — My hero brainstorm web

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: My hero.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Learners create a small labelled drawing, map or idea web representing one accurate example from the lesson, then explain it to a partner using a sentence frame.

ICQ: Do you draw and label before presenting? Will your partner ask a question?

CCQ: How does your drawing show the concept from today's lesson?

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Hero biography question ladder

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: My hero.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Hero timeline organizer

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: My hero.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — My hero writing workshop

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: My hero.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Heroes’.
In this lesson, children will apply their learning from the unit to write about their hero.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
