Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: An interview with an explorer. Original outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2d, 3.2e. Unit: Amazing sea.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Elicit from children what explorers find – for instance, treasure (artefacts), new places, new species, and new foods.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: On the board, write the five Wh– question words – ‘What’, ‘When’, ‘Where’, ‘Who’, ‘Why’.

Oxford opening: Elicit a present tense question and/or a past tense question for each of the words.

Oxford opening: If anyone makes a mistake in forming the question, highlight that this is a good opportunity to learn. Allow children to discuss why the mistake was made (possibly a translation from L1), and encourage them to think about how they can remember the correct question formation in the future.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit from children what explorers find – for instance, treasure (artefacts), new places, new species, and new foods.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that in today’s lesson they are going to plan and act out an interview with an (imaginary) sea explorer. They will work in pairs, with A taking the role of the interviewer and B taking the role of the explorer.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Put As together to come up with Wh– questions to ask the explorers, and put Bs together to think of their exploration story. They can compile their ideas in a mind map.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Encourage groups to think about the topics explored in the ‘Amazing sea’ unit and any related vocabulary that they can remember and use in their role-plays.

Oxford task — guided: Remind groups of their learning of want to and need to / have to in Week 31, Lesson 3 – can they incorporate these forms into questions?

Oxford task — guided: Tell children to think about the best time to interview an explorer – while they are exploring, or after they have returned from an exploration trip? This is important because it will determine the verb tense.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain that in today’s lesson they are going to plan and act out an interview with an (imaginary) sea explorer. They will work in pairs, with A taking the role of the interviewer and B taking the role of the explorer.

Oxford pair/group task: Encourage groups to think about the topics explored in the ‘Amazing sea’ unit and any related vocabulary that they can remember and use in their role-plays.

Oxford pair/group task: Remind groups of their learning of want to and need to / have to in Week 31, Lesson 3 – can they incorporate these forms into questions?

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: To offer support, turn to ‘An Interview with Marco Polo’ in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.184. Read the interview and identify the question words. Invite two volunteers to perform the interview at the front of the class.

Oxford source task: Once children have had enough preparation time, put them into AB pairs. Give them time to practise asking and answering their questions.

Oxford source task: Invite pairs to perform their role-play to the rest of the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Support less able children to write five questions, one for each Wh– word.

Oxford differentiation: Form mixed-ability groups during the preparation stages, but pair up similar abilities during the role-play task so children can work at their own pace and stretch each other’s thinking.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to give more detailed answers in which they connect their ideas and give descriptions.

Oxford additional tasks: Swap roles or partners in the role-play activity so children have further opportunity to practise and develop their speaking skills.

Oxford additional tasks: Read the interview in ex.A in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.170.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have recalled Wh– questions. They have role-played an interview with a sea explorer.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 32, LESSON 4

IEC supplemental game — Explorer interview question builder

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: An interview with an explorer.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Explorer role-play hot seat

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: An interview with an explorer.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Undersea discovery news report

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: An interview with an explorer.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

YEAR 3 Amazing sea
Week 32, Lesson 4: An interview with an explorer
Learning outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2d, 3.2e

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Amazing sea’.
In this lesson, children will practise their speaking skills by role-playing an interview 
with an explorer.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
