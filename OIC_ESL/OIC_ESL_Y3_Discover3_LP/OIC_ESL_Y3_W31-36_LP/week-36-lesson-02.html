Source alignment note: Oxford supplies one shared weekly review plan. The five IEC lesson divisions are adaptations, not separate Oxford lesson titles.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Heroes’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford opening: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford opening: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to at the end of the school year to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

Oxford task — guided: In small groups, children create a picture book about heroes. Ask them to choose a topic, such as ‘Heroes from my country’ or ‘Heroes in sport’. They should research information about these people and find out why they are considered heroes. Do they help or inspire others? Do they do volunteer work? Have they overcome past challenges? Give them the freedom to write a variety of text types – a description (see Week 35, Lesson 5), a comic strip story (see Week 34, Lesson 4), a fact file, an interview text (see Week 35, Lesson 3), or a blog post from the point of view of their chosen hero. Encourage children to illustrate their work, give their book a title, and write their names as authors.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford pair/group task: In small groups, children create a picture book about heroes. Ask them to choose a topic, such as ‘Heroes from my country’ or ‘Heroes in sport’. They should research information about these people and find out why they are considered heroes. Do they help or inspire others? Do they do volunteer work? Have they overcome past challenges? Give them the freedom to write a variety of text types – a description (see Week 35, Lesson 5), a comic strip story (see Week 34, Lesson 4), a fact file, an interview text (see Week 35, Lesson 3), or a blog post from the point of view of their chosen hero. Encourage children to illustrate their work, give their book a title, and write their names as authors.

Oxford pair/group task: In groups, children write and act out a (super)hero story with a problem and a resolution. This could be a series of short role-plays involving different people that, when put together, tells a complete story about different kinds of hero. Encourage children to think creatively when making and sourcing recycled materials for costumes and props. Remind them that they will also need to promote their show by handing out invitations to other groups in the class, or to other year groups.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: In groups, children write and act out a (super)hero story with a problem and a resolution. This could be a series of short role-plays involving different people that, when put together, tells a complete story about different kinds of hero. Encourage children to think creatively when making and sourcing recycled materials for costumes and props. Remind them that they will also need to promote their show by handing out invitations to other groups in the class, or to other year groups.

Oxford source task: Children identify local community heroes and send out thank you cards. These could be emergency workers, hospital workers, or perhaps there is someone who did something very special for your community. Provide the necessary resources to help children – for instance, they could read local newspapers, look at charity leaflets and posters, or research local news or charity blog posts online. Give them a few names or websites as helpful starting points. Challenge children to distinguish the heroes in the stories from those who might not be as heroic. Remind them to reflect on what makes a hero. Children design their cards and write messages or letters saying why they are grateful for these people in the community. If appropriate, send their messages to their chosen real-life heroes.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the projects, provide a revision handout of key vocabulary, sentence starters, and useful phrases. For the letter-writing task, in which children say thank you to a community hero, you could give them a simple template for writing a letter or a thank-you card. You might want to assign team leaders for each group to guide less confident children through the task, or break the task down into more manageable stages. Assign roles fairly based on children’s strengths. To support team leaders, provide a checklist of items that each project should have.

Oxford differentiation: Children who are less able linguistically might excel in more creative tasks. Acknowledge their strengths as they work.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to think about how they will present their work effectively at the end of the week. What language can they use (including new grammatical forms learned)? What do they need to do to maintain the audience’s interest?

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Hero project storyboard

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Model and plan extended activity.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Hero project rubric match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Model and plan extended activity.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Project proposal pitch

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Model and plan extended activity.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
