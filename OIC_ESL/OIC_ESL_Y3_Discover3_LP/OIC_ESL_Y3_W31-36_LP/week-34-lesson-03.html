Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Superhero qualities. Original outcomes: 3.3b, 3.3c, 3.3d, 3.4c. Unit: Heroes.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Write ‘SUPERPOWER’ on the board and ask children what it means. Break the word down into ‘super’ and ‘power’ to support their thinking.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Say: Even superheroes get nervous! Ask children what sort of breathing activity they think a superhero might do to feel calmer. Hopefully, by now, they will think of deep breathing. Deep breathing can be easier for children, with something tangible to blow.

Oxford opening: Hand out a piece of paper to each child. Instruct them to hold it at the top, and then to breathe in through their nose and out through their mouth like a superhero. Do they notice the effect of their breathing on the paper? Repeat this a few times.

Oxford opening: Tell children that today, at least once, you’d like them to stop, think, and breathe in and out before they answer a question. They may use their paper to help them. Make sure they are clear that this doesn’t have to be before every answer!

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Write ‘SUPERPOWER’ on the board and ask children what it means. Break the word down into ‘super’ and ‘power’ to support their thinking.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Using the flashcards or pictures, or by offering simple definitions, elicit the lesson’s vocabulary. Ask the class: Are these superpowers?

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit other superpowers superheroes in books and films have. Write them on the board.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Tell children that they are going to read a story about a superhero. Ask them to guess what superpowers they think will be in the story. Read the story as a whole class or in pairs.

Oxford task — guided: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.98–99. (Children might have listened to this story for gist in Week 34, Lesson 1, but today they are going to study the text for main information, details, and interesting language.)

Oxford task — guided: Give children time to discuss their reaction to the story in pairs. Then encourage them to reread and consider how they might feel if they were in the story (as the superhero and/or as a witness to the superhero).

Oxford task — guided: Invite volunteers to share their ideas with the whole class.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Tell children that they are going to read a story about a superhero. Ask them to guess what superpowers they think will be in the story. Read the story as a whole class or in pairs.

Oxford pair/group task: Give children time to discuss their reaction to the story in pairs. Then encourage them to reread and consider how they might feel if they were in the story (as the superhero and/or as a witness to the superhero).

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Explain that superheroes in stories are usually the best. This means they are better than others at doing something. Elicit an adjective that relates to the story and write a sentence on the board using the superlative form. For example:

Oxford source task: Hector the Energy Saver is the fastest (on his lightning bike).

Oxford source task: Indicate to the ‘–est’ at the end of the adjective. You may also write a comparative sentence if this will be helpful to children’s understanding: e.g. X is stronger than Y. X is the strongest. See also Oxford Discover SB 3 p.113 ex.F.

Oxford source task: Ask children to practise superlative adjectives by writing a couple of sentences about their favourite superhero.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Spend more time teaching superlative adjectives to less confident children.

Oxford differentiation: Ask fast finishers to find out which adjectives take ‘–est’ and which ones take ‘–iest’.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.101.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to write superlative sentences to describe their real-life hero.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a story about a superhero. They have learned how to use superlatives of short adjectives to describe them.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Superpower adjective matching

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superhero qualities.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Superhero strengths ranking

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superhero qualities.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Qualities interview carousel

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superhero qualities.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Superhero profile design

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superhero qualities.

YEAR 3 Heroes
Week 34, Lesson 3: Superhero qualities
Learning outcomes: 3.3b, 3.3c, 3.3d, 3.4c

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Heroes’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and writing skills by reading a story 
about a superhero, then writing sentences about their favourite superhero.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
