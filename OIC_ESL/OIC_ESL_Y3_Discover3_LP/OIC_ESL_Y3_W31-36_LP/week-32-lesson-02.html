Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: A wildlife guide. Original outcomes: 3.3e, 3.3f, 3.3g. Unit: Amazing sea.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Introduce children to some of the wildlife from today’s lesson’s input text. Hold up or display some pictures to start a discussion. Find out if they already know the names of these creatures, and if they know any facts about them. Write their ideas on the board.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Show the class a short video clip that will give them a taster of how immense and awe-inspiring the ocean is, and how diverse sea creatures are. Suggested online search: ‘one hour of amazing ocean moments’ or ‘what lurks in the midnight zone? Blue planet’. You can mute or turn down the commentary.

Oxford opening: Encourage children to describe their reactions to the video. Ask: What surprised you? What sea creature did you like the most? How do you feel when you see the sea? Are you curious about what lives under the sea?

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Introduce children to some of the wildlife from today’s lesson’s input text. Hold up or display some pictures to start a discussion. Find out if they already know the names of these creatures, and if they know any facts about them. Write their ideas on the board.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: You can also show children pictures of sea animals from Oxford Discover SB 3 p.176 and SB 4 p.176.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children to write down a question about one (or more) of the animals that they would like to know the answer to. Give them time to share their questions in pairs.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: See the text provided. Ask children to scan the text quickly to see if they can find the answer to their question.

Oxford task — guided: Bring the class back together. Talk about where we might find this type of information. Take ideas, hopefully eliciting ‘wildlife guide’, ‘encyclopaedia’, or ‘non-fiction book about animals’.

Oxford task — guided: Read the text as a whole class, inviting confident volunteers to read out a few sentences. Pause after each section and elicit the main points about the animal it describes.

Oxford task — guided: Direct children to complete some comprehension questions in pairs. Write the suggested questions on the board.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children to write down a question about one (or more) of the animals that they would like to know the answer to. Give them time to share their questions in pairs.

Oxford pair/group task: Direct children to complete some comprehension questions in pairs. Write the suggested questions on the board.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs to think of one more thing that they can do to help protect sea wildlife. Ask them to present their ideas beginning with ‘Do …’ or ‘Don’t …’.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Review answers as a whole class.

Oxford source task: Write the word ‘endangered’ on the board. Elicit or take suggestions for its meaning. Confirm or correct children’s understanding.

Oxford source task: Ask: What can we do to help save sea wildlife? (e.g. Use less plastic and don’t leave plastic on the beach – sea creatures and sea birds might get trapped in or swallow plastic products, causing them harm.)

Oxford source task: Put children into pairs to think of one more thing that they can do to help protect sea wildlife. Ask them to present their ideas beginning with ‘Do …’ or ‘Don’t …’.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them some ideas to decide if they are ‘Do’ or ‘Don’t’.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage fast finishers to find out about an endangered sea creature.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 32, Lesson 2

Oxford differentiation: Wonderful wildlife

Oxford additional tasks: Make signs to display children’s Dos and Don’ts.

Oxford additional tasks: Use the internet or reference books to find out more about how humans are endangering sea wildlife (e.g. through fishing nets, toxins in the water, overfishing).

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read about wild sea animals. They have thought about endangered animals, and what we can do to help them.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 32, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — Wildlife guide text detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A wildlife guide.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Animal description matching

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A wildlife guide.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Guidebook page planning

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A wildlife guide.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 3 Amazing sea
Week 32, Lesson 2: A wildlife guide
Learning outcomes: 3.3e, 3.3f, 3.3g

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Amazing sea’.
In this lesson, children will build on their reading skills by using a range of strategies to identify and understand main points and new vocabulary in a wildlife guide.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
