LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3

Week: 35

Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.3c, 3.3f, 3.3g

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Community heroes. Original outcomes: 3.3c, 3.3f, 3.3g. Unit: Heroes.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Elicit the vocabulary using the flashcards or pictures. For each one, ask children if they are volunteers or not (e.g. in some countries, firefighters are volunteers).

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Do a warm-up exercise. Put children into groups of three or four. Tell them that you are going to spell a word and you want them to make the letters with their bodies.

Oxford opening: Spell out: v-o-l-u-n-t-e-e-r. After the physical activity, see if children can remember the letters and write them on their whiteboard. Can they work out what the word is?

Oxford opening: Say ‘volunteer’ again and write it on the board. Talk about what volunteering is and find out if anyone is or knows a volunteer.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit the vocabulary using the flashcards or pictures. For each one, ask children if they are volunteers or not (e.g. in some countries, firefighters are volunteers).

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Put children into pairs or small groups to think about why volunteering is a good thing, and how it can help communities. Invite them to share their ideas after a set time limit.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain to the class that they are going to read a text about a community hero. Give them one minute to predict what the text will mention, noting down ideas on their whiteboard.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask a pre-reading question (depending on the text you choose): e.g. Why do we need mountain rescue/firefighters/community heroes?

Oxford task — guided: Distribute handouts of the text provided, or use the text in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.88 (the language level should be acceptable for most children approaching the end of Year 3, and will encourage them to stretch their thinking).

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to read the text independently or in pairs. Then read again as a whole class, asking vocabulary-related questions to check children’s understanding.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs or small groups to think about why volunteering is a good thing, and how it can help communities. Invite them to share their ideas after a set time limit.

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children to read the text independently or in pairs. Then read again as a whole class, asking vocabulary-related questions to check children’s understanding.

Oxford pair/group task: Now ask children to close their books or turn over their handout. Put them into pairs to tell each other what they can remember about the text, including any important lessons.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: For the text ‘Community hero of the week!’, write the questions on the board.

Oxford source task: For the text, ‘Mayor for Another Day!’, ask children to complete exs.A–C on p.89.

Oxford source task: Now ask children to close their books or turn over their handout. Put them into pairs to tell each other what they can remember about the text, including any important lessons.

Oxford source task: Bring the class back together. Tell children to imagine a day in the volunteer’s life. Ask: What do you think this person is grateful for? Discuss, then follow up by asking: Why should we be grateful for community heroes? Do you think volunteers are heroes?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Guide less able children through the text in an adult-led small group. Reassure them that they don’t need to understand every single word to get the gist of a text.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage fast finishers to note down ideas for volunteer work in their community.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 35, Lesson 3

Oxford differentiation: Teacher note: Pre-teach the words ‘award’ and ‘emergency’.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete ex.D in Oxford Discover WB 4 p.89.

Oxford additional tasks: Role-play an interview with a community hero.

Oxford additional tasks: Find out about a local community hero. Write a short biography about what they do.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read about a local hero or volunteer. They have understood that not all heroes have superpowers.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 35, LESSON 3

IEC supplemental game — Community hero job match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Community heroes.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Local helper interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Community heroes.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Community problem solution cards

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Community heroes.
