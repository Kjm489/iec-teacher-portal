Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Model and plan extended activity. Original outcomes: Unit summary. Unit: Amazing sea.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Source alignment note: Oxford supplies one shared weekly review plan. The five IEC lesson divisions are adaptations, not separate Oxford lesson titles.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Amazing sea’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford opening: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford opening: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

Oxford task — guided: In groups, children design and make a poster for a ‘Save Our Oceans’ festival. Encourage them to think of different activities for different age groups. They could also interview an explorer who has returned from the bottom of the ocean, or people whose livelihoods depend on the oceans. Allow groups to use the internet to research the closest sea and/or ocean to them, and any sea creatures that are endangered. At the end of the week, invite them to present their poster to the class in which they expand on their ideas. Ask each group member to give their opinion on the festival, and to say which activity they like best and why.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford pair/group task: In groups, children design and make a poster for a ‘Save Our Oceans’ festival. Encourage them to think of different activities for different age groups. They could also interview an explorer who has returned from the bottom of the ocean, or people whose livelihoods depend on the oceans. Allow groups to use the internet to research the closest sea and/or ocean to them, and any sea creatures that are endangered. At the end of the week, invite them to present their poster to the class in which they expand on their ideas. Ask each group member to give their opinion on the festival, and to say which activity they like best and why.

Oxford pair/group task: In groups, children work together to make a 3D papier-mâché model of the water cycle, painted and labelled, showing salt and water going into the sea. They will need to plan what they are going to do first before doing it, e.g. it might be a good idea to make a smaller model to check that it works. At the end of the week, hold a water cycle model exhibition in which children and other invited guests inspect and provide feedback on other groups’ models. When presenting their model, challenge groups to describe the water cycle process using vocabulary learned in the unit.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: In groups, children work together to make a 3D papier-mâché model of the water cycle, painted and labelled, showing salt and water going into the sea. They will need to plan what they are going to do first before doing it, e.g. it might be a good idea to make a smaller model to check that it works. At the end of the week, hold a water cycle model exhibition in which children and other invited guests inspect and provide feedback on other groups’ models. When presenting their model, challenge groups to describe the water cycle process using vocabulary learned in the unit.

Oxford source task: Participate in a sea creature research project. Put children into pairs or small groups to agree on an animal to research, the information they want to find out, and how they are going to present their findings. Encourage them to look for information on appearance, diet, and habitat, but also on fun and curious facts to interest their audience. They should also find out if their chosen animal is endangered, and why. For ideas on which animals to research, children could look back at the animals in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.176 and SB 4 p.176.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the projects, provide a revision handout of key topic vocabulary, sentence starters, and useful phrases for children’s presentations. Assign team leaders for each small-group activity to guide less confident children, or break the task into more manageable stages. To support team leaders, provide a checklist of items that each project should have.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to think about how they will present their work effectively. What language can they use (e.g. new grammatical forms learned)? How can they maintain the audience’s interest?

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills they would like to improve or consolidate and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Ocean project planning board

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Model and plan extended activity.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
