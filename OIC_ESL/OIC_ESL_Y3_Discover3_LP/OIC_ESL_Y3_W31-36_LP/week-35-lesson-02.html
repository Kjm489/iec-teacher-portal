Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Superheroes on holiday!. Original outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2e, 3.4b, 3.4f. Unit: Heroes.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Encourage children to reflect on their work on superheroes in the unit so far. If you have time, you can show them the story about a superhero in Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.98–99. Play the story and ask children to follow the reading. Check children understand why Hector is a hero. (He’s good and he helps people.)

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Show children some holiday destination pictures.

Oxford opening: Elicit where they are and why people go there (to go on holiday, relax, and have fun).

Oxford opening: Talk about holiday activities that children enjoy the most.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Encourage children to reflect on their work on superheroes in the unit so far. If you have time, you can show them the story about a superhero in Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.98–99. Play the story and ask children to follow the reading. Check children understand why Hector is a hero. (He’s good and he helps people.)

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask: Did you know that superheroes also feel tired after working hard and they need to relax sometimes? Explain that, in today’s lesson, children are going to imagine their favourite superheroes on holiday together for a week. They will need to work together to plan and write about an exciting superhero holiday. Allocate the first half of the lesson to planning.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Put children into small groups of three or four. Ask them to come up with a list of steps to follow to complete the task successfully. For instance, they will need to decide on a destination and how to get there, asking themselves: What places do our superheroes like? How will they all get there? Can they all fly? If not, what form of transport can they use? What are they good at doing? How can they relax? Give groups time to discuss and note down their ideas. Remind them to listen respectfully to each other’s contributions and to ensure everyone’s participation.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Bring the class back together. Write some ideas on the board, and find out if groups have similar or different ideas. Remind them that their holiday plan should address the prompts ‘Where?’, ‘When?’, and ‘What will they do and see?’. They could also consider what clothes to take – will a superhero costume be suitable on holiday?

Oxford task — guided: Set groups a time limit to revise and expand on their ideas following the class feedback.

Oxford task — guided: Allocate the second half of the lesson to writing.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into small groups of three or four. Ask them to come up with a list of steps to follow to complete the task successfully. For instance, they will need to decide on a destination and how to get there, asking themselves: What places do our superheroes like? How will they all get there? Can they all fly? If not, what form of transport can they use? What are they good at doing? How can they relax? Give groups time to discuss and note down their ideas. Remind them to listen respectfully to each other’s contributions and to ensure everyone’s participation.

Oxford pair/group task: Bring the class back together. Write some ideas on the board, and find out if groups have similar or different ideas. Remind them that their holiday plan should address the prompts ‘Where?’, ‘When?’, and ‘What will they do and see?’. They could also consider what clothes to take – will a superhero costume be suitable on holiday?

Oxford pair/group task: Set groups a time limit to revise and expand on their ideas following the class feedback.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Children should work together in their groups to write one or two paragraphs outlining the details of their superhero holiday. To accompany this text, ask groups to also produce an itinerary (plan) for their week-long holiday. This could be in the form of a timetable. Encourage groups to work out how to organize their workload and delegate the tasks between them – they could work on each part together; or divide the group into pairs, with one pair writing the final text and the other pair making the itinerary.

Oxford source task: Remind children to check their work for correct spelling, punctuation, and language use.

Oxford source task: In pairs, ask children to imagine that they are their superhero, and to ask and answer the following questions in this role: Did you have a good holiday? What was the best part? What was the worst part? Why?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Form-mixed ability groups so more confident children can encourage ideas from their less confident peers and support them with language use.

Oxford differentiation: Less confident children could present their ideas in a more visual format, like a timetable.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to use a range of tenses in their written work, as well as one gerund phrase with ‘good at …’ (Week 34, Lesson 2) and one superlative sentence (Week 34, Lesson 3).

Oxford additional tasks: Write a postcard from a superhero on holiday.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a comic strip story about the superhero holiday.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have planned and written about a holiday for a superhero.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Superhero holiday destination map

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superheroes on holiday!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Learners create a small labelled drawing, map or idea web representing one accurate example from the lesson, then explain it to a partner using a sentence frame.

ICQ: Do you draw and label before presenting? Will your partner ask a question?

CCQ: How does your drawing show the concept from today's lesson?

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Holiday packing superhero challenge

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superheroes on holiday!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Holiday postcard role-play

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superheroes on holiday!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Superhero itinerary builder

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superheroes on holiday!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Holiday problem solving

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superheroes on holiday!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Holiday adventure mini-story

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Superheroes on holiday!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Heroes’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and writing skills by planning a holiday for a superhero. They will practise communicating ideas in a small group.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
