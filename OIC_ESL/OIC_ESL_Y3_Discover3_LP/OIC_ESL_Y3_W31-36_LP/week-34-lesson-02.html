Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Heroes are good at helping others. Original outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2b. Unit: Heroes.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Introduce the lesson’s vocabulary using flashcards or pictures. Give children the opportunity to listen and repeat the phrases. Encourage them to assign a mime to each phrase to help them remember them.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children what activities they do to reduce their stress levels. They might suggest reading a book, drawing, or doing some physical activity.

Oxford opening: Talk about the importance of being able to identify activities that help them to feel calmer. Reinforce that these will be different for different people, and may change.

Oxford opening: Give children a moment to consider what their go-to activity is when they feel stressed.

Oxford opening: Invite them to share, if they want to. Find out what they haven’t yet tried but are now going to try following this class discussion.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Introduce the lesson’s vocabulary using flashcards or pictures. Give children the opportunity to listen and repeat the phrases. Encourage them to assign a mime to each phrase to help them remember them.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Play a game by saying the verb and asking children to say the noun – for instance, you could say ‘tell’ and children will (hopefully) respond with ‘jokes’.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Put children into pairs or small groups to continue to play the game.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Bring the class back together. Ask: Which of these activities can heroes do? Remind them of the different heroes they identified in the previous lesson (see Week 34, Lesson 1).

Oxford task — guided: Follow up by asking a confident child or another adult in class: Which of these activities are you good at doing? Write their response on the board: e.g. I’m good at writing stories.

Oxford task — guided: Focus children’s attention on how the sentence is structured and see if they notice features of the present continuous tense (i.e. the ‘–ing’ form of a verb), which they learned at the beginning of the year. Explain that when it functions as a noun, like in the example given, it is called a ‘gerund’. Remind children of their previous learning. They will be familiar with ‘I’m good at’ from the speaking activity in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.175 ex.E. In Level 2, they learned to say ‘I’m good at + a noun.’ This time, they are learning how to use it with a verb.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs or small groups to continue to play the game.

Oxford pair/group task: Explain that, in pairs, children are going to make simple survey questions to ask another pair. They should choose four or five activities to use in the question ‘Are you good at [solving problems]?’. They will need to work together to transform the verb root (e.g. ‘solve’) into the gerund.

Oxford pair/group task: Give children time to write their questions, then ask and answer them in groups of four.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Elicit the question ‘What are you good at doing?’.

Oxford source task: Explain that, in pairs, children are going to make simple survey questions to ask another pair. They should choose four or five activities to use in the question ‘Are you good at [solving problems]?’. They will need to work together to transform the verb root (e.g. ‘solve’) into the gerund.

Oxford source task: Give children time to write their questions, then ask and answer them in groups of four.

Oxford source task: Bring the class back together. Find out the results of each survey. Keep a tally on the board to find out the activity that the class is best at. Return to the initial question: Which of these activities can heroes do? You can probably declare that you have a class of heroes!

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Spend more time explaining gerunds to less confident children. Write a few examples on the board and encourage them to ask two or three questions.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to think of more activities that a hero might do and to convert them into gerund phrases.

Oxford additional tasks: Play a guessing game. In pairs, one child chooses a phrase and their partner must find out what it is by asking: Are you good at …?

Oxford additional tasks: Ask children to note down activities they are good at and one activity they want to get better at. Encourage them to think about how they can improve. Often, it is practice!

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned new vocabulary to describe different activities that heroes might do. They have talked about what they are good at doing in a survey activity.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Helping actions mime

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Heroes are good at helping others.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 3 Heroes
Week 34, Lesson 2: Heroes are good at helping others
Learning outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2b

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Heroes’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by discussing activities that heroes do and answering the question ‘What are you good at doing?’ with gerund phrases.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
