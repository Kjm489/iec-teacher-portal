Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Do you want to make a sandcastle?. Original outcomes: 3.1c, 3.2b, 3.2c, 3.2e. Unit: Amazing sea.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Remind children of their learning of want to + verb from the previous lesson (see Week 31, Lesson 2). Ask a volunteer to use this construction correctly in a sentence: e.g. I want to go for a walk. Elicit from children that ‘to’ always comes after ‘want’.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Revise beach activities phrases from the previous lesson with children (see Week 31, Lesson 2). Either show a flashcard or mime an activity for children to guess. Remind them that they need to practise vocabulary over and over in order to remember it.

Oxford opening: Go through all the phrases in this way. Note which words the class is finding difficult, and repeat them until most children’s pronunciation has improved.

Oxford opening: Invite a volunteer to the front to do the same. Then put children into small groups to continue playing the revision mime game.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Remind children of their learning of want to + verb from the previous lesson (see Week 31, Lesson 2). Ask a volunteer to use this construction correctly in a sentence: e.g. I want to go for a walk. Elicit from children that ‘to’ always comes after ‘want’.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Then explain that need to and have to follow the same pattern. Write three simple sentences on the board modelling each construction: e.g. I want to go to bed early. I need to go to bed early. I have to go to bed early.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask: How do ‘want to’, ‘need to’, or ‘have to’ change the meaning of the sentences? Give children time to read the three sentences and to think about the nuance in meaning. To give them a clue, explain that two of the sentences have a similar meaning.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Invite children to share their ideas. Elicit or explain that need to and have to indicate something that is necessary – you don’t have a choice. Conversely, want to implies choice.

Oxford task — guided: To check understanding, hold a class discussion about things that children need to or have to do. They will probably suggest chores, being at school, or aspects of their daily routine. Then encourage them to say what they want to do.

Oxford task — guided: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.181 and work together to complete ex.F.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs (AB) to design a puzzle for another pair. Tell them to write three names for three characters, and to draw four beach activities for each character. Then encourage them to design a colour-coding system (e.g. red for need to or have to; green for want to). For example, they could draw a spade to represent ‘build a sandcastle’, then colour it red for one character and green for another character.

Oxford pair/group task: Tell pairs to swap puzzles. In each pair, Child A chooses a character name and writes it secretly on their whiteboard. Child B must guess the character by looking at the picture and asking questions: e.g. Do they want to …? Do they need to / have to …? Their aim is to arrive at the right answer using as few questions as possible.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Introduce the question form Do/Does … [want/need/have] to …? Model an example, encouraging children to focus on the verb and your intonation. Say it together.

Oxford source task: Put children into pairs (AB) to design a puzzle for another pair. Tell them to write three names for three characters, and to draw four beach activities for each character. Then encourage them to design a colour-coding system (e.g. red for need to or have to; green for want to). For example, they could draw a spade to represent ‘build a sandcastle’, then colour it red for one character and green for another character.

Oxford source task: Tell pairs to swap puzzles. In each pair, Child A chooses a character name and writes it secretly on their whiteboard. Child B must guess the character by looking at the picture and asking questions: e.g. Do they want to …? Do they need to / have to …? Their aim is to arrive at the right answer using as few questions as possible.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with an example puzzle.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to use need to and have to interchangeably in questions.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.166–167.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have recognized the difference in meaning between want to and need to / have to. They have practised asking and answering questions using these forms.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Sandcastle materials detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Do you want to make a sandcastle?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Sandcastle request dialogue

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Do you want to make a sandcastle?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Build a sandcastle sequence

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?
