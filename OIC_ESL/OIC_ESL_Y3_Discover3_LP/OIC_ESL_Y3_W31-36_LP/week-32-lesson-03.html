Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Under the sea!. Original outcomes: 3.3a, 3.3b, 3.3c, 3.3d. Unit: Amazing sea.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Pre-teach the lesson’s vocabulary using flashcards or pictures. Drill the words carefully, paying attention to spelling-sound patterns.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play a quick warm-up game. Ask children to write down three things we can wear on our feet. Set a time limit (e.g. 1 minute). Repeat with three things we can wear on our head, and three things we can wear on our body.

Oxford opening: Review answers as a class and write correct ones on the board. Award one point for each correct answer, and a bonus point for correct spelling. The child with the most points at the end of the warm-up wins!

Oxford opening: Model and drill the pronunciation of any difficult words.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Pre-teach the lesson’s vocabulary using flashcards or pictures. Drill the words carefully, paying attention to spelling-sound patterns.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit that a diver or a sea explorer will need some or all of these items. Ask children if they have ever swum, snorkelled, or dived, and talk about what it feels like to wear the clothing and equipment. Share experiences as a class.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Play a flashcard game. Put the cards on the board and number them. In pairs, ask children to secretly write down a number. They must try to guess their partner’s item by asking: Have you got …? / Do you have …? / Are you wearing …?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask the class what they think sea explorers do and why they are important.

Oxford task — guided: Tell children that they are going to read about a sea explorer (Jacques Cousteau). Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.178–179.

Oxford task — guided: Read the biography as a whole class. First, establish with children that a biography is a non-fiction text about a real person and their life. It shares interesting facts and exciting experiences.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Play a flashcard game. Put the cards on the board and number them. In pairs, ask children to secretly write down a number. They must try to guess their partner’s item by asking: Have you got …? / Do you have …? / Are you wearing …?

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into small groups to come up with realistic ideas for protecting our oceans. If you don’t live near the ocean, you may consider waterways like rivers or lakes.

Oxford pair/group task: Urge groups to write down their ideas in order of preference. At the end of the lesson, invite a spokesperson from each group to feed back their ideas to the class.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask children how they know the explorer tried, or is trying, to protect the sea.

Oxford source task: Referring back to their learning in Week 32, Lesson 1, ask children to identify up to five unknown words. Encourage them to remember how they tackled difficult vocabulary previously, and to use the same technique(s) now. Then hand out dictionaries for children to look up their meanings and to check their guesses.

Oxford source task: Put children into small groups to come up with realistic ideas for protecting our oceans. If you don’t live near the ocean, you may consider waterways like rivers or lakes.

Oxford source task: Urge groups to write down their ideas in order of preference. At the end of the lesson, invite a spokesperson from each group to feed back their ideas to the class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them two words to look up in a dictionary. Reassure them that they don’t need to understand every word to follow the gist of a text.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge fast finishers to look up the word ‘marine’ and its part of speech category, and then to use it in an example sentence.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover SB 3 p.180.

Oxford additional tasks: Find out about any local initiatives to protect waterways.

Oxford additional tasks: Draw a picture of a sea explorer and label their clothing and equipment.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read about a sea explorer. They have considered how we can protect our oceans and waterways.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 32, LESSON 3

IEC supplemental game — Underwater soundscape storytelling

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Under the sea!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Sea scene description relay

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Under the sea!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Ocean adjective word bank

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Under the sea!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Diver observation information gap

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Under the sea!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
