LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: Amazing sea
Week: 32
Lesson: 5
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.4b, 3.4c, 3.4f

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Let’s explore!. Original outcomes: 3.4b, 3.4c, 3.4f. Unit: Amazing sea.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Tell children that they are going to pretend to be a sea explorer and write a short blog post or diary entry about their experiences. They may choose a real or an invented explorer.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Show children pictures of past and present explorers. Put them into pairs to say as much as they can about each explorer, including what they can see in the picture and any predictions about what the explorer did and/or discovered.

Oxford opening: Invite volunteers to share their ideas. Write any useful vocabulary on the board.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children that they are going to pretend to be a sea explorer and write a short blog post or diary entry about their experiences. They may choose a real or an invented explorer.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: As children are becoming more confident in their writing, talk about how they can make their written production more detailed.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: First, remind children of Week 31, Lesson 3 when they learned about want to and need to / have to. Put them into pairs to make a list of things on their whiteboard that explorers want to do (e.g. discover exciting, new places; think of solutions to problems; learn about the world) and things that explorers need to do (e.g. make a safety plan).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.183 and complete ex.F. Then complete WB 3 p.169 ex.A.

Oxford task — guided: For the writing task, children can write their blog post in the role of an explorer they have learned about in the unit (i.e. Jacques Cousteau, Sylvia Earle, or Marco Polo); or, if they have time, they can research the work of another explorer online. If they choose the latter, encourage them to find local examples. Alternatively, children can use their imagination to write about the experiences of a fictional sea explorer.

Oxford task — guided: Elicit the features of a simple blog post or diary entry. Write them on the board.

Oxford task — guided: Include a date. Sometimes include a title (more so in blog posts than in diary entries).

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: First, remind children of Week 31, Lesson 3 when they learned about want to and need to / have to. Put them into pairs to make a list of things on their whiteboard that explorers want to do (e.g. discover exciting, new places; think of solutions to problems; learn about the world) and things that explorers need to do (e.g. make a safety plan).

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Describe events in chronological order using ‘first’, ‘next’, ‘then’, and ‘finally’.

Oxford source task: Include observations, thoughts, and feelings.

Oxford source task: Use description to capture the attention of the reader.

Oxford source task: Give children time to plan and write their short blog post or diary entry.

Oxford source task: Tell them to swap their work with a partner to check that they have fulfilled the above criteria. They should also check for correct spelling, punctuation, and grammar.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide some useful language and sentence starters.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to include one sentence with although and a mix of tenses in their writing.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a final, illustrated draft of the blog post or diary entry for display.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how although connects two contrasting ideas in a sentence. They have imagined the life of a sea explorer in a short blog post or diary entry.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 32, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — Ocean expedition route planning

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Let’s explore!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Equipment selection challenge

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Let’s explore!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
