Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: What’s amazing about the sea?. Original outcomes: 3.1b, 3.1c, 3.1d. Unit: Amazing sea.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Write the question ‘What’s amazing about the sea?’ on the board, but mixed up. Challenge children to work out the correct order and to tell you the question. Give them one minute to confer in pairs. Take answers and write the question on the board.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Write ‘SEA’ and ‘OCEAN’ on the board and display a large picture of a body of water. See if children can tell you what the difference is between them – they are both bodies of water; seas are smaller than oceans, partially enclosed by land; oceans are much bigger, deeper, and cover approximately 70% of the Earth’s surface. Explain that this is gigantic!

Oxford opening: Give or elicit an example of a sea and an example of an ocean close to your country. Support a volunteer to find them on a world map, atlas, or globe.

Oxford opening: Challenge children to name any other seas or oceans around the world. Allow them to tell you in their L1, then write the English names on the board.

Oxford opening: Depending on your location, ask: Have you seen a sea or an ocean, or visited a beach? Did you like it there? Why? / Why not? Hold a whole-class discussion.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Write the question ‘What’s amazing about the sea?’ on the board, but mixed up. Challenge children to work out the correct order and to tell you the question. Give them one minute to confer in pairs. Take answers and write the question on the board.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Using the Think-Pair-Share technique, now ask children to come up with some ideas to answer the question, based on their existing knowledge of seas and oceans.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Play a video to generate interest in the new unit and to introduce some topic vocabulary. Play the Big Question 9 Wrap Up video (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.185). Play a small section of the video from 2:10 to 2:55.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Tell children to watch and listen carefully, and to see if any of their ideas were mentioned. Then play the video again and ask children to note down any new words they hear. Elicit these words and write them on the board.

Oxford task — guided: After watching, ask children the question again: What’s amazing about the sea?

Oxford task — guided: Teach the lesson’s vocabulary (see also Oxford Discover SB 3 p.176 ex.A). Drill the words and play a flashcard game. Quickly flash the card and ask children to say the word.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Write the question ‘What’s amazing about the sea?’ on the board, but mixed up. Challenge children to work out the correct order and to tell you the question. Give them one minute to confer in pairs. Take answers and write the question on the board.

Oxford pair/group task: Using the Think-Pair-Share technique, now ask children to come up with some ideas to answer the question, based on their existing knowledge of seas and oceans.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs to think of two categories for the new vocabulary. Ask them to write their two categories on a strip of paper. Monitor actively. Collect the strips of paper, put them into a bag, and shake. Invite one child from each pair to choose a strip of paper. If they choose their own, they must return it.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Draw a Venn diagram on the board. Talk with children about how it works: there are two categories – some items go in the first category, some items go in the second category, and some items belong in both categories so go in the middle where the circles overlap. Children should already be familiar with this method of presenting information.

Oxford source task: Put children into pairs to think of two categories for the new vocabulary. Ask them to write their two categories on a strip of paper. Monitor actively. Collect the strips of paper, put them into a bag, and shake. Invite one child from each pair to choose a strip of paper. If they choose their own, they must return it.

Oxford source task: Set a time limit for pairs to complete their Venn diagram on a whiteboard.

Oxford source task: Review and discuss each other’s Venn diagrams as a whole class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them choices for categories (e.g. colours, animals).

Oxford differentiation: More confident children can use a dictionary to add more words to their Venn diagram.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover SB 3 p.176 ex.B and WB 3 p.162.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned new vocabulary and watched an introductory video about our amazing seas and oceans.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 31, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Ocean wonder photo reveal

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What’s amazing about the sea?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Sea facts question carousel

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What’s amazing about the sea?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Ocean or land sorting challenge

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What’s amazing about the sea?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Amazing sea prediction cards

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What’s amazing about the sea?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 3 Amazing sea
Week 31, Lesson 1: What’s amazing about the sea?
Learning outcomes: 3.1b, 3.1c, 3.1d

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Amazing sea’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by identifying new vocabulary 
and interesting facts about the sea through teacher input and an introductory video.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
