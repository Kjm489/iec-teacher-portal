PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Tell children to stand up. Elicit the adjectives and ask them to do an action to match. For example, for ‘high’, children might stand on their toes and reach up to the ceiling.

Oxford opening: As you introduce the words, write them on the board.

Oxford opening: Then invite a confident child to call out a word for children to mime.

Oxford opening: Continue playing in small groups. Children take turns being the speaker.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: On the board, draw a stick person and a car. Ask: Which is quicker? Elicit or teach the comparison sentence: A car is quicker than a person. Write it on the board.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Make more comparison sentences using different adjectives to make sure that children grasp how they are formed.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Help children to categorize the adjectives that take an ‘–er’/‘–ier’ ending and those that are preceded by ‘more’ (e.g. ‘cheaper’ / ‘more expensive’ and ‘easier’/ ‘more difficult’).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Elicit the difference between ‘quicker’ (describes the noun) and ‘more quickly’ (describes the verb).

Oxford task — guided: To extend the class, you can also teach superlative sentences – what they mean and how they are formed: e.g. The car is the fastest. / The car travels the fastest.

Oxford task — guided: You can use exs.D–F in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.141 to support your grammar teaching.

Oxford task — guided: Explain to children that they are now going to apply this new grammar to the ‘Inventions’ unit. In small groups, they will compare two inventions: e.g. A person sews more slowly than a sewing machine. / A drone is the most exciting invention!

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain to children that they are now going to apply this new grammar to the ‘Inventions’ unit. In small groups, they will compare two inventions: e.g. A person sews more slowly than a sewing machine. / A drone is the most exciting invention!

Oxford pair/group task: Ask groups to decide on two inventions to compare, then write four or five sentences together, using comparative or superlative adjectives or adverbs. Allow them enough time to do this, monitoring actively to make sure everyone is trying and making a contribution.

Oxford pair/group task: Encourage children to swap their work with another group to check for spelling, accurate punctuation, and the accurate use of adjectives and/or adverbs.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask groups to decide on two inventions to compare, then write four or five sentences together, using comparative or superlative adjectives or adverbs. Allow them enough time to do this, monitoring actively to make sure everyone is trying and making a contribution.

Oxford source task: Encourage children to swap their work with another group to check for spelling, accurate punctuation, and the accurate use of adjectives and/or adverbs.

Oxford source task: Praise those children who are supporting each other in their groups.

Oxford source task: Invite each group to read aloud their favourite sentence. Write it on the board and elicit how it is formed, and if it models the use of a comparative/superlative adjective or adverb.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For less confident children, focus on comparative and superlative adjectives only. If/When they are feeling more confident, they can move on to adverbs. Provide additional grammar support to these children, and/or group them with more able children.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to write an example sentence for each grammatical form: a comparative adjective, a superlative adjective, a comparative adverb, and a superlative adverb. Fast finishers can make a poster explaining the difference between these forms.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–E in Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.128–129.

Oxford additional tasks: Choose two more inventions to compare.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to compare two items using comparative and superlative adjectives and/or adverbs. They have worked together to compare two inventions.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Invention comparison Venn

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Comparing inventions.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

YEAR 3 Inventions
Week 22, Lesson 5: Comparing inventions
Learning outcomes: 3.3d, 3.4c, 3.4f

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Inventions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and writing skills by making comparison sentences, understanding the difference between adjectives and adverbs.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
