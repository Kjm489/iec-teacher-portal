Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.1b, 3.1i, 3.2c, 3.2d. Lesson: Six simple machines. Unit: Inventions.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Show children your toy. Ask: How does this work?

Oxford opening: If it’s a seesaw, elicit that it moves up and down, and that things can travel from the lower end to the higher end, or vice versa. Elicit that not much effort is needed. (Adapt for the toy you have.) Say: Many playgrounds have simple machines like this. Do all playground rides work in the same way? Today, we’re going to find out about how things move!

Oxford opening: Tell children that today is quite a difficult lesson, so they will be working in the stretch zone. This will help to extend their understanding of how everyday machines work.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Using flashcards or realia, talk with children about six different ways that machines can work. See the fact sheet provided to support your teaching, or use Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.130–131. Go through each kind of machine one by one, showing the moving parts and eliciting as much knowledge as possible from children. Encourage them to think critically and logically about how these mechanisms might work before giving them the answers. Give them plenty of time to make suggestions and ask questions.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Remind the class that they can ask you questions if they don’t quite understand, such as Can you say that again, please? or I don’t quite understand. Can you help, please?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Praise children for their efforts in explaining the moving parts, even if they don’t get it quite right.

Oxford task — guided: See if children can give you more examples of these kinds of machine.

Oxford task — guided: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.128 exs.A–C, or play some flashcard games (e.g. listen and repeat, then ‘Which is missing?’).

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children to think about which machines they use every day, sometimes, and never. For instance, if they live in a flat and use the lift (elevator), they use a pulley – which needs a wheel and axle to function – every day. They note down their ideas in pairs.

Oxford pair/group task: To end the lesson, play a definition game. Describe how a machine works using simple terminology, or give an example, and children tell you what the machine is. Alternatively, say the machine and children work together to make a definition in small groups.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask children to think about which machines they use every day, sometimes, and never. For instance, if they live in a flat and use the lift (elevator), they use a pulley – which needs a wheel and axle to function – every day. They note down their ideas in pairs.

Oxford source task: Discuss as a class.

Oxford source task: To end the lesson, play a definition game. Describe how a machine works using simple terminology, or give an example, and children tell you what the machine is. Alternatively, say the machine and children work together to make a definition in small groups.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, focus on just three or four machines. Use realia and simple terminology to help them to understand the different mechanisms.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write some True/False sentences to challenge a partner: e.g. A stapler is an example of a wedge. (False – It’s a lever).

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 22, Lesson 2

Oxford differentiation: How machines work

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.116–117.

Oxford additional tasks: Draw a picture or diagram of one of the machines.

Oxford additional tasks: Count the number of machines in the classroom. You could make this a team game, with each team looking for a different kind of machine.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about six kinds of everyday machine and how they work, using their problem-solving skills.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 22, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — Six-machine picture hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Six simple machines.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Machine movement mime

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Six simple machines.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Machine sorting stations

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Six simple machines.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Build-a-machine diagram

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Six simple machines.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Simple machine explanation relay

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Six simple machines.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
