Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.1d, 3.1f, 3.2a. Lesson: How do inventions change our lives?. Unit: Inventions.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Write the word ‘invention’ on the board. In pairs or small groups, give children 2 minutes to make as many words as they can using the letters (e.g. ‘invent’, ‘invite’, ‘oven’, ‘vote’, ‘not’, ‘ten’, ‘in’). Review as a class and give a point for each correct answer. The pair or group with the most points wins!

Oxford opening: Elicit the meaning of ‘invention’ or offer a simple explanation – something new that someone has made, normally to make our lives better or easier.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children to look around the classroom. Ask: What inventions can you see? This can range from a doorknob or pen to an electronic whiteboard or tablet.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Encourage children to think about how these inventions have changed our lives. Consider how people completed tasks, did chores, and had fun before they were invented.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell the class they are going to watch a video about inventions or be shown some pictures. Ask them to predict what they are going to see. In pairs, they note down their ideas.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.127 ex.A. Play the video. Discuss as a class whether any of children’s predictions were correct.

Oxford task — guided: Then write the following words on the board and number them: ‘machine’, ‘mobile phone’, ‘telephone’, ‘water bottle’, ‘light bulb’, ‘modern invention’, ‘computer’, ‘inventor’, ‘glasses’, and ‘television’. Check children’s understanding of the words using flashcards and drill as a class. Encourage them to listen carefully to the pronunciation of each.

Oxford task — guided: Play the video again. Instruct children to note down the numbers of the items they see in the correct order. (All appear except for a light bulb and a television.)

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Tell the class they are going to watch a video about inventions or be shown some pictures. Ask them to predict what they are going to see. In pairs, they note down their ideas.

Oxford pair/group task: Form small groups. Tell children to choose one of the inventions, then discuss the following questions: What is it? What is good about it? What did people do / use before?

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Tell the class what your favourite invention is and why, using linking words like ‘because’.

Oxford source task: Form small groups. Tell children to choose one of the inventions, then discuss the following questions: What is it? What is good about it? What did people do / use before?

Oxford source task: Share ideas in a whole-class discussion.

Oxford source task: Make a KWL chart about ‘How do inventions change our lives?’.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Less confident children should be able to participate relatively easily in this vocabulary-focused lesson, as most words and inventions will be familiar to them. To support them, play more flashcard games in a small group.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to orally summarize the ideas in the video or the importance of each invention shown.

Oxford additional tasks: Draw a picture of a favourite invention and write a few sentences about how it improves our lives.

Oxford additional tasks: In small groups, give a short presentation about a favourite invention, arguing why it is the most important invention of all time.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the questions about the Big Question video in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.116.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have been introduced to the idea of inventions and how they help us.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 22, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Invention brainstorm race

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do inventions change our lives?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Then-and-now picture pairs

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do inventions change our lives?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Invention video evidence hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do inventions change our lives?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Classroom invention tour

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do inventions change our lives?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

YEAR 3 Inventions
Week 22, Lesson 1: How do inventions change our lives?
Learning outcomes: 3.1d, 3.1f, 3.2a

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Inventions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by listening to new topic vocabulary and discussing their ideas in small groups.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
