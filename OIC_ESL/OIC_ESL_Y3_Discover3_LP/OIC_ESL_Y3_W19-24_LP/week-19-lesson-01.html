Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.1a, 3.1e. Lesson: How do we measure time?. Unit: Measuring time.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to tell you what the time is. Hold up your toy clock showing the correct time. Quickly revise ‘hour’ times: one o’clock, five o’clock … Elicit/Pre-teach ‘a.m.’ and ‘p.m.’.

Oxford opening: Ask children if it is morning, afternoon, or night. Establish the difference (e.g. after midday = afternoon; after dark – depending on the time it gets dark in your country – = night).

Oxford opening: Instruct the class to stand in a line. If you say a time that is ‘a.m.’, they jump to the right; if you say a time that is ‘p.m.’, they jump to the left.

Oxford opening: e.g. Say: Four o’clock in the afternoon – children jump to the left because it’s ‘p.m.’.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: As this unit is all about time, it is important that children can tell the time. Draw a clock on the board or use a toy clock to recap telling the time.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: In pairs, ask children to make a list of all the different ways they know of measuring time (see also Week 5, Lesson 1).

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Take answers from pairs. When a child gives a correct answer (e.g. an egg timer or a stopwatch), show the realia.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Drill the words in a fun way. For instance, you could shout the words and children repeat them as a whisper. You could also ask (incorrectly): e.g. Is this a calendar? (while indicating to a clock). Children reply: e.g. No, it isn’t. It’s a clock.

Oxford task — guided: Play the Big Question video in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.67 to familiarize them with the topic.

Oxford task — guided: Show children your sundial. Elicit how it works.

Oxford task — guided: Put children into groups. Ask them if they think they can make their own sundial, and elicit what they are going to need. As they say what they need, show them the items.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, ask children to make a list of all the different ways they know of measuring time (see also Week 5, Lesson 1).

Oxford pair/group task: Take answers from pairs. When a child gives a correct answer (e.g. an egg timer or a stopwatch), show the realia.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into groups. Ask them if they think they can make their own sundial, and elicit what they are going to need. As they say what they need, show them the items.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Read the instructions from the additional information clearly. Children make the sundial in unison. Monitor actively.

Oxford source task: When everyone has finished, take children outside on the hour (o’clock). Set up their sundials and mark the time on the paper plate. If possible, return with children an hour later and note where the shadow has fallen.

Oxford source task: Tell children to draw a picture of both times.

Oxford source task: Encourage the class to reflect on how sundials work, helping them to understand that the Earth rotates on its axis (as it orbits the sun), so it appears that the sun is moving across the sky. This causes objects in its path of light to make shadows. This is covered in more depth later this week.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Form mixed-ability groups for the creative task so more confident children can support less confident children to understand and follow the instructions.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 19, Lesson 1

Oxford differentiation: How to make a sundial

Oxford differentiation: Listen carefully. First, let’s check you have everything you need. Have you got a paper plate? Have you got a kebab stick or something to go in the middle? Have you got your pens and a pencil? You will also need a ruler to measure.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to make a list of important times in their day.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a KWL chart on ‘How do we measure time?’.

Oxford additional tasks: Write up the instructions for making a sundial, based on children’s understanding.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have thought about how we measure time and revised vocabulary for telling the time. They have followed spoken instructions to make a sundial in small groups.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

YEAR 3 Measuring time
Week 19, Lesson 1: How do we measure time?

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Measuring time’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by understanding and following spoken instructions.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
