Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.2e, 3.2f, 3.3d. Lesson: I did, too!. Unit: Inventions.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Divide the classroom into six areas. Label each area ‘lever’, ‘wheel and axle’, ‘pulley’, ‘screw’, ‘ramp’, and ‘wedge’ respectively.

Oxford opening: Say the name of an invention and children decide what type of machine it is. Give them ten seconds to go to their chosen area.

Oxford opening: Award a point for each correct answer. Award a bonus point to a volunteer who can explain how the type of machine works in this invention.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Turn to ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.133. Play the song. Children follow in their books on the first play. Encourage them to join in on the second play.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit from children a positive sentence and a negative sentence on the topic of inventions. Give an example, if necessary: e.g. A hammer is a lever. A wedge isn’t a lever.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children you are going to say more sentences. Ask them to stand up. If the sentence is positive, they smile and reach up high to the ceiling; if the sentence is negative, they give you a sad face and cross their arms. You can adapt the actions, as you wish, to suit your class.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Children sit back down. Ask them to give you two more examples of levers (e.g. scissors and a stapler). Write the sentences on the board in speech bubbles. Then add too to the second sentence: e.g. ‘Scissors are a lever. A stapler is a lever, too.’

Oxford task — guided: Elicit what too means here – it expresses agreement. Then say: A lightbulb is a screw. Invite a child to make an agreement sentence using another example and too.

Oxford task — guided: Once the class has grasped the new language, write a sentence with either on the board: e.g. ‘A seesaw isn’t a pulley. A lightbulb isn’t, either.’ Elicit that the second sentence also shows agreement. Ask: Do we use ‘too’ in positive or negative sentences? (positive). Do we use ‘either’ in positive or negative sentences? (negative).

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: For grammar practice, turn to exs.E–F in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.133, or write the suggested exercises on the board. Children complete them in pairs. Review answers.

Oxford pair/group task: Form new pairs. Instruct them to make up a silly conversation using too and either. Give them an example: e.g. Today, I climbed a mountain before school. Elicit I did, too!

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: For grammar practice, turn to exs.E–F in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.133, or write the suggested exercises on the board. Children complete them in pairs. Review answers.

Oxford source task: Form new pairs. Instruct them to make up a silly conversation using too and either. Give them an example: e.g. Today, I climbed a mountain before school. Elicit I did, too!

Oxford source task: Children have fun engaging in their silly conversations. Encourage them to gently correct each other at the end of each sentence if they didn’t use the grammar correctly, and explain why.

Oxford source task: Invite volunteers to perform part of their conversation (one or two sentences) to the class. Peer-correct if necessary, then hold a whole-class vote on the silliest dialogue.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: You can spend more time on grammar practice with less confident children while more confident children begin the silly conversation activity. Pair up similar abilities so children can work at their own pace and stretch each other to understand the new grammar.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–D in Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.120–121.

Oxford additional tasks: Teach how we can also use so and neither to express agreement: e.g. A light bulb is a screw. So is a jar lid. / I like exciting inventions! So do I. / A tree isn’t an invention. Neither is a flower. / I didn’t play football today. Neither did I.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to express agreement using too and either in positive and negative sentences. They have practised using both in a silly conversation.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 23, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Agreement action game

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: I did, too!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — I did too dialogue

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: I did, too!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Neither and too match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: I did, too!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Simple-machine sentence test

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: I did, too!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Partner agreement survey

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: I did, too!.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

YEAR 3 Inventions
Week 23, Lesson 1: I did, too!
Learning outcomes: 3.2e, 3.2f, 3.3d

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Inventions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by learning new grammar to express agreement, then introduce it into conversation.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
