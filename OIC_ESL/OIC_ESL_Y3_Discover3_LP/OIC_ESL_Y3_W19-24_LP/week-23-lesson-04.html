Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.2b, 3.2e. Lesson: Do inventions make our lives better?. Unit: Inventions.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Show the picture of yourself (or of somebody else that children know) and say: This is [me] when I was [xx years old]. I’m different now. I used to have short hair and now I have long hair. I used to [sleep with a teddy bear], but now I don’t.

Oxford opening: Ask: Do I still [sleep with a teddy bear]? How do you know? Elicit used to. Establish that the verb describes a past habit or state that no longer happens or is no longer true.

Oxford opening: Encourage children to reflect on how they are different now to when they were aged five.

Oxford opening: Play a game. Sit in a circle and prompt each child to make a sentence about what they used to do: e.g. I used to drink milk from a bottle.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask the class: How did people use to communicate? (Acknowledge that we use use to not used to in question forms.) Children might suggest handwritten letters or smoke signals.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask: Do you think our lives are better with new inventions like mobile phones? Have a short discussion. Extend by asking children which inventions they like and why, using interesting adjectives (see Week 22, Lesson 5): e.g. I love my mobile phone because it’s useful and educational. I can find information on the internet.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Then ask children if all inventions are useful. Write ‘useful’ and ‘useless’ on the board. Ask: Which is full of something? Which is not full of (or without) something? How do we know? Focus on the suffixes ‘–less’ and ‘–ful’. Elicit any other words children know with these suffixes. To prompt them, write ‘care’, ‘fear’, and ‘harm’ on the board. In pairs, children work together to create the adjectives.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: To support teaching, you can also refer to ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.143.

Oxford task — guided: Put children into groups. Tell them you want to know what their favourite inventions are. Ask: How can we find out? Give groups time to discuss ideas.

Oxford task — guided: Elicit doing a survey in a whole-class feedback session. Talk to children about different types of survey question, e.g. Yes/No, multiple choice, ranking options, open questions.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Then ask children if all inventions are useful. Write ‘useful’ and ‘useless’ on the board. Ask: Which is full of something? Which is not full of (or without) something? How do we know? Focus on the suffixes ‘–less’ and ‘–ful’. Elicit any other words children know with these suffixes. To prompt them, write ‘care’, ‘fear’, and ‘harm’ on the board. In pairs, children work together to create the adjectives.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into groups. Tell them you want to know what their favourite inventions are. Ask: How can we find out? Give groups time to discuss ideas.

Oxford pair/group task: Instruct children to write three to five survey questions in their groups. They can write their final draft on a large sheet of paper.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Instruct children to write three to five survey questions in their groups. They can write their final draft on a large sheet of paper.

Oxford source task: Children walk around the classroom and answer the surveys (using tally marks, if possible).

Oxford source task: Look at each other’s survey results together as a class. Establish which invention (or type of invention) is Year 3’s favourite. Ask: How does this invention make your life better?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident children, make a whole-class survey guided by the teacher instead of a small-group one.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage fast finishers to research and make a list of more ‘–less’ and ‘–ful’ adjectives for their future reference.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.130.

Oxford additional tasks: Provide children with additional exercises on ‘–less’ and ‘–ful’ adjectives.

Oxford additional tasks: Write up the survey results. Children could also create a bar or pie chart to display the results in a visual way.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have practised using used to and ‘–less’ and ‘–ful’ adjectives. They have carried out a survey to find out Year 3’s favourite invention.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Helpful or harmful continuum

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Do inventions make our lives better?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Pros and cons debate

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Do inventions make our lives better?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Display two or three topic-related choices. Students vote individually, discuss evidence with a partner, and give a complete sentence explaining their choice.

ICQ: Do you vote before discussing? Must you give a reason?

CCQ: Which detail from the lesson supports your choice?

Assessment: A clear choice and a relevant explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
