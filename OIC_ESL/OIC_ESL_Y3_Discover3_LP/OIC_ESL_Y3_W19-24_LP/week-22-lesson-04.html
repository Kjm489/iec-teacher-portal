Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.2e, 3.3b, 3.3c, 3.3h. Lesson: Simple machines in our daily lives. Unit: Inventions.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children if they are enjoying the topic so far. Give them an opportunity to reflect on what they have learned about inventions. Ask everyone to close their eyes. Slowly and clearly, say a few of the activities you’ve done, some of the words children have learned, and some of the concepts.

Oxford opening: Tell children to open their eyes and to take a deep breath. Remind them that it’s OK if they don’t grasp all of the concepts straightaway.

Oxford opening: Invite volunteers to share something they have learned in the unit so far.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show the class pictures of the different machines and elicit their names. You could show children some of the machines from Oxford Discover WB 3 p.121, e.g. bike, car, light bulb, a seesaw.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: To extend, elicit how they work (see Week 22, Lesson 2), giving examples.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children they are going to read a short text about one of the machines. During the lesson, ask them to find something in the text they didn’t know before – this could be a fact or a new word.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Divide the class into five groups. Hand out a different text/machine to each group.

Oxford task — guided: Assign or ask each group to nominate a leader (a confident, or quiet but able child) who will guide their group through the reading and ensure everyone’s participation. Elicit that team leaders must be fair, be a good listener, and be able to recognize the strengths of their group members.

Oxford task — guided: In their groups, children read through the text together, discuss what they have learned, and try to remember the information. Encourage children to talk together about the best way to remember things in general, then how to remember this text in particular. They might suggest making notes, drawing pictures, reading out loud, repetition, focusing on key points/words only, testing each other, and making quizzes, for example.

Oxford task — guided: Allow children enough time to read the text, identify the main points, and learn them.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Divide the class into five groups. Hand out a different text/machine to each group.

Oxford pair/group task: Assign or ask each group to nominate a leader (a confident, or quiet but able child) who will guide their group through the reading and ensure everyone’s participation. Elicit that team leaders must be fair, be a good listener, and be able to recognize the strengths of their group members.

Oxford pair/group task: In their groups, children read through the text together, discuss what they have learned, and try to remember the information. Encourage children to talk together about the best way to remember things in general, then how to remember this text in particular. They might suggest making notes, drawing pictures, reading out loud, repetition, focusing on key points/words only, testing each other, and making quizzes, for example.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Remove the texts. Regroup children so that there is a child from each group. In turn, they now share what they have understood from their text with each other.

Oxford source task: Discuss the success of the team activity as a class. Help children to understand that by trying to explain new concepts in their own words to their classmates and by listening to others, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

Oxford source task: Turn to ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.132, or write everyday examples of each machine on the board. Make a table and challenge children to classify the items by machine type.

Oxford source task: Review answers as a class. Invite children to share their newly learned fact or word.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Allow less confident children to work in pairs or use prompt cards when sharing their information.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children to explain different machines to their classmates.

Oxford additional tasks: Make a list of the machines children:

Oxford additional tasks: see on their way to school

Oxford additional tasks: use before 10 a.m. or after 3 p.m. every day

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Working together in small groups, children have completed a jigsaw reading about everyday machines. They have shared information with each other.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Everyday machine safari

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Simple machines in our daily lives.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Kitchen machine detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Simple machines in our daily lives.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Inventions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by doing a jigsaw reading together and sharing information.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
