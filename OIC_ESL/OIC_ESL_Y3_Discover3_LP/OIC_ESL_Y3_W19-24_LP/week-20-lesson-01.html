PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Take children to an open space. Play ‘What’s the time, Mr Wolf?’. You are the wolf and the children are sheep. They ask you (in chorus): What’s the time, Mr Wolf? You say the time: e.g. It’s three o’clock. Children take three steps towards you. This continues until you say: It’s lunchtime and time to eat you! You have to try to ‘catch’ (gently tap on the back) a child while children try to return to the designated ‘safe area’ where they started. The objective of the game is to try to tap the ‘wolf’ on the back without being ‘caught’ by the wolf first. Once children grasp how to play, invite a confident child to be the wolf.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Revise how to say different times, using a clock to support teaching. Say a time and children write it on their whiteboards. Ask them to either draw the clock hands, write the numerals, or, for a challenge, write the words in full (e.g. three forty-five or half past nine).

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit/Pre-teach the words ‘midday’, ‘midnight’, and ‘noon’, ensuring that children understand the similarities and differences between them.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Teach the grammar point from Oxford Discover SB 3 p.73 ex.F. There are further explanations in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.63 ex.A. On the board, write two sentences – one about the time now, and the other saying what the time will be in a few hours using will. Say: It’s ten o’clock in the morning. In two hours, it will be midday. Elicit from children which sentence is talking about right now, and which is talking about the future. Ask: Can you see which word we use for the future?

Oxford task — guided: Help children to understand that we use will + the base form of the verb to talk about future facts: e.g. The sun will rise at 5 a.m. We will eat dinner at half past six. Encourage them to make a few sentences in pairs first, then to share answers as a class. Turn to ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.73. Listen to and sing the song together. Identify all the future facts using will.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Help children to understand that we use will + the base form of the verb to talk about future facts: e.g. The sun will rise at 5 a.m. We will eat dinner at half past six. Encourage them to make a few sentences in pairs first, then to share answers as a class. Turn to ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.73. Listen to and sing the song together. Identify all the future facts using will.

Oxford pair/group task: Form small groups of four. Tell children they are going to work together to create a game. Hand out a blank 4 x 4 grid to each group. Instruct them to decide on a range of different times and to write them in some of the boxes. They will also need to add start and finish points, indicate the direction that players must follow on the board, and embellish some of the squares with phrases like: ‘It’s midnight. Miss a turn. / Go forward three spaces. / Go back to the start.’ Encourage children to be as creative as they like!

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Form small groups of four. Tell children they are going to work together to create a game. Hand out a blank 4 x 4 grid to each group. Instruct them to decide on a range of different times and to write them in some of the boxes. They will also need to add start and finish points, indicate the direction that players must follow on the board, and embellish some of the squares with phrases like: ‘It’s midnight. Miss a turn. / Go forward three spaces. / Go back to the start.’ Encourage children to be as creative as they like!

Oxford source task: Explain the game instructions. In turn, players throw the dice and land on a time. First, they say the time: e.g. It’s three forty-five / quarter to four. Then they throw the dice again to get another number (e.g. 3) and make a sentence using that number of hours: e.g. In three hours’ time, it will be six forty-five / quarter to seven. If both the sentence and the calculation are correct, they stay on the square. If not, they go back. Remind children that this game is designed to stretch their thinking and it might feel challenging to begin with.

Oxford source task: Children play their game. Monitor discreetly, but provide support where necessary.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Vary the difficulty level of the game by using different time formats (numerals, words, analogue, and digital formats, a.m./p.m.).

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 20, Lesson 1

Oxford differentiation: An example board game

Oxford differentiation: [Adapt the difficulty level to suit class ability.]

Oxford additional tasks: Groups swap games and play with different classmates.

Oxford additional tasks: Write down the instructions of the game using modals (e.g. can/can’t/must).

Oxford additional tasks: Complete ex.B in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.63 ex.B.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: In a game, children have practised telling the time and making sentences with will to talk about future facts.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 20, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Mr Wolf time chase

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Noon here, night there.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Noon-midnight sorting

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Noon here, night there.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Clock-dice board game

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Noon here, night there.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Time phrase dominoes

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Noon here, night there.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Future fact dialogue

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Noon here, night there.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Noon here night there interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Noon here, night there.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Measuring time’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by talking about the future 
in a game.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
