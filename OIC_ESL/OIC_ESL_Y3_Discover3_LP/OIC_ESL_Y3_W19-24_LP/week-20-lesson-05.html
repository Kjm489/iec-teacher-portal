Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.3a, 3.4c, 3.4e, 3.4f. Lesson: A trip to another time zone. Unit: Measuring time.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Revise vocabulary learned in the ‘Measuring time’ unit. Give a definition orally and ask children to say the word: e.g. This is the shortest day of the year. (winter solstice).

Oxford opening: Put children into groups of three. Ask them to write three definitions in their own words.

Oxford opening: Bring the class back together, or pair up groups. Encourage children to take turns reading out their definitions to their classmates. See if anyone knows the word.

Oxford opening: Explain that by thinking again about new words and their meanings, children are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.75 to teach children how to use the definite article ‘the’ before some countries.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Using a globe or atlas to generate interest, ask children which country they would like to visit. Alternatively, spin the globe and randomly assign children a country. For best results, children should have a country with at least a six-hour time difference from their own.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Children imagine they are going to their chosen or assigned country. Encourage them to think about where the country is, how they will get there, how long it will take, and what the time difference is between this country and their home country. They can be creative with supporting details, but must retain factual (geographical and time zone-related) information.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Encourage children to think about the kind of activities that other children might do in that country, and to incorporate these ideas into their writing.

Oxford task — guided: Tell them to organize their writing as a report (see Week 20, Lesson 4 to recap the features of a report). Elicit that it will need an interesting title, (a) topic sentence(s), and supporting sentences.

Oxford task — guided: Remind children to use will when writing about future facts and/or ideas.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask them to check their own work for correct spelling and punctuation. They then swap their work with a partner who will complete an additional check (of spelling, punctuation, and correct use of will).

Oxford pair/group task: If there is time, write a final draft in neat handwriting for display.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: As children write their reports (a paragraph or two – quality is more important than quantity!), monitor actively and talk to children about their ideas. Praise them for their effort and perseverance when trying to include difficult grammar and topic vocabulary.

Oxford source task: Ask them to check their own work for correct spelling and punctuation. They then swap their work with a partner who will complete an additional check (of spelling, punctuation, and correct use of will).

Oxford source task: If there is time, write a final draft in neat handwriting for display.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with questions to answer in their writing.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more confident children to choose a place that they don’t know anything about.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.66.

Oxford additional tasks: Illustrate the final draft of children’s written work.

Oxford additional tasks: Use the internet to research the destination further and what activities children do there.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written (a) paragraph(s) about a trip to a different time zone, incorporating the grammar and topic vocabulary they have learned in the ‘Measuring time’ unit.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 20, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — Time-zone itinerary

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A trip to another time zone.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Travel agent interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A trip to another time zone.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — World clock relay

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A trip to another time zone.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Jet lag choices

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A trip to another time zone.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Display two or three topic-related choices. Students vote individually, discuss evidence with a partner, and give a complete sentence explaining their choice.

ICQ: Do you vote before discussing? Must you give a reason?

CCQ: Which detail from the lesson supports your choice?

Assessment: A clear choice and a relevant explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Travel diary timeline

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A trip to another time zone.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
