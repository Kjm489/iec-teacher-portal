Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): Unit summary. Lesson: Review the unit and choose a focus. Unit: Measuring time.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

Source alignment note: Oxford supplies one shared weekly review plan. The five IEC lesson divisions are adaptations, not separate Oxford lesson titles.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Measuring time’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford opening: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford opening: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford pair/group task: Children make a mobile of the planets in small groups. They need to plan the activity first. Ask them to consider the following questions: How many planets are there? What colour are they? Where are they in relation to Earth? Are you going to colour them or use coloured paper? Name them. Hand out paper for children to mind map their ideas. They may need to refer to reference books or the internet. They can also refer to the illustration of the planets in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.71 and the article in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.61. They also need to consider what they are going to hang the planets from. This could be a twig, for example. Children could also try to make the mobile planets from degradable materials (not plastic). Children make their planets mobile and present it to the class, including any difficulties they had making it, and how they resolved the issues.

Oxford pair/group task: Children create a challenge for their group. They think of ten things to do, such as write their name ten times, say the alphabet twice, run around the playground. Encourage them to think creatively so that groups aren’t all doing the same things. You could ask them to have two challenges at their desk, two outside, and so on. Hand out paper for each group to mind map their ideas and then they choose the top ten to do. They need to create a table with their names, the activities, estimates, and actual times included. They estimate how long each activity will take and then they time themselves doing it. Children then make some conclusions (e.g. ‘X took longer’ / ‘Y was faster because …’). When children write up the challenge, encourage them to illustrate it. Display the work either on the wall or in a class book.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For children requiring extra support, provide word banks, gapped texts, and/or sentence starters.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to think about how they will present their work effectively at the end of the week. What language can they use (including new grammatical forms learned)? What do they need to do to maintain the audience’s interest?

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-life context.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 21, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Measuring-time vocabulary retrieval

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Review the unit and choose a focus.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — World-time checkpoint

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Review the unit and choose a focus.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Time-science quiz exchange

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Review the unit and choose a focus.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
