Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.2b, 3.2e, 3.2f. Lesson: In the next ten minutes …. Unit: Measuring time.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children quizzically: What will happen in the next 10 minutes? Elicit some ideas: e.g. We will open our books. Remind children to use will to talk about future facts – they need to practise this new grammar over and over in order to remember and master it.

Oxford opening: Go over the grammar on the board if children need extra practice. Refer to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.73 ex.F and Oxford Discover WB 3 p.63 ex.A. You can also review the items, whose visuals may help provide more context, in ex.B on the same page

Oxford opening: Tell the class that three children will make some changes to the classroom, such as move some books, swap places with another child. Encourage them to do this quietly! Then ask a confident child to close their eyes and to say what they think they will see when they open their eyes: e.g. María-Luz will change places with Rafael.

Oxford opening: Play this a few times, ensuring that everyone uses will correctly.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children they are going to practise using will in a short play about the future. They can be as creative as they like with their ideas, as long as they try to incorporate the new grammar and vocabulary from the unit as much as possible.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Specifically, the short play must include three to five sentences with will and a time piece (e.g. a clock, a watch, an egg timer, a stopwatch, a sundial).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Encourage children to choose a main character, an object, and/or place to use in their play. Hand out prompt cards to inspire their thinking.

Oxford task — guided: Allow children to form their own small groups (between three and five children), or make your own. They will need enough time in the lesson to plan, practise their ideas, and rehearse their play.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Allow children to form their own small groups (between three and five children), or make your own. They will need enough time in the lesson to plan, practise their ideas, and rehearse their play.

Oxford pair/group task: At the end of the lesson, invite groups to perform their short play to the rest of the class. Challenge watching groups to identify the main character, the object, and/or the place.

Oxford pair/group task: Write some simple success criteria on the board for watching groups to use to evaluate their classmates’ performances. Encourage children to mention one or two aspects they really liked, and one or two aspects that could be improved (in terms of the performance, accuracy of language use, correct use of will, range of language used, pronunciation, and creative ideas).

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: At the end of the lesson, invite groups to perform their short play to the rest of the class. Challenge watching groups to identify the main character, the object, and/or the place.

Oxford source task: Write some simple success criteria on the board for watching groups to use to evaluate their classmates’ performances. Encourage children to mention one or two aspects they really liked, and one or two aspects that could be improved (in terms of the performance, accuracy of language use, correct use of will, range of language used, pronunciation, and creative ideas).

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Form mixed-ability groups so peer-support can be provided. All children should have a speaking role, although the length of their contribution can be varied according to their ability.

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident children, write useful sentence starters and key vocabulary on the board. They can write them on some prompt cards to use in their performance.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to include a ‘twist’ (an unexpected event) in their story.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 20, Lesson 3

Oxford additional tasks: Write a summary or script of the short play.

Oxford additional tasks: Convert the short play into the format of a graphic novel or cartoon.

Oxford additional tasks: Think about the future and what life will be like in ten years’ time. Encourage children to share their ideas: e.g. I will be at university. I will live in a different city.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have created and performed a short play about the future, practising will to express future facts or plans.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Ten-minute prediction wall

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: In the next ten minutes ….

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 3 Measuring time
Week 20, Lesson 3: In the next ten minutes …
Learning outcomes: 3.2b, 3.2e, 3.2f

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Measuring time’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by creating and performing a short play about the future, using recently learned grammatical forms and topic vocabulary.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
