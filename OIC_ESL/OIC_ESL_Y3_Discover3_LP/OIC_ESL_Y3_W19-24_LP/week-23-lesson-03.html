PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Encourage children to tell you a key word learned in the previous lesson (Week 23, Lesson 2). Use the flashcards to remind them, if necessary. Explain to the class that by thinking again about new vocabulary, they are building their long-term memory of this learning. It will also help them in today’s reading lesson!

Oxford opening: Show children each flashcard in a random order. Challenge them to write the word on their whiteboards. When everyone has written the word, count to three and ask everyone to show you their board. If a child got a word or spelling wrong, encourage them to think about where they went wrong so they can self-correct. Don’t focus on who got the answer wrong, but move on to the next word.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: On the board, write ‘summarize’ and ‘summary’ and elicit what they mean. Establish that the former is a verb (when you briefly give the main points of a text in your own words) and the latter is a noun (the short oral or written description).

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children they are going to practise this skill. First, they will read a short paragraph and underline/highlight/note down the important words or ideas.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Turn to ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.137, or use the text provided. Alternatively, you can provide your own text for this exercise.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Independently or in pairs, ask children to write one sentence as a summary.

Oxford task — guided: Hold a whole-class feedback session. Check if there are any subject/verb agreement problems (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.135).

Oxford task — guided: Children compare their summaries in small groups. Ask them to agree on the best one. Elicit when a summary might be useful (e.g. when choosing a book or a film). Tell the class they are now going to read a short story about how something was invented. Ask: Do you think inventors plan their inventions, or do they happen by chance?

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Independently or in pairs, ask children to write one sentence as a summary.

Oxford pair/group task: Children compare their summaries in small groups. Ask them to agree on the best one. Elicit when a summary might be useful (e.g. when choosing a book or a film). Tell the class they are now going to read a short story about how something was invented. Ask: Do you think inventors plan their inventions, or do they happen by chance?

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into small groups. Say: A famous filmmaker wants to make a film about an invention, but she is very busy. She needs a summary of a story about an invention. Set groups the task of summarizing the story together, using the key words they already know to help them. Encourage groups to decide on the best strategy for doing this.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Show children the glasses and take ideas about how they were invented.

Oxford source task: Read the story as a class or independently (see Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.138–139 or the text provided). Ask: What did Clara/George invent?

Oxford source task: Put children into small groups. Say: A famous filmmaker wants to make a film about an invention, but she is very busy. She needs a summary of a story about an invention. Set groups the task of summarizing the story together, using the key words they already know to help them. Encourage groups to decide on the best strategy for doing this.

Oxford source task: Tell groups to swap their summaries with another group to check for subject/verb agreement, spelling, and punctuation.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Form mixed-ability groups for the summary activity so peer-support can be provided. Give less confident children one of the key words to include in a sentence.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to write an even shorter summary on a sticky note – this will help to make their writing more concise.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.140.

Oxford additional tasks: Identify parts of speech in the story (verbs, nouns, adjectives, adverbs).

Oxford additional tasks: Read the story in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.138. Children highlight the important words and ideas. They read the Think boxes and then summarize the story in up to four sentences.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have worked together to summarize a story about the origins of an invention.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Invention story detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How inventions happen (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Inventions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and writing skills by learning how to summarize a text.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
