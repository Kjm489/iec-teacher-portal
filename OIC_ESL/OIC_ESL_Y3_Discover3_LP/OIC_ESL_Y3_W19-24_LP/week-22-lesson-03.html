Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.3a, 3.3b, 3.3h. Lesson: Inventions in the 19th and 20th centuries. Unit: Inventions.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask the class: What century are we in? Elicit the ‘21st century’. Explain how this century refers to years beginning with ‘20’ (e.g. 2021). The ‘19th century’ therefore refers to years beginning with ‘18’ – the 1800s and the 19th century are the same thing.

Oxford opening: Practise naming different centuries using ordinal numbers. You might need to pre-teach ordinal numbers first in order to do this.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Say: The 19th and 20th centuries were very busy for inventors. See if children know of anything that was invented in these centuries. Guide the conversation so that they focus on items that come up in the reading text.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain to the class that they are going to read a short text about 19th- and 20th-century inventions. Each century has three inventions – ask children to predict what they are in pairs and explain their reasoning.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Turn to the paragraph in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.129, or use the text provided. Ask children to scan the text for the invention names. Were any predictions correct?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: On the board, write ‘classify’ and ‘categorize’. Elicit that the words mean putting things that are similar into groups. They are synonyms and can be used interchangeably.

Oxford task — guided: Tell children to read the text again independently and to complete a table, classifying and categorizing inventions in the 19th century (1800s) and 20th century (1900s).

Oxford task — guided: Ask: Is there any other way we could classify these inventions? (e.g. where the invention is used, type of invention, material, nationality of inventor).

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain to the class that they are going to read a short text about 19th- and 20th-century inventions. Each century has three inventions – ask children to predict what they are in pairs and explain their reasoning.

Oxford pair/group task: On the board, write ‘classify’ and ‘categorize’. Elicit that the words mean putting things that are similar into groups. They are synonyms and can be used interchangeably.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into groups. Using a selection of resources (e.g. the internet, infographics, reference books, and even by asking adults), set them the task of researching more inventions to classify (in any way that they wish). They don’t need to write sentences, but they should make lists with headings indicating the classification.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Encourage children to imagine life without these inventions, so that they appreciate how they make our lives easier. Elicit sentences including ‘before’ and ‘after’ (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.143).

Oxford source task: Put children into groups. Using a selection of resources (e.g. the internet, infographics, reference books, and even by asking adults), set them the task of researching more inventions to classify (in any way that they wish). They don’t need to write sentences, but they should make lists with headings indicating the classification.

Oxford source task: Review findings as a class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident children in the first classification activity, add the number of letters each word has, or the first letter of the word, to the table.

Oxford differentiation: Form mixed-ability groups for the research task so peer-support can be provided.

Oxford additional tasks: Read the text in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.118 and complete the exercises on p.119.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to think about which machines (from Week 22, Lesson 2) might be found in the inventions (e.g. a sewing machine has a pulley – a wheel and axle). For this activity, it would be helpful for children to refer to pictures or simple diagrams.

Oxford additional tasks: Ask children to choose an invention that they use regularly (e.g. headphones) and research when it was first invented.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read about 19th- and 20th-century inventions, and learned how to classify and categorize information. They have practised conducting research in small groups.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 22, LESSON 3

IEC supplemental game — Invention history timeline

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Inventions in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Inventor fact detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Inventions in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Old versus new object match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Inventions in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

YEAR 3 Inventions
Week 22, Lesson 3: Inventions in the 19th and 20th centuries
Learning outcomes: 3.3a, 3.3b, 3.3h 

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Inventions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by learning how to classify 
and categorize.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
