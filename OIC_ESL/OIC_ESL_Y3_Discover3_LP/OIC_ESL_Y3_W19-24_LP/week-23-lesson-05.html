1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Tell children that today they are going to invent and design a machine. They need to think about what it will do. Give them some silly ideas to inspire their thinking, such as a reusable water bottle that also makes ice cream, or a pen that does their homework.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Remind children of the characteristics of inventions – they fulfil a purpose or respond to a need, they often happen by chance, and aim to make an aspect of our lives easier, faster, or more fun. You may like to show them some pictures to recap some of the types of inventions they have studied, e.g. in ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.128, in ex.C on p.132, or on pp.130–131.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Give children enough time to think about their design. If they can be stretched, encourage them to think about the type of machine (e.g. a lever, a pulley – see Week 22). Remind them that this is challenging and they are in the stretch zone.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask them what noises their machine will make. Help them to write the noises phonetically (e.g. ‘chooka chooka’, ‘swoosh’, ‘ker-ching!’).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Monitor actively and praise children for their creative ideas – you might wish to target children who struggle with English, but excel at arty and imaginative tasks. This will help to boost their confidence.

Oxford pair/group task: When children finish their first draft, remind them to check their work for correct spelling and punctuation, and accurate use of language.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text for their description.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more confident children to add as much detail as possible to their invention, including what it is made of and who it would be most useful to.

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 23, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — My invention problem hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: My invention.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Invention sketch lab

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: My invention.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Prototype role-play

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: My invention.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Inventions’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by designing and writing about their own invention.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
