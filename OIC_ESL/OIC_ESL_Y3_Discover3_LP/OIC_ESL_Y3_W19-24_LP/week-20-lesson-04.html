Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.3a, 3.3f, 3.3g. Lesson: The longest and shortest days. Unit: Measuring time.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Using a globe or atlas, ask children if they know what ‘hemisphere’ means. Show them the northern and southern hemispheres, and establish which hemisphere your country is in.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children if they know when the longest and shortest days are in your country. Pre-teach ‘winter solstice’ and ‘summer solstice’. Don’t go into too much detail – children will be uncovering more information about the solstices during the rest of the lesson.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children they are going to read a report. First, ask them to think of five questions about solstices that they want to find out the answer to in pairs. Write question words on the board (e.g. ‘Why?’/‘When?’/‘Where?’/‘What?’/‘How?’) to encourage their thinking.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.84 or hand out the reading text provided.

Oxford task — guided: First, ask children to read the text in pairs to see if they can find the answers to their questions. Review as a class. See who had the most questions answered.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Tell children they are going to read a report. First, ask them to think of five questions about solstices that they want to find out the answer to in pairs. Write question words on the board (e.g. ‘Why?’/‘When?’/‘Where?’/‘What?’/‘How?’) to encourage their thinking.

Oxford pair/group task: First, ask children to read the text in pairs to see if they can find the answers to their questions. Review as a class. See who had the most questions answered.

Oxford pair/group task: Reinforce children’s understanding of the topic by answering the comprehension questions in pairs or small groups.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Read the text again more carefully, as a class or independently. Elicit the structure of the report by asking children to identify the title, the topic sentences, and the supporting sentences.

Oxford source task: Reinforce children’s understanding of the topic by answering the comprehension questions in pairs or small groups.

Oxford source task: Check answers as a class.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident children, look at the reading text in an adult-led small group. Spend more time teaching the hemispheres and explaining the solstices using props (e.g. a globe, a tennis ball for the sun, a blue or green ball for planet Earth).

Oxford differentiation: Prompt more confident children to summarize what they have learned from the text in their own words.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.75.

Oxford additional tasks: Research online or look in reference books to find the answers to unanswered questions.

Oxford additional tasks: Draw a picture of the summer/winter solstices with the Earth and the sun in the correct positions.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about the summer and winter solstices. They have predicted the content of a reading text by thinking of questions they hope to learn the answer to, understood the layout of a report, and used this layout to identify main information.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Measuring time’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by understanding the layout of a report, then reading a report about the winter and summer solstices.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
