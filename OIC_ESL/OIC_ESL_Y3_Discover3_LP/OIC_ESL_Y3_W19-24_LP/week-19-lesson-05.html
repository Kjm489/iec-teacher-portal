Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.2a, 3.2b. Lesson: Times around the world. Unit: Measuring time.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Tell children they are going to play a game to recap their learning this week. Draw a 3x3 grid on the board, labelling each square 1 to 9. Divide the class into two teams and allocate each team a special symbol (e.g. a star, a circle). In turn, teams choose a number and you read the corresponding question. If they get it right, their symbol goes on the number. The objective is to get three in a row – horizontally, diagonally, or vertically.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain to children that there are 24 time zones in the world. Show them the time zone map in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.71. You can point to your location on the map and the time zone your location is in.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Using the globe (and lamp, if necessary), find a country opposite yours and ask: What are children in x country doing now? Elicit why. You can check the exact time on the internet to confirm answers.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Find an interactive world clocks website. Invite volunteers to suggest what other children are doing in different parts of the world at this time.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: e.g. It’s 3 p.m. here in Ho Chi Minh City. We’re studying English. However, in Mexico City it’s 3 a.m. Children are still sleeping! It’s 9 a.m. in London and children are starting school.

Oxford task — guided: Tell children they are going to make a short presentation about what children around the world are doing at different times. They can work individually, in pairs, or in small groups.

Oxford task — guided: Instruct them to choose four different places – one of which should be their own town/country – or allocate places. They will then use the internet to find out what the time is in the different places and suggest what children will be doing at that time.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Tell children they are going to make a short presentation about what children around the world are doing at different times. They can work individually, in pairs, or in small groups.

Oxford pair/group task: Allow children enough time to prepare, rehearse, and present their research to the rest of the class, or to other pairs or small groups.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Remind them to use the present continuous tense. Recap how it is formed: subject + present tense of the verb to be + present participle (base form of verb + ‘–ing’):

Oxford source task: e.g. Children are playing in the playground at lunchtime. It is 12.30 p.m.

Oxford source task: Allow children enough time to prepare, rehearse, and present their research to the rest of the class, or to other pairs or small groups.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Pair up less confident children with more confident children so peer-support can be provided. All children should have a speaking role in the presentation.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more confident children to choose lesser-known places, perhaps in the countryside instead of in a big city, so they can think more deeply about the kinds of activities that take place in those places.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 19, Lesson 5

Oxford differentiation: Suggested trivia questions

Oxford additional tasks: If possible, use the internet to explore satellite imagery and road views of the different places researched.

Oxford additional tasks: Write and illustrate the presentation text as a final draft.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have continued to explore time zones and considered what other children are doing around the world at different times, using the present continuous tense to express their ideas.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.
