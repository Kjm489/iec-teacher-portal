PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play a warm-up game to practise some of the vocabulary introduced this week.

Oxford opening: For instance, to play ‘Snowman’, draw a blank line (dash) for each letter in the word (e.g. four dashes for ‘axis’). Children have to guess the letters. If correct, you write the letter. If not, you start drawing a snowman in nine steps (three circles for the snowman, two sticks for its arms, then a hat, two eyes, and a line for its mouth). These correspond to each wrong guess.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Teach children about the planets and about time zones. Elicit what they already know – they might recall some of their learning from Week 5, Lesson 1. You can turn this into a team game and write children’s suggestions on the board.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Using a model or cut-outs of the planets, elicit or show the class where they are positioned in the solar system. For example, say: This is Earth – where we live. It’s the third planet from the sun. It takes 23 hours and 56 minutes to spin around on its axis. That’s the length of one day on Earth. This is Jupiter. It’s further from the sun than Earth and much bigger than Earth. But it spins fast – so a day on Jupiter is only 9 hours and 56 minutes! It takes 12 years for Jupiter to orbit the sun. Continue with the other planets.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Now with a lamp and a globe, show children how the Earth spins on its axis, explaining how it can’t be daytime everywhere at the same time.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Now turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.71, or hand out the reading text provided. The texts are slightly different, but the steps are the same.

Oxford task — guided: Pair up children (AB) – Child A reads the paragraph about the planets and Child B reads the paragraph about time zones. Allow them enough time to absorb and understand what they have read. Then tell them to close their books or turn their handouts face-down.

Oxford task — guided: Encourage children to tell their partner as much as they can remember in turn.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Teach children about the planets and about time zones. Elicit what they already know – they might recall some of their learning from Week 5, Lesson 1. You can turn this into a team game and write children’s suggestions on the board.

Oxford pair/group task: Pair up children (AB) – Child A reads the paragraph about the planets and Child B reads the paragraph about time zones. Allow them enough time to absorb and understand what they have read. Then tell them to close their books or turn their handouts face-down.

Oxford pair/group task: Take a look at ‘Additional information’ and write questions 1 to 4 on the board. Challenge children to answer them in pairs. Make available reference books, the internet, and a time zone map to support them. This activity will stretch children and help to extend their understanding of the topic.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Check children’s understanding as a class, eliciting the main points in both texts.

Oxford source task: Take a look at ‘Additional information’ and write questions 1 to 4 on the board. Challenge children to answer them in pairs. Make available reference books, the internet, and a time zone map to support them. This activity will stretch children and help to extend their understanding of the topic.

Oxford source task: Hold a whole-class discussion. Ask children how they felt about today’s lesson: Was it easy or difficult? Why do you think that? How are you feeling now?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For the reading activity, support less able children in a small group guided by an adult. Spend more time explaining the Earth’s orbit, rather than focusing on other planets.

Oxford differentiation: Pair up similar abilities in the reading activity so children can work at their own pace and stretch each other’s thinking.

Oxford additional tasks: Read the text in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.61 and complete exs.A–D on p.62.

Oxford additional tasks: Draw and label the planets.

Oxford additional tasks: Use suitable websites to find out more facts about the planets.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about or revised the planets and time zones in a reading comprehension activity.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Measuring time’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and reading skills by identifying and summarizing main points in a reading text.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
