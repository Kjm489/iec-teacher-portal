Measurable Learning Objectives

Original Year 3 outcome(s): 3.2a, 3.2c. Lesson: How long does it take?. Unit: Measuring time.

Success criteria: Explain, demonstrate or apply the original lesson focus; communicate a complete response; and produce independent evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Instruct children to draw a clock face on their whiteboards.

Oxford opening: Say a time and see if children can draw the hands in the correct position. Give them enough time to think and draw. When everyone is ready, they show their boards at the same time. You also show yours. Don’t comment on who got it right or wrong, as children can see for themselves. Do this a few more times.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children to tell you any words they know connected to the topic of time. Elicit/Pre-teach the vocabulary. Play a definition game: you say a definition of the word – e.g. ‘There are 60 of these in one minute.’ (seconds) – and children give the answer.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Children play the same game in pairs. See also exs.A–C in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.68.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children how long they think it takes to say a phrase of your choice: e.g. There are 32 children in this class. They note their guesses on their whiteboards. Then ask a child to time you or a confident child saying the phrase. Ask: Who was closest with their guess?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask children what they can do in one minute to help them to understand how long a minute really is. Write their ideas on the board. Then tell them to sit comfortably (possibly on the floor/cross-legged), close their eyes, and sit still for one minute. Ask: What did you notice? (Probably that one minute is longer than they thought!)

Oxford task — guided: Tell children they are going to design a ‘How long does it take?’ survey. They will think of some questions for other classmates to predict the answers to, before testing them out to find the answers. Suggested questions: How long does it take to write your full name? How long does it take to do ten star jumps? How long does it take to read a page of your book?

Oxford task — guided: In small groups, children decide on their five questions. Remind them that they must be able to test the answers in class!

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children play the same game in pairs. See also exs.A–C in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.68.

Oxford pair/group task: In small groups, children decide on their five questions. Remind them that they must be able to test the answers in class!

Oxford pair/group task: Monitor actively and praise children for their teamwork.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Monitor actively and praise children for their teamwork.

Oxford source task: Children ask another group their questions and note down their classmates’ predictions.

Oxford source task: Allow enough time for children to time each other doing the different activities.

Oxford source task: Bring the class back together to discuss children’s findings. How accurate were their predictions?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Less able children or groups can ask fewer than five questions, while more confident children can ask more than five.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to consider what might affect how long it takes to do something.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete ex.A in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.59. Encourage children to time themselves doing each activity.

Oxford additional tasks: Compare how long children take to do something with a friend. Ask them to write down their results: e.g. I take five minutes to do x, but my friend is faster. She takes three minutes.

Oxford additional tasks: Make a plan of the school day, noting different times / the length each activity takes.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have asked and answered questions about how long different activities take to complete. They have made sensible predictions and tested their assumptions.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 19, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — How long prediction lab

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How long does it take?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Duration match-up

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How long does it take?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Minute challenge

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How long does it take?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Duration interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How long does it take?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

YEAR 3 Measuring time
Week 19, Lesson 2: How long does it take?
Learning outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2c

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Measuring time’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by carrying out a class survey.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
