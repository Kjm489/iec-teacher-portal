PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children what a ‘warm-up’ is. Elicit that it is a physical activity to get our muscles warmed up, but it is also a good way to release some energy and focus our attention before sitting down for a long time.

Oxford opening: Do a quick warm-up exercise – a series of jumps, runs on the spot, and hops on each leg, or adapt to suit the needs of your class. Encourage children to recommend actions.

Oxford opening: Start quite quickly, but gradually slow the activity down so that children are quiet, calm, and ready to sit down by the end.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children the vocabulary flashcards (see above for key words) one by one. Elicit or explain the meaning of each. Reassure children that these are difficult words, so they aren’t expected to remember them all straightaway. However, they will be useful to know for the reading task in the next lesson.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Children sit in a circle. Pass one of the flashcards to the right and say the word clearly, modelling the correct pronunciation. The child to your right continues. When you feel everyone is confident, increase the challenge level by passing a card to the left and another card to the right, and so on.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Turn to ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.136. Show children the pictures and play the audio.

Oxford task — guided: Hand out an A4 piece of paper to each child. Show them how to fold it into the number of key words. Now ask them to draw a picture of each vocabulary item in each space. Instruct children to cut them out carefully so that they have mini playing cards.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into groups of four to play ‘Happy families’ with their cards. Shuffle all their cards together. The aim of the game is to try to get four of the same card (e.g. four pairs of glasses or four vases). Each player starts with four cards (dealt at random) and the rest are in a pile face down. The first player asks another player Have you got [a pitcher]? If that player has the card, they must hand it over. If not, they pick up another card from the pile. At the end, the player with the most ‘families’ (sets of four) wins!

Oxford pair/group task: Allow groups time to complete or make good progress in their game. Monitor actively.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

Oxford source task: Ask children to complete ex.B in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.136.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them fewer words to focus on to begin with. They can play ‘Happy families’ in a small group supported by an adult.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more confident children to demonstrate leadership skills in the game by clearly explaining the instructions to unsure classmates, helping them with difficult pronunciations, and ensuring that each child takes their turn at the right time.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–D in Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.124–125.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to write their own definitions of the words to accompany their picture cards. They can then play a definitions game in a small group – one child says their definition and the others guess the word.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have consolidated their understanding of new topic vocabulary in a game of ‘Happy families’, practising their pronunciation of each word.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?
