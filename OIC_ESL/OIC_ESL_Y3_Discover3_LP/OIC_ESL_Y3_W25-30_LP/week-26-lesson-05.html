Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Writing instructions. Original outcomes: 3.4b, 3.4c, 3.4d, 3.4f. Unit: Plants.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Turn to SB Oxford Discover 3 p.164.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask the class: How do we find out about how to do something? Elicit different places where we might find this kind of information.

Oxford opening: Ask: When was the last time you read some instructions? (e.g. for playing a game) Write children’s suggestions on the board.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Turn to SB Oxford Discover 3 p.164.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children if they know how to [grow spinach / make a smoothie]. Elicit some simple steps, ideas, and related vocabulary.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Instruct children to read the text and to note down the sequence adverbs. Remind children why these words are important (to break up the text, and so we know the order of the steps).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Tell children that today they are going to write their own ‘How to …’ instructions. They don’t necessarily have to be about plants – they can be about anything they know how to do really well, such as mend a bicycle tyre, make a puppet, jump a horse, wrap a present, make a pizza topping, grow some herbs, or make a photo frame.

Oxford task — guided: Ask them to mind map some ideas first, including key words and steps for their process. Hand out dictionaries to support them.

Oxford task — guided: Give children time to write out their steps in draft format. Remind them to look back over their work from the past two weeks for ideas.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Encourage pairs to discuss each other’s work, highlighting things their partner did really well and some things that aren’t quite right yet.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Tell them to swap their work with a partner. Encourage them to ask themselves the following questions when checking their partner’s work: Do the instructions make sense? Can you follow them easily? Do they use sequence adverbs to break the text down into steps? Also remind them to check for correct spelling, punctuation, and grammar.

Oxford source task: Encourage pairs to discuss each other’s work, highlighting things their partner did really well and some things that aren’t quite right yet.

Oxford source task: Allow children to do a thorough check first, before writing out the instructions neatly.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide some ideas, a gapped text, or sentence starters. Allow them to agree on a process and to write out their instructions in pairs.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to write more, including an introduction similar to that in the input text. This could explain why they chose their idea and how they learned the process themselves.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete activities B and C on p.151 in Oxford Discover WB 3.

Oxford additional tasks: Put the work together in a class book. Talk with children about how to categorize the instructions (e.g. how to do something useful, how to make food, how to do something quickly).

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written clear ‘How to …’ instructions for a familiar process.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 26, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — Instruction verb charades

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing instructions.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Growing steps ordering

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing instructions.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Imperative sentence surgery

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing instructions.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — How-to partner demonstration

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing instructions.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Plants’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by writing the instructions for a process they know well.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
