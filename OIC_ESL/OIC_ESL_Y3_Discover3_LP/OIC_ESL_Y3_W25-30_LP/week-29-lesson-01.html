Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Special food from around the world (2). Original outcomes: 3.2e, 3.3g, 3.3h. Unit: Food.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Hand out or display the text provided.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children if they can recall the names of some dishes from different countries (see Week 28, Lesson 4). Write their suggestions on the board.

Oxford opening: Then find out if they know anything about Fiji. Show some captivating or visually interesting pictures to generate interest in the country. Elicit what children can see. Ask: Does this place look similar or different to our country? Why do you say that?

Oxford opening: Invite a volunteer to try to find the country on a map, atlas or on a globe. You could support them by indicating if they are ‘hot’ (close) or ‘cold’ (far away).

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Hand out or display the text provided.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: First, instruct children to scan the text quickly to find the names of traditional dishes from the country.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Put the vocabulary on the board and explain that sometimes foods can be translated, whereas in other cases they cannot. If possible, give an example from your own language where a translation wouldn’t work at all.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Put children into pairs. Ask them to read the text again and to note down the ingredients in the dishes identified earlier. Then encourage them to think about whether these dishes are a starter, a main, a dessert, or a drink.

Oxford task — guided: Ask them to identify any cooking methods that might be important for the dish (e.g. steaming or cooking in the ground).

Oxford task — guided: Do a speaking activity. Tell Child A to ask for either the ingredients or the cooking method: e.g. What are the ingredients in the dish? How do you make the dish? Child B responds with the information from the text. Child A says what the dish is without checking the text.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs. Ask them to read the text again and to note down the ingredients in the dishes identified earlier. Then encourage them to think about whether these dishes are a starter, a main, a dessert, or a drink.

Oxford pair/group task: Ask them to identify any cooking methods that might be important for the dish (e.g. steaming or cooking in the ground).

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, ask children to think of some traditional dishes they might include if they were writing a text about their own country. Tell them to list the ingredients that they know and any other interesting information – for instance, if it is a celebration meal or if it is only found in a specific area or region of the country.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Use the gap-fill activity provided. Go through the answers as a class.

Oxford source task: Encourage children to think back to their lesson on the food pyramid (Week 28, Lesson 2) and to decide if the foods they have read about sound healthy or not so healthy.

Oxford source task: Widen the discussion by asking: Is it important to continue making traditional dishes? Why? What is important about them? What can we learn through food?

Oxford source task: In pairs, ask children to think of some traditional dishes they might include if they were writing a text about their own country. Tell them to list the ingredients that they know and any other interesting information – for instance, if it is a celebration meal or if it is only found in a specific area or region of the country.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, read the text more slowly in an adult-led small group and work together on the gap-fill exercise. Alternatively, pair up mixed abilities.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to identify the different parts of speech and punctuation marks in the text, and to think about why they are used.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 29, Lesson 1

Oxford differentiation: Delicious food in Fiji

Oxford additional tasks: Create a poster about one of the traditional dishes learned about.

Oxford additional tasks: Read the text about Hungarian Goulash in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.56 and note down the ingredients in the dish and where they come from.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a text for detailed information about food from a different country. They have considered why traditional dishes are culturally important.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — World foods comparison chart

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from around the world (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

IEC supplemental game — Ingredient evidence sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from around the world (2).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

YEAR 3 Food
Week 29, Lesson 1: Special food from around the world (2)
Learning outcomes: 3.2e, 3.3g, 3.3h

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Food’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by practising reading 
a factual text for detailed information and consolidating their learning in pairwork.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
