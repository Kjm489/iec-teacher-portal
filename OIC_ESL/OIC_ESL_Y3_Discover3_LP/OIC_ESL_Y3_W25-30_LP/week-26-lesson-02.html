Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Asking for permission. Original outcomes: 3.2b, 3.2c, 3.2e. Unit: Plants.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Say to the class: I want to borrow a pencil. What can I say? Elicit from children some different phrases that we can use when we want to ask for something politely, such as ‘Can I have …, please?’ / ‘May I have …, please?’ / ‘I’d like …’.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask a confident child, in a fairly gruff manner, to give you something: e.g. You, give me your pencil.

Oxford opening: Then ask children in your usual tone: Did I ask [child’s name] nicely for their pencil? Elicit that you were quite rude.

Oxford opening: Talk with children about why this isn’t good. Tell them that today they are going to learn to be polite in English. Apologize to the child for your manner.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Say to the class: I want to borrow a pencil. What can I say? Elicit from children some different phrases that we can use when we want to ask for something politely, such as ‘Can I have …, please?’ / ‘May I have …, please?’ / ‘I’d like …’.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit different ways of replying, such as ‘Yes, you can.’ / ‘No, I’m using it.’ / ‘No, I haven’t finished with it.’ / ‘Sure, in a minute.’

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Run through the dialogue in ex.C Oxford Discover SB 3 p.154 with a volunteer. Then give children time to practise the dialogue in pairs and ask about two or three items.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Then, tell children that they are going to create an ‘asking for permission’ game. Ask them to think of all the times in the classroom when they ask for something (e.g. to turn the fan on, to open the window, to turn on/off the light, to get some water, to go to the toilet).

Oxford task — guided: Allow children to walk around the class silently for one minute, making a note of different situations. Alternatively, they could draw something to remind them of a situation when they need to ask permission on their whiteboard. Organize groups of children to stand up while the rest of the class is seated.

Oxford task — guided: Put children into groups. Tell them to compare their ideas and to choose their favourite six. Then instruct them to make some cards for these situations. They can either draw pictures or write full sentences: e.g. It’s really hot and you want to open the window. What do you say?

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Run through the dialogue in ex.C Oxford Discover SB 3 p.154 with a volunteer. Then give children time to practise the dialogue in pairs and ask about two or three items.

Oxford pair/group task: Allow children to walk around the class silently for one minute, making a note of different situations. Alternatively, they could draw something to remind them of a situation when they need to ask permission on their whiteboard. Organize groups of children to stand up while the rest of the class is seated.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into groups. Tell them to compare their ideas and to choose their favourite six. Then instruct them to make some cards for these situations. They can either draw pictures or write full sentences: e.g. It’s really hot and you want to open the window. What do you say?

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Now instruct them to make some ‘yes/no/maybe’ answer cards.

Oxford source task: Explain the rules of the game. A child turns over a ‘situation’ card and asks the relevant permission using one of the expressions learned earlier. Then the next child turns over an answer card and answers accordingly.

Oxford source task: Encourage children to further develop their game so that there is a winner. Say: I think this game could be better. How can we have a winner? Do you get points? How?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Allow less able children to draw the situation or just write a few words (e.g. ‘open window’) on their cards.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to add more details to the situation, especially if they get a ‘no’ answer card.

Oxford additional tasks: Write an ‘asking for permission’ dialogue using speech bubbles.

Oxford additional tasks: Make up a role-play that involves asking for permission. Tell children to think about a location, a character, and what the characters are doing.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a game review.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have practised asking for permission politely by creating and playing a game.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 26, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — Permission phrase ladder

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Asking for permission.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — May I...? classroom role-play

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Asking for permission.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Permission yes/no scenario sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Asking for permission.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Polite request correction game

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Asking for permission.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Garden permission dialogue

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Asking for permission.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
