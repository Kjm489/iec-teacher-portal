LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3

Week: 25

Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.3a, 3.4c, 3.4d

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Cause and effect. Original outcomes: 3.3a, 3.4c, 3.4d. Unit: Plants.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Ask for some examples of cause and effect in the natural world. To guide children, elicit what happens if a plant doesn’t receive any water or light.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play a game. Sit in a circle with children. Explain that they are going to make up a chain story. Say a sentence: e.g. I kicked a ball. Then invite the child on your right to say something that happened next: e.g. It went high in the air. Each child should try to say a sentence.

Oxford opening: Ask children what the first thing to happen was (you kicked a ball). Elicit that the action caused a chain of events (the effects).

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask for some examples of cause and effect in the natural world. To guide children, elicit what happens if a plant doesn’t receive any water or light.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show the class the un-watered plant and ask: What’s happened to this plant? Help them to understand that it has been very hot and sunny (if suitable in your context), and that the plant’s soil is now dry.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Establish which is the cause and which is the effect. The cause is the hot sun on the plant, and the effect is what happens next (dry soil). Ask: Which happened first?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Elicit that we can use ‘First …’ and ‘Next …’ to make clear the order of events.

Oxford task — guided: Turn to ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.149. Put children into pairs to complete the activity. Go through answers as a class, checking children’s understanding.

Oxford task — guided: Put children into small groups. Ask them to think of one or two situations with a clear cause and effect. Encourage them to value each other’s suggestions and to support their less confident peers to understand the difference between cause and effect. Share ideas as a whole class.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Turn to ex.D in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.149. Put children into pairs to complete the activity. Go through answers as a class, checking children’s understanding.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into small groups. Ask them to think of one or two situations with a clear cause and effect. Encourage them to value each other’s suggestions and to support their less confident peers to understand the difference between cause and effect. Share ideas as a whole class.

Oxford pair/group task: Display the coloured notes in two groups. Work together to identify the cause and effect pairs.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Once children have warmed up their thinking, give them time to write their own example in two or three sentences. Remind them to check their writing for correct spelling and punctuation.

Oxford source task: Hand out sticky notes. Instruct them to write their cause onto one coloured sticky note, and their effect onto a different coloured sticky note.

Oxford source task: Display the coloured notes in two groups. Work together to identify the cause and effect pairs.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them some prompts to help them think of examples of cause and effect in their everyday lives. For example, you could ask: What happens if you go to bed late? What happens if you do exercise? What happens if you don’t drink enough water?

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to continue the cause and effect chain: e.g. I saw the dry soil (cause) and I watered the plant (effect). I watered the plant (cause) and the plant grew (effect).

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 p.137.

Oxford additional tasks: Make an infographic to illustrate cause and effect in plants.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified cause and effect in a short text about plants. They have written their own cause and effect sentences.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Plant cause-effect dominoes

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Cause and effect.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
