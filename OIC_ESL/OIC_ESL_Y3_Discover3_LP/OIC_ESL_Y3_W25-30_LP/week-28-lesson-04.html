PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to sit comfortably with their water bottles. This might be in a circle on the floor, cross-legged. Tell them that you are going to guide their thoughts and that they don’t need to say anything, just follow your voice. They can close their eyes if they wish.

Oxford opening: Ask them to take a sip of their water. Do they notice the taste in their mouth?

Oxford opening: Then instruct them to open their mouth slowly and to close it slowly. Encourage them to notice the different parts of their mouth by guiding their thoughts: e.g. Notice your teeth.

Oxford opening: Explain that today they are going to be exploring taste.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: If you have brought in some different foods from around the world, invite a few volunteers to the front of the class. Make sure that you are aware of any allergies in advance. Blindfold the children and give them some of the foods to taste. See if they can guess what they are. Follow the guidance in the introductory activity.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Take off the blindfolds and present each food item in turn to the class, describing its taste/texture and saying its country of origin/where it is eaten regularly, e.g. They eat paella in Spain.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Work together to complete the matching activity on the board. Encourage children to make guesses or to draw upon their existing knowledge – they aren’t expected to know all the answers. The aim is to ignite their curiosity.

Oxford task — guided: Pair up children and ask them to choose one of the dishes from the previous activity that they would like to know more about. They are going to conduct some online research to find out how to prepare their chosen dish.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Pair up children and ask them to choose one of the dishes from the previous activity that they would like to know more about. They are going to conduct some online research to find out how to prepare their chosen dish.

Oxford pair/group task: Revise how to structure instructions with steps to describe a process (see Week 26, Lesson 5). Then invite pairs to complete activity A on p.151 in Oxford Discover WB 3. Check answers with the class and tell children this will be a model text for their writing.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: First, ask them to think of two or three questions that they want to answer so that their search is more focused. Support children to find the answers to their questions online. You might want to make a list of suitable websites first.

Oxford source task: Revise how to structure instructions with steps to describe a process (see Week 26, Lesson 5). Then invite pairs to complete activity A on p.151 in Oxford Discover WB 3. Check answers with the class and tell children this will be a model text for their writing.

Oxford source task: Ask children to work individually and write the recipe for their chosen dish, adding the information they found during their research in their introduction (e.g. its origins or details such as whether the dish is usually eaten during a festival or on special occasions).

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: You may adapt the food grid provided to suit the ability level and interests of your class.

Oxford differentiation: Give less confident pairs one or two questions to answer.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to summarize their findings in their own words, and to find out further information on the ingredients that used in their chosen dish.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 28, Lesson 4

Oxford additional tasks: Produce a leaflet about the dish researched.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have found out where different foods come from and have written the recipe for a dish.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 28, LESSON 4

IEC supplemental game — World food photo passport

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from around the world (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Paella ingredient detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from around the world (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Country dish matching

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from around the world (1).

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — International food market role-play

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 3 Food
Week 28, Lesson 4: Special food from around the world (1)
Learning outcomes: 3.3c, 3.3g

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Food’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and research skills by finding out information about a dish. They will write a recipe for their chosen dish.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
