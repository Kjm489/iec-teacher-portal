PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Food’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford opening: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford opening: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. For any of the main activities, children can refer to Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.46–65 and Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.40–58 for ideas about food.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

Oxford task — guided: In groups, children work together to design and make a poster for an imagined food festival. Ask them to choose a festival name and theme – the type of food that will be showcased. It could be a festival in celebration of home-grown fruit and vegetables, of breakfast foods, or of homemade foods. They will also need to think about what cooking classes, music, food trucks, or tasting stations might be there. They can use the internet to research international food festivals, or you can provide example posters and pictures from local food festivals. At the end of the week, children present their poster to the class. Ask each group member to give their opinion on the festival, and to say which food activity they like the most and why.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. For any of the main activities, children can refer to Oxford Discover SB 4 pp.46–65 and Oxford Discover WB 4 pp.40–58 for ideas about food.

Ask them to choose a festival name and theme – the type of food that will be showcased. Oxford pair/group task: Hold a cooking competition. In pairs, children follow a simple recipe (e.g. for bliss balls). Provide the ingredients and any equipment or utensils they need. After children have made and tasted them, ask them to think of variations with other ingredients and allow them to experiment. Children then showcase the best variation. Check allergies to any food items before bringing them into the classroom.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Hold a cooking competition. In pairs, children follow a simple recipe (e.g. for bliss balls). Provide the ingredients and any equipment or utensils they need. After children have made and tasted them, ask them to think of variations with other ingredients and allow them to experiment. Children then showcase the best variation. Check allergies to any food items before bringing them into the classroom.

Oxford source task: Children produce a TV advertisement for a new flavour of crisps. Revise different advertising techniques beforehand. In groups of six, ask them to decide on the new flavour, create a jingle (chant), a slogan, and a short dialogue. Allow them to mind map their ideas, assign roles, and rehearse. They then present their advertisement to the class. Invite the rest of the class to give constructive feedback to each group.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the projects, provide a revision handout of key topic vocabulary, sentence starters, and useful phrases for children’s presentations. You might want to assign team leaders for each small-group activity to guide less confident children through the task, or break the task down into more manageable stages. Assign roles fairly based on children’s strengths. To support team leaders, provide a checklist of items that each project should have.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Food fair exhibition

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Share, reflect and assess.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
