LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Special food from my country. Original outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4b, 3.4c, 3.4d. Unit: Food.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Ask children to tell you about some food festivals in their country.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Divide the class into three and number children 1 to 10.

Oxford opening: Explain that when you say their number, they need to come to the front of the class and write the food word you say on the board.

Oxford opening: Give points for spelling accuracy, nice handwriting, or if their team is quiet. Begin with easier words like ‘egg’ and end with more challenging words like ‘tomato’ or ‘delicious’.

Oxford opening: Use this activity as an opportunity to revise food vocabulary from the unit.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children to tell you about some food festivals in their country.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit what happens at these festivals, when they are, and what the typical foods are.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Explain that in today’s lesson they are going to write about a special dish that they normally eat at festivals, or on a special occasion (like a wedding or family celebration).

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Work together as a class to mind map some ideas on the board. For instance, consider who usually makes the dish in their home or at a festival, what the ingredients are, if it is a traditional recipe, or if it is a seasonal dish. You may wish to refer children to the table in ex.B on p.56 of Oxford Discover WB 4. Ask children to ensure that their writing includes all five things from the table. You could also show them the report in ex.A as an example.

Oxford task — guided: For the main writing task, children can work individually or in pairs. If they choose to work by themselves, encourage them to talk with a partner first about how they are going to approach the task. Remind them to look at the clock and to assign a time for each part of their planning and writing.

Oxford task — guided: Give children time to make their plan.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: For the main writing task, children can work individually or in pairs. If they choose to work by themselves, encourage them to talk with a partner first about how they are going to approach the task. Remind them to look at the clock and to assign a time for each part of their planning and writing.

Oxford pair/group task: When children have completed their first draft, ask them to swap it with a partner or another pair, who will check for sense, good spelling and handwriting, and correct grammar and punctuation.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Next, tell them to use their plan to write full sentences about their special dish. Encourage them to look back over the input texts from the previous two weeks to remind them of useful vocabulary, sentence starters, and expressions.

Oxford source task: If you made a whole-class list of adjectives for describing food, share this with children too. Alternatively, provide dictionaries.

Oxford source task: When children have completed their first draft, ask them to swap it with a partner or another pair, who will check for sense, good spelling and handwriting, and correct grammar and punctuation.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with sentence starters or a gapped text about food from your region.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to give as much detail as possible about the ingredients and where they come from, as well as why the dish is important to their country and to them personally. They might also want to include a recipe (unless it is a family secret!).

Oxford additional tasks: Ask children to produce an illustrated final draft of their written work.

Oxford additional tasks: Present the written work as a spoken activity to the class. Don’t read the work out loud, but rather talk about it: e.g. I wrote about celebration food because I love making special biscuits with my mum. It’s a tradition in our family.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written about a special meal.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 29, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — National dish ingredient hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from my country.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Food tradition family interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from my country.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Recipe step sorting

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from my country.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Special dish information gap

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from my country.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — My country's food poster

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Special food from my country.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Learners create a small labelled drawing, map or idea web representing one accurate example from the lesson, then explain it to a partner using a sentence frame.

ICQ: Do you draw and label before presenting? Will your partner ask a question?

CCQ: How does your drawing show the concept from today's lesson?

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
