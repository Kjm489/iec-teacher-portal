Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Plant life cycles. Original outcomes: 3.3d, 3.3i. Unit: Plants.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show children a bean and ask if they know how it grows. Accept all reasonable ideas.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Review why we need plants (see Week 25, Lesson 1).

Oxford opening: Ask children to stand up, then say a sentence: e.g. We can eat some plants. If it is true, they must jump up and down; if it is false, they must freeze.

Oxford opening: After a few turns, invite confident children to call out sentences.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children a bean and ask if they know how it grows. Accept all reasonable ideas.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children that today they are going to find out what beans need to grow.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Pre-teach/Elicit the lesson’s vocabulary and hand out the instructions.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to read and to identify what the bean needs to grow (water, air, and light).

Oxford task — guided: Ask children what they think will happen if the bean doesn’t have these elements. Put them into groups to discuss ideas, then write their predictions on the board.

Oxford task — guided: Put children into pairs to carry out the experiment. As a class, you will need at least three beans that have access to everything (as per the instructions). Then deprive some beans of light. Let children work out how to do this (e.g. by covering the side of the jar). Deprive some beans of water and deprive some beans of air.

Oxford task — guided: Either assign the experiments or let children choose. Ask some instruction-checking questions before you begin. Draw simple illustrations on the board as children explain the method to you.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children what they think will happen if the bean doesn’t have these elements. Put them into groups to discuss ideas, then write their predictions on the board.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs to carry out the experiment. As a class, you will need at least three beans that have access to everything (as per the instructions). Then deprive some beans of light. Let children work out how to do this (e.g. by covering the side of the jar). Deprive some beans of water and deprive some beans of air.

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, children follow the instructions and put their bean in the jar, depriving it of a chosen element where required.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: See if children can identify the imperative verbs that begin each instruction.

Oxford source task: In pairs, children follow the instructions and put their bean in the jar, depriving it of a chosen element where required.

Oxford source task: Remind the class that they are going to check the beans’ progress over the next few days.

Oxford source task: Read the long text in Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.150–151 and answer the comprehension questions on p.152.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Mix together abilities in pairwork so more confident children can support their peers to read and understand the instructions, modelling key words.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children to write down and illustrate their predictions and assumptions for each scenario (i.e. with/without water, air, and light).

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 25, Lesson 4

Oxford differentiation: How to grow a bean in a jar

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 p.138.

Oxford additional tasks: Plant other seeds and watch them grow. You could set up a class garden in the classroom with micro herbs.

Oxford additional tasks: Keep a daily journal to record children’s beans’ progress. Encourage children to think about what is happening and why.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have considered what plants need to grow successfully. They have tested their hypotheses by growing a bean in a jar.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Seed to plant sequencing

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Plant life cycles.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Life-cycle movement relay

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Plant life cycles.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Missing life-stage detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Plant life cycles.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
