Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Advertising food. Original outcomes: 3.1a, 3.1f, 3.2a, 3.2f. Unit: Food.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Tell children that today they are going to look at food advertising and what makes us buy certain foods.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Begin the lesson by humming or saying a few words from a well-known advertisement that children will recognize. Ask them why they can remember it and accept any reasonable ideas.

Oxford opening: Ask children how often they see food advertisements on TV, on the radio, or online.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children that today they are going to look at food advertising and what makes us buy certain foods.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit some ideas as to how advertisements persuade us to buy foods, and ask children to think of some examples for foods they know. When children see/recall an advertisement, encourage them to think about the following questions regarding the techniques used:

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: What food is the advertisement for?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: What words describe the food?

Oxford task — guided: Is there any music? Is it catchy?

Oxford task — guided: Are there any celebrities in the ad? Do you want to be like the person in the ad? Why?

Oxford task — guided: Is there anything else that makes you want to buy the food?

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Tell children that they are going to make their own advertisement (or commercial) in groups and then present it to the class.

Oxford pair/group task: Invite pairs to present their advertisement to the class. During their presentation, encourage them to outline the techniques they used to make it appealing to a consumer.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Play the sequence of real-life food advertisements, pausing after each one. Ask children to identify the techniques used, referring back to the above questions. Then challenge them to identify any other techniques – for instance, if they ‘put down’ (discredit) a competitor (e.g. ‘Unlike other tortillas, ours …’) or if they express urgency (e.g. ‘Offer only lasts until …’ or ‘Buy now before it is too late!’).

Oxford source task: Tell children that they are going to make their own advertisement (or commercial) in groups and then present it to the class.

Oxford source task: Before beginning the presentations, write a list of simple success criteria on the board for the audience to refer to when giving constructive feedback on each other’s work.

Oxford source task: Invite pairs to present their advertisement to the class. During their presentation, encourage them to outline the techniques they used to make it appealing to a consumer.

Oxford source task: Hold a whole-class vote on the best advertisement.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with some sentence starters and key words to guide them through the creative task. Challenge more confident children to use rhetorical questions in their presentation. Encourage fast finishers to compare the advertising of fast food and healthy food.

Oxford additional tasks: Children design a crisp packet in pairs using some of the techniques discussed. It should be a bestseller! They should also include the name of the crisps, the flavour, and the nutritional value information. Refer back to the food pyramid from Week 28, Lesson 2 to support them with this. Hand out the brown paper bags for children to transform them into bestselling crisp packets.

Oxford additional tasks: Use dictionaries and thesauruses to make a whole-class list of adjectives for describing food. Consider: Which words make you want to buy a certain food? (e.g. ‘delicious’, ‘tasty’, ‘fresh’, ‘juicy’) You can refer children to the ‘Interesting Adjectives’ box in Oxford Discover SB 4 p.55.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified some food advertising techniques and have made their own advertisement.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 29, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — Food advert slogan studio

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Advertising food.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Persuasive word detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Advertising food.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Truth versus exaggeration sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Advertising food.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Food’.
In this lesson, children will apply their understanding of the language of advertising in a creative task.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
