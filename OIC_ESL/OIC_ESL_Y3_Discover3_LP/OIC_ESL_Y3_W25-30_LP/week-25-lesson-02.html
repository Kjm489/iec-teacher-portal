Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: What do we know about plants?. Original outcomes: 3.2e, 3.4e. Unit: Plants.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Pre-teach/Elicit the different parts of a plant using realia or flashcards.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: If possible, take children outside to the playground. Tell them that they are going to go on a ‘listening walk’. If your school has a field or garden, this would be an ideal place.

Oxford opening: Tell children where they are going and ask them what they think they are going to hear.

Oxford opening: As you walk, stop frequently and ask children to close their eyes and listen very carefully.

Oxford opening: Sit down (either back in the classroom or somewhere outside) and invite children to tell you what they heard.

Oxford opening: If you cannot go outside, you could play them some sounds recorded from nature.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Pre-teach/Elicit the different parts of a plant using realia or flashcards.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Complete Oxford Discover SB 3 p.148 exs.A–B.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Drill the lesson’s vocabulary and play a game. Say a word and children draw it on their whiteboard. Count down from three to one. Ask everyone to hold up their drawing.

Oxford task — guided: Put children into small groups. Explain the game instructions: one child draws a part of a plant and the other children try to guess which part it is. The person drawing can’t say anything or give any clues.

Oxford task — guided: In the same groups, tell children that they are going to work together to draw and label their own flowering pot plant. They must include all the words.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into small groups. Explain the game instructions: one child draws a part of a plant and the other children try to guess which part it is. The person drawing can’t say anything or give any clues.

Oxford pair/group task: In the same groups, tell children that they are going to work together to draw and label their own flowering pot plant. They must include all the words.

Oxford pair/group task: Explain to groups that they will need to plan how they are going to do this. Will they copy from a book or online? How big will their drawing be? (You could give the option of small sticky notes or using a large sheet of paper.) Will different children do different tasks? Are they going to do one each or one together? Advise children to plan these steps and to estimate how long it will take them.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Explain to groups that they will need to plan how they are going to do this. Will they copy from a book or online? How big will their drawing be? (You could give the option of small sticky notes or using a large sheet of paper.) Will different children do different tasks? Are they going to do one each or one together? Advise children to plan these steps and to estimate how long it will take them.

Oxford source task: Give children enough time to complete the drawing and labelling activity.

Oxford source task: Hold a whole-class discussion. Ask children to reflect on how well they planned their work, and how accurate their timings were.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with a drawing to label. Go to Oxford Discover WB 3 p.136.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to find out about plants or trees with different roots (e.g. aerial roots or roots we eat).

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.135–136.

Oxford additional tasks: Find out what each part of the plant does (e.g. the roots hold the plant and find water).

Oxford additional tasks: Challenge children to create a wordsearch for each other using the new vocabulary.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have drawn and labelled the different parts of a plant.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 25, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — Plant parts mystery bag

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What do we know about plants?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Know-wonder-learn board

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What do we know about plants?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Plant facts true or false

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What do we know about plants?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Plant scientist interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What do we know about plants?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Plants’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and writing skills by drawing and labelling a diagram of a plant.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
