Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Growing your own food. Original outcomes: 3.3g, 3.3i. Unit: Plants.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.155 ex.E.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children why growing (some of) our own food is a good idea. Elicit that it can taste great, be fun, be healthier, and be fresher. There is no plastic packaging. We can also learn how to grow food, save money, and be responsible for something. Find out which plants children grow at home.

Oxford opening: Encourage children to think of questions they would like to answer about growing their own food.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.155 ex.E.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children to stand up. Put them into small groups of three or four. Spell out some words and ask groups to make the letters with their bodies. Make it more challenging by saying the letters in a random order. After each word, tell children to sit back down and to write the letters out to spell the word. Ask them to keep their words to themselves. The words to spell out are the sequence adverbs ‘first’, ‘second’, ‘third’, and ‘finally’. If children can predict ‘third’, let them write this down.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Write the sequence adverbs on the board. Elicit that these are words we use when we are making a list of steps, such as a set of instructions. Check that everyone understands ‘first’ = 1, ‘second’ = 2, and ‘third’ = 3.

Oxford task — guided: Show the class the picture of the relevant food (the potato or pineapple). Pre-teach key vocabulary for each text.

Oxford task — guided: Put children into pairs and ask them to think about how they might grow these foods. Encourage them to focus on the steps – first, second, third, and finally – and to make notes. Share ideas as a whole class.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children to stand up. Put them into small groups of three or four. Spell out some words and ask groups to make the letters with their bodies. Make it more challenging by saying the letters in a random order. After each word, tell children to sit back down and to write the letters out to spell the word. Ask them to keep their words to themselves. The words to spell out are the sequence adverbs ‘first’, ‘second’, ‘third’, and ‘finally’. If children can predict ‘third’, let them write this down.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs and ask them to think about how they might grow these foods. Encourage them to focus on the steps – first, second, third, and finally – and to make notes. Share ideas as a whole class.

Oxford pair/group task: Say that you now want them to write three questions to ask each other about the text. Give children time to write their questions and then to answer them in a group. To make this more fun, they could write their questions on individual pieces of paper, put them into an envelope, and then pull them out in turn.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Finally, give children time to read the short text and check their ideas.

Oxford source task: Say that you now want them to write three questions to ask each other about the text. Give children time to write their questions and then to answer them in a group. To make this more fun, they could write their questions on individual pieces of paper, put them into an envelope, and then pull them out in turn.

Oxford source task: Hold a whole-class discussion. Ask: How easy or difficult would it be to grow this food?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with some question words to use.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to draw pictures illustrating the process explained in the input text. This can support their less able peers.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 p.142 exs.A–B.

Oxford additional tasks: If you have a school garden, plant potatoes or pineapples. If not, ask children to plant something at home – it could be a herb in a plant pot – and observe its growth. Refer back to Week 25, Lesson 4 for the steps to grow a plant. If children do this, ask them to look after it and to bring it into class when it is ready (if possible).

Oxford additional tasks: Make a class book about how to grow a particular food, using sequence adverbs. The book could be in the shape of the food it is about.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a text about the process of planting and growing food. They have revised sequence adverbs.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Grow a vegetable step race

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Growing your own food.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Seeds soil water matching

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Growing your own food.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Gardening instruction mime

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Growing your own food.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — My growing plan calendar

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Growing your own food.

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Plants’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by identifying main points in a short text about growing food.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
