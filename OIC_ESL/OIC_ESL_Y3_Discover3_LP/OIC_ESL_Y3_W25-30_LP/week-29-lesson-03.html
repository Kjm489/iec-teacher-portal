LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: Food
Week: 29
Lesson: 3
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.3g, 3.3i

Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: A science experiment with food. Original outcomes: 3.3g, 3.3i. Unit: Food.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show children your paper. Ask them what is on it and elicit (mysteriously) that there is nothing … or so it seems!

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children if they have ever written or sent a secret message. Establish how these messages were secret. They may have been hidden in a secret place, or written in code.

Oxford opening: To illustrate the latter, do an example on the board: e.g. gdkkn = hello (each letter comes before the actual letter in the alphabet). Put children into pairs to try to ‘crack’ the secret code in 2 minutes.

Oxford opening: Take ideas from children about why some messages need to be secret – for instance, when organizing a surprise party or trip.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children your paper. Ask them what is on it and elicit (mysteriously) that there is nothing … or so it seems!

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Hold the paper up to a lamp or torch to reveal your secret message.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask if anyone knows how to do this. If possible, elicit what is needed and what to do. Children might be able to work it out for themselves.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Form small groups and hand out the experiment materials. Tell children to check that they have everything they need.

Oxford task — guided: Give them time to follow the instructions to write a secret message to another group.

Oxford task — guided: Once the papers have dried, re-distribute the secret messages to other groups.

Oxford task — guided: Bring the class back together. Ask if we might be able to do this with other liquids.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Form small groups and hand out the experiment materials. Tell children to check that they have everything they need.

Oxford pair/group task: Give them time to follow the instructions to write a secret message to another group.

Oxford pair/group task: Once the papers have dried, re-distribute the secret messages to other groups.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Assign a new liquid to each group. Instruct them to predict what they think will happen, and note it down on their whiteboard. Allow them to test their assumption by redoing the experiment.

Oxford source task: Read the Writing Study box on p.183 of Oxford Discover SB 3 whilst children follow in their book. Explain that they can use although to describe the results of their experiments (e.g. Although lemon juice was invisible, beetroot juice didn’t disappear. Although beetroot juice didn’t make invisible ink, it was interesting to do the experiment.)

Oxford source task: Invite groups to share their findings about the other liquids, and whether or not their predictions were accurate. Emphasize that it doesn’t matter if they were wrong – this is why scientists do experiments in the first place. They don’t often get it right first time. Encourage children to include although in their sentences.

Oxford source task: Hold a whole-class discussion, eliciting how effective or not the other liquids were.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Group together mixed abilities so more able children can support less able children to understand the instructions.

Oxford differentiation: In each group, encourage a less confident but able child to be the team leader. This will allow them to be stretched and challenged, but in an unintimidating context.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to find another food science experiment or to write about one which they remember.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 29, Lesson 3

Oxford additional tasks: Find out why the lemon juice ‘disappears’ (oxidation) and what actually happens by going online or looking in reference books.

Oxford additional tasks: Find out more about the uses of lemon juice (e.g. It prevents peeled apples or avocados from going brown).

Oxford additional tasks: Write up the experiment and the results.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have followed instructions to carry out a fun science experiment with food.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Food experiment prediction cards

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A science experiment with food.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
