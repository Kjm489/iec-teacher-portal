Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: What is fun about food?. Original outcomes: 3.1a, 3.1b, 3.2b. Unit: Food.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Using the pictures, talk with children about different places to eat and the foods that we can eat in these places. Add any new vocabulary to the list on the board, sounding out difficult words and drilling them together.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Invite children to share with the rest of the class the last food they ate, and to say something about it. For instance, if the food came from a plant or an animal, if they eat it every day or on a special occasion, what it tastes like, or if they enjoyed it. If necessary, you may first like to review food items from Oxford Discover SB 1 p.96 and SB 2 p.42.

Oxford opening: Elicit as much food vocabulary as possible and write it on the board. Children could make a mind map of ideas first in small groups.

Oxford opening: Explain to the class that the next unit is going to be all about food. Using the Think-Pair-Share technique, ask children to consider: What’s fun/interesting about food?

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Using the pictures, talk with children about different places to eat and the foods that we can eat in these places. Add any new vocabulary to the list on the board, sounding out difficult words and drilling them together.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Work together as a class to categorize different foods (e.g. foods that we like for picnics, foods that we like to eat at home). Encourage children to suggest and agree on their own categories.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Put children into pairs (AB). Tell Child A to describe a picture and Child B to listen carefully to identify it. They swap roles after each picture is guessed correctly. Ensure that each child has the opportunity to describe and listen within the time limit.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs (AB). Tell Child A to describe a picture and Child B to listen carefully to identify it. They swap roles after each picture is guessed correctly. Ensure that each child has the opportunity to describe and listen within the time limit.

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into small groups. Give each group a timer and a dice or spinner. Do an example with a confident child first. Instruct the child to roll the dice and then talk about the related topic (that matches to the number on the dice) for 30 seconds.

Oxford pair/group task: Give children enough time to play the game in groups, listening to each other’s ideas as they play and sensitively prompting their less confident peers with ideas and vocabulary suggestions. If they throw the same number, they should try to say something different.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: On the board, write ‘eating in a restaurant’, ‘eating at home’, ‘favourite snack’, ‘favourite drink’, ‘foods I hate’, and ‘foods I love’. Number these categories 1–6.

Oxford source task: Put children into small groups. Give each group a timer and a dice or spinner. Do an example with a confident child first. Instruct the child to roll the dice and then talk about the related topic (that matches to the number on the dice) for 30 seconds.

Oxford source task: Give children enough time to play the game in groups, listening to each other’s ideas as they play and sensitively prompting their less confident peers with ideas and vocabulary suggestions. If they throw the same number, they should try to say something different.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with some ideas for the group speaking game, and give them some time to think about what they might say before they begin. You can also reduce the time limit to 15 or 20 seconds.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children to speak for 45 seconds on the topic, using because and an interesting range of adjectives (e.g. ‘delicious’, ‘tasty’, ‘sweet’, ‘sour’, and ‘juicy’) to express their opinions and preferences.

Oxford additional tasks: As a class or in small groups, write a type of food for every letter of the alphabet.

Oxford additional tasks: Cut out pictures from magazines to create a food collage.

Oxford additional tasks: Hand out a KWL chart for each child to complete individually, answering the question: What is fun/interesting about food? Encourage them to compare their ideas in pairs.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned and revised vocabulary related to food and practised using it in conversation. They have shared their food preferences.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 28, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Food texture guessing clues

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about food?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Favourite food survey

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about food?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Food adjective sorting

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about food?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Food tasting vocabulary mime

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about food?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — My fun food drawing

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about food?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Learners create a small labelled drawing, map or idea web representing one accurate example from the lesson, then explain it to a partner using a sentence frame.

ICQ: Do you draw and label before presenting? Will your partner ask a question?

CCQ: How does your drawing show the concept from today's lesson?

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Food choice explain and defend

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about food?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Display two or three topic-related choices. Students vote individually, discuss evidence with a partner, and give a complete sentence explaining their choice.

ICQ: Do you vote before discussing? Must you give a reason?

CCQ: Which detail from the lesson supports your choice?

Assessment: A clear choice and a relevant explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

YEAR 3 Food
Week 28, Lesson 1: What is fun about food?
Learning outcomes: 3.1a, 3.1b, 3.2b

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Food’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by playing a game in which they talk about familiar aspects of food.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
