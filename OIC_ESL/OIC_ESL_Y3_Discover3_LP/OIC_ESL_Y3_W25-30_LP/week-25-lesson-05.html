Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: What’s in your shopping bag?. Original outcomes: 3.2c, 3.3b. Unit: Plants.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Elicit/Pre-teach the lesson’s vocabulary using the flashcards or realia. Focus on the container words, such as ‘a box of’ or ‘a jar of’. Carefully model the pronunciation of the phrases, emphasizing the weak ‘of’ .

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children how often they go food shopping with their parents or carers, and if they take a list. Elicit a few items that they bought on their last shopping trip.

Oxford opening: Now show children your shopping bag and ask them to guess what is inside.

Oxford opening: You could make this a feely bag activity, eliciting the items as you take them out.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit/Pre-teach the lesson’s vocabulary using the flashcards or realia. Focus on the container words, such as ‘a box of’ or ‘a jar of’. Carefully model the pronunciation of the phrases, emphasizing the weak ‘of’ .

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Drill the words and play a clapping game. Sit children in a circle. Invite one child to say ‘a’, the next child to say ‘jar’, the third child to say ‘of’, and the fourth child to say a food item that could be inside the container. Encourage children to think sensibly about what this could be (e.g. it can’t be bread). For the clapping, there are four beats. Children slap their thighs (beat 1), then clap their hands together (beat 2), then click their right fingers (beat 3), and finally, click their left fingers (beat 4). Practise this until everyone is confident and has had the opportunity to say something. This normally takes about 10 minutes if it is the very first time you are doing a clapping game.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Mix up the word cards and the flashcards. Stick them on the board. Ask children to read and match them together, then copy the vocabulary down.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Monitor actively, helping with vocabulary if necessary.

Oxford task — guided: With a confident child, elicit a mini-dialogue outside a shop. For example:

Oxford task — guided: A: Hello! How are you?

Oxford task — guided: B: Good! I’m going shopping!

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into pairs to create and act out a similar mini-dialogue.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: B: I need a loaf of bread, a jar of olives, and a packet of biscuits. What about you?

Oxford source task: A: I need a carton of milk, a bottle of juice, and a box of cereal.

Oxford source task: B: OK. Let’s go shopping!

Oxford source task: Put children into pairs to create and act out a similar mini-dialogue.

Oxford source task: As a class, relate the container words to plants. Ask: What might we find in a garden or at a garden centre? (e.g. a bag of soil, a packet of seeds, a plant pot, a sack of compost).

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, write a gapped model mini-dialogue on the board.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to add more sentences to their mini-dialogue.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 3 p.139 exs.A–B.

Oxford additional tasks: Draw and label all the container words.

Oxford additional tasks: Play a matching game. Divide the class into teams of four or five. Number children so that they know when to speak. Then, for example, say ‘Number 1s’ and then a container word (e.g. ‘bag’). The Number 1s must say a food item that you can find in a bag (e.g. ‘carrots’).

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned vocabulary for different containers. They have practised these words in a mini-dialogue.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 25, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — Shopping bag plant product reveal

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What’s in your shopping bag?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Food or non-food plant product sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What’s in your shopping bag?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Shopkeeper customer role-play

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What’s in your shopping bag?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — My shopping bag illustration

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What’s in your shopping bag?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Shopping choices explanation

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What’s in your shopping bag?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Display two or three topic-related choices. Students vote individually, discuss evidence with a partner, and give a complete sentence explaining their choice.

ICQ: Do you vote before discussing? Must you give a reason?

CCQ: Which detail from the lesson supports your choice?

Assessment: A clear choice and a relevant explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
