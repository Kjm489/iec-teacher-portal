Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Brilliant bees. Original outcomes: 3.1a, 3.1g. Unit: Plants.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Show the picture of the bee and, in pairs, ask children to share everything they know about bees. You can guide this activity by writing some ideas on the board (e.g. their colour, how many legs they have got, what they do, what they make, and where they live).

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to name some insects and write them on the board. See if anyone can say anything about them: e.g. An insect has got six legs.

Oxford opening: Ask children whether they like insects, and to explain why or why not. Ask: Do you think insects are useful?

Oxford opening: Encourage children to think about and tell you how many bees or insects they have seen today or in the last week.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show the picture of the bee and, in pairs, ask children to share everything they know about bees. You can guide this activity by writing some ideas on the board (e.g. their colour, how many legs they have got, what they do, what they make, and where they live).

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Hold a whole-class discussion, encouraging everyone to contribute.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Pre-teach/Elicit the lesson’s vocabulary by showing the flashcards or pictures.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask: What is a pollinator? Children may know that bees are pollinators, but they may not know what this means. Explain that they are going to listen to find out.

Oxford task — guided: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.154 and play track 2.47.

Oxford task — guided: Give children time to talk about what they understood, then share ideas as a class.

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to listen again and to make a list of the steps of pollination. Remind them that this is a process and that words like ‘first’, ‘second’, ‘third’, ‘next’, and ‘finally’ will help them.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Show the picture of the bee and, in pairs, ask children to share everything they know about bees. You can guide this activity by writing some ideas on the board (e.g. their colour, how many legs they have got, what they do, what they make, and where they live).

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 p.154 and complete ex.B.

Oxford source task: Give children time to share their answers, then go through them as a class.

Oxford source task: Tell children that bees are in danger. Explain why – largely because of pesticides and the destruction of their habitats. Ask them to think of some ways they can help bees. You could remind them that they need flowers. Encourage them to apply their own understanding of bees and the natural world, and/or to research some ideas on the internet.

Oxford source task: Invite children to present their ideas to the class. If possible, implement some of the ideas in order to increase the bee population in your school.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a copy of the listening transcript for them to follow.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for fast finishers, encourage them to role-play the pollination process to present to the class.

Oxford additional tasks: Find out about the dangers facing bees and how we can save them. Think about activities for World Bee Day on 20th May each year.

Oxford additional tasks: Find out about the life cycle of a bee.

Oxford additional tasks: Look for videos showing the pollination process online.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have considered and learned about the importance of bees. They have listened to understand the process of bee pollination.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 26, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Bee pollination role-play

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Brilliant bees.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Bee and flower route game

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Brilliant bees.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Pollination picture sequencing

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Brilliant bees.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Display two or three topic-related choices. Students vote individually, discuss evidence with a partner, and give a complete sentence explaining their choice.

ICQ: Do you vote before discussing? Must you give a reason?

CCQ: Which detail from the lesson supports your choice?

Assessment: A clear choice and a relevant explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Why bees matter evidence hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Brilliant bees.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Save the bees awareness card

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Brilliant bees.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Bee expert question time

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Brilliant bees.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Plants’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by listening for detailed information.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
