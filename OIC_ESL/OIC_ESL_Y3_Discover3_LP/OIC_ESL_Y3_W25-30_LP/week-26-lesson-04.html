PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to stand in a line. Then say some food items. Explain that if they come from plants, children must jump to the right; if they come from animals, children must jump to the left. If they are from both (e.g. strawberry yoghurt), they must stand still.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Challenge children to write down as many foods from plants as they can on their whiteboard (e.g. tomato, potato, pumpkin seeds). Give them 2 minutes. Share answers as a class and write them on the board. Then elicit the different parts of plants that are (sometimes) edible. Make sure children understand that some berries, leaves, and fungi are harmful. If they don’t recognize a plant as food, they shouldn’t eat it, or should ask an adult if it is safe to eat.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children if we could live without plants. Talk about what else we can get from them. Try to elicit materials, furniture, paper, medicine. Ask: Do you think there is one plant that is more useful than any other?

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Read the text about plants in ex.B in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.137. Do the uses described in the text match some of the things discussed in the class discussion? Add new ideas to the board.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: First, show children the following questions for them to consider:

Oxford task — guided: What is the plant?

Oxford task — guided: What is it used for?

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Put children into small groups. Encourage them to allocate different questions to each other. They then research their answers on the internet or in reference books. You might want to provide suitable, child-friendly websites to support them in their search.

Oxford pair/group task: Remind groups to work together to write out their information in a logical order. They should think about how they can communicate it in their own words, if possible.

Oxford pair/group task: Give groups time to rehearse their presentation. Can they use an engaging tone of voice?

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, make sure they have very specific information to research and guide them with useful websites. Alternatively, provide them with a fact sheet.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to find out more about their plant, such as where it is from, where you can grow it, what climate it needs, and its history – has the plant always been used in this way?

Oxford additional tasks: Find out about another plant, or a plant that has no use at all (or very little use).

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

Oxford learning review: Children have researched a plant and its different uses.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

IEC supplemental game — Plant product mystery clues

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Useful plants.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Medicine food fabric material sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Useful plants.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Useful plant invention cards

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Useful plants.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Plant use interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Useful plants.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
