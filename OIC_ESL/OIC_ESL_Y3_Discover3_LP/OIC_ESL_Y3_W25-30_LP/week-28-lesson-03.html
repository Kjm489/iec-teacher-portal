Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: Pizza toppings. Original outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2b. Unit: Food.

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes through the source activity: Ask the class: Do you like pizza? (If pizza isn’t familiar or available in your context, choose another food item that can have different fillings or toppings.)

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Form a circle of chairs and ask children to sit down. Choose four of five foods from the vocabulary list and assign one to each child. Explain that when you say and they hear their word, they have to swap places with another child with the same word. However, when you say ‘Pizza!’, everyone has to swap places. As children are swapping places, remove a chair. The last child left standing is ‘out’. Depending on the size of your class, be mindful of the number of children who are out and have nothing to do – you could invite them to call out one of the words in turn, or to remove a chair.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask the class: Do you like pizza? (If pizza isn’t familiar or available in your context, choose another food item that can have different fillings or toppings.)

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Using the flashcards or pictures, elicit possible pizza toppings.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Draw a pizza on the board and describe what you are doing as you add different toppings, modelling There is … / There are … and the negative forms: e.g. There is some tuna. There are some mushrooms. There aren’t any tomatoes. There isn’t any spinach.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: See if children notice ‘is’ (singular) and ‘are’ (plural) and talk about the difference between them. Help them to work out if a food item is countable or uncountable by asking some concept-checking questions: e.g. Can you count spinach? No, we count spinach leaves. There isn’t any spinach. There aren’t any spinach leaves.

Oxford task — guided: In pairs, ask children to copy the words down and to organize them into two columns, ‘Countable’ and ‘Uncountable’. Check answers as a class.

Oxford task — guided: Read the Writing study box on p.95 in Oxford Discover SB 3 to children, then ask them to complete the activity. Ask children to complete activity A on p.85 in Oxford Discover WB 3 to practise countable and uncountable nouns further.

Oxford task — guided: Tell children that they are going to design their own pizza toppings. Encourage them to think creatively – they could add more unusual items like mango or nuts.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, ask children to copy the words down and to organize them into two columns, ‘Countable’ and ‘Uncountable’. Check answers as a class.

Oxford pair/group task: Pair up children and explain the activity. They are going to take turns to describe what is on their pizza using There is … and There are …, and their partner has to listen carefully and draw what they understand from the description on the second template.

Oxford pair/group task: Give children enough time to complete the pairwork activity, swapping roles halfway. Monitor actively and note down any common errors related to the lesson’s grammar.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Hand out two pizza templates to each child. On the first template, instruct them to draw their invented toppings. They should keep this part secret!

Oxford source task: Pair up children and explain the activity. They are going to take turns to describe what is on their pizza using There is … and There are …, and their partner has to listen carefully and draw what they understand from the description on the second template.

Oxford source task: Give children enough time to complete the pairwork activity, swapping roles halfway. Monitor actively and note down any common errors related to the lesson’s grammar.

Oxford source task: Bring the class back together. Write down a few examples of common mistakes on the board, keeping children anonymous. Encourage the class to think about what isn’t quite right and to suggest corrections. Go through the grammar again to reinforce their learning.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Provide less confident children with sentence starters. Ask them to focus only on the affirmative forms.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children to ask their partner questions: e.g. Are there any olives?

Oxford additional tasks: Invite children to present their partner’s pizza topping orally to the rest of the class.

Oxford additional tasks: Ask children to write four sentences to describe their pizza, using There is …, There isn’t …, There are …, and There aren’t ….

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used There is … and There are … in a pizza-related speaking activity.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Pizza topping preference corners

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Pizza toppings.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
