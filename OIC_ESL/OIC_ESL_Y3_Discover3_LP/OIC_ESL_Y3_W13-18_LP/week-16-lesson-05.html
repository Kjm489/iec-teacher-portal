LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: What is fun about music?
Week: 16
Lesson: 5
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.2b, 3.2e, 3.2f

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: Do you play an instrument?. OIC outcomes: 3.2b, 3.2e, 3.2f. Unit: What is fun about music?.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Question words are covered in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.23. Check children understand all the questions listed in the Learn box in ex.E.

Oxford opening: Stand in a circle with children. Throw a confident child a ball and ask them a music-related question: e.g. Who is your favourite musician? They answer the question, then throw the ball to another classmate, posing them a different question: e.g. What musical instrument do you play? Repeat until all children have had the opportunity to ask and answer a question.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Use the example dialogue provided or create your own.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: With another adult or a confident child, read the dialogue aloud, and ask children to listen and read along. Alternatively, pre-record the dialogue before class.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit the question words.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Invite two children to perform the dialogue in front of the class.

Oxford task — guided: Write several prompts on the board, including a child’s name, an instrument, and a day of the week. Children practise the dialogue in pairs using the prompts.

Oxford task — guided: Invite pairs to perform their dialogue in front of the class without naming the child. The rest of the class makes a guess based on the clues in the dialogue.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Write several prompts on the board, including a child’s name, an instrument, and a day of the week. Children practise the dialogue in pairs using the prompts.

Oxford pair/group task: Invite pairs to perform their dialogue in front of the class without naming the child. The rest of the class makes a guess based on the clues in the dialogue.

Oxford pair/group task: Take a child to one side and explain the next activity to them clearly – in newly formed pairs, children are going to pretend to be a musician and practise interviewing each other using their own questions. Check the child’s understanding, then instruct them to communicate the instructions to the rest of the class.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Take a child to one side and explain the next activity to them clearly – in newly formed pairs, children are going to pretend to be a musician and practise interviewing each other using their own questions. Check the child’s understanding, then instruct them to communicate the instructions to the rest of the class.

Oxford source task: Allow children enough time to practise their role-play. Encourage them to be as creative as possible with their answers, using a variety of question words.

Oxford source task: Before the end of the lesson, encourage children to reflect on their own and their partner’s performance. Did they use a variety of question words? Did they use rising intonation at the end of their questions? Did they show enthusiasm and use gestures to support communication?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: In the introductory activity, you can vary the level of difficulty by asking less confident children ‘Do you …’ questions that require only a ‘Yes, I do’ or ‘No, I don’t’ response.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children to explain their answer: e.g. A: Which musical instrument do you prefer between a piano and a keyboard? B: I prefer the keyboard because you can add funny sound effects to music.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 16, Lesson 5

Oxford differentiation: Example dialogue

Oxford additional tasks: With permission, record children’s role-plays so they can listen back to their dialogue as part of the peer-evaluation process. This will allow them (and you) to focus on their pronunciation and intonation.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have revised question words and interviewed each other about music.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Instrument player interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Do you play an instrument?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Practice timetable information gap

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Do you play an instrument?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Instrument learning roleplay

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Do you play an instrument?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Musician profile writing

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Do you play an instrument?.

ICQ: Do you listen twice? Do you compare with a partner before answering?
