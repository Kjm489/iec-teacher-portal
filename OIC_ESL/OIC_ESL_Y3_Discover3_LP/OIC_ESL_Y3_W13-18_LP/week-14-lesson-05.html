LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: Exploring the past
Week: 14
Lesson: 5
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.4b, 3.4c, 3.4f

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: Writing a diary entry. OIC outcomes: 3.4b, 3.4c, 3.4f. Unit: Exploring the past.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to find the three questions they wrote down in Week 13, Lesson 1. In pairs, they discuss whether they found the answers to their questions in the unit, what they have learned and found interesting, what they haven’t learned yet, and what activities they have enjoyed. Remind them to listen actively to their partner’s ideas and opinions, then respond with their own thoughts. Bring the class back together and invite a few volunteers to share their ideas.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children that they are going to pretend to be a character from the past and write their own diary entries in today’s lesson.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: You could start them off with some ideas by looking at the black-and-white photos in Oxford Discover SB 2 pp.50–51. Quickly discuss as a class life in the past to help them. Children volunteer what they remember from their previous learning.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Remind children of the lesson on diary entries in Week 14, Lesson 3.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Quickly review the past simple tense. Elicit from children common regular and irregular verbs in the past tense that they could use in their diary entries. Do this in a game by miming different actions for children to guess, or by playing past tense ‘Tic-tac-toe’ (in a 3x3 grid, children have to get three correct present–past tense conjugations in a row). Explain that they have to practise new tenses over and over again in order to master them!

Oxford task — guided: Encourage children to plan their diary entries in a graphic organizer, with a different section for each day. Draw a basic table on the board for children to copy.

Oxford task — guided: Allow children time to complete the graphic organizer with their diary entry ideas.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children swap their diary entries with a partner. Tell them to read their partner’s work and give helpful feedback, including one thing they really liked, and one area to improve on (such as spelling, use of punctuation, use of tenses, and interesting language choices).

Oxford pair/group task: Bring the class back together and gauge how children are feeling. It’s not easy to plan and write diary entries, so commend their efforts. Elicit from children if they’ve enjoyed learning about the past.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Encourage them to think of a name for their character and the year that they are writing in, or allocate this information. Each day should include a short description of the activity, who they were with, and how it made them feel. Remind children to think about people’s day-to-day lives and hobbies in the past – they didn’t have video games and tablets!

Oxford source task: Now tell children to use their plan to write their diary entries, using a range of verbs that they know (past simple, present simple, present continuous) and connecting their ideas with but and because. They can refer back to the input texts from the unit to guide them.

Oxford source task: Children swap their diary entries with a partner. Tell them to read their partner’s work and give helpful feedback, including one thing they really liked, and one area to improve on (such as spelling, use of punctuation, use of tenses, and interesting language choices).

Oxford source task: Bring the class back together and gauge how children are feeling. It’s not easy to plan and write diary entries, so commend their efforts. Elicit from children if they’ve enjoyed learning about the past.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Less confident children can write a short diary entry for one day of the week, while more confident children can write entries for a five-day or seven-day week.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children and fast finishers to support less able children with past tense conjugations.

Oxford additional tasks: Ask children to revise and improve their diary entries following their partner’s feedback, then write a final, illustrated draft for display.

Oxford additional tasks: Draw a picture of a favourite day from the diary.

Oxford additional tasks: Provide dressing-up clothes for children to pretend to be characters from the past.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have planned and written short diary entries, using their understanding of the past and learning from the unit. They have practised using the past simple.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 14, LESSON 5

IEC supplemental game — Historical fact or opinion

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing a diary entry.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Then-and-now comparison quiz

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing a diary entry.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

IEC supplemental game — Historical knowledge interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing a diary entry.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Exploring the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using their learning in the unit to plan and write diary entries of a character from the past.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
