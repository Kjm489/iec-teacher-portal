LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: Exploring the past
Week: 13
Lesson: 2
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2b, 3.2c

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: How do places change?. OIC outcomes: 3.2a, 3.2b, 3.2c. Unit: Exploring the past.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Divide the class into groups of four. Give each group a set of vocabulary word cards for today’s lesson. Place the cards on the table face down. In turn, a child picks up a word card and reads the word aloud. The rest of the group tries to remember the meaning of the word (see Week 7, Lesson 1) and draws a picture on their whiteboard to match. Children shouldn’t confer with each other. When everybody has finished their drawing, they raise their hands and check with the teacher if they have the correct answer.

Oxford opening: Award one point for each correct answer, then move on to the next word.

Oxford opening: Bring the class back together and check children’s understanding of the vocabulary. Remind them that they learned these words earlier in the year, but they need to practise them over and over in order to remember them.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Hold up or project photos of your local area now and from a long time ago. If possible, place two photos of the same area taken decades or a century apart next to each other, so children can look closely and identify any major or minor changes. Ask: What do you notice? What is the same? What is different? Which photo do you prefer and why?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask: How do you think a city starts? What do you think there is before a city? Write these questions on the board. Tell children to discuss these questions in pairs, using the vocabulary recalled in the introductory activity. Discuss as a whole class.

Oxford task — guided: Help children to understand that building big cities can be good for people, but it can also cause problems. Briefly explain how green areas and woods are homes for lots of different creatures, and trees give us oxygen that we need to breathe! Replacing these spaces with big buildings and roads can harm animal habitats and the environment.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask: How do you think a city starts? What do you think there is before a city? Write these questions on the board. Tell children to discuss these questions in pairs, using the vocabulary recalled in the introductory activity. Discuss as a whole class.

Oxford pair/group task: Read part of the text ‘The Garden of Happiness’ on pp.158–159 of Oxford Discover SB3 (up to the end of the second paragraph, ending in ‘Its leaves were turning brown and dry.’). Elicit from children what it is happening to Marisol’s sunflower (it is dying) and why (because it is the end of the summer). Ask them to read the next nine lines (ending in ‘That’s the way of a garden. You must save the seeds and plant again next spring.’). In pairs, children discuss what the story teaches us about plants.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask children if they can think of any other effects. They might suggest that natural landscapes are beautiful, and people like to spend time relaxing and walking in these places. This makes us feel good, while cities can be busy and loud. Discuss as a whole class.

Elicit from children what it is happening to Marisol’s sunflower (it is dying) and why (because it is the end of the summer). In pairs, children discuss what the story teaches us about plants.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For the introductory activity, group together similar abilities so children can work at their own pace.

Oxford differentiation: For the settlement design task, group together mixed abilities so children can support each other and explain new words and difficult concepts to less able children.

Oxford additional tasks: In small groups, set children the task of designing a brand new ‘settlement’ (town) in a very big field surrounded by woods. Hand out large pieces of paper and coloured pencils/pens. Encourage children to think about how their settlement might benefit people without being bad for the environment.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about how areas change over time. They have considered the impact of building cities on green spaces and reflected on the life cycle, importance, and effects of plants.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Changing places photo match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do places change?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Change spotting race

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do places change?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Place comparison interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How do places change?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
