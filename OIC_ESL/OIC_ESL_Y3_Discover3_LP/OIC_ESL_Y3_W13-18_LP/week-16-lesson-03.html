Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: Comparing musical instruments. OIC outcomes: 3.3d, 3.3g, 3.3h. Unit: What is fun about music?.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play a guessing game. Stick musical instrument flashcards on the board and write a number under each one. Allow children to look at the flashcards for ten seconds before turning them face down. Call out a number at random and children guess which instrument name it corresponds to. If they get the answer right, turn the flashcard around. If they get the answer wrong, quickly show them the vocabulary item then turn it face down again. Call out a different number. Repeat until children have guessed all the musical instrument words correctly. By thinking again about new topic vocabulary, children are building their long-term memory of this learning.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit from children what it means to ‘compare’ and ‘contrast’. Accept all sensible answers and establish that it means saying what is the same and what is different.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Use Oxford Discover SB 3 p.117 to teach language for comparing and contrasting. Alternatively, write some phrases on the board:

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Compare – ‘similar’, ‘like’, ‘alike’, ‘the same as’, ‘both’, ‘some’.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Contrast – ‘different’, ‘unlike’, ‘not the same as’, ‘other’.

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to complete ex.D in SB 3 p.117 in pairs. Check answers.

Oxford task — guided: Turn to the reading text in SB 3 pp.118–119. Divide the class into five groups. Assign each group a section (‘Introduction’, ‘Strings’, ‘Woodwinds’, ‘Brass’, or ‘Percussion’).

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children to complete ex.D in SB 3 p.117 in pairs. Check answers.

Oxford pair/group task: Turn to the reading text in SB 3 pp.118–119. Divide the class into five groups. Assign each group a section (‘Introduction’, ‘Strings’, ‘Woodwinds’, ‘Brass’, or ‘Percussion’).

Oxford pair/group task: Groups read their section and work together to identify the main points, using the key words, linking words, and pictures they recognize to help them to understand unfamiliar language (e.g. ‘vibrate’, ‘bamboo’, ‘curved’, ‘blow’, ‘pitch’).

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Groups read their section and work together to identify the main points, using the key words, linking words, and pictures they recognize to help them to understand unfamiliar language (e.g. ‘vibrate’, ‘bamboo’, ‘curved’, ‘blow’, ‘pitch’).

Oxford source task: Ask groups to note down or underline language used to compare and contrast.

Oxford source task: Form new groups, consisting of one child from each ‘section’. In turn, children share what they have learned about what is the same and what is different about instruments. Encourage children to use key words, to gesture to pictures, and (for more able children) to practise using their own words to summarize main points.

Oxford source task: Hold a whole-class discussion. Gauge how children are feeling. Praise them for their effort to understand and communicate main points in a text. It isn’t easy and they used a range of useful strategies.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Support less able children in an adult-led small group by guiding them through the text and eliciting the main information. Alternatively, pair up more confident children with less confident children and ask them to present their text together.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.120.

Oxford additional tasks: Choose two instruments from different categories (e.g. string and percussion). Write a paragraph comparing and contrasting them, using new language learned.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have worked collaboratively to identify main points in a reading text.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Instrument comparison Venn

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Comparing musical instruments.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Louder softer clue cards

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Comparing musical instruments.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘What is fun about music?’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by identifying main points in a paragraph, using key words and visual features to support their understanding.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
