LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: What is fun about music?
Week: 16
Lesson: 1
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.1d, 3.1e, 3.2d

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: What is fun about music?. OIC outcomes: 3.1d, 3.1e, 3.2d. Unit: What is fun about music?.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Tell children that they are going to learn about music in a brand new unit.

Oxford opening: Ask them to get out of their seats and spread themselves out, or take them to an open space. Play a few pieces of music from different genres, starting with upbeat ones, which children can dance to, and ending with more relaxing tracks to calm children down. Encourage the class to move their bodies to each song in a way that feels natural to them. Have some fun!

Oxford opening: Back in their seats, ask children: What do you want to learn about in this unit? Ask children to write down three questions that they would like to find the answers to.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: In speaking and listening lessons, it’s OK if children don’t quite follow. Remind them to alert the teacher or their classmate(s) if they need something repeated or explained. Elicit some helpful expressions: e.g. Sorry, could you repeat that, please? Sorry, what did you say? Encourage them to do this in today’s lesson. Model a few times.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 3 pp.106–107.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Instruct children to read the title and look at the pictures. In pairs, they guess what they think they are going to see and hear in the video.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Play the video (read aloud the captions). Children watch for gist and note down any key words that they understand. As a class, discuss whether children’s predictions were correct. Invite volunteers to call out any key words they heard and elicit their meanings.

Oxford task — guided: Introduce more difficult words and place them in context (within a sentence). Encourage children to guess their meanings based on the words they know around them.

Oxford task — guided: Check and clarify answers. Play the video again.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Instruct children to read the title and look at the pictures. In pairs, they guess what they think they are going to see and hear in the video.

Oxford pair/group task: In small groups of four, ask children to discuss the questions: What is your favourite song or piece of music? Do you prefer listening to or dancing to music? Do you play a musical instrument? How many types (‘genres’) of music can you think of? In each group, nominate a scribe to take notes. Feed back to the class and discuss.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Complete exs.B–C.

Oxford source task: In small groups of four, ask children to discuss the questions: What is your favourite song or piece of music? Do you prefer listening to or dancing to music? Do you play a musical instrument? How many types (‘genres’) of music can you think of? In each group, nominate a scribe to take notes. Feed back to the class and discuss.

Oxford source task: Remind children that we all have different ‘tastes’ and it is normal for us to prefer different songs and genres. Some people might live and breathe music, whereas others might find it noisy and distracting! All of these opinions are valid. Encourage children to explain their thoughts and try to understand why their classmates might think differently.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children to support less confident children with writing their unit questions.

Oxford differentiation: Write key words on the board to support children in the small-group discussion.

Oxford differentiation: Mix abilities for this activity and allow less confident children to use their L1 where necessary. This will allow them to express their interest in music in a meaningful way.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the Big Question 6 activities in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.97.

Oxford additional tasks: Design a whole-class playlist of children’s favourite songs and pieces of music.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: To introduce the new unit, children have practised moving their bodies to music and watched a short video. They have understood that we all have different musical tastes.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 16, LESSON 1

IEC supplemental game — Music movement freeze

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about music?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Music vocabulary picture hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about music?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Sound and feeling vote

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about music?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Display two or three topic-related choices. Students vote individually, discuss evidence with a partner, and give a complete sentence explaining their choice.

ICQ: Do you vote before discussing? Must you give a reason?

CCQ: Which detail from the lesson supports your choice?

Assessment: A clear choice and a relevant explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Genre listening corners

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about music?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Play or read a short lesson-related sound or utterance twice. Learners identify a key detail, compare answers in pairs, then explain which sound or word they heard.

ICQ: Do you listen twice? Do you compare with a partner before answering?

CCQ: Which word or sound helped you decide?

Assessment: Accurate listening response and evidence

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Music preference survey

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is fun about music?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
