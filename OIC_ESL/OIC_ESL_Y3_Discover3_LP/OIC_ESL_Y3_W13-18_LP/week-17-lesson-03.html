Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: How was the singer’s singing?. OIC outcomes: 3.2b, 3.2c, 3.2f. Unit: What is fun about music?.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Divide the class into groups of four or five. Set groups the challenge of thinking of as many nouns related to music and music festivals as they can on their whiteboard. First, check that all children understand what you mean by the word ‘noun’ (a person, a place, or a thing). Then encourage them to think back to all the lessons in the unit. Set a time limit for them to complete the activity.

Oxford opening: Go through the answers as a class. Write ideas on the board. Award a point for each correct answer – the team with the most points wins.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Quickly review the past simple tense (see Week 13, Lesson 5). By thinking again about past tense verb endings, children are building their long-term memory of this learning.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Using the nouns on the board from the introductory activity, children practise making past tense sentences orally in pairs: e.g. The band often played jazz music. / I ate a cupcake at the music festival.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Introduce possessive nouns (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.115). Explain the rules using examples. Ensure children grasp the singular rule before moving on to the plural rules.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Using the nouns on the board from the introductory activity, children practise making past tense sentences orally in pairs: e.g. The band often played jazz music. / I ate a cupcake at the music festival.

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, encourage children to talk about the different rules for possessive nouns and which endings are used for each.

Oxford pair/group task: Play a game. In the same pairs, Child A makes up a sentence that includes a possessive noun. Child B writes the noun with the correct affixation on their whiteboard. They discuss the answer and try to explain their reasoning. They then swap roles. Remind children to check their answer with the teacher before moving on.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Pair up more confident children with less confident children. The former can explain the new grammar rules to the latter and help them to work out the correct affixation.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete ex.A in Oxford Discover WB 3 p.104.

Oxford additional tasks: Write gapped sentences for a partner to complete with the correct possessive noun.

Oxford additional tasks: Make a poster that explains the different rules for singular and plural possessive nouns.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to use singular and plural possessive nouns in a sentence, paying attention to affixation. They have practised saying possessive nouns in past tense sentences.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

IEC supplemental game — Singer performance adjective cards

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How was the singer’s singing?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
