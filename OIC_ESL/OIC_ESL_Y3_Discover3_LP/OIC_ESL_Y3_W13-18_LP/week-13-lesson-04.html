LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES

Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: Comparing two photos: past and present. OIC outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4c, 3.4e. Unit: Exploring the past.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Show children two photos of the same scene. One should be from a long time ago and one should be a recent photo. If your school is an old school, you could source a photo of a classroom from the past, then use children’s existing school or classroom photo to compare it against.

Oxford opening: Challenge children to guess the year when each photo was taken. Encourage them to explain why they think it’s a particular year. Provide the correct dates.

Oxford opening: Ask: What is the same? What is different? In small groups, ask children to discuss and note down the similarities and differences that they observe.

Oxford opening: Share answers as a whole class. Draw a two-column table on the board with the headings ‘Same’ and ‘Different’ and organize children’s ideas between the two.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Introduce the writing task. Tell children that in today’s lesson, they are going to compare two photos – these can be the same as in the introductory activity, or a different pair.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit from children what ‘compare’ means. Establish that it is when we look at two or more things and say what is the same and what is different. For example, if we compared an apple and a banana, we could say that both are fruits, but an apple is crunchy and a banana is soft. An apple is round while a banana is long.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: If you do not have any photos, use the photo of a rural area and an urban area from Oxford Discover SB 3 p.36. Ask children to imagine that the photo of the rural area is how the urban area was before.

Oxford task — guided: Briefly recap how we can describe things in the present using there is / there are and things in the past using there was / there were.

Oxford task — guided: Play a game. Divide the classroom into two. One side of the class is ‘Now’ and one side of the class is ‘The past’. Say a sentence using these present/past phrases and children move to the side of the room that it refers to. For example, say: There wasn’t a computer at home. Children (hopefully) move to the side of the classroom that represents the past.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Introduce the writing task. Tell children that in today’s lesson, they are going to compare two photos – these can be the same as in the introductory activity, or a different pair.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Give children 15–20 minutes to write a short paragraph comparing the two photos. Remind them to say what there was in the past and what there is now, using but to connect their ideas: e.g. In (1960), there was a blackboard in the classroom, but now there is a digital whiteboard.

Oxford source task: They can also talk about the photos using the present continuous tense: e.g. In the past, there was a school uniform. The girl in the photo is wearing a blue dress and black shoes. Now, there isn’t a uniform. In our school photo, we are wearing different clothes.

Oxford source task: Invite volunteers to read out their paragraphs to the class. Encourage children to keep a tally of every time they hear an example of there is / there are and there was / there were.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For the writing activity, less able children can bullet point simple sentences instead of connecting their observations together in a paragraph.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to rank the differences they notice, using the phrase ‘The biggest difference is that …’. Encourage them to give their opinion about whether the changes they notice are good or bad, and why.

Oxford additional tasks: Tell children to swap paragraphs with a partner. They could read each other’s work and discuss what they liked, and if there are any grammar, spelling, or punctuation mistakes. Encourage them to underline the mistakes for their partner to look at again and correct.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have compared two photos in writing and have revised the use of there was / there were.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Two photos spot the changes

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Comparing two photos: past and present.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Comparison sentence builder

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Comparing two photos: past and present.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Photo evidence scavenger hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Comparing two photos: past and present.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Photographer interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Comparing two photos: past and present.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Exploring the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using visual input to describe the similarities and differences between two different time periods.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
