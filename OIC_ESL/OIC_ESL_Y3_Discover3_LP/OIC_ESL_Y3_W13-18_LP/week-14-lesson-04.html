Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: Researching a historic place. OIC outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4c, 3.4e. Unit: Exploring the past.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask: How can we find or ‘research’ information? What tools do we have in our classroom to help us research? Try to elicit all the different options that are available to children, such as the internet, non-fiction and reference books, leaflets and brochures, and asking adults questions.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Remind children about the text they read in Week 14, Lesson 2 (about Neuschwanstein Castle or Predjama Castle).

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Check they understand the meaning of a paragraph. Review the definition in the Learn box in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.17 ex.D.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Hand out the table provided or draw one on the board for children to copy.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Using the information from the text, ask children to complete the second column (Part 1) of the table in pairs. Check answers as a whole class.

Oxford task — guided: Now ask children to think of a historic building in their local area, or provide three options and write them on the board.

Oxford task — guided: Using the research tools available, support children to find out information about their building and to make notes in the third column (Part 2) of the table. Alternatively, hand out some pre-prepared information cards.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Using the information from the text, ask children to complete the second column (Part 1) of the table in pairs. Check answers as a whole class.

Oxford pair/group task: In pairs, children read their completed text aloud and check each other’s work. Ask: What is the most interesting fact you’ve learned about your building?

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: To break up the research task into manageable chunks, stop the class every 8–10 minutes. Take a few deep breaths together and encourage children to reflect on the work they’ve done so far. Then continue. This will help to develop their attention and executive function.

Oxford source task: Use the paragraph template provided. Tell children to complete it with the information they have learned in today’s lesson. Remind them to use clear handwriting and to copy difficult spellings carefully. This will help others to read their research.

Oxford source task: In pairs, children read their completed text aloud and check each other’s work. Ask: What is the most interesting fact you’ve learned about your building?

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the research activity, help children to think of suitable search engine terms, and direct them to child-friendly websites, chapters in books, or information leaflets.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to synthesize their research in their own words, combining what they have learned from different sources, instead of using the template.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 14, Lesson 4

Oxford differentiation: Complete the table.

Oxford additional tasks: Design an information poster about the local historic building children chose, including headings, facts, photos, and illustrations.

Oxford additional tasks: Ask children to consider which was their most effective research tool and why.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have researched a historic building using different research tools, and brought their findings together in a short paragraph.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 14, LESSON 4

IEC supplemental game — Historic place research question cards

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Researching a historic place.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Source reliability sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Researching a historic place.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — History research stations

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Researching a historic place.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Historic site information poster

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Researching a historic place.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Learners create a small labelled drawing, map or idea web representing one accurate example from the lesson, then explain it to a partner using a sentence frame.

ICQ: Do you draw and label before presenting? Will your partner ask a question?

CCQ: How does your drawing show the concept from today's lesson?

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Tour guide rehearsal

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Researching a historic place.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Research evidence gallery

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Researching a historic place.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Exploring the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using their research notes to complete a short paragraph.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
