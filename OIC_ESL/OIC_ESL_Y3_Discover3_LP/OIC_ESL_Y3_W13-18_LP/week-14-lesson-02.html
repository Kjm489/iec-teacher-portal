LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: A historic building. OIC outcomes: 3.3c, 3.3f, 3.3g. Unit: Exploring the past.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Write the word ‘historic’ on the board. Establish that it means ‘from the past’ or ‘very old’, and comes from the word ‘history’, which children learn at school.

Oxford opening: Ask children: What historic buildings do you know in our country? Children write a list in small groups then share answers as a class.

Oxford opening: Repeat the activity for historic buildings in different countries.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children pictures of interesting historic buildings from around the world. Ask: Which buildings do you know? Where in the world are they? Discuss as a class.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Hand out the reading text provided.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Ask children: What type of text is this? (an email) How do you know? As this is a handout, you may like to show children an authentic email or zoom in on the photo in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.48 ex.A.

Oxford task — guided: Tell children to look at the picture(s) and read the subject line. In pairs, they note down three things that they expect to read about (e.g. how old the building is, where the building is, who lived in the building).

Oxford task — guided: Read the email aloud and children follow on their handouts. Alternatively, invite volunteers to read one paragraph each.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Tell children to look at the picture(s) and read the subject line. In pairs, they note down three things that they expect to read about (e.g. how old the building is, where the building is, who lived in the building).

Oxford pair/group task: Ask a few comprehension questions: e.g. Where is the castle? Why is the castle popular? What will Liza do in the spring? Can you find another word that means ‘enormous’? To make this activity more fun, create a quiz and divide the class into teams.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Encourage children to use reading strategies to work out the meaning of unknown words (using context or co-text, looking at the type of word [e.g. a noun or verb], and if there are any capital letters [e.g. Füssen = a proper noun]). Check as a class.

Oxford source task: Elicit the main points and write them on the board. Were children’s predictions correct?

Oxford source task: Ask a few comprehension questions: e.g. Where is the castle? Why is the castle popular? What will Liza do in the spring? Can you find another word that means ‘enormous’? To make this activity more fun, create a quiz and divide the class into teams.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide additional support in a small group guided by an adult. Look at the email paragraph by paragraph, focusing on the main point in each one.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to read the text independently. Ask them to note down the main point in each paragraph in their own words. Encourage them to share their ideas with the class and explain how they found the answers.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 14, Lesson 2

Oxford differentiation: To: liza@happymail.net

Oxford additional tasks: Write an email to a penfriend about a visit to a historic building in your local area.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read and understood an email about a historic building.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 14, LESSON 2

IEC supplemental game — Historic building clue hunt

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A historic building.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Architectural feature bingo

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A historic building.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Building then-and-now sketch

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A historic building.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give small groups mixed word cards selected from the lesson. They build a meaningful target sentence, read it aloud, then replace one word and explain the change.

ICQ: Do you arrange the words first? Will you read the complete sentence aloud?

CCQ: Does the sentence make sense for this lesson example? Which word changes the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate sentence order and meaning

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Heritage tour guide

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A historic building.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Save the building debate

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A historic building.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Display two or three topic-related choices. Students vote individually, discuss evidence with a partner, and give a complete sentence explaining their choice.

ICQ: Do you vote before discussing? Must you give a reason?

Assessment: A clear choice and a relevant explanation

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Exploring the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by predicting the content of an email and identifying the main points.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
