Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: How can we explore the past?. OIC outcomes: 3.1d, 3.1e. Unit: Exploring the past.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Introduce children to the new topic. Say: In this unit, we are going to learn more about life in the past. When we think about the past, we think about a very long time ago when life was very different! Elicit from children what they already know about the past.

Oxford opening: Ask: What do you think life was like 100 years ago? How do you think it was different to now? Can you think of anything that hasn’t changed? Accept all sensible answers, write them on the board, and discuss as a class.

Oxford opening: Challenge children to consider why learning about the past could be useful. They might suggest that it helps us to empathize with other people’s experiences; it helps us to appreciate advances in technology, science and medicine, and transport; it helps us to ‘paint a picture’ about what life was like, which we can explore further in museums; it teaches us to deal with and react to situations in different ways (e.g. if something went wrong in the past, we can learn from it).

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Show children the map on p.157 of Oxford Discover WB 3. Elicit what a map is (a flat drawing of an area, which can help us to see the shape of landforms and understand where something is).

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children that they are going to read a journal about the first voyage of Christopher Columbus. Ask: Who was Christopher Columbus? Why is he famous today?

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Children read the text on p.156 of Oxford Discover WB3 individually, then discuss it in pairs. Encourage children to help their partners in case there is anything they are not sure about, then check understanding by asking the class to complete activity B on p.157 of Oxford Discover WB3.

Oxford task — guided: Ask: Why do you think a journey to unknown lands is so difficult? Encourage children to discuss ideas and then, in pairs, write a list of reasons. Pair children with a different partner and ask the new pairs to compare their lists. Encourage children to add to their list as they discuss their reasons.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Children read the text on p.156 of Oxford Discover WB3 individually, then discuss it in pairs. Encourage children to help their partners in case there is anything they are not sure about, then check understanding by asking the class to complete activity B on p.157 of Oxford Discover WB3.

Oxford pair/group task: Ask: Why do you think a journey to unknown lands is so difficult? Encourage children to discuss ideas and then, in pairs, write a list of reasons. Pair children with a different partner and ask the new pairs to compare their lists. Encourage children to add to their list as they discuss their reasons.

Oxford pair/group task: Ask: What other ways are there to learn more about the past? In pairs, children list all the resources that they can think of (e.g. talking to older people, going to museums, reading history books and autobiographies, visiting historical sites, looking at old photos and letters).

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Encourage children to look at the structure of the journal. Ask children to write down seven dates and draw a fictional map with the destination reached on each date.

Oxford source task: Ask: What other ways are there to learn more about the past? In pairs, children list all the resources that they can think of (e.g. talking to older people, going to museums, reading history books and autobiographies, visiting historical sites, looking at old photos and letters).

Oxford source task: Invite volunteers to share their ideas and write them on the board.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For more support with the introductory activity, show children some photos from the past (of modes of transport, clothes, school life, buildings) to help children to think about similarities and differences.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to write a journal about a fictional exploration in the past.

Oxford additional tasks: Children could make their own journal entries based on their map ideas about journeys to unknown lands.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have understood the concept of ‘the past’ and considered what life was like a long time ago. They have learned about the importance of past explorations.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Artifact detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How can we explore the past?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Provide three to five event or process cards from the lesson. Pairs place them in order and narrate the sequence using first, next and finally.

ICQ: Do you arrange the cards before telling the sequence?

CCQ: What happens first? What clue shows the next step?

Assessment: Logical sequence and use of sequencing language

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: How can we explore the past?.

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Exploring the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by analysing a journal and reflecting on a past exploration.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
