LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: What is fun about music?
Week: 17
Lesson: 2
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.1d, 3.1h

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: A music festival. OIC outcomes: 3.1d, 3.1h. Unit: What is fun about music?.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Show children a video clip of a family-friendly music festival suitable for your context.

Oxford opening: In a follow-up, ask: Have you been to a music festival with your family, or listened to live music at a concert? Where was it? Did you have a good time? What did you do?

Oxford opening: Make a whole-class list on the board about what people might do at music festivals (e.g. watch their favourite singer or band, listen to an orchestra, take part in fun games and activities, eat delicious food, go on fairground rides, go camping, sing songs, dance!).

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: You could review the different facial expressions in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.134. Point to each child and say: Imagine the children are listening to music. Ask: How do the children feel?

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Use the listening script provided or create your own. Pre-record the dialogue with another colleague, making sure to clearly communicate emotion in the voice and any changes in intonation.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Read the title of the podcast and show children a picture that accurately represents the atmosphere at the music festival. Ask children to make a sensible guess about what they are going to hear and read, based on this information.

Oxford task — guided: Play the audio. Discuss what happens as a class and check children’s predictions.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Pair up children. They practise reading aloud the dialogue and communicating the correct emotion in their voices.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Play the audio again, pausing it frequently after each speaker. Elicit how the characters are feeling at these points, and why. Encourage children to think about 1. the words they use, 2. the intonation in speech (how their voices rise and fall), and 3. how they would feel in a similar situation.

Oxford source task: Ask children to show you a facial expression that matches the emotion in their voices. They can be as exaggerated as possible!

Oxford source task: Pair up children. They practise reading aloud the dialogue and communicating the correct emotion in their voices.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident children with the listening activity, pre-teach difficult words.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to expand the dialogue/story, adding more emotions.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 17, Lesson 2

Oxford differentiation: Fun at the festival

Oxford additional tasks: Write a sentence on the board. It could be a topic-related sentence (e.g. ‘I’m going to a music festival.’) or a fun tongue-twister (e.g. ‘I scream, you scream, we all scream for ice cream.’). Assign each child an emotion, such as ‘excited’, ‘scared’, ‘worried/nervous’, ‘sad’, ‘happy’, ‘angry’, or ‘confused’ and tell them to relate the emotion in their sentence. In pairs, children guess how their partner is feeling.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have recognized how the speaker is feeling in a dialogue about music festivals.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Music festival timetable puzzle

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: A music festival.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Play or read a short lesson-related sound or utterance twice. Learners identify a key detail, compare answers in pairs, then explain which sound or word they heard.

ICQ: Do you listen twice? Do you compare with a partner before answering?

CCQ: Which word or sound helped you decide?

Assessment: Accurate listening response and evidence

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
