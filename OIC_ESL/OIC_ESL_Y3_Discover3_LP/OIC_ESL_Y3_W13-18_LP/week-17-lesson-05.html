LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: What is fun about music?
Week: 17
Lesson: 5
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4b, 3.4c

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: Writing about an instrument. OIC outcomes: 3.4a, 3.4b, 3.4c. Unit: What is fun about music?.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Ask children to find the three questions they wrote down in Week 16, Lesson 1. In pairs, they discuss whether they found the answers to their questions in the unit, what they have learned that they found interesting, what they haven’t learned yet, and what activities they have enjoyed. Remind children to listen actively to their partner’s ideas and opinions, then respond with their own thoughts.

Oxford opening: Bring the class back together and invite a few volunteers to share their ideas.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Remind children of their task in today’s lesson – they are going to write a descriptive paragraph about a musical instrument of their choice.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Allocate enough time in the lesson for children to complete activity B on p.113 of Oxford Discover WB 3. Ask them to research their chosen musical instrument, using the research plan from the previous lesson. Encourage children to make a mind map of describing words, verbs, and facts they have found about the instrument to use in their written work next lesson. Monitor children and offer help if needed.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Make available the reading texts from Week 17, Lesson 4 to remind children of how a descriptive paragraph is organized. Write the headings on the board.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Topic sentence

Oxford task — guided: Researched facts

Oxford task — guided: Conclusion

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to write the first draft of their descriptive paragraph. Producing an extended piece of writing using children’s newly acquired knowledge of vocabulary and grammar is challenging. Remind children that they are in the stretch zone – they might feel a little worried or uncomfortable, but it will help to stimulate their brain and extend their understanding of difficult language.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Help children to understand that making mistakes is a positive thing – it encourages us to understand what went wrong and why. This helps us to improve and do better next time.

Oxford pair/group task: Invite volunteers to read their paragraphs to the rest of the class. Applaud and give praise.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: When they are finished, ask children to check their work carefully for correct spelling, grammar, and punctuation. They can now complete the checklist on p.113 of Oxford Discover WB 3.

Oxford source task: When everyone has finished, tell children to swap their paragraph with a partner. They read their partner’s work and give helpful feedback, including one thing they really liked, and one area to improve on (such as spelling, use of punctuation, use of tenses, and interesting language choices). If there is time, children revise their work following their partner’s feedback.

Oxford source task: Help children to understand that making mistakes is a positive thing – it encourages us to understand what went wrong and why. This helps us to improve and do better next time.

Oxford source task: Invite volunteers to read their paragraphs to the rest of the class. Applaud and give praise.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident children, provide a bank of common adjectives and gapped sentences for them to complete with their research.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to use new grammar learned in the unit, notably regular and irregular past simple verbs, adverbs of frequency, comparative and superlative adjectives, and possessive pronouns. They can also write about their personal experiences of using the instrument.

Oxford additional tasks: Display the final paragraphs around the classroom. Encourage children to read each other’s work.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written a descriptive paragraph about a musical instrument of their choice.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Instrument writing organizer

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Writing about an instrument.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Show a concrete example from the lesson. Learners observe, describe it using the target words, compare answers with a partner and justify one detail to the class.

ICQ: Do you describe the example before comparing answers?

CCQ: What in this example supports your description?

Assessment: Correct description and supporting detail

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
