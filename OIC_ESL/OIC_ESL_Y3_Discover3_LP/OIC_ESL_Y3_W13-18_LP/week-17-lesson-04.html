LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: What is fun about music?
Week: 17
Lesson: 4
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.3d, 3.3e, 3.3h

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: Describing an instrument. OIC outcomes: 3.3d, 3.3e, 3.3h. Unit: What is fun about music?.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Call out an adjective that could be used to describe a musical instrument (e.g. ‘big’, ‘small’, ‘smooth’, ‘wooden’, ‘loud’, ‘quiet’, ‘heavy’, ‘expensive’, ‘long’, ‘triangular’, ‘black and white’, ‘shiny’). Challenge children to think of an instrument that matches the word. Accept all correct answers.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Tell children the instrument they are going to read about (flute). If available, show children a real flute or acoustic guitar and pass it around carefully. Ask children to write two to three sentences describing the instrument. They can share their sentences with a partner and compare their choice of adjectives.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Look at the descriptive paragraph in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.124.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Ask children to read the text independently or in pairs for gist.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Elicit the main points. Encourage children to take a few deep breaths before they respond to consider their answer.

Oxford task — guided: Then ask them to scan the text for describing words and write them down. Take answers and write them on the board.

Oxford task — guided: Compare children’s descriptions with the ones in the text. Focus on the simile ‘like a bird singing’ and ask children why they think the writer chose this comparison.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Ask children to read the text independently or in pairs for gist.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Complete ex.B in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.124. Check answers as a whole class.

Oxford source task: Focus on the organization of the descriptive paragraph. Elicit from children the four sections: title, topic sentence, researched facts, conclusion.

Oxford source task: Allow children time to choose an instrument to write about in the next lesson. Provide musical instrument realia, computers, books, and music magazines for them to explore and plan their research about their chosen instrument.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: You can increase the difficulty level of the introductory activity by using more complex adjectives (e.g. ‘twisted’ or ‘silver’) or by narrowing down the possible answers by using two adjectives (e.g. I’m thinking of a musical instrument that is tall and wooden. = a cello).

Oxford differentiation: In the reading activity, help children to understand difficult parts of the text by paraphrasing, or by showing them what the writer means on the real instrument (e.g. by demonstrating blowing air or covering up the holes).

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a descriptive paragraph about a musical instrument.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 17, LESSON 4

IEC supplemental game — Mystery instrument clues

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Describing an instrument.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Instrument shape and sound match

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Describing an instrument.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Instrument description charades

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Describing an instrument.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Demonstrate an action connected to the lesson without saying the answer. Teams take turns acting, guessing with the target phrase, and giving one reason for their guess.

ICQ: Do actors say the answer? Does the team guess before the reveal?

CCQ: What action or clue helped you identify the meaning?

Assessment: Accurate spoken guess supported by a clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Describe and draw instrument

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Describing an instrument.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Learners create a small labelled drawing, map or idea web representing one accurate example from the lesson, then explain it to a partner using a sentence frame.

ICQ: Do you draw and label before presenting? Will your partner ask a question?

CCQ: How does your drawing show the concept from today's lesson?

Assessment: Accurate labelled example and explanation

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Instrument comparison interview

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Describing an instrument.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Model one question-and-answer exchange using the lesson language. Partners ask and answer, record one key response, then change partners and report what they learned.

ICQ: Do both partners ask and answer? Do you change partners after recording a response?

CCQ: Does your reply answer the question asked? Which detail supports it?

Assessment: An understandable question, relevant answer and report

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Musical instrument auction pitch

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: Describing an instrument.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Play or read a short lesson-related sound or utterance twice. Learners identify a key detail, compare answers in pairs, then explain which sound or word they heard.

ICQ: Do you listen twice? Do you compare with a partner before answering?

CCQ: Which word or sound helped you decide?

Assessment: Accurate listening response and evidence

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
