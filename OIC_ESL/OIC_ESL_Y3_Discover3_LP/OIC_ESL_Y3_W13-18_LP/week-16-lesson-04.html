LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: What is fun about music?
Week: 16
Lesson: 4
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.1c, 3.2a, 3.2b

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: What is as fun as playing a musical instrument?. OIC outcomes: 3.1c, 3.2a, 3.2b. Unit: What is fun about music?.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Explore comparative and superlative adjectives with children (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.113).

Oxford opening: Show children a selection of familiar instruments. Model using the target language.

Oxford opening: Challenge children to line the instruments up in size order, from ‘smallest’ to ‘biggest’. Hold up two instruments and ask which is ‘smaller’ or ‘bigger’ than the other. Repeat with different combinations of instruments.

Oxford opening: Repeat with ‘light’/‘heavy’, ‘loud’/‘quiet’, ‘long’/‘short’, ‘good’/‘bad’ (i.e. ‘better’/‘best’, ‘worse’/‘worst’), ‘most/least interesting’, ‘high’/‘low’ (for describing pitch).

Oxford opening: Draw a three-column table on the board and write the words in each form: e.g. loud louder loudest / heavy heavier heaviest / good better best.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Look at the song lyrics in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.121. Play the song. Children listen and follow. On the second play, encourage children to sing along.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Introduce comparative adjectives with as … as … (see Oxford Discover SB 3 p.121). Write a few examples on the board, such as:

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Playing the guitar is as fun as playing the piano.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: The sound of a saxophone is as relaxing as the sound of a flute.

Oxford task — guided: A trumpet can be as loud as a drum!

Oxford task — guided: A recorder is not as easy to learn as a triangle.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Pair up children. Play two different instrument sounds. Repeat a few times. Allow children time to make a sentence that compares the two instruments using as … as … on their whiteboard. Ask them to hold up their whiteboard and check their answers. Write good examples on the board. Repeat with other instruments. This activity will stretch children and extend their understanding of comparative language.

Oxford pair/group task: Hand out the instruments for children to continue the game in pairs or small groups. This time, they say their sentences orally instead of writing them down.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Invite volunteers to circle the adjective and underline the as … as … construction on the board. Can they find the negative form? How do they know? (the word ‘not’)

Oxford source task: Pair up children. Play two different instrument sounds. Repeat a few times. Allow children time to make a sentence that compares the two instruments using as … as … on their whiteboard. Ask them to hold up their whiteboard and check their answers. Write good examples on the board. Repeat with other instruments. This activity will stretch children and extend their understanding of comparative language.

Oxford source task: Hand out the instruments for children to continue the game in pairs or small groups. This time, they say their sentences orally instead of writing them down.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For less confident children, spend more time on comparative and superlative adjectives until they have a clear understanding of the concept and the language used.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to think more poetically and compare the sounds of instruments with different kinds of sounds: e.g. The sound of a flute is as light as air. / Drums can be as noisy as our class at breaktime!

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.A–E in Oxford Discover WB 3 pp.101–102, 109–110.

Oxford additional tasks: Design an instrument and label its parts. Describe the instrument and the type of sound it produces using comparative and superlative adjectives, and the as … as … construction.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about comparative and superlative adjectives, and comparative adjectives with as … as … They have practised using them in sentences.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Music or sport preference line

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is as fun as playing a musical instrument?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Play or read a short lesson-related sound or utterance twice. Learners identify a key detail, compare answers in pairs, then explain which sound or word they heard.

ICQ: Do you listen twice? Do you compare with a partner before answering?

CCQ: Which word or sound helped you decide?

Assessment: Accurate listening response and evidence

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — As-as dialogue challenge

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: What is as fun as playing a musical instrument?.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Place topic vocabulary and picture cards around the room. Teams take turns choosing one, using it accurately in a sentence, and checking with the teacher before scoring.

ICQ: Does a team earn a point for speed alone? Must the answer be correct?

CCQ: Why does your sentence match this picture or example?

Assessment: Correct target-language use, not speed

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.
