LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 3
Theme: Exploring the past
Week: 13
Lesson: 3
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45 minutes)
Original Learning Outcomes: 3.3c, 3.3g

Measurable Learning Objectives

Topic: The countryside then and now. OIC outcomes: 3.3c, 3.3g. Unit: Exploring the past.

Success criteria: Students use lesson vocabulary and skills to complete a meaningful supported task and produce independent evidence of learning.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford opening: Play a guessing game. Stick pictures of the vocabulary from Week 13, Lesson 2 on the board. Number the pictures 1–10. Allow children to look at the pictures and their location for ten seconds and then turn them around. Call out the numbers in a random order. Invite children to guess what the picture is by saying the correct word. If it is correct, turn the picture around for children to see. If it is wrong, show children the picture quickly, but keep it face down. Continue until children guess all the pictures correctly.

Oxford opening: Give praise to children who think strategically and try to work out the correct answers through a process of elimination, even if they don’t remember a picture’s exact location.

ICQ: Are we working independently or with our partner/group?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Elicit from children what we say when we are explaining to somebody what we can and cannot see – there is / there isn’t. Use this as a basis for pre-teaching there was / there wasn’t. Ask: What tense do you think this is? Confirm that it is the past tense. We use it to talk about the past.

Oxford task — teacher demonstration: Write a few example sentences on the board: e.g. There was a green gate in the field. There wasn’t a busy motorway. You could also use the text about a farmer in the past in Oxford Discover SB 3 p.103, in particular, looking at the examples of There are.

Teacher modeling: Demonstrate the first source task aloud and visibly before inviting learner responses.

CCQ: What does the model communicate? Which words or clues help you understand it?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided: Hand out one text between two children.

Oxford task — guided: Read the text aloud for children to follow. Tell children to listen and look for familiar words, including there was / there wasn’t.

Oxford task — guided: Ask children to scan the text to find what we use to describe more than one thing in the past – there were / there weren’t.

ICQ: Will we complete the first example together before working alone?

CCQ: Which detail in the source activity supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Oxford pair/group task: Invite a few volunteers to suggest sentences using there was / there were, or the negative forms, to describe your local area in the past. Write them on the board.

Oxford pair/group task: Then ask all children to write one more sentence on their whiteboard. Walk around the class and check children’s individual answers.

Scaffold: Use vocabulary from the Oxford task and provide a spoken sentence frame before removing support.

ICQ: Does each learner take a turn and listen to their partner?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford source task: Ask children to read the text through again individually. As a class, summarize the main points on the board.

Oxford source task: Invite a few volunteers to suggest sentences using there was / there were, or the negative forms, to describe your local area in the past. Write them on the board.

Oxford source task: Then ask all children to write one more sentence on their whiteboard. Walk around the class and check children’s individual answers.

Independent evidence: Each learner completes or reports an individual response based on the Oxford activity, without copying the teacher model.

ICQ: Should you attempt your own response before asking for help?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: On the second reading of the text, support less able children in a small group.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children to explain the use of there was / there were in their own words.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 13, Lesson 3

Oxford differentiation: Changing spaces

Oxford additional tasks: Ask children to write five more sentences about their local area in the past, leaving spaces for there was(n’t) / there were(n’t). Tell them to swap their sentences with a partner and complete them with the correct grammatical form.

IEC extension: Use the original Oxford task at a greater level of independence, or add a reason/example when learners are ready.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to describe a place in the past using there was / there were. They have identified the new grammar in a reading text.

Formative assessment: Check the original OIC outcome through one independent spoken, written, or practical demonstration. Record independent / prompted / not yet.

CCQ: What evidence shows the learner understood the task?

IEC supplemental game — Countryside past-present sort

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: The countryside then and now.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Give pairs 6–8 labelled picture/word cards drawn from the lesson. Learners sort or match them, explain one choice, then exchange sets to check.

ICQ: Are you sorting or writing a story? Do you explain one choice?

CCQ: Which feature tells you this card belongs in that group?

Assessment: Correct grouping plus a spoken reason

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

IEC supplemental game — Village photo detective

Purpose: Practise the Year 3 lesson focus: The countryside then and now.

Materials: Lesson textbook visuals, vocabulary cards, board and student notebooks.

How to play: Reveal three visual or verbal clues from the lesson one at a time. Students make a prediction, revise it after each clue, and explain the final answer.

ICQ: Do you guess after each clue or wait until the end?

CCQ: Which clue makes your final answer fit?

Assessment: A plausible answer with one specific supporting clue

Support: Pre-teach 3–4 key words; provide a model, picture cues and a sentence starter.

Challenge: Ask learners to explain a second example or justify a different answer.

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Exploring the past’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by identifying new grammatical forms in a reading text to support their understanding.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
