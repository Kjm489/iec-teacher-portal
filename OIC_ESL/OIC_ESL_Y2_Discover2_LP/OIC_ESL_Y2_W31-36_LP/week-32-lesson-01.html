Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: See how much shape vocabulary children can remember from Week 31. Challenge them to draw a picture on their whiteboard with, for example, three triangles, two circles, and a star. Encourage children to compare their drawings in pairs.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “What material or shape was used to make this?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Pre-teach/Elicit the lesson’s vocabulary using flashcards or pictures. Say the words slowly, and ask children to listen carefully and repeat. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.174 and do ex.A together. Play a flashcard game. Place one of the cards on your head. You can’t see what it is, but children can. Ask children: Is it a photograph? Continue in this way until you guess correctly. Do this a few more times. Then invite a volunteer to take your role. Ask if anyone has already done or enjoys doing any of these art forms. Find out which ones your class would like to try. Together with children, make up some mimes or gestures to represent each of the words. Say a word and ask children to do the action. If you have space (or take children outside to the playground), you could also tell a story (see suggestion provided) that includes the new words. When they hear one of the words, children must do the action. Ask children if they know why we can’t take photos in places like museums and art galleries (plagiarism issues, light – a camera flash can damage artwork, etc.). Using the Think-Pair-Share technique, ask children which art form they like best and why. They may have others that they wish to include (e.g. sculpture, watercolour painting). 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about art forms. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.152 exs.A–B. Create mini flashcards of the words and play ‘Snap!’. Find examples of the different art forms online.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, encourage them to talk about just one art form and why they like it. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to write their own narrative with all the art form words included. 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have learned new vocabulary to exchange ideas about different art forms.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to art forms; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

1. Mime and Guess — Art forms

Lesson-specific language focus: asking permission and sharing

Original teaching source: Original warm-up: See how much shape vocabulary children can remember from Week 31. Challenge them to draw a picture on their whiteboard with, for example, three triangles, two circles, and a star. Encourage children to compare their drawings in pairs.

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Mime and Guess”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: One learner silently acts out a source action; partners identify it and say a complete sentence.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.

YEAR 2 Shapes
Week 32, Lesson 1: Art forms
Learning outcomes: 2.1a, 2.2b

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Shapes’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by talking about different art forms.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
