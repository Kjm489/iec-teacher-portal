Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 5

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Say a shape and ask children to draw it on their whiteboard. Ask everyone to hold up their whiteboard after a count of three. Do this with all the shapes to check how well the class can remember shape vocabulary.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “How many sides does this shape have? Which shape is it?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Ask children to look at your shape collage, prepared in advance. What shapes do they see? Using your collage, elicit or model some sentences that illustrate countable and uncountable nouns. Use a lot and any: e.g. There are a lot of stars. There aren’t any triangles. There is a lot of paper. There isn’t any blue water. Depending on the ability level of your class, you could also use some. Encourage children to identify more things in your picture that can be counted. Help them to understand that we can’t count the water, but we can count the number of rivers, for example. Support them to make positive and negative sentences using quantifiers. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.173 and read exs.D–E together. Tell children that they are now going to make their own shape collage. First, they will need to plan their design in pencil. Encourage children to be as creative as possible. When they are happy with their design, show them how to stick on the shapes. They can also draw extra details. Give children enough time to experiment with the shapes and complete their collage. Ask children to write two or three sentences about their collage using some/any/a lot of. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about let’s make a shape collage. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Ask children to write sentences about a partner’s collage. For a further challenge, ask children to write sentences about a collage, but not to say which. Their partner must try to find the correct collage displayed around the classroom.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: You might need to spend more time teaching quantifiers depending on class ability. To support less able children, write some example sentences to describe your collage on the board. Group together mixed abilities in the peer-correction exercise so more confident children can support their less confident peers in identifying where they have gone wrong.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have distinguished between countable and uncountable nouns and begun to use quantifiers in sentences. They have made and written about a shape collage.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to let’s make a shape collage; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

1. Find Your Pair — Let’s make a shape collage

Lesson-specific language focus: asking permission and sharing

Original teaching source: Original warm-up: Say a shape and ask children to draw it on their whiteboard. Ask everyone to hold up their whiteboard after a count of three. Do this with all the shapes to check how well the class can remember shape vocabulary.

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Find Your Pair”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Give each learner a picture or word card. Learners circulate to find their match, then use both in a sentence.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.
