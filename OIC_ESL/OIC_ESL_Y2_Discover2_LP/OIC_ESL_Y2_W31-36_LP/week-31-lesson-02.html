Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Elicit from the class the names of different types of art from the previous lesson. Play a warm-up game. Ask children to stand up and indicate places in the room for ‘school’, ‘home’, and ‘town’. Say a type of art (e.g. ‘a big art installation’) and tell children to move to the place where they can see it. Some examples, like paintings, can be found in most places, so encourage children to move to the place that is their favourite out of the three options.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “How many sides does this shape have? Which shape is it?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Display a picture of an artwork composed of different shapes. Alternatively, draw a face on the board with two circles for eyes, a triangle for a nose, and a crescent for a mouth. Indicate to the circle and ask children: What shape is this? Elicit ‘circle’. Continue with the other shape names. Pre-teach/Elicit the remaining vocabulary using flashcards or by drawing them on the board. Drill the words carefully, paying attention to correct pronunciation (e.g. the consonant cluster ‘str’ in ‘straight’, the different initial ‘c’ sounds in ‘circle’ and ‘crescent’, and the final ‘gle’ sound in ‘rectangle’ and ‘triangle’). Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.168 and do ex.A together. Play a game in which everyone sits in a circle. Whisper a word to the child on your right, who then whispers it to the child on their right, and so on. The child on your left then says the word they heard and/or chooses the correct flashcard. Repeat with all the words. Now put children into groups of three or four and ask them to stand up. Say one of the shape words and challenge groups to make it with their bodies. Encourage children to be as creative as possible – some words will be easier than others! Tell children to return to their seats calmly. Then refer back to the artwork or drawing of a face and say: W

Think aloud, highlight key language and demonstrate an accurate answer before students try. Use gestures, visual evidence or a short example as appropriate.

CCQs: “What did I do first?” “What makes the example accurate?”

Work through one example together; students answer on mini-whiteboards or orally. Gradually remove the sentence starter.

ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about shapes in art. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.146 ex.A. Colour in the pictures. This could also be in the form of a pairwork dictation activity. Cut out shapes from coloured paper and create a picture using them.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, focus on the most common shapes – circle, square, rectangle, triangle – then increase the challenge level when they feel more comfortable. Challenge more confident children to include all of the shapes in their drawing and follow-up dictation.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have learned shape names and identified them in different places. They have understood how shapes can be used in art, then drawn their own picture using shapes.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to shapes in art; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
