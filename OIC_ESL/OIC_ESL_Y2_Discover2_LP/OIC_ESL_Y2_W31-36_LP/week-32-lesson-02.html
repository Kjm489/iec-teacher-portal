PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Play a game using the flashcards (or pictures) of the words from Week 32, Lesson 1. Show the cards one by one. Ask children to call out the words. Then take one card and very quickly ‘flash’ it. What is the word? You might have to flash the card a couple of times. Invite a confident child to come to the front to do the same.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “What material or shape was used to make this?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Draw some shapes on the board and elicit their names from children. Remind them that they have to practise new vocabulary over and over in order to remember it. Explain that in today’s lesson children are going to talk about art they like and don’t like. First, ask them to listen carefully. Discuss some phrases they can use if they don’t understand a word or phrase. Remind children that it is polite to ask for repetition at the end, and not while somebody is talking. Encourage them to use the following phrases: What does [xxx] mean? I don’t understand [xxx]. Can you read it again, please? Next, read the model text provided, inviting a few children to take turns reading the sentences. Before sharing with the class, adapt the text so that it is true for you – perhaps refer to a drawing on the board that you did recently, or to one of your artistic hobbies. Ask children if there are any words or phrases they don’t understand. Check comprehension by asking follow-up questions: e.g. What art do I do? What do I draw? What shapes are there? Read the text again to the class. Try to make it as natural as possible. Now tell children that you want them to tell you about their favourite kind of art in a mini presentation. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about my favourite kind of art. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.153 Writing exs.A–C. Talk with the class about good presentation skills and what they can do to improve their public speaking.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text as a support and/or some sentence starters. Challenge more able children to give more details in their presentation about the art form they are talking about. For example, they could contrast it with their least favourite kind of art, or use a range of adjectives to describe shapes, colours, and how it makes them feel. Additional information: Week 32, Lesson 2 [Adapt the text so that it is true for you.] My favourite kind of art I like all kinds of art. I like taking photographs of my friends and family. I usually take photos on my phone. I haven’t got a camera. I also like drawing. I’m not very good, but I use shapes to help me.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have given a mini presentation about their favourite kind of art. They have practised using shape vocabulary.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to my favourite kind of art; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

1. Flashcard Relay — My favourite kind of art

Lesson-specific language focus: past and present

Original teaching source: Original warm-up: Play a game using the flashcards (or pictures) of the words from Week 32, Lesson 1. Show the cards one by one. Ask children to call out the words. Then take one card and very quickly ‘flash’ it. What is the word? You might have to flash the card a couple of times. Invite a confident child to come to the front to do the same.

Materials: past-and-present picture pairs and a timeline

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Flashcard Relay”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “In the past, people ___. Now, they ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about past and present.

YOU DO — game procedure: Teams take turns retrieving the correct flashcard after hearing a definition or example and using it in a sentence.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is yesterday in the past or the future?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about past and present.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “In the past, people ___. Now, they ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of past and present.
