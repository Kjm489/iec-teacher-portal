PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Do a warm-up activity. Ask children to sit in a circle in chairs. Give each child an art and craft word from the unit (e.g. ‘glue’, ‘scissors’, ‘paint’, ‘origami’, etc.). When you say a word (e.g. ‘scissors’), all children with that word must change places. As they do this, take away a chair. The last child standing is out. When you say ‘art and craft’, everyone must change places! Invite children who are no longer playing to take turns calling out a word. If you have a large class, play the game in two smaller groups.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know??”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about where do craft materials come from?. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Write sentences describing the different combinations of materials and decisions made in the game. Make up a chant about the natural resources and the things we can make with them.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Form mixed-ability groups for the final game so more confident children can explain the instructions to their peers and model natural resources vocabulary. Write the gapped sentence ‘I want to make a(n) ___ out of ___ and ___.’ on the board. Encourage more able children to explain why some natural materials might not work in some crafts and to suggest which materials would be better. 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have read about and recalled the natural origins of some art and craft materials. They have consolidated their understanding of these materials and their uses in a game.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to where do craft materials come from?; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
