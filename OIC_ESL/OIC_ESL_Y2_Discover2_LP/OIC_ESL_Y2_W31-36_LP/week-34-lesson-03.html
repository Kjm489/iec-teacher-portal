PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Introduce the game ‘Rock, paper, scissors’ to children. They might already be familiar with it in their own language. Demonstrate with another adult or a confident child first. First, practise the chant and actions together. You can make the scissors by moving two fingers on one hand; for rock, you show your fist; and for paper, you place your hand flat. Explain that scissors ‘beats’ paper because they can cut it, rock beats scissors because it blunts them, and paper beats rock because it wraps around the rock. After the final ‘1, 2, 3 …’ in the chant, children choose and do an action at the same to find out who ‘wins’. If they both choose the same action, they can replay the round. Put children into pairs to play the game. Who is the winner after three tries?

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know??”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Elicit from children what materials they need to do art and craft activities. Drill the lesson’s vocabulary carefully and play a flashcard game. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.182 and complete exs.A–B together. Now do a reading activity. Hand out the listening transcripts for ex.C in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.182. Read as a whole class or in pairs, then ask children to complete ex.D independently. As children work, write out the gap-fill activity on the board. Hold up some glue and ask: What is this for? Elicit: We use it to stick things together. Then ask the class to read the gapped sentences on the board and to complete them with the correct words. Review answers as a class. Now challenge children to write their own definition for a pencil sharpener. They may use dictionaries to support them. Put children into groups of three or four. Tell them to choose two art and craft materials and to think of something that they have in common. It can be anything (e.g. colour, size, use, etc.). Ask them to write down their findings, then turn over their notes. Explain the final speaking activity. One child in each group mentions two craft materials in a sentence: e.g. I choose glue and coloured pencils. The rest of the group has to try to guess/remember what the common factor is.

Think aloud, highlight key language and demonstrate an accurate answer before students try. Use gestures, visual evidence or a short example as appropriate.

CCQs: “What did I do first?” “What makes the example accurate?”

Work through one example together; students answer on mini-whiteboards or orally. Gradually remove the sentence starter.

ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about what do you need to make things?. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Make a papier mâché model using a range of art and craft materials. Put children into pairs to make a wordsearch for each other with the new vocabulary.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, put them into groups with more confident children. Use realia to support their understanding of any new vocabulary. More confident children can do the reading tasks independently. Fast finishers can use dictionaries to find more art and craft material words. 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have completed a range of reading and speaking activities about art and craft materials and how they are used.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to what do you need to make things?; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Art and craft’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and reading skills when testing their knowledge of art and craft materials and their uses.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
