PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Introduce children to ‘square breathing’ (or ‘box breathing’). Instruct them to breathe out slowly, expelling all air from their lungs. Tell them to slowly breathe in through their nose to the count of four. Count for them. Tell them to hold this breath for the count of four. Count for them. Tell them to slowly breathe all the air out again to the count of four. Count for them. Remind children that breathing like this is a very good thing to do, especially when they feel anxious, worried, nervous, or excitable. Explain that today’s lesson might feel challenging, so they can use this useful breathing strategy at regular intervals.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “What material or shape was used to make this?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Ask children if they know of any famous artworks, such as paintings. Present your chosen artwork to the class, answering the guiding questions and highlighting anything relevant as you go along – for instance, the different shapes that you can see in the artwork and why these are effective. Elicit from the class anything else you could say about the artwork. Encourage feedback. Now show children some more pictures of artworks. Ideally, you will have examples of different art forms from local and global artists, including women artists. Pass them around so that they can look at them closely. Take some comments about each one, but don’t spend too long on this. Tell children that they are going to choose one of these artworks in pairs. They are going to present it to the class like you did earlier. Elicit what kind of information they can talk about, referring back to your example presentation, and then write the questions on the board to guide their thoughts. Do a quick ‘square breathing’ exercise before children begin their preparations. This will help them to focus their attention, reflect on the task, and feel calmer. Work through one example together; students answer on mini-whiteboards or orally. Gradually remove the sentence starter.

ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about describing an artwork. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Do a reflection activity in which pairs identify what they think went well (in terms of their teamwork and final presentation), and what they would like to improve. Remind them to identify their partner’s strengths during this activity and to consider their own. Research another artwork by the same artist, or a similar artwork by a local artist.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Pair up similar abilities so you can spend more time supporting less confident children with their sentences, while more able children can support and challenge each other. Additional information: Week 32, Lesson 4 Guiding questions for children’s presentations What is the artwork? Who is the artist? Where do they come from? When did they make it? What shapes and colours can you see? What do they represent? Do you like the artwork? Say why.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have described an artwork in a short presentation, recalling shape vocabulary and expressing their likes and dislikes.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to describing an artwork; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
