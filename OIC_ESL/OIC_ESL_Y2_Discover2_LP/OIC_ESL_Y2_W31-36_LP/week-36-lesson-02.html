PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Study the unit project choices, model a good example and create a plan.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know?”

ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about model and plan extended activity. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Check that children have completed all the relevant Workbook and Activity Book activities recommended in the unit. Complete the KWL charts.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Recognize individual children’s strengths – for instance, those who struggle linguistically might excel in more creative activities. As they focus on these activities, offer praise while drip-feeding in useful topic vocabulary, expressions, and questions. For more able children, encourage them to do more. For example, they could provide more details in writing and speaking activities, or take the lead in a small-group creative activity.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity that has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to model and plan extended activity; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

1. Sort and Explain — Model and plan extended activity

Lesson-specific language focus: asking permission and sharing

Original teaching source: Original warm-up: Study the unit project choices, model a good example and create a plan.

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Sort and Explain”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Teams sort six lesson cards into two meaningful groups and orally explain the rule.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.

YEAR 2 Reflection and review
Week 36, Lessons 1–5: Reflection on ‘Art and craft’
Learning outcomes: Unit summary

Independent reflection
Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Art and craft’. 
 Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, and Writing. Lead a discussion with children about these skills. For example: Tell me an activity when we practised speaking in English. When we [played XX game], what skills did we use? Did we speak? Did we listen? What did you find easy/difficult? Why?
Help children to assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children who may find this self-reflective process quite challenging.
Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to at the end of the school year to track their progress and achievements.
Main activity
  Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity or activities. Some are designed for individual work and others are for group work. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.
Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:
Make a class book, with pictures and simple sentences, about art and craft activities.
Make a papier-mâché model of an animal of choice. Do this as a whole class or in small groups, then put children into pairs to write a set of instructions outlining the process.
Children make colourful masks for a story to act out. This might be a popular story in your location, or one that you are reading with them in class. Encourage them to think critically and to share ideas about the best way of making and attaching a mask.
Children choose and do a suitable art and craft activity for a special day. For example, this could be a photo frame for a special family day.
Children make friendship bracelets or another craft item. If appropriate in your context, they could sell them as a fundraiser.
Children create a set of greeting cards. This would make a great gift.
