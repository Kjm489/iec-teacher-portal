PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Ask children if they ever make homemade presents for others. Do you like making presents? What things can you make as presents? Have you made any presents recently? Who have you made presents for? Have you ever received a homemade present? Tell children that today they are going to read a short story about a homemade present.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Pre-teach/Elicit the lesson’s vocabulary using flashcards and/or realia. Put children into pairs. Show them the six strips of paper you have prepared for the first part of the story. Explain the running dictation activity. Say: You are going to work in pairs. One of you runs and reads the sentence on the strip of paper. You must remember the sentence, run back to your partner, and dictate it (say it to them out loud). They write it down. Swap roles for the next sentence. There are six sentences. Make sure they are in the correct order at the end! You might want to adjust these instructions according to your situation – for instance, if you would prefer children to walk and not run. To avoid children bumping into each other, display sets of sentences in different places around the classroom. Assign particular pairs to particular sets. Set a suitable time limit for children to complete the running dictation to the best of their ability. At the end of the activity, elicit the six sentences in the correct order. Write them on the board. Give pairs time to check their work, identify where mistakes (in spelling, in incorrect/missing words, and in chronology) have been made, and think about why. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about a sticky story!. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Encourage children to give the story a new title. Interpret the story in a role-play in small groups. Encourage children to add more details and/or characters. Rewrite the story with a different ending. Read the story and answer the questions in Oxford Discover SB 2 pp.178–180.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children in the running dictation, number the sentences so that they know the order. You could also give them gapped sentences to complete so they only need to find the missing word, instead of having to remember the whole sentence. Original review: Children have completed a running dictation activity, before reading a short story in detail. They have answered comprehension questions to check their understanding of the text.

1. True or False Corners — A sticky story!

Lesson-specific language focus: home and family

Original teaching source: Original warm-up: Ask children if they ever make homemade presents for others. Do you like making presents? What things can you make as presents? Have you made any presents recently? Who have you made presents for? Have you ever received a homemade present? Tell children that today they are going to read a short story about a homemade present.

Materials: room and family picture cards, a house diagram

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “True or False Corners”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “This is ___. We use it to ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about home and family.

YOU DO — game procedure: Read a source-based claim. Learners move to true or false and point to evidence before the answer is revealed.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is a kitchen a place for cooking?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about home and family.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “This is ___. We use it to ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of home and family.

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Art and craft’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and writing skills in a running dictation activity, in which they read sentences and share information in pairs. They will predict the content of and understand the main events and details in a short story.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
