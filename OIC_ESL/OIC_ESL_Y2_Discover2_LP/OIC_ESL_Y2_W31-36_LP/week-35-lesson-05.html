Measurable Learning Objectives

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Ask children to stand up. Indicate that each corner of the room represents a different natural material, such as ‘wood’, ‘wool’, ‘clay’, and ‘paper’. Say an item (e.g. ‘jumper’) and children should go to the correct corner (i.e. ‘wool’). Do some easy ones first. Then put children into groups of three and say some items that are made out of two or more things (e.g. ‘clay photo frame’ = ‘wood’ + ‘clay’).

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Keep children in the same groups of three from the introductory activity or form new ones. Explain that they are going to write instructions for how to make something – for instance, a photo frame or a badge. Show children the various craft examples you have brought in. Otherwise, encourage them to use their imagination or existing knowledge of craft projects. In their groups, give children time to choose what they are going to write instructions for. Check that they know how to make the items. Let them look at them closely, if necessary. First, ask children to talk together about how to do their craft project. You may hand out graphic organizers for them to organize their ideas, or they can use their whiteboards to sketch and note down key words and steps. Remind them to think about the order of instructions. Elicit sequence words. If you have more than one group writing instructions for the same craft project, place it where everyone can easily see it. Remind children of Week 35, Lesson 3, in which they studied a set of instructions for making a cake. Recall its format as a whole class, including the ‘You need’ list of equipment/ingredients, action words without the subject (imperative verbs) beginning each sentence, and numbered bullets. ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about writing instructions for a craft project. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Ask groups to create a final, illustrated draft of their instructions on display paper. Encourage children to write a set of instructions for another craft project. This time, they could do it independently. Choose a set of instructions and try to make the item. Are the instructions clear and correct?

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text to complete, or work together in an adult-led small group. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to consider other ways of doing the craft project, or perhaps decorating it in a different way.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have written instructions for a craft project of choice in small groups.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to writing instructions for a craft project; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
