6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

YEAR 2 Reflection and review
Week 33, Lessons 1–5: Reflection on ‘Shapes’
Learning outcomes: Unit summary

Independent reflection
Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Shapes’. 
 Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, and Writing. Lead a discussion with children about these skills. For example: Tell me an activity when we practised speaking in English. When we [played XX game], what skills did we use? Did we speak? Did we listen? What did you find easy/difficult? Why?
Help children to assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children who may find this self-reflective process quite challenging.
Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.
Main activity
  Explain that this week, children will participate in one or more extended activities in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.
Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:
Take children on a nature walk to look for and draw shapes outdoors.
Make a class book, with pictures and simple sentences, about shapes.
Encourage children to measure some shapes around school. Can they find the biggest and smallest shape? What is the most interesting shape they can find?
Write a story including at least two shapes.
Work together to make and play a homophone game. For example, children could draw pictures of the homophones and play ‘Snap!’, matching homophones together.
Plan and create an art exhibition. Agree on a theme together and create a variety of different artworks representing different forms (e.g. a drawing of a flower, a painting of a flower, a flower mobile, a mosaic, an origami flower, and a photograph). If there is time, children could also create a poster advertising their exhibition to other classes. Encourage them to write descriptions of their artwork to display underneath. This makes a wonderful group activity that could be set up in different ways. Some or all children could be the artists, some could be the curators, some could be the programme/ticket designers, and some could be the exhibition guides. Allow children to choose a role that best suits their interests and skills.

Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate and have done so over the week’s activities. They have participated in an extended activity that has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.
Differentiation
Recognize individual children’s strengths – for instance, those who struggle linguistically might enjoy creative or outdoors activities. As they focus on these activities, drip-feed useful topic vocabulary, expressions, and questions.
For more able children, encourage them to do more. For example, they could write more sentences in the class book and for the exhibition, and then help their less confident peers with their sentences.
