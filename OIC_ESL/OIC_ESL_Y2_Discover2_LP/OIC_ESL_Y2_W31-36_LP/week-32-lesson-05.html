PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Put children into small groups of four or five. Spell out an art form (e.g. p-a-i-n-t-i-n-g) and ask groups to make the letters with their bodies. Then ask children to sit down, write out the letters, and tell you the word. To increase the challenge level, say the letters in the wrong order. Ask children to solve the anagram to make an art form word.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “What material or shape was used to make this?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: On the board, write a list with commas after each word: e.g. I can see a rectangle, a square, an oval, and a crescent. Elicit how we can use commas to separate items in a list. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.183 and ask children to complete ex.F. Ask children to recall what they did last lesson (see Week 32, Lesson 4), and what they spoke about in their presentation. Explain that in today’s lesson they are going to write up their presentation in the form of a report. (If they want, children can write about a different artwork, although they will probably prefer to write about one that is familiar.) Think about how a report is presented. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.185 and read ‘The Starry Night report’. Elicit what kind of information is included in the art report. Using the guiding questions from Week 31, Lesson 4, and any other information children might have, give children time to write their report. They can use the input text to inspire their thinking. Remind them to include factual information, as well as their own opinions to express their likes and dislikes. Now ask children to swap their work with a partner to check for correct spelling and punctuation, including commas in a list. Hand out poster paper for children to write up their final drafts. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about writing an art report. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.161 ex.A. Write an art report about a different art form (e.g. a sculpture or an art installation).

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text, or write a group report in an adult-led small group. Prompt children to think about the ideas and shape words they can use. Fast finishers can exchange their pictures with a partner and write another art report.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have used commas to separate items in a list. They have applied their learning from the unit to write an art report about a familiar artwork.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to writing an art report; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

1. Venn Diagram Detectives — Writing an art report

Lesson-specific language focus: past and present

Original teaching source: Original warm-up: Put children into small groups of four or five. Spell out an art form (e.g. p-a-i-n-t-i-n-g) and ask groups to make the letters with their bodies. Then ask children to sit down, write out the letters, and tell you the word. To increase the challenge level, say the letters in the wrong order. Ask children to solve the anagram to make an art form word.

Materials: past-and-present picture pairs and a timeline

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Venn Diagram Detectives”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “In the past, people ___. Now, they ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about past and present.

YOU DO — game procedure: Partners place source examples into two overlapping categories and explain one similarity and one difference.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is yesterday in the past or the future?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about past and present.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “In the past, people ___. Now, they ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of past and present.
