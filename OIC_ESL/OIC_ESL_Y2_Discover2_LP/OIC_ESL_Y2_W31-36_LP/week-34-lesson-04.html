Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 4

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Ask children what they need for origami and elicit ‘paper’! As they learned about shapes in Weeks 31–32, show them a paper square and see if they can tell you the shape. Give a paper square to each child. Instruct them to fold the paper carefully in half and to open it out. Identify the corner, the fold, and the middle as a class. Explain that they will need these words for the main part of the lesson.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Show children one of your pre-made origami cat faces and explain that they are going to make one in today’s lesson. Elicit the words ‘ears’ and ‘face’. Look in your bag as though you are looking for the instructions, and then declare (dramatically) that you have forgotten them! Ask children if they think they can work it out themselves if you give them a cat face to look at and open. Hopefully, they will say ‘yes’! Divide the class into mixed-ability groups of four or five. Make sure that each group has at least one confident child. First, discuss some useful language to use in group work: e.g. Let’s fold like this. / We have to do … / How about …? / I don’t think that’s quite right. / What do you think? / Yes, that’s good! Write these phrases, or similar ones, on the board for children’s reference. Hand out a pre-made cat face to each group and another paper square to each child. Set children the task of exploring the folds and the corners to make their own cat face. Assign, or encourage children to nominate, a team leader in each group. They will make sure that everyone is working together well and has the opportunity to participate in a meaningful way. This activity will work best if children are working in lockstep together. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about an origami cat face. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Allow children to colour in their cat faces, adding eyes, a nose, and a mouth. They could also cut out and stick on some thin strips of paper for whiskers, or use pipe cleaners. Make a display of origami cat faces. Write out the instructions for making a cat face using sequence words. Find out how to make a different origami animal.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Mixed-ability groups will motivate less confident children to follow the lead of their more confident peers and participate. Ensure that there are extra paper squares if required. Challenge more able children by giving them a bigger or smaller square to make their cat face. Remind them to model recently learned vocabulary such as ‘fold’ and ‘corner’ when problem-solving in their groups.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have worked together in a group to make an origami cat face.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to an origami cat face; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
