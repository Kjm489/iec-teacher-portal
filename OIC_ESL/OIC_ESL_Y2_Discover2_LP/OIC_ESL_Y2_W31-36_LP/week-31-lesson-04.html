Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Draw a rectangle on the board. Invite a confident child to come to the front and indicate to the side and the corner. Then ask: Are all the sides the same length? Establish that two are shorter and two are longer. Check that the class understands the new vocabulary: ‘side’, ‘corner’, and ‘length’. Now tell children to look around their desks or tables. Can they see any rectangles? Perhaps the desk or table is a rectangle or a square. Challenge children to identify the smallest and biggest rectangle in the classroom.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “How many sides does this shape have? Which shape is it?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Tell children that they are going to read a text about shapes. Ask them to tell you what shapes they think will be in the text and in what order. For example, if the easiest shapes were first, which would they be? Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.170 (don’t focus on p.171 for now). First, ask children to quickly read the text to check their ideas. Discuss their observations as a class. Next, tell them to scan the text for any shape words. They can underline them or note them down. Then ask them to give you examples of nouns, verbs, and adjectives. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.172 and ask children to complete ex.B. Ask children to look around the classroom to identify any other shapes. Put them into pairs to make a list of the shapes and where they are: e.g. There’s a star on the wall. Hold a whole-class discussion and see if everyone has the same answers. Find out if children have found the shapes you hid before the lesson. Now ask children to stand up. Put them into groups of eight to ten. Explain that they are going to explore the properties of different shapes with their bodies. They will need to problem-solve in their groups to work out how to do this in the best way – for instance, a three-sided star will need six children. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about shapes around us. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.147 ex.C. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.167, play the video again in ex.A, and ask the class to identify the different shapes. Draw the shapes found in the classroom. For example, a ruler is a rectangle with two very long sides and two short sides. A child can draw this rectangle and then challenge a partner to guess what object the shape represents. If they guess correctly, they can fill in the details (i.e. the numbers and markings) to make it a ruler.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Give less confident children a choice of shape words to match to the definitions. Challenge more confident children to write a definition of a crescent or a spiral. Fast finishers can also read the text in SB 2 p.171.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have read about the properties of different shapes. They have explored shapes further in group work.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to shapes around us; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
