PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Ask children if they like it when people say nice and positive things about their work. Elicit why. Explain that this is called a ‘compliment’ (they don’t need to learn this word). Talk with the class about who often gives them compliments, and if they give compliments to others. If so, who do they tend to compliment? Discuss why it is important to show kindness in this way. Say that we all have strengths and are good at something. If they feel comfortable, encourage children to tell you what they think their strengths are, or what they think the strengths of the person sat next to them are. They can also write this down.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Introduce some homophones – two words that are pronounced the same but have different meanings. To illustrate, ask children to write the word ‘road’/‘rode’ on their whiteboards (or use a different example). Don’t give the meaning – just say the word and ask children to write it down. Now ask children to hold up their whiteboards on the count of three. Hopefully some children have written ‘road’, and others ‘rode’. Establish that both words are correct. They sound exactly the same, but are spelled differently and mean different things. Then draw a picture of a bag of flour and a picture of a flower. Elicit the word. Show both spellings on the board. Tell children that there are quite a few words in English that are like this. However, emphasize that we can tell which word somebody means from the context of the sentence (i.e. what it is about). 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about complimenting. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.153 Word Study ex.A. Create a useful homophone poster for the classroom. Ask children to note down one or two things they would like to be better at and to consider what they can do to improve. Remind them that lots of practice helps!

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, write a model role-play dialogue on the board. Challenge fast finishers to find some more homophones online and to add them to the list on the board.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have learned about homophones. They have practised giving compliments.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to complimenting; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

Materials: community helper picture cards and workplace cards

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Odd One Out”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “A ___ helps by ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about people and community.

YOU DO — game procedure: Present four source items. Teams identify the odd one out, name the grouping rule and defend a second possible answer.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Does a doctor help sick people?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about people and community.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “A ___ helps by ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of people and community.
