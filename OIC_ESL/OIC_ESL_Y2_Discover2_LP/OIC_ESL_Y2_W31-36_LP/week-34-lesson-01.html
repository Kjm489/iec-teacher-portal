PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Show children a homemade item and an item bought from a shop. Establish the difference between them. Ask children whether they prefer homemade or shop-bought for: firstly, clothes; secondly, games; and thirdly, food. Elicit that when we make something ourselves with our hands, we give it more value because we have taken time to get the materials/ingredients, to make it, and to understand how it is made. Sometimes we also save money! Personalize the discussion by asking children to tell you the last thing that they made, and whether they enjoyed making it. During this discussion, introduce the term ‘art and craft’ to describe creative activities.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know??”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Using the flashcards or realia, talk about some of the different things we can make. Sound out and drill the words carefully, asking children to listen and repeat as you say them. Say the words in different ways – very slowly, very quickly, loudly, and softly. Then mouth a word for children to identify and repeat aloud. Play a flashcard game. Stick the cards to the board face down, three by three (so you only need nine cards). Divide the class into two teams (noughts and crosses). Number the cards. Children must guess the card by asking: e.g. Is number 9 the calendar? If it is, that team wins the card, with the aim of getting three in a row – horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. Put children into pairs. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about what is fun about making things?. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete a KWL chart on the question ‘What is fun about making things?’. Make a class list of things children would like to make. Make some mini flashcards of the new vocabulary and play a game of ‘Happy families’.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, focus on the first discussion question in a small group. Spend more time playing the flashcard games to familiarize them with the new words. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to give more details in their answer – for instance, why did they make a particular thing and for whom?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have thought about how we can make different things with our hands and learned related vocabulary. They have practised reporting a discussion in small groups.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to what is fun about making things?; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

YEAR 2 Art and craft
Week 34, Lesson 1: What is fun about making things?
Learning outcomes: 2.1a, 2.1c, 2.1h, 2.2e

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Art and craft’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by learning new words to talk about different crafts.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
