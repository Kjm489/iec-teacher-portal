Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 3

Measurable success: Learners complete the original a food survey task and communicate one accurate response with appropriate evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Play a mime game to revise topic vocabulary. You mime and children guess what you’re doing. Do this with a confident child to demonstrate. Children play the game in pairs or small groups. Encourage them to remember whether the activity is healthy or unhealthy.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Keeping healthy?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about a food survey using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Complete all the surveys in the class (not just one). Look at the answers children’s surveys generated. Make conclusions, and either write or draw them.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, give them some questions to complete. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to ask more questions in their surveys. Challenge more confident children by asking them think about what makes an activity healthy.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have created and carried out a survey about healthy living.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “A food survey”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”

1. Mime and Guess — A food survey

Lesson-specific language focus: food and healthy choices

Original teaching source: Activate: Play a mime game to revise topic vocabulary. You mime and children guess what you’re doing. Do this with a confident child to demonstrate. Children play the game in pairs or small groups. Encourage them to remember whether the activity is healthy or unhealthy.

Materials: food picture cards and two labelled sorting mats

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Mime and Guess”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about food and healthy choices.

YOU DO — game procedure: One learner silently acts out a source action; partners identify it and say a complete sentence.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is an apple a fruit?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about food and healthy choices.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of food and healthy choices.
