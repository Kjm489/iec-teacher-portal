PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Ask children if they like their town or local area. Ask why, and elicit different places in town. As you do this, show the flashcards and pre-teach/elicit the words for different places around town. Ask children to tell you different things they can do in these places (e.g. post a letter and buy a stamp at the post office). Play a game. Mime doing an activity and children say where you are. Put children into groups and they continue playing.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Past and present?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: Ask children which is their favourite place in town and why. Tell them they are going to talk about this further in the past tense (what they did there). Hand out or present a map on the board. If you have added places, check that children know them. Go through the places with them. Ask children to choose three places on the map and keep them secret. They are going to say what they did without saying where they are: e.g. I saw some animals (zoo). You may wish to teach children, or check their understanding of, some useful irregular past simple verbs, such as ‘saw’, ‘ate’, ‘went’, ‘spoke’, ‘took’, ‘bought’, and ‘fed’ to help them make their sentences. Monitor actively and praise children for their efforts. Children say what they did and their partner guesses the three places they visited. They then swap roles. Ask children to tell you where their partners went: e.g. Guido, where did Mina go? She went to the supermarket and bought some food, then she … and so on. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about i went around town using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Encourage children to write down what they did in their imaginary place. They can compare their activities with their partners: e.g. We both went to the park, but I took a photo and Lin fed the ducks. Illustrate a day in town.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, provide a completed map with suggested activities at each place. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to think of more activities that they could do at each place and to use a variety of past tense verbs.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have exchanged information about different activities they did in different places around town. They have learned and practised using irregular past simple verbs.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “I went around town”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”

Week 8, Lesson 2: I went around town

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Past and present’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by exchanging information about what they did in their town with their partners.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.


Introductory activity
Ask children if they like their town or local area. Ask why, and elicit different places in town. As you do this, show the flashcards and pre-teach/elicit the words for different places 
around town. Ask children to tell you different things they can do in these places (e.g. post a letter and buy a stamp at the post office). Play a game. Mime doing an activity and children say where you are. Put children into groups and they continue playing.

Main activity
Ask children which is their favourite place in town and why. Tell them they are going to talk about this further in the past tense (what they did there). Hand out or present a map on the board. If you have added places, check that children know them. Go through the places with them. Ask children to choose three places on the map and keep them secret. They are going to say what they did without saying where they are: e.g. I saw some animals (zoo). You may wish to teach children, or check their understanding of, some useful irregular past simple verbs, such as ‘saw’, ‘ate’, ‘went’, ‘spoke’, ‘took’, ‘bought’, and ‘fed’ to help them make their sentences. Monitor actively and praise children for their efforts. Children say what they did and their partner guesses the three places they visited. They then swap roles. Ask children to tell you where their partners went: e.g. Guido, where did Mina go? She went to the supermarket and bought some food, then she … and so on. Pre-teach the questions: Where did you go? and What did you do there?. Children get into groups. They stand up and mingle, trying to find someone who went to the same places and did the same things as them. Hold a whole-class feedback session, and encourage children to think about where else they would like to go and other things they could do in the places that they’ve already chosen.

Additional tasks

Differentiation
To support less able children, provide a completed map with suggested activities at each place. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to think of more activities that they could do at each place and to use a variety of past tense verbs.
