PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Practise regular past tense verbs. Say a verb in the infinitive. Then ask children to say it in the past tense. Explain to children that they have to practise certain things over and over in order to remember them. Tell children you are going to act out three things that happened to you yesterday. Do this and elicit the sentences: e.g. Yesterday, you walked to school. Hand out some verbs for children to use if they want to. Put children in pairs – one mimes, and the other says what happened yesterday. Encourage children to use full sentences: e.g. Yesterday, you washed the dishes. Don’t just say ‘washed’.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Past and present?”

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Past and present’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by reading and talking about a text.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.


Introductory activity
Practise regular past tense verbs. Say a verb in the infinitive. Then ask children to say it in the past tense. Explain to children that they have to practise certain things over and over in order to remember them. Tell children you are going to act out three things that happened to you yesterday. Do this and elicit the sentences: e.g. Yesterday, you walked to school. Hand out some verbs for children to use if they want to. Put children in pairs – one mimes, and the other says what happened yesterday. Encourage children to use full sentences: e.g. Yesterday, you washed the dishes. Don’t just say ‘washed’.

Main activity
Briefly recap the main idea versus details structure from Week 7, Lesson 2. Tell children you are going to give them a short text. Put them into groups of three. 
Give one of the three texts to each group (distributing them roughly evenly: e.g. three groups have ‘Communication’, three groups have ‘News’, three groups have ‘Travel’). As a group, they underline the main idea and the details of their texts. Tell children that they need to remember as much as they can of their text for a game they’re going to play. This is a stretch zone activity, so ensure that children understand it’s fun and not a test. Encourage children to read the text together and to help each other remember it. They can turn the paper over and see if they can recall the information. Number each child in each group one to three. Then invite all the ones to sit together, all the twos, and all the threes. Children share what they can remember from their texts, so that everybody has read one text in depth and listened to classmates talking about the other two texts. Encourage the listening children to retell the information using phrases such as ‘So you 
said …’. This helps everybody to remember what was said. Children return to their original groups. Tell them you’re going to ask some questions about the texts. The first hand in the air can answer, and if they get it right, they get a point. Make the activity fun. Ask questions about the texts: e.g. How did people communicate in the past? Round up by asking children to reflect on how well they remembered their texts.

Additional tasks
Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.40–41. Do the same activity with different texts – perhaps three short stories. Write a class story. In groups, each child writes a sentence including either ‘communication’, ‘news’, or ‘travel’. Make the sentences into a story. Illustrate the stories.

Learning review
Children have read a short text, memorized it, and shared it with their classmates.

Differentiation
To support less able children, give them some questions to guide their retelling of the text. Let them write down some key words. Adapt the ‘Additional information’ text in line with class ability. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write a short summary of the aspects of past life which they found the most interesting. Additional information: Week 7, Lesson 4 In the past Communication: In the past, people posted letters to share news with each other. At first, people on horses delivered letters, and then people used trains or boats. Nowadays, planes move letters from place to place. We can also use technology to talk to our family and friends. News: Long ago, people talked to each other to find out about events in their village or town. Then people shared these stories in newspapers for others to read. Later, people listened to news stories on the radio. Then people watched them on TV. Today, we watch or listen to the news on the internet. Travel: In the past, people walked everywhere. Can you believe it? Sometimes they travelled by horse and cart, or by train or boat. It was slow and journeys were very long. Today, we can travel by car, train, and plane. Nowadays, travel is fast!
