PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Tell children you are going to tell them a story. Show them a picture of immigrants arriving on Ellis Island, USA. Explain what the place is, what it was used for, and where it is. Ask: What can you see? When did this happen – in the past or now? Why do you think that? Show the remaining pictures to pre-teach the rest of the vocabulary.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Past and present?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: Ask children to take a piece of paper and divide it into six. Tell them that you are going to read six parts of a story about immigrants arriving on the island. After each part, you will pause, and they will draw a picture. Read the first part of the story to children. Stop and say: Now draw a picture. Children draw a picture. When everyone has finished, continue with the next part, and so on, until you have told the whole story and children have drawn six pictures. Challenge children to retell the story in pairs by looking at their pictures. Prompt them with questions. Refer children to Oxford Discover SB 2 pp.58–59. Encourage children to read as much of the text as they can and compare what they heard, drew, and read. Ask children if they liked the story. Can they explain to you why they liked it (or didn’t like it)? Put children into groups. They are going to act out the story. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about great grandparents using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Complete the comprehension activities in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.60 and WB 2 pp.48–49. Make some changes to the story, retell it, and see if their partner can spot the differences.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, provide pictures to illustrate the key words as support. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to describe people and places in the story.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have listened to, read, and performed a story set in the past.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “Great grandparents”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”

In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by sequencing events in a story.
