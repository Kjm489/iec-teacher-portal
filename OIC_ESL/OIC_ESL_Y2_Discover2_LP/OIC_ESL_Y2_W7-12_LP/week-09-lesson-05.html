Measurable success: Learners complete the original share, reflect and assess task and communicate one accurate response with appropriate evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Present a project, assess and reflect.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Past and present?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: Use the original Past and present reflection and review instructions as the source for this session. Independent reflection Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Past and present’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achieveme

Think aloud to show how to identify evidence, pronounce key words and produce a complete answer. Contrast one correct and one incorrect example. CCQs: “What tells us this answer is correct?” “Which detail in the source supports it?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Whole-class scaffold: Work through the first part of the original activity together. Use the original Past and present reflection and review instructions as the source for this session. Use visuals, chunk instructions, give response options, then fade the model. ICQs: “Do we work together first?” “Where do we find the answer?” CCQs: “How can we check our response?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about share, reflect and assess using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Choose a unit-related project or portfolio task; adapt to the class.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: For children who need more support, provide sentences, questions, and words, either on the board or in gapped texts. Pair them with more able children. They may look back at their work to help them, or produce less. Give them choices: e.g. ask: Would you prefer to say or write for this activity? Challenge more able children to write and say more sentences, providing additional details and using new language and grammatical forms learned.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have consolidated their knowledge and learning on the topic of ‘Past and present’.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “Share, reflect and assess”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”

Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Sort and Explain”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Teams sort six lesson cards into two meaningful groups and orally explain the rule.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.

Learning outcomes: Unit summary 

Independent reflection Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Past and present’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements. Main activity Explain that this week, children will participate in extended activities in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas: Write a letter to a friend or family member in a different town or country. Ask them to reply by letter. Make a poster about life now and life in the past. Hold an ‘In the past’ week, and ask children to bring in things from the past. They write exhibition labels and create posters to display around the school, inviting other year groups to view their exhibition. Create a review game. Draw a racetrack on the board or provide children with a blank racetrack. Ask them to think of some questions and vocabulary from the past two weeks. They can look at their books to help them, if necessary. Questions can be written or pictorial. They play the game themselves, testing their knowledge of the unit and practising new language learned. Children read picture books or stories set in the past. After reading, instruct them to complete a review card about them, saying whether or not they recommend the story, what the story is about, what they learned, and how it made them feel. Children revisit their KWL charts from Week 7, Lesson 1. Additional task Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.43–46, p.50, and p.53.
