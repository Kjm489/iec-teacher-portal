Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 2

Measurable success: Learners complete the original doing sports task and communicate one accurate response with appropriate evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Make up a movement sequence and ask children to copy you: e.g. stand up, step to the right, jump three steps to the left, do four star jumps. Ask children how they feel. They will probably feel energized. Ask what other reasons there are for moving our bodies (strong bones, feeling good, feeling happy, etc.).

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Keeping healthy?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: Write ‘Sports’ on the board. Ask children to tell you the sports they know and add their words to the board. You could use flashcards or projected pictures. Pre-teach or elicit any words that children didn’t mention. Refer to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.114 ex.D. Children listen to the conversations and put the activities in order. They listen again and circle can or can’t. Hold a whole-class feedback session. In pairs, children discuss which activities require the most and least movement. Ask a confident child: Can you play tennis? Do you go skiing? Ask children to share which activities they would like to do. Encourage them to find out about a new activity or sport through books or the internet. Give them some headings to guide their research, such as ‘Number of players’, ‘Equipment’, ‘Where it is practised’ (e.g. a field, a pool, or a track). They write about their chosen activity or sport. If you don’t have the facility to provide research materials, children can write about a sport that everyone knows, then their friends try to guess what it is. Hold a whole-class feedback session.

Think aloud to show how to identify evidence, pronounce key words and produce a complete answer. Contrast one correct and one incorrect example. CCQs: “What tells us this answer is correct?” “Which detail in the source supports it?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Whole-class scaffold: Work through the first part of the original activity together. Write ‘Sports’ on the board. Ask children to tell you the sports they know and add their words to the board. You could use flashcards or projected pictures. Pre-teach or elicit any words that children didn’t mention. Refer to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.114 ex.D. Children listen to the conversations and put the activities in order. They listen again and circle can or can’t. Hold a whole-class feedback session. In pairs, children discuss which activities require the most and least movement. Ask a confident child: Can you play tennis? Do you go skiing? Ask children to share which activities they would like to do. Encourage them to find out about a new activity or sport through books or the internet. Give them some headings to guide their research, such as ‘Number of players’, ‘Equipment’, ‘Where it is practised’ (e.g. a field, a pool, or a track). They write about their chosen activity or sport

Use visuals, chunk instructions, give response options, then fade the model. ICQs: “Do we work together first?” “Where do we find the answer?” CCQs: “How can we check our response?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about doing sports using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.98. Draw a picture of themselves doing their favourite activity, and write more about it. Think about ‘when’, ‘where’, ‘with’, and ‘who’ as a guide.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, provide some information about a new activity or sport in a gapped text. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think of other sports and activities to write about. They could ask their friends about equipment needed for the different sports they do: e.g. Do you need a ball? No, I need a surfboard for surfing. You could also ask them to think about the difference in meaning between can do and do (can do = have the ability to; do = you do it, probably on a regular basis).

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have learned new sports and activities vocabulary, carried out a listening task, and researched and written about a new sport/activity.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “Doing sports”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”
