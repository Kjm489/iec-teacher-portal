Measurable Learning Objectives

Measurable success: Learners complete the original what foods do you like to mix? task and communicate one accurate response with appropriate evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Tell children: Today, we’re going to learn the STOP technique. Ask if they have any idea what it might mean. S = stop what you’re doing T = take a breath O = observe what is happening P = proceed (continue) Practise with children. They busy themselves doing something, then signal for them to stop, breathe, observe, and then proceed. Elicit that this is a good technique to use to feel calmer, especially when things get busy.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Keeping healthy?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: Show children either flashcards of food or actual food you have brought in. Before bringing in food, consider any possible allergies within the class. Ask them to tell you which foods they like to mix: e.g. Do you like jam on your cucumber? Do you like milk on your cereal? Have some fun, and elicit both normal and silly combinations. Ask children to think of foods that are always mixtures, such as salad, soup, curry, pasta. Refer children to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.42 exs.C–D. If you brought in foods, ask children to imagine different combinations. Encourage them to consider what the food is going to taste like, such as sour, sweet, bitter – or it may taste delicious or disgusting! They discuss their reasons for their thoughts. Children write some sentences using the following model: We want to mix … and … We think it tastes … and …. Let children mix the foods and taste them. Were their predictions correct? If you didn’t bring in foods, children might be able to do this at home. At this point, be mindful that children may need to STOP. Children work in groups, asking each other: Do you like … and …? 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about what foods do you like to mix? using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.34. Make a class (or group) book of food combinations. Divide pages in half horizontally, so that the top and bottom are different. Play with different combinations.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with some examples of food combinations that always work, such as chocolate and orange. Then suggest ones that don’t work as a combination, such as candy and carrots. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think about where they might have their food combinations (e.g. a sandwich, a smoothie, or a pizza topping).

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have considered different food combinations and tested their predictions.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “What foods do you like to mix?”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Keeping healthy’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by saying food combinations that they think go together, and then trying them out.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
