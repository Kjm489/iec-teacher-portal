Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 3

Measurable success: Learners complete the original the hare and the tortoise task and communicate one accurate response with appropriate evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Ask children if they know any stories with animals as the main characters, and then ask if they know a story with a hare and a tortoise. Use flashcards or pictures to elicit or pre-teach ‘hare’ and ‘tortoise’. Children may know other Aesop’s Fables. Explain that these fables are about animals and always have a ‘moral’ – they teach us something.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Keeping healthy?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: You may use Rabbit on the Run or source an alternative version of the traditional story. Challenge children to read quickly and tell you who won the race. Then ask children to read more slowly and to tell you why Tortoise won the race (because he was slow and steady, and didn’t stop). You could also ask: Why did Hare lose the race? Elicit how Hare and Tortoise saw the race in a different way (Hare was boasting. Tortoise knew he would never win running, but hoped that Hare would take a rest, which he did.) Encourage children to discuss their views and perspectives on the fable. Help them to understand that not everybody may have the same viewpoint. In some versions of this story, Hare begins by boasting. Take a moment to talk with children about boasting. Ask: Why do people boast? Is it important to tell people how good you are at something? Use visuals, chunk instructions, give response options, then fade the model. ICQs: “Do we work together first?” “Where do we find the answer?” CCQs: “How can we check our response?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about the hare and the tortoise using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Read another of Aesop’s Fables. Design a poster advertising the race between Hare and Tortoise. Act out the story or create four tableaux vivant from different parts of the story.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, give them question starters for the pairwork activity. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write a description or a character profile of either Hare or Tortoise.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have read a fable and learned about the importance of taking your time.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “The Hare and the Tortoise”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”

1. Two Clues, One Answer — The Hare and the Tortoise

Lesson-specific language focus: animals and their abilities

Original teaching source: Activate: Ask children if they know any stories with animals as the main characters, and then ask if they know a story with a hare and a tortoise. Use flashcards or pictures to elicit or pre-teach ‘hare’ and ‘tortoise’. Children may know other Aesop’s Fables. Explain that these fables are about animals and always have a ‘moral’ – they teach us something.

Materials: animal picture cards, action-verb cards and the original animal examples

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Two Clues, One Answer”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “An animal can ___, but it cannot ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about animals and their abilities.

YOU DO — game procedure: Give two clues about a lesson concept; pairs predict, justify and compare with the original source.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Can a whale swim? Can it fly?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about animals and their abilities.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “An animal can ___, but it cannot ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of animals and their abilities.
