PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Sit children in a circle. Tell them you are going to give them a name and they must remember it. The names are ‘lemon’, ‘sugar’, ‘water’, ‘jug’, and ‘spoon’. When you say their name, those children swap places. As you do this, remove one chair so that the last child is out. When you say ‘lemonade’, everyone changes place. Continue until you have a winner, or choose the right moment to stop. Ask if anyone has ever made or tried lemonade.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Keeping healthy?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: Elicit or pre-teach the vocabulary and play some flashcard games. You can say the verb and children complete with the noun: e.g. ‘drink …’ (‘lemonade’ or ‘milk’ or ‘water’). Ask: Is drinking lemonade healthy? Elicit that it isn’t very healthy but it’s OK to drink sometimes (as part of a balanced diet). Go through the rest of the vocabulary, establishing which is healthy and which isn’t so healthy. Ask children to copy down the words. They categorize them into ‘healthy’ or ‘unhealthy’ / ‘not so healthy’. Let them do this individually first. After, they can compare with a partner. Then hold a whole-class feedback session. Ask: Is drinking lemonade always unhealthy? Discuss with children about why it’s unhealthy (sugar, preservatives [‘nasties’], etc.). Ask if homemade lemonade is healthy. Put children into small groups. Hand out the lemonade recipe, ask them to read it, and to get the utensils and ingredients. Before beginning this part of the lesson, ensure that no children have allergies to any of the ingredients or materials about to be used. 6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, pair them with more able children to make lemonade. Ask them to choose just one item that is healthy and one that is unhealthy (rather than categorizing all of them). As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write out their favourite lemonade quantities (e.g. sweeter, sourer, etc.). They can also find and compare other homemade lemonade recipes.
