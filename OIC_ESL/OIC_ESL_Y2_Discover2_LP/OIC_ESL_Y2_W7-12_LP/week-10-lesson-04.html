Measurable Learning Objectives

Measurable success: Learners complete the original role-playing healthy living task and communicate one accurate response with appropriate evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Ask children to stand up and do five star jumps, if they are able. Say some activities that have come up so far this week. If they are healthy, children don’t need to do anything. If they are unhealthy, they must counterbalance it by doing five more star jumps. Invite a confident child to call out some activities. Put children into small groups and they do the activity themselves.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Keeping healthy?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: Tell children you have a friend who loves eating chips and drinking sugary lemonade. You don’t think it’s healthy. Ask children what they think. What do they think you can say to your friend? They’ll tell you to tell your friend to stop (or do star jumps!). Say: But my friend goes to school, and the food is chips and the drink is lemonade. Elicit that your friend could bring food from home or drink water. If necessary, allow for a conversation about it not always being easy to just change habits, depending on the age or maturity of children. Return to the topic of your friend, and say that you want to give them some ideas. Elicit language for making suggestions (in a nice way!): e.g. Why don’t you …? How about …? What about …? Tell children they are going to make up a role-play about a friend with a ‘healthy living’ problem (be sensitive about this). Either hand out some role-play ideas to children (see ‘Additional information’ page) or encourage them to think of their own situations. Talk to them about being polite, as well as using the language of suggestion. Put children into groups, and monitor to make sure everybody is contributing and has a part. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about role-playing healthy living using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Do another role-play on a different theme. Draw a storyboard of children’s role-play. If you recorded children’s role-plays, play them back and ask them to see if they would do anything differently.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: Encourage more confident children to guide less confident children through the role-play. Challenge more able children by asking them to give reasons for making changes. Additional information: Week 10, Lesson 4 Role-play prompts Your friend is eating a big bag of sweets. Your friend is drinking sugary lemonade. Your friend doesn’t want to play outside. Your friend goes to bed late and feels tired at school. Your friend …

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have created and performed a role-play about healthy living. They have learned how to politely make suggestions.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “Role-playing healthy living”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”

Materials: food picture cards and two labelled sorting mats

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Flashcard Relay”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about food and healthy choices.

YOU DO — game procedure: Teams take turns retrieving the correct flashcard after hearing a definition or example and using it in a sentence.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is an apple a fruit?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about food and healthy choices.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of food and healthy choices.
