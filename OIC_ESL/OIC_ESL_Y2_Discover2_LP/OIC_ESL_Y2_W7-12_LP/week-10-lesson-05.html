Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 5

Measurable success: Learners complete the original traffic light plates task and communicate one accurate response with appropriate evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Tell children what you ate the day before. Ask them if they think it was a healthy food intake. Encourage them to think about why or why not, and elicit what it means to have a ‘balanced diet’. Invite a confident child to tell the class what they ate. Be sensitive about being judgemental and explain this to children.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Keeping healthy?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: Show children the pictures of healthy and unhealthy foods. Check that they know what they are in English. Try to include some local food items. Hand out three paper plates or three paper plate handouts to each group of three. Ask children to colour each plate (or draw a coloured line around each plate) in red, yellow, and green. On the board, write: ‘I can eat lots of these foods.’ (green), ‘I can eat these foods sometimes.’ (yellow), ‘I can only eat these foods occasionally.’ (red). Tell children that they are going to draw or write the names of the foods on the correct plate, based on their understanding of how healthy each food item is. If you brought in magazines, children can cut out more foods and stick them on to the correct plate. Ask children to think about the best way to complete the group task. This could be that each child completes one plate, or that they plan their ideas first, etc. Children complete their plates and write a simple description using topic vocabulary learned this week.

Think aloud to show how to identify evidence, pronounce key words and produce a complete answer. Contrast one correct and one incorrect example. CCQs: “What tells us this answer is correct?” “Which detail in the source supports it?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Whole-class scaffold: Work through the first part of the original activity together. Show children the pictures of healthy and unhealthy foods. Check that they know what they are in English. Try to include some local food items. Hand out three paper plates or three paper plate handouts to each group of three. Ask children to colour each plate (or draw a coloured line around each plate) in red, yellow, and green. On the board, write: ‘I can eat lots of these foods.’ (green), ‘I can eat these foods sometimes.’ (yellow), ‘I can only eat these foods occasionally.’ (red). Tell children that they are going to draw or write the names of the foods on the correct plate, based on their understanding of how healthy each food item is. If you brought in magazines, children can cut out more foods and stick them on to the correct plate. Ask children to think about the best way to complete the group task. This could be that each child completes one plate, or that they plan their ideas f

Use visuals, chunk instructions, give response options, then fade the model. ICQs: “Do we work together first?” “Where do we find the answer?” CCQs: “How can we check our response?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about traffic light plates using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Design a weekly meal plan. Think about what children have eaten so far today. Which ‘plate’ does most of their food belong to? Hopefully, this should be green or yellow! Think about food choices children have. For example, for most children, they will have to eat what their parents or carers give them. Their choices can be about drinks at school, sweets, etc.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text for the writing task. Put a word bank of food items on the board for them to copy. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to create a plate of food they ate one day last week.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have considered how healthy different food items are and sorted them into groups in a team activity. They have written about the contents of each plate.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “Traffic light plates”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of food and healthy choices.
