Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 4

Measurable success: Learners complete the original can you say that again? task and communicate one accurate response with appropriate evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate: Tell children you are going to say some sentences about what you did yesterday, but they are backwards. They need to listen and sort them into the right order with their partner: e.g. I ate lunch, I watched TV, I had breakfast, I travelled to school, I received an email. Then instruct pairs to do the same: one child says three things they did the day before in the wrong order, and their partner has to work out the correct order.

ICQs: “Are we sharing ideas first or writing a final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “What is the lesson about?” “Which example belongs to Past and present?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher models the original task: Ask a confident child to tell you what their friend did. Pretend that you don’t understand and say: Can you say that again, please? Keep doing this, but using different phrases, for example: Pardon? What was that? Could you say that again? Sorry? I didn’t get that. Elicit the phrases you used from children and write them on the board. Explain to children that these are polite ways of asking somebody to repeat something. Put children into groups and hand out card and marker pens. Each group chooses one phrase to write in big writing. Encourage them to organize themselves to carry out the task successfully. When children have finished, display the posters. Then, when needed, you can indicate to the phrases and children can choose one to use. Praise children for working consistently throughout the lesson. Tell children they are going to listen to a song about activities. Ask children to listen and write down the activities. You can also write out three verbs on the board, such as ‘visit’, ‘travel’, ‘climb’. When children hear them, they stand up. Refer children to SB 2 p.61 and sing the song. Children ask and answer in pairs. Encourage them to ask for repetition.

Think aloud to show how to identify evidence, pronounce key words and produce a complete answer. Contrast one correct and one incorrect example. CCQs: “What tells us this answer is correct?” “Which detail in the source supports it?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Use visuals, chunk instructions, give response options, then fade the model. ICQs: “Do we work together first?” “Where do we find the answer?” CCQs: “How can we check our response?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner practice: Pupils take turns explaining or questioning each other about can you say that again? using the original lesson materials. Partners identify a detail from the task, explain it, then switch roles.

ICQs: “Do both partners speak?” “Do you change roles?” CCQs: “Does your answer agree with the picture, text or example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Learners independently complete an age-appropriate product from the original task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.51 exs.C–D. Role-play a shop scene where someone is asking for repetition.

Collect an oral answer, short written response, labeled picture, comparison, sequence, or review project as appropriate to the original learning outcome.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: visual choices, word bank, teacher sentence model and rehearsal. Developing: complete response with a supporting detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison or follow-up question.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, give them a handout with set phrases for asking for repetition to consult during their English lessons. For the poster, give them a set phrase to copy. As an extension task for more able children, ask them listen for instances when people ask for repetition.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review original goal: Children have revised the past simple tense, practised asking and answering questions, and learned how to ask for repetition.

Exit ticket: Respond independently to a new question or visual directly related to “Can you say that again?”. Mark independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: “What have we learned about this topic?”
