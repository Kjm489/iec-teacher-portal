5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

1. Odd One Out — Independent extended activity

Lesson-specific language focus: asking permission and sharing

Original teaching source: Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Independent extended activity?”

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Odd One Out”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Present four source items. Teams identify the odd one out, name the grouping rule and defend a second possible answer.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 2 Reflection and review
Week 15, Lessons 1–5: Reflection on ‘School subjects’
Learning outcomes: Unit summary

Independent reflection
Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘School subjects’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.
Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.
Main activity
Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.
  Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas:
Make a curriculum mobile (see Oxford Discover SB 2 p.145). For this, children make a mobile and write about different subjects they like. They write what they like about them and what they have learned in them recently. They can then hang their decorated mobiles around the classroom.
Make a maths ‘think board’. A think board can be a page divided into three that shows a maths problem in different ways. Children have to draw the problem, write the problem, and represent the problem in a concrete or abstract way.
Make an infographic about how to solve maths problems.
Make a zigzag book about activities children do at certain times, writing present continuous sentences.
Write a mystery story in the past tense involving a maths problem.
Make a spinner with a variety of maths problems, such as a word subtraction problem.
Children can ask their parents or carers about their favourite school subjects and display the information in a bar chart. Do they notice similarities and differences?
Children can test each other by creating a secret code with a message to be cracked. Letters of the alphabet = symbols, numbers, emojis, etc. They can work in pairs or individually for this task.
Use the computer to find out about schools in different countries, focusing on the different subjects children study in those countries. 
Learning review
Children have consolidated their knowledge and learning on the topic of ‘School subjects’.
Differentiation
For children who need more support provide sentences, questions, and words, either on the board or in gapped texts. Pair them with more able children. They may look back at their work to help them, or produce less. Give them choices: e.g. ask: Would you prefer to speak or write for this activity?
Challenge more able children to write and say more sentences, providing additional details and using new language and grammatical forms learned.
