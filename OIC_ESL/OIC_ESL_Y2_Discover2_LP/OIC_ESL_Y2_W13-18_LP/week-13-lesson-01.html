PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: To introduce the topic, ask children: Where are we? (School!) Why do we come to school? (To learn!) What do we do at school? (Lots of things!) In pairs, ask children to think of all the things they’ve done at school this week or today (e.g. had lunch with friends, played in the playground, listened to a story, learned Maths, etc.). Ask children if they enjoy coming to school and why. Write their reasons on the board. If there are children in class who have been to school in a different country, ask them to share their experiences.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to What do we do at school??”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Name a subject and describe what students learn or do. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Using flashcards or pictures, introduce the lesson’s vocabulary. Play some flashcard games. Stick the cards onto the board. Once children have listened and repeated, turn the cards over and number them. Children guess by asking: Is it Maths? Is it History? Repeat until all the cards have been turned over. Put children into pairs to play the game themselves. One child notes down the number of the card they are thinking of in secret, and the other child has to guess what the card is. Now tell children you are going to play a video about what they do at school. First, ask them to predict the activities they are going to see. Source a suitable video online. Alternatively, you can pre-prepare a short script to read aloud and mime the different actions. Then play the audio only. Children check their answers. Finally, show them the video again with the audio. On the board, draw a table or infographic showing information about school. Ask: What can we see on this? Elicit that it gives information. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use school-subject picture cards and timetable samples to explain, question or solve a new example related to What do we do at school?. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: Is this an activity or a school subject? What clue tells you?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Make a personal timetable, subject preference response or illustrated school activity. Encourage children to draw a picture of themselves at school and write a sentence: e.g. This is me. I am in the playground with my friends. Make an infographic about online school learning. Give children a couple of vocabulary items. Ask them to make a role-play using the words (e.g. ‘Art’ and ‘meet our friends’).

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, pair them with a more confident child. Provide a partially completed infographic. As an extension task for more able children, give them a choice of infographic designs to complete.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have learned new topic vocabulary and produced an infographic about what they do at school.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to What do we do at school? without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: Is this an activity or a school subject? What clue tells you?
