PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Revise categories from the previous lesson by playing a game. Children get into groups of five. Say a category and everyone in that group must think of an item from it. You can make it competitive by awarding points, or just play for fun. Show children the road STOP sign from your country. Elicit what it means, and that the red border indicates something you must do. Tell children that this is a ‘rule’. Ask them if they think rules are important and why. Guide the conversation to getting along with people and how we have rules for reasons.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Following rules?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Model an appropriate rule or response in a familiar situation. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Show the picture of the classroom first. Ask children to identify it and ask them: What are some of our rules? Elicit things they must do and must not do. Show the other places and identify them and their rules. Tell children that the pictures are to accompany a text. What do they think the text is going to be about? Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 pp.90–91. Read the text aloud for children to follow, or ask children to read quickly by themselves and check if any of their ideas were mentioned. Ask children to re-read and to see if there are any other rules that are mentioned. Ask children: What are the main points of the text? Where are the rules? What rules are there? Who makes rules? Why? Elicit the main points of each text. Write them on the board. Say some rules and ask children to tell you which place you’re referring to: e.g. You mustn’t run by the water. (Swimming pool) Challenge children to write an extra rule for one of the places. They read it out and their classmates have to guess where it is. As a wrap-up, refer back to the i

CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful? “Can you point to the evidence?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use scenario pictures, classroom rule cards and role cards to explain, question or solve a new example related to Following rules. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Write, illustrate or role-play a practical rule and its consequence. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.76–77. Choose three new words to remember. Copy them into children’s vocabulary books and illustrate them, or write a new sentence including these words. Make one of the rules into a traffic sign – a red border indicates you must do it. Draw any traffic signs children see on their way to school.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, provide a gapped text to write some ideas for rules. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write more rules for the places.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have predicted the content of a short text and read it to check their ideas. They have identified the main points.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Following rules without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?
