Partners use unit flashcards, student work samples and a project planner to explain, question or solve a new example related to Guided work and feedback. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: What evidence shows you can use this language? What will you improve?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete and present the assigned stage of the unit reflection project. Offer an optional extension or portfolio reflection.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: Use paired rehearsal and reduce the support once pupils demonstrate understanding.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Invite pupils to explain what they learned and check their response against the success criteria.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Guided work and feedback without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: What evidence shows you can use this language? What will you improve?

YEAR 2 Reflection and review
Week 18, Lessons 1–5: Reflection on ‘Let’s follow rules’
Learning outcomes: Unit summary

Independent reflection
Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Let’s follow rules’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
Children can discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.
Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.
Main activity
Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.
  Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas:
Create a zoo or an animal sanctuary using recyclable materials. Invite children to bring in toy animals and write information cards about them. Using cardboard boxes, they could divide the box into different parts of the zoo. Encourage them to write a list of rules for visitors and stick it on the side.
Create some new rules for the classroom. For example: You must hop on one leg to your seat. These can be funny, silly, or creative.
Draw a map of an imaginary town, including a police station, traffic lights, roundabouts, zebra crossings, a school, and a park. Tell children that this town has no rules! In an exaggerated way, tell children that nobody knows what to do there. They need to write the rules for this new town. You can turn this into a speaking activity by asking children to present the rules to the class.
Encourage children to create their own rule book or poster of their favourite rules, including who made the rule and the consequence of breaking the rule.
Children draw a picture of their house and write the rules they have at home.
Children imagine they have a ‘No Rules Day’ at school. What would they do? This could be a writing or a role-play activity.
Learning review
Children have consolidated their knowledge and learning on the topic of ‘Let’s follow rules’.
Differentiation
For children who need more support provide sentences, questions, and words, either on the board or in gapped texts. Pair them with more able children. They may look back at their work to help them, or produce less. Give them choices: e.g. ask: Would you prefer to speak or write for this activity?
Challenge more able children to write and say more sentences, providing additional details and using new language and grammatical forms learned. Encourage them to support less confident children and ensure that they are contributing.
