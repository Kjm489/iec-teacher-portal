PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Dictate the following letters to children to write down: m, e, a, g, s. In pairs, ask them to see if they know what the word is by rearranging the letters (‘games’). Give the first letter, if necessary. Tell children that today they’re going to think about their favourite games. Take a collective deep breath with the class.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Rules of the game?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Model an appropriate rule or response in a familiar situation. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Elicit some familiar games from children. Encourage children to categorize the games: e.g. dice / spinner games, playground games, paper and pencil games, word games, etc. Tell children to imagine that a new child is joining their class from a different country, but this person doesn’t know how to play any games. Ask them how this child can play games with them. Do this as a Think-Pair-Share activity. Elicit that they need to know the rules of the games. Hand out the rules for ‘tic-tac-toe’ without saying what the game is. Ask children to read the rules and see if they can identify the game. Ask children to underline the language of the rules in the text, such as the word ‘must’. Also elicit that rules tell us who the winner is. Put children into pairs to write the rules of their favourite game. Ask them to write a maximum of four rules (two each). Children read each other’s work, checking for correct spelling and punctuation.

CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful? “Can you point to the evidence?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Step 1: Complete the first original task example together. Step 2: Provide a visual and partial language frame. Step 3: Invite learners to complete another example with fewer prompts. Original task anchor: Elicit some familiar games from children. Encourage children to categorize the games: e.g. dice / spinner games, playground games, paper and pencil games, word games, etc. Tell children to imagine that a new child is joining their class from a different country, but this person doesn’t know how to play any games. Ask them how this child can play games with them. Do this as a Think-Pair-Share activity. Elicit that they need to know the rules of the games. Hand out the rules for ‘tic-tac-toe’ without saying what the game is. Ask children to read the rules and see if they can identify the game. Ask children to underline the language of the rules in the text, such as the word ‘must’. Also elicit

ICQs: “Are we doing this one together?” “Where do we check our answers?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use scenario pictures, classroom rule cards and role cards to explain, question or solve a new example related to Rules of the game. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Write, illustrate or role-play a practical rule and its consequence. Play each other’s games following only the written rules. Write the rules for another game. Create a new rule or rules for a familiar game. Create a new game with new rules.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, pair them with more able children. Provide some rules or a gapped text for them to follow. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think why their new rules would improve the game. Additional information: Week 16, Lesson 4 What game is this? Draw a 3x3 grid with a word on each square. Players throw the dice to see who goes first. The first player chooses a square. They must say a sentence with that word. If it is right, they keep that square. They mark it with a circle. The second player chooses a square. They must say a sentence with that word. If it is right, they keep that square. They mark it with a cross. The winner is the player with three in a row.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have written rules for a familiar game.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Rules of the game without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Let’s follow rules’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by writing out the rules of a 
familiar game.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
