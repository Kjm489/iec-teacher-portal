PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Elicit six places with rules. Write them on the board and number them 1–6. Ask children to tell you a rule for each place. Make this a team game.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Fun with rules?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Model an appropriate rule or response in a familiar situation. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Tell children that they are going to create a short role-play to act out. Put children into small groups. Each group rolls the dice or spins the spinner to discover their ‘place’ (according to the numbered places in the introductory activity). In groups, children think of a rule at that place and how it might be broken. The role-play should show someone breaking the rule and what happens next. Allow children time to talk about how they are going to do this. If available, encourage them to use props and dressing-up clothes to get into character and have some fun. Monitor actively and help children with vocabulary. Ensure everyone is involved, even if they don’t have a speaking part (there can be non-speaking parts). Give children enough time to rehearse their role-plays. Monitor actively. Ask each group to perform their role-play for the other groups. Each watching group should give a tick or a cross on their whiteboards depending on if the role-play showed a rule being broken or not.

CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful? “Can you point to the evidence?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Step 1: Complete the first original task example together. Step 2: Provide a visual and partial language frame. Step 3: Invite learners to complete another example with fewer prompts. Original task anchor: Tell children that they are going to create a short role-play to act out. Put children into small groups. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use scenario pictures, classroom rule cards and role cards to explain, question or solve a new example related to Fun with rules. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Write, illustrate or role-play a practical rule and its consequence. Write some sentences about the children’s role-plays. Create another role-play in a different place. With permission, film children’s role-plays and then let them watch.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, put them in groups with more able children. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to include more spoken turns in their role-plays.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have created and acted out a short role-play about following rules.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Fun with rules without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

1. Mystery Bag — Fun with rules

Lesson-specific language focus: asking permission and sharing

Original teaching source: Teacher: Elicit six places with rules. Write them on the board and number them 1–6. Ask children to tell you a rule for each place. Make this a team game.

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Mystery Bag”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Learners feel or inspect a concealed source object or picture clue and ask two questions before guessing.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.
