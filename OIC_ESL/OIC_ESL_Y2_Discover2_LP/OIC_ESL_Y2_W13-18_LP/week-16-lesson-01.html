PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Ask children what ‘getting along with someone’ means. Then introduce the topic by asking: How do people get along with each other? Accept all reasonable answers. Ask children to think if there are any reasons why we get along with some people more than others. Try to establish that to share spaces with others (e.g. home, school, etc.), we need to get along with people.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to How do we get along with each other??”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Model an appropriate rule or response in a familiar situation. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Encourage children to think of someone they get along with. Ask them to close their eyes and to take a mindful moment to think about the time they spend with this person, and how they feel when they are around this person. If appropriate, children can share, but respect their privacy. Elicit the activities they do with their chosen person: e.g. watching films, sitting next to each other, playing games together, etc. Write suggestions on the board. Then write the days of the week on the board and ask children to write down what they did last week with people they get along with. This is to raise awareness that we usually do things together with people we like. Ask children to observe if the activities are the same or different. They are probably different. This is a good moment to also underline that even if we have different ideas, they are all valid. Tell children they are going to watch a short video about ‘getting along with people’. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use scenario pictures, classroom rule cards and role cards to explain, question or solve a new example related to How do we get along with each other?. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Write, illustrate or role-play a practical rule and its consequence. Write down the names of three people children get along with, and sentences describing what they like about them and what they do together. Watch the video again and write a question for a partner to answer: e.g. How many people are in the video? What are they wearing?

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, pair them with more able children. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to consider what happens when we don’t get along with people.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have begun to think about the topic of getting along with each other by watching a video and reflecting on what they do with people they get along with.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to How do we get along with each other? without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

Week 16, Lesson 1: How do we get along with each other?
Learning outcomes: 2.1b, 2.1d, 2.1e 

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Let’s follow rules’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video or listening to you talking about aspects of getting along with other people.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
