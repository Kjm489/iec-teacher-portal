PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Ask children what they think is the most important rule in the classroom. Tell them you’re going to act out not following a classroom rule and they need to tell you what the rule is. Act out the rule (e.g. dropping litter / speaking in children’s first language / shouting). Ask a confident child to act out not following a rule and everyone has to guess the rule. Continue with two or three more children.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Writing the rules?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Model an appropriate rule or response in a familiar situation. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Ask children to think about what makes a good rule and write some ideas on the board. They might suggest that a rule should be clear, fair, in big writing, and easy to follow. Revise the language of rules: Don’t / Do / You should … / You must … Tell children that they’re going to work in small groups to create a rules poster, with illustrations and sentences, for a place of their choice. Put children into groups and ask them to think of the place they’re going to write their rules poster for. Encourage them to think of a place that hasn’t yet been covered in other lessons. They can include information about who makes the rules, what the consequences of not following the rules are, and so on. Children write their sentences in draft first before copying to the poster. Encourage them to check their spelling and punctuation carefully. Display the finished posters and allow children to look at each other’s work.

CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful? “Can you point to the evidence?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Step 1: Complete the first original task example together. Step 2: Provide a visual and partial language frame. Step 3: Invite learners to complete another example with fewer prompts. Original task anchor: Ask children to think about what makes a good rule and write some ideas on the board. They might suggest that a rule should be clear, fair, in big writing, and easy to follow. Revise the language of rules: Don’t / Do / You should … / You must … Tell children that they’re going to work in small groups to create a rules poster, with illustrations and sentences, for a place of their choice. Put children into groups and ask them to think of the place they’re going to write their rules poster for. Encourage them to think of a place that hasn’t yet been covered in other lessons. They can include information about who makes the rules, what the consequences of not following the rules are, and so on.

ICQs: “Are we doing this one together?” “Where do we check our answers?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use scenario pictures, classroom rule cards and role cards to explain, question or solve a new example related to Writing the rules. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Write, illustrate or role-play a practical rule and its consequence. Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.81 Writing exs.A–C. Children draw themselves following a rule and write about the picture.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, allow them to just write the rule without giving more information. Encourage them to write two or three rules. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to give as much information as possible about the rules including consequence, who, why, if the rules are fair, etc.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have worked in small groups to create a rules poster for a place of their choice. They have applied their learning from the unit to complete the task successfully.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Writing the rules without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Let’s follow rules’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by making a rules poster.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
