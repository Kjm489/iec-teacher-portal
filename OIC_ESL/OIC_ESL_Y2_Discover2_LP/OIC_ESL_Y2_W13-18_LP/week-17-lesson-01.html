PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: As you do these introductory activities, borrow some things from children without them noticing: e.g. a pen, a pencil case, a cap, a bookmark, a book bag, etc. Tell children they are going to line up according to the order you say. You could choose one or more from the following fun suggestions: height, shoe size, month of birth, etc. Now ask children to think about their bedroom at the moment. Ask: Is it clean or messy? What about the classroom? And your tables? Ask children if they prefer things to be clean or messy. Tell children that today’s lesson is about cleaning up, which is an important rule for getting along with others.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Help me clean up?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Model an appropriate rule or response in a familiar situation. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.93 ex.D. Play the song and ask children to stand up when they hear ‘mine’. Sing the song with children. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use scenario pictures, classroom rule cards and role cards to explain, question or solve a new example related to Help me clean up. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Write, illustrate or role-play a practical rule and its consequence. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.78–79. Draw a Venn diagram of items that belong to two children, with one item in the middle that belongs to both (= theirs). Write sentences underneath. Make up a short role-play including the question: Whose is it?

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, provide additional adult support in a small group. They might benefit from additional grammar practice as possessive pronouns can be difficult. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write some questions about classroom items using the question stem: Whose …?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have asked and answered questions about who things belong to, using a range of possessive pronouns.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Help me clean up without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Let’s follow rules’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by asking and answering about who things belong to.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
