PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Put children into small groups. Tell them you’re going to say some letters of three words and they spell them out with their bodies. Spell out: ‘places’. Children sit down and try to remember the letters and the order. Continue with the next two words: ‘with’ and ‘rules’.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Places with rules?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Model an appropriate rule or response in a familiar situation. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Elicit from children 12 different places with rules (e.g. a school, a swimming pool, etc), building on the places from previous lessons. Use your flashcards to help with ideas. Write them on the board and number them. (Children will refer to the numbers later.) Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.94 exs.A–B for some different places. Ask children if the rules in these different places are always the same. Elicit some rules. Ask children to think of different places where you can’t run around. Ask them to identify places where it is ‘dangerous’ to run around (e.g. a swimming pool, a road, train tracks). Play track 2.14 in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.94 ex.C. Children check their ideas, then read aloud or play the audio once more. Remind children of the previous lessons about rules and getting along with people. What language can they remember? Explain that they have to practise certain things over and over in order to master them. Draw a Venn diagram on the board and show children how they can compare two places, such as the library and their be

CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful? “Can you point to the evidence?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Step 1: Complete the first original task example together. Step 2: Provide a visual and partial language frame. Step 3: Invite learners to complete another example with fewer prompts. Original task anchor: Elicit from children 12 different places with rules (e.g. a school, a swimming pool, etc), building on the places from previous lessons. Use your flashcards to help with ideas. Write them on the board and number them. (Children will refer to the numbers later.) Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.94 exs.A–B for some different places. ICQs: “Are we doing this one together?” “Where do we check our answers?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use scenario pictures, classroom rule cards and role cards to explain, question or solve a new example related to Places with rules. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Write, illustrate or role-play a practical rule and its consequence. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 2 p.80. Compare two more places. Reflect on what it would be like to go to these places if there were no rules.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, pair them with more able children. Write a rule on the board that they can use as an example for their own work. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to count the number of rules they have followed today for different places.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have listened to rules and identified specific information. They have considered how rules might vary in different places and presented their ideas in a Venn diagram.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Places with rules without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: What happens if the rule is followed? Why is it fair or useful?

1. Teacher Mistake Detective — Places with rules

Lesson-specific language focus: asking permission and sharing

Original teaching source: Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Places with rules?”

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Teacher Mistake Detective”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Teacher deliberately gives a wrong source example. Learners signal the error and model the correct response.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Let’s follow rules’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by listening for specific information.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
