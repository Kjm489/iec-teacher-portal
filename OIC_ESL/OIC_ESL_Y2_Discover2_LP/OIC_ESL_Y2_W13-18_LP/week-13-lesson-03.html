PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Mime reading a book and ask: What am I doing? Elicit: You’re reading a book. Do this with other school activities. Ask a confident child to come to the front and mime. Put children into small groups to play the same game. Encourage them to incorporate movement into their mimes. Explain to them that they don’t need to move around the classroom to incorporate movement.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to What are you doing??”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Demonstrate one source example and explain the meaning. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Ask children to imagine that they all have phones and that their parents or carers can call them during lessons. What questions might their parents ask? Elicit: e.g. Where are you? What are you doing? Elicit answers: e.g. I’m in the playground. I’m jumping. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2, pp.69, 75, and 77. Say: You are the children in the pictures. What are you doing? Elicit answers. Elicit some phrases we can say if we don’t understand or can’t hear: e.g. Sorry. Can you say that again, please? Encourage children to use these phrases. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use original source illustrations, picture cards and vocabulary prompts to explain, question or solve a new example related to What are you doing?. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: Which part of the source proves this answer?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete a new example independently. Have the same dialogue, but add in different places such as a swimming pool, shops, etc. Make a poster of phrases to ask for repetition.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, write the example dialogue on the board for them to follow. Challenge more confident children to add more details about what they’re doing (e.g. who they are with and why they are doing the activity). Additional information: Week 13, Lesson 3 Example dialogue (Takes place on telephone, so children have their backs to each other.) [Sound effects: Ringing of telephone] A: Hello? B: Hello! It’s me, Mom/Dad. A: Oh! Hello, Mom/Dad! B: Where are you? A: I’m in the playground. B: Sorry. Can you say that again, please? A: I’m in the playground. B: What are you doing? A: Sorry. Can you say that again, please? B: What are you doing? A: I’m playing tennis! B: Cool!

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have asked and answered questions about what they are doing.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to What are you doing? without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: Which part of the source proves this answer?

Original teaching source: Teacher: Mime reading a book and ask: What am I doing? Elicit: You’re reading a book. Do this with other school activities. Ask a confident child to come to the front and mime. Put children into small groups to play the same game. Encourage them to incorporate movement into their mimes. Explain to them that they don’t need to move around the classroom to incorporate movement.

Materials: story illustrations and four event cards

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “True or False Corners”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “First ___, then ___, finally ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about stories and reading.

YOU DO — game procedure: Read a source-based claim. Learners move to true or false and point to evidence before the answer is revealed.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Does the beginning come before the ending?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about stories and reading.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “First ___, then ___, finally ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of stories and reading.
