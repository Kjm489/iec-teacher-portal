PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Play a number game as a warm-up. Count with the class in twos, threes, fives, and tens. Count around the class from zero upwards, or count backwards from 20. Put children in pairs and ask them to practise counting in a new way (e.g. in threes or backwards). Give them some time to practise, and then invite them to count for the class. Ask children if they have anyone in their family who is really good at maths.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Subtraction maths problems?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Say the operation and explain what each number means. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Pre-teach these words and write them on the board: a ‘minus’ sign, an ‘equals’ sign, a ‘single-digit’ number (e.g. 8), and a ‘double-digit’ number (e.g. 18). Say some numbers and ask children to tell you if they are single- or double-digit numbers. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 pp.70–71. Encourage children to re-read the part about rows and columns. Ask children to underline the key words. Put children in groups by asking them to solve a maths problem: e.g. 4 − 1 = ? (children get into groups of three). In their groups, children re-read the subtraction maths problem text. Then, taking turns, one person says a word and the others have to say a sentence including that word, or say what the word means. Monitor actively and praise children consistently for their teamwork. Ask children to take out their whiteboards. Instruct them to create a single-digit subtraction maths problem for another group to read out. They should give the answer. Repeat this with a double-digit subtraction math problem. Create your own maths subtraction activities.

CCQs: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know? “Can you point to the evidence?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Step 1: Complete the first original task example together. Step 2: Provide a visual and partial language frame. Step 3: Invite learners to complete another example with fewer prompts. Original task anchor: Pre-teach these words and write them on the board: a ‘minus’ sign, an ‘equals’ sign, a ‘single-digit’ number (e.g. 8), and a ‘double-digit’ number (e.g. 18). ICQs: “Are we doing this one together?” “Where do we check our answers?” CCQs: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use number cards, counters and worked examples to explain, question or solve a new example related to Subtraction maths problems. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Solve or write a new number or word problem, then check it. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 2 p.57. Make a list of significant numbers that are single or double digits, such as today’s date, children’s birth date, and so on. Create some more subtraction problems for a partner.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, put them in a group with more able children. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write a maths problem and label the parts (e.g. ‘plus sign’).

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have worked together to read, create, and solve maths subtraction problems.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Subtraction maths problems without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know?

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘School subjects’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by reading maths subtraction problems and identifying key words.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
