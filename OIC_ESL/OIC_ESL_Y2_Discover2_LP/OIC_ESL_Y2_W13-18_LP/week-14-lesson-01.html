PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Elicit from children the different school subjects they have in a week. Ask them if they enjoy Maths. Say: I’m going to say three sentences. Two of them are things we learn in Maths and one isn’t. Listen carefully and say which is the odd one out: e.g. 1. We learn about sums. 2. We draw and paint. 3. We count things. (2 is the odd one out.) Repeat this with other subjects.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Maths words?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Say the operation and explain what each number means. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Write these maths symbols on the board: +, −, =. Elicit from children what they are. Write up some simple maths problems using the symbols, and ask children to read them out with you: e.g. Four take away two equals two. Three plus three equals six. Say some problems that children have to solve in their heads (e.g. 1 + 3 = ?). Keep these problems simple. Ask children to stand up. They are going to get into groups according to the answer of the problem you tell them. Do a simple example first. Say: One plus three equals …? Children get into groups of four. Repeat this with ‘plus’ and ‘minus’ / ‘take away’. Ask children to write a sum on their whiteboard, ensuring that the answer isn’t greater than the number of children in the class. Put children into groups. In turn, they read out their sum for their group to solve. Copy the ‘four-in-a-row’ game, from the template provided, on the board. Roll two dice (or spin a spinner twice) and take the smaller number away from the larger number. For example, if you roll a 5 and a 3, your answer is

CCQs: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know? “Can you point to the evidence?”

ICQs: “Are we doing this one together?” “Where do we check our answers?” CCQs: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use number cards, counters and worked examples to explain, question or solve a new example related to Maths words. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Solve or write a new number or word problem, then check it. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.68. Make a ‘four-in-a-row’ addition or multiplication board. Write out three maths problems for another child to solve. This could be done in teams.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, put them in a group with more able children. When playing the game on their whiteboard, encourage them to use only single-digit maths problems. Challenge more confident children by suggesting they try to do all the maths operations in their heads (mental arithmetic). Additional information: Week 14, Lesson 1 Four-in-a-row You need: Two dice (or a spinner) Four different coloured sets of counters Objective: To get four in a row. How to play: Roll two dice (or spin the spinner twice). Take the smaller number away from the larger number. Find the number on the board and put one of your counters there. Take it in turns.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have learned some maths vocabulary and played some maths games in English.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Maths words without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know?

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Display six source picture cards for 30 seconds; hide one and ask learners to name and describe the missing item.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.

This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘School subjects’.
