PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Play a quick number game with children from Week 13, for example, ‘Smash, Beep!’. Children count, but when they get to a 3, a multiple of 3, or a number with 3 in it (e.g. 13, 23, etc.) they say: Beep! For example: 1, 2, Beep!, 4, 5, Beep!, 7, 8, Beep!, etc. You can also add 5, multiples of 5, or a number with 5 in it to be replaced with Smash!, so you get: 1, 2, Beep!, 4, Smash!, Beep!, 7, 8, Beep!, Smash!, 11, Beep!, Beep!, etc. Tell children that today’s lesson will require them to be ‘detectives’ in reading. Elicit that detectives look for clues and they work very carefully. Hand out the cut-out magnifying glasses to remind children to read carefully. Today’s lesson will stretch children and extend their understanding.

Students: Think silently, discuss in pairs, share one idea. ICQs: “Do you discuss first or write the final answer?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which picture or example is connected to Subtraction?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher think-aloud: Say the operation and explain what each number means. Show a complete example, pronounce target words, and explain why it is correct. Original main activity: Ask children when they use maths in everyday life. See if they can give examples from their own lives, and to think about when they are with their parents or carers. You might have to guide the discussion, eliciting that maths can be ‘subtracting’ (or ‘taking away’). Ask: When does someone take something away? Give an example: e.g. Taking a slice of pizza is maths, as you have to work out if there are enough slices for everyone! Ask children to tell you what they think ‘re-reading’ is and when they might have to ‘re-read’. Establish that re-reading is a good thing because you are gaining a better understanding of the text. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.69 ex.C. Children read the text to themselves first. Ask them to close their books, and try to remember a sentence or word from the text. Take answers. Children can then read the text aloud, going around the class. Ask children to read the questions and answer the first two in pairs. Do questions 1 and 2, then check answers. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners use number cards, counters and worked examples to explain, question or solve a new example related to Subtraction. Partner A gives an answer; Partner B asks for evidence; switch roles and compare. ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “Do you show your reason?” CCQs: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Solve or write a new number or word problem, then check it. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.58–59. Ask children to look through their coursebooks and find a text to re-read. Re-read a story. What other details did children notice while re-reading? Decorate the magnifying glasses or stick them onto card and use them regularly.

Collect an individual oral, written or illustrated response that directly demonstrates the source learning outcomes; do not let pupils copy the teacher’s example.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: use picture choices, labeled vocabulary, gesture and an oral sentence frame. Developing: give a full answer with one accurate detail. Extending: justify, compare or design an original example. Original source differentiation: To support less able children, pair them with more able children. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write another question. They could also re-write the paragraph about how they use maths every day, beginning with: ‘I use maths every day …’

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review: Children have read a short text about how maths is used in everyday life. They have been introduced to the skill of re-reading.

Exit ticket: Complete one new response related to Subtraction without copying. Record independent / prompted / not yet. CCQ: Which operation changes the amount? How do you know?

Materials: original textbook pictures, vocabulary cards and the teacher’s source examples

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Picture Evidence Hunt”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “I think ___ because ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about subtraction.

YOU DO — game procedure: Show four source pictures; learners locate a requested detail and explain what in the picture supports it.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: What is one correct example of subtraction?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about subtraction.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “I think ___ because ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of subtraction.
