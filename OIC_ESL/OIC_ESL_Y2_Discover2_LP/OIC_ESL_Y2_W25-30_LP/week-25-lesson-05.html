Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Ask children to sit very still and to hold out both hands. Tell them to imagine that they have a sweet-smelling flower in their left hand and a lit candle in the other. When they breathe in slowly, they imagine smelling the flower; when they breathe out slowly, they imagine blowing out the candle. Do this together a few times to relax the class. Ask which instrument(s) require(s) very good breathing. (Arguably all instruments, but perhaps wind/brass more than most.)

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Is the rhythm fast or slow? How does it make you feel?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Say a time (e.g. ‘10 a.m.’), or draw a clock and the hands on the board. Ask: Is this in the morning, in the afternoon, or in the evening? Elicit other ‘in the morning’ times (e.g. 9 a.m., 11 a.m.), then do the same with ‘in the afternoon’ and ‘in the evening’. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.133 and complete the speaking activity in ex.E. Tell children that today they are going to work in groups to plan a special music day at school. Generate excitement for this kind of event, and establish if this is something children would be interested in. Elicit different activities that the school might offer on a music day (e.g. singing or dancing lessons, writing songs, making themed playlists, drumming tutorials, a talent show, etc.). In groups of four to five, give children time to think about how to plan their music day in a mind map. They will need to make a list of things to plan and prepare – for instance, posters, invitations, a timetable, food, a dress code, invited guests, etc. Tell them to prioritize writing their timetable. This will show you what activities they are planning for the morning, afternoon, and evening. They will need to vary their day and to include breaks. ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about music day. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.114–115. Ask groups to complete the sentence: ‘Come to our music day because …’ Make a poster advertising the school’s special music day.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Form mixed-ability groups so more confident children can guide their less confident peers through the task. Challenge more able children to plan parallel events for their music day.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have worked together in small groups to plan a music day at school.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to music day; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” before learners respond.
