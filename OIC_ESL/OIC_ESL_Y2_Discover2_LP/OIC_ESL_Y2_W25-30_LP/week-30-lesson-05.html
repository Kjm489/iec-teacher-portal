YEAR 2 Reflection and review
Week 30, Lessons 1–5: Reflection on ‘Movement and force’
Learning outcomes: Unit summary

Independent reflection
Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Movement and force’.
 Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, and Writing. Lead a discussion with children about these skills. For example: Tell me an activity when we practised speaking in English. When we [played XX game], what skills did we use? Did we speak? Did we listen? What did you find easy/difficult? Why?
Help children to assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children who may find this self-reflective process quite challenging.
Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.
Main activity
  Explain that this week, children will participate in one or more extended activities in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.
Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:
Children can create a ‘push and pull’ board game by cutting out pictures from magazines and sticking them on a racetrack template. When they land on a picture, the player must say if it shows a ‘push’ force, a ‘pull’ force, or both.
Support children to write a story and then make a lift-the-flap book about movement, with doors that have to be pushed and pulled (to find something/someone). Show them how to cut out doors and stick them onto the page to either pull or push through to the next page. Help children with their story ideas, plots, and characters. Encourage them to plan their story in note form first.
Make a ‘cause and effect’ paper chain. Work together with children to write sentences on strips of paper and then put them in order: e.g. I went to bed late. I didn’t hear the alarm. I was late for school. My teacher was unhappy.
Children can put on a play of one of the stories from the unit – ‘The Hare and the Tortoise’ or ‘The stubborn goats’. Support them to make animal masks and scenery for their play.
Identify cause and effect in a selection of nursery rhymes or stories available to you. Children can make a poster showing their observations.
