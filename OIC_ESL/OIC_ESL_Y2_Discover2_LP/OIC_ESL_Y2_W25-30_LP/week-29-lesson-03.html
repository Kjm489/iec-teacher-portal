PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Elicit from children some phrases they can use when they don’t quite hear/understand and need to ask for repetition (e.g. Can you say that again, please? or Sorry?). Remind them to try to listen to their friends and, if they hear a mistake, to say: Sorry? This will encourage their friend to think and self-correct. Ask children to quickly talk to each other about their favourite things, using these repetition phrases and self-correction strategies. Put some useful expressions on the board for children to refer to in today’s lesson.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know??”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Invite three children to the front of the class. Ask the class: Who is taller, [Jon] or [Aurelia]? Is [Aurelia] taller than [Sara]? Then ask children: Who is the tallest? Write the sentences on the board. Focus children’s attention on the ending ‘–er’, ‘than’, and then ‘–est’. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.161 and p.163 and complete the exercises. Find two points in the classroom (e.g. the board and the door). Ask children how long they think it takes to hop, jump, run, or walk between these two points. You could also take children outside to the playground for this activity. Do an example yourself by asking a child to time you so that they have an idea of how long it might take them. Encourage them to make a prediction before you start. Children make their predictions, write these on their whiteboards, and then carry out their timed activities. Remind groups that all children should actively participate. CCQs: “What did I do first?” “What makes the example accurate?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Original task guided by the teacher: Invite three children to the front of the class. Ask the class: Who is taller, [Jon] or [Aurelia]? Is [Aurelia] taller than [Sara]? Then ask children: Who is the tallest? Write the sentences on the board. Focus children’s attention on the ending ‘–er’, ‘than’, and then ‘–est’. Work through one example together; students answer on mini-whiteboards or orally. Gradually remove the sentence starter.

ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about who is the fastest?. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.140–141. Do some more timed activities. Record the results in a graph of choice.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Form mixed-ability groups so more confident children can guide their less confident peers through the activity, modelling the correct use of comparative and superlative forms. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to extend their thinking to other activities (e.g. how long it takes to … put everything on their desk away, learn five new words in English, ask a question, etc.). Additional information: Week 29, Lesson 3

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have practised using comparative and superlative forms. They have made predictions about how fast they, and their classmates, can complete an activity.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to who is the fastest?; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
