Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 5

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: On the board, write ‘and’. Invite a volunteer to give you a sentence using ‘and’. Repeat with ‘but’. Ensure that children understand the meaning of both. Explain that today they are going to do some writing and you would like them to try to include ‘and’ and ‘but’ in their sentences.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Is the rhythm fast or slow? How does it make you feel?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Complete ex.F in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.143. Tell children that they are going to write about their favourite (piece of) music. They will have to think deeply about their learning in the unit and try to apply this to their writing. To support, elicit any key vocabulary and grammar children have learned recently that they could incorporate into their sentences (as well as ‘and’ and ‘but’). Play some music while children think of ideas. As they listen, encourage them to draw a picture, write some words, or describe how the music makes them feel in a sentence. As a class, talk about what kind of information they could include in their paragraph. Write the following questions on the board. What is your favourite music? Why do you like it? How does it make you feel? Describe the music. What instruments can you hear in the music? Where do you like listening to music? Who do you listen to music with? Give children enough time to write the first draft of their paragraph, using the above questions as useful prompts to help structure their ideas. Remind them to ask for help if they need it, although encourage them to try to find the answer in their coursebook or in their notes first. They could also ask a classmate. Remind children to try to use ‘and’ and ‘but’ at least once in their work.

Think aloud, highlight key language and demonstrate an accurate answer before students try. Use gestures, visual evidence or a short example as appropriate.

CCQs: “What did I do first?” “What makes the example accurate?”

Work through one example together; students answer on mini-whiteboards or orally. Gradually remove the sentence starter.

ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about writing about music and feelings. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.125. Encourage children to share and read each other’s work. You could ask them to read it aloud to their peers in a small group, with the listening children raising their hand each time they hear ‘and’ or ‘but’.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Provide sentence starters and a vocabulary bank for less confident children. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to write another paragraph from the perspective of a friend or family member. What type of music do they enjoy listening to at home or in school?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have written about their favourite piece of music using ‘and’ and ‘but’ to connect their ideas.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to writing about music and feelings; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

1. Teacher Mistake Detective — Writing about music and feelings

Lesson-specific language focus: food and healthy choices

Original teaching source: Original warm-up: On the board, write ‘and’. Invite a volunteer to give you a sentence using ‘and’. Repeat with ‘but’. Ensure that children understand the meaning of both. Explain that today they are going to do some writing and you would like them to try to include ‘and’ and ‘but’ in their sentences.

Materials: food picture cards and two labelled sorting mats

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Teacher Mistake Detective”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about food and healthy choices.

YOU DO — game procedure: Teacher deliberately gives a wrong source example. Learners signal the error and model the correct response.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is an apple a fruit?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about food and healthy choices.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of food and healthy choices.

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Music and feelings’.
In this lesson, children will describe their favourite music in a piece of written work.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
