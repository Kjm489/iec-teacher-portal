Measurable Learning Objectives

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Ask the class: What are musical instruments made of? Elicit some materials, such as ‘wood’, ‘brass’, etc. Follow up by asking: Have you ever made a musical instrument? Invite volunteers to share their experiences. Play your pre-prepared homemade musical instrument without showing it to children. Elicit that it is a [percussion] instrument from the sound, and encourage children to suggest what it is made of. They will need very good listening skills! Accept all ideas, but don’t show children your instrument until the end of the lesson.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Is the rhythm fast or slow? How does it make you feel?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Elicit/Pre-teach the materials you have for children to make their own homemade musical instrument. Put children into groups. Explain that they must be able to play the instrument they make, and that everyone in their group should have one that makes an identical sound (or nearly identical). This means that children will need to work out that they will need the same amount of [dried beans/peas] each. First, tell groups to plan what instrument they are going to make and what they will need. In order to do so, they will need to think about how they are going to make it – you aren’t going to give them instructions to follow! Encourage them to draw ideas and to make a list of materials. Allow children to collect the materials from you once they are clear about what they need. They work together in their groups to make their instrument. Ask them to agree on a name for their instrument. It can be as silly as they like! Invite groups to present their finished instrument to the class, explaining what it is called and how they made it. Praise groups for working out what they needed to do in order for their instrument to produce a sound. Show the class your instrument from the beginning of the lesson. Work through one example together; students answer on mini-whiteboards or orally. Gradually remove the sentence starter.

ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about making a musical instrument. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Make a different homemade instrument. Ask children to draw and label a picture of their instrument. Challenge more able children to write a simple set of instructions for making it to accompany their picture.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Form mixed-ability groups so more confident children can guide their less confident peers. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to think about what adjustments (small changes) they could make to improve their instrument, and why.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have made a musical instrument from recyclable materials.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to making a musical instrument; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
