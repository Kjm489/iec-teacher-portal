PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Put children into groups of three or four. They are going to make letters with their bodies. Say the letters: m-o-v-e-m-e-n-t. Give groups enough time to make the different shapes. Tell children to return to their desks and to write out the order of the letters. Elicit the word. If you have already done this activity with children and they know what comes next, you could mix up the letters and see if they can make a word with them. For instance, you could ask them to spell out: m-o-v-e. Ask children to look around the room. Encourage them to identify what things move and what makes them move. Accept all ideas and introduce the new unit on movement.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Is the object moving because of a push or a pull? Show the direction.?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Put children into two teams one behind the other in a line, with the first two children facing each other. Give them a rope and tell them to sit on the floor. You are going to instruct them to either push or pull the rope. Ask them to listen carefully as you place emphasis on either ‘push’ or ‘pull’ in your instructions. Tell both teams to pull the rope. Tell one team to push the rope. Tell one team to push and the other to pull. When children have returned to their seats, talk about what happened in each situation. Establish the difference between ‘push’ and ‘pull’. Help children to pronounce the words through drilling, focusing on the initial ‘p’ sound and the digraphs ‘sh’ and ‘ll’. With the realia or the flashcards, elicit/pre-teach the lesson’s vocabulary. In pairs, ask children to sort the words into categories: ‘push’, ‘pull’, or ‘both’. They could also do this as a Venn diagram. Discuss answers as a whole class. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.147 ex.A and play the video. Hand out a strip of paper to each child. Explain that they are going to make a mini ‘push and pull book’, drawing pictures of things we push and things we pull. Instruct them to write the correct verb underneath each picture. Work through one example together; students answer on mini-whiteboards or orally. Gradually remove the sentence starter.

ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about what is movement?. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: In teams, tell children to work together to create a quiz for another team about things we push and things we pull, or both. Ask children to look through their coursebooks to find examples of each force. Complete the Big Question chart in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.147 ex.D.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Pair up mixed abilities in the sorting activity so more able children can support their less confident peers, giving explanations where required. Encourage fast finishers to add their own examples to each category.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have learned about ‘push’ and ‘pull’ forces in a practical exercise. They have sorted everyday items into the two categories and presented their findings in a whole-class mini book.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to what is movement?; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Movement and force’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by talking about what makes things move.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
