PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Stand in a circle with children. Tell them that they are going to use their voice. They must make a noise from their stomach. Show them how by saying ‘ah’ from your stomach and ‘ah’ from your lungs. Ask everyone to put their hands on their stomach so that they can feel it move when they produce a sound. Jump to the left and face the child on your left and say ‘ah’. The child then jumps to the left and says the same sound to the child on their left. This should then continue around the circle. When children get the idea, you can then add more sounds such as ‘ooh’. Ask children what topic they think is next and elicit ‘music’ or ‘sounds’.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Is the rhythm fast or slow? How does it make you feel??”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Ask: Do you like music? Do you like fast/slow music? What do you do when you hear it? Tell children that they are going to listen to some pieces of music. Ask them to tell you if each track is ‘fast’ or ‘slow’. Play your chosen music, one track at a time. Now incorporate some movement. Ask everyone to stand up. Play each track and encourage children to move (by dancing or hopping, etc.). They can be as creative as possible with their movement. If the music is slow, you could model miming sleeping and waking up. Ask everyone to choose one action, such as pretending to sweep the floor. Play a fast track and then a slow one, turning the volume up for loud music and down for quiet music. For example, you could play some very loud orchestral music and some quiet piano music. Give children time to reflect on how the music made them feel. Ask: How did you feel when the music was fast? Elicit adjectives such as ‘excited’, ‘relaxed’, ‘calm’, and ‘happy’. Write these words on the board and ask children to copy them down carefully. This could done be as a Venn diagram (with the headings ‘fast music’ and ‘slow music’). 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about do you like fast or slow music?. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete the Big Question chart in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.127 ex.D. Create a questionnaire asking children in class whether they like fast or slow music best. Ask children to illustrate the adjectives they copied down with musical pictures.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, create the Venn diagram as a whole class rather than independently. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think of other words to express how they feel when they hear fast or slow music. Show them how they can use a dictionary from their first language into English to research new words.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have participated in musical activities and reflected on how music makes them feel.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to do you like fast or slow music?; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”

This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Music and feelings’.
