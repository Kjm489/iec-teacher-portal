Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 3

Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: On the board, write the numbers 1 to 4. Say: When you play music on your own, it’s called a ‘solo’. Indicate to number 1. Ask if anyone knows the word for two musicians (a ‘duo’), then continue for ‘trio’, and ‘quartet’. Ask children to stand up. Say: You are a quartet. Challenge children to get into groups of that number (groups of four). Continue with ‘solo’, ‘duo’, and ‘trio’. Tell children that they are going to read a story on the theme of friendship. Ask: What makes a good friend? Take answers, then revisit this discussion topic later.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Is the rhythm fast or slow? How does it make you feel?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Read ‘Olga’s Flute’ in Oxford Discover SB 2 pp.138–139. Give children the title of the story. Put them into groups to predict and imagine its plot. You may need to offer a few more key words to help them. Show or hand out the first part of the story. Read it together as a class, inviting volunteers to take turns reading out sentences, if possible. After each part, give children a short amount of time to draw a picture to illustrate what they understood from that part. Continue with the remaining parts, allowing enough time for reading and drawing. In their groups, ask children to compare their drawings, then use them to retell the story together. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.140. Refer back to your initial question about friendship. Ask: How was Olga’s teacher a good friend? Help children to understand that he gave her confidence and believed in her. Consider how the actions of the main characters affected the other characters. Encourage children to think of a time when their actions affected someone in a good way.

Think aloud, highlight key language and demonstrate an accurate answer before students try. Use gestures, visual evidence or a short example as appropriate.

CCQs: “What did I do first?” “What makes the example accurate?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Original task guided by the teacher: Read ‘Olga’s Flute’ in Oxford Discover SB 2 pp.138–139. Give children the title of the story. Put them into groups to predict and imagine its plot. You may need to offer a few more key words to help them. Show or hand out the first part of the story. Read it together as a class, inviting volunteers to take turns reading out sentences, if possible. After each part, give children a short amount of time to draw a picture to illustrate what they understood from that part. Continue with the remaining parts, allowing enough time for reading and drawing. In their groups, ask children to compare their drawings, then use them to retell the story together. Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.140. Refer back to your initial question about friendship. Ask: How was Olga’s teacher a good friend? Help children to understand that he gave her confidence and believed in her. Consider how the actions of the main characters affected the other characters. Encourage children to think of a time when their actions affected someone in a good way.

Work through one example together; students answer on mini-whiteboards or orally. Gradually remove the sentence starter.

ICQs: “Are we working together for this first example?” CCQs: “Where is the evidence for your answer?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about a music story. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.120–121. Challenge children to summarize their favourite part of the story in pairs, recalling their learning in the main activity. Provide props for children to role-play the story, or create their own story with a musical theme.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: Pre-teach key words to help children understand the main events in the story. Put these words into context for more confident children to guess their meaning. To support less able children, show them some illustrations to match to the text. Encourage fast finishers to extend the story. What happens next?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have read and retold a story with a musical theme. They have explored the theme of friendship.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to a music story; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
