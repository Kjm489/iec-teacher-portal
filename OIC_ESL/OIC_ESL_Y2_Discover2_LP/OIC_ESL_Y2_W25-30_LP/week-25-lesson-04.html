Measurable goals: Students demonstrate the language and skills required by the original lesson, use topic vocabulary in a meaningful response, and produce one independent piece of evidence.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Instruct children to stand in a line. Explain the game instructions: when you say a musical instrument, they mime playing it. When you say a group of instruments (e.g. woodwind), they huddle into groups of four and pretend to play different instruments that are woodwind (e.g. clarinet, flute, recorder, saxophone, etc.). Write some musical instrument names on the board to help them. When children have grasped the game, they can continue to play in their groups.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Is the rhythm fast or slow? How does it make you feel?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Pre-teach/Elicit the adjectives by giving some concrete examples. For example, say: I have a chocolate cake. How do I feel? Elicit ‘happy’. Continue with the rest of the adjectives. Ask children to copy them down. Put children into groups to play a game. Ask groups to decide on an order of adjectives before starting. The first child rolls the dice or spins the spinner, then says that number of situations when they feel that feeling – if the adjective is ‘sad’ and the child rolls four, they say four situations when they might feel sad (e.g. if they feel lonely at playtime). Monitor actively, encouraging children to prompt their classmate with clues if they are struggling to think of ideas. Hold a whole-class discussion, eliciting the best or funniest ideas from each group. Play the music clips one by one. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about music and imagination. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

ICQs: “Who speaks first?” “When do you swap?” CCQs: “Does your response fit the original example?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent task: Ask children to write one or two sentences underneath their picture, describing the music and how it makes them feel. Play another long track with changes in tempo. In pairs or groups, encourage children to create a story to go with the music. What is happening at each section? Support children to write sentences about what they do when they feel particular emotions: e.g. When I feel excited, I run a lot.

Students create an original response, illustration with labels, written explanation, or review-project artifact. Teacher checks accuracy against the original learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, provide them with some cut-out magazine pictures to choose from to represent their feelings. This will give them a good starting point. Encourage fast finishers to research more adjectives to describe their emotional responses to music.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review: Children have expressed their feelings about music using a range of adjectives and other artistic methods.

Exit ticket: Give one new example directly linked to music and imagination; students respond without copying. Mark I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet. CCQ: “What did you learn and how can you show it?”
