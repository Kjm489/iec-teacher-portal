PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Original warm-up: Pre-teach/Elicit the lesson’s vocabulary using the flashcards. Drill the words carefully. Play a flashcard game. Sit children in a circle. Pass one of the cards around, explaining to children that they only need to look quickly and pass it on to their neighbour. When you say ‘Stop!’, the child holding the card has to say the word. You could also play music. When you stop the music, the child holding the card says what the word is.

Scaffold: Show a relevant picture or object; invite think–pair–share before asking for full sentences.

ICQs: “Are you thinking first or answering immediately?” “Will both partners speak?” CCQs: “What is happening in this example?” “Which example belongs to this unit? How do you know?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Model the original task with a worked example: Tell children that today they are going to read a story. It includes all of the words from the introductory activity. There is also a connection to forces, movement, and speed, which they will work together to identify later. Elicit some key words from the unit. Reinforce that they need to practise new words over and over in order to remember them. First, put children into small groups and give them a time limit (e.g. 5 minutes) to imagine a story using the words they learned at the beginning of the lesson. Invite groups to share their story. Did any of the groups have the same story? Read the story together. Invite confident readers to read a section each and for the rest of the class to follow. Discuss how similar or different children’s story ideas were to the actual story. Ask children to read the story again, individually or in pairs. Explain that it has a ‘moral’ (a lesson about right and wrong) in it. Encourage them to think about what it might be. Invite volunteers to share their ideas with the class. Help them to understand that the moral of the story is that being flexible and helpful is better than being ‘stubborn’ (not prepared to change your mind or decision). Being stubborn doesn’t end well. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner application: Partners use the original lesson’s pictures, vocabulary, reading, listening, demonstration or project materials to explain, ask and respond about the stubborn goats. Swap roles after two turns; require a meaningful response, not just a single word.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture choices, word bank, modeled phrase, gestures and oral rehearsal. Developing: complete the target response independently with one detail. Extending: add a reason, comparison, prediction or further example.

Original support: To support less able children, read the story aloud for them to follow. Pause after each section to check their understanding and to explain difficult vocabulary like ‘locked horns’. Provide three morals for them to choose from. As an extension task for more able children, challenge them to retell the story orally without looking at the text. This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Movement and force’.
