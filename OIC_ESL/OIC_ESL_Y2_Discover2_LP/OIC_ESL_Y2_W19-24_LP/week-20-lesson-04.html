PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Describe a situation, for example: You are using your coloured pencils. Your friend wants to use them. She grabs a pencil. Ask: Was she thoughtful or rude? Elicit ‘rude’. Describe more situations and ask children to decide if the person was thoughtful or rude.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to thoughtful friendships?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise a thoughtful friend helps / listens / shares through thoughtful friendships prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Complete ex.A in Oxford Discover WB 2 p.89. If they feel confident enough, children can test each other on common mistakes by making their own incorrect sentences. Monitor actively.

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently read and write about thoughtful friendship without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using a thoughtful friend helps / listens / shares. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text or a text with word choices. Encourage more confident children to explain common mistakes to their less confident peers in their own words. Fast finishers can write another paragraph about an imaginary rude friend. Original teaching source: Teacher: Describe a situation, for example: You are using your coloured pencils. Your friend wants to use them. She grabs a pencil. Ask: Was she thoughtful or rude? Elicit ‘rude’. Describe more situations and ask children to decide if the person was thoughtful or rude.

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “True or False Corners”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Read a source-based claim. Learners move to true or false and point to evidence before the answer is revealed.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.
