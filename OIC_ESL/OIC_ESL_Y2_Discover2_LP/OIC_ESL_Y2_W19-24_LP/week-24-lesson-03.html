YEAR 2 Reflection and review
Week 24, Lessons 1–5: Reflection on ‘Looking after our places’
Learning outcomes: Unit summary

Context
This week, children will reflect on their individual learning from Weeks 22–23.
Children will also participate in an extended activity, practising the skills they have learned and focusing their work on areas of particular interest.
Lessons this week will vary, depending on the scope of the chosen activity. Break up the activity into manageable stages to fit into the ESL lessons this week.


Independent reflection
Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Looking after our places’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

Main activity
Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity in pairs or small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas: Create a fun game to remind other children in school about sorting rubbish between recyclable and non-recyclable items. Create a way of showing people (other children, staff, and visitors) where all the bins are in your school. For example, it could be an illustrated map, a list, or a leaflet. Draw a map of your local area, labelling the places and any natural resources. Think about how these resources can be looked after. Make a definitions game to revise new vocabulary learned in the unit. Children can produce a guide to their city, town, or village. What can they do and see? What is the best thing? Where are the places located? Make a group zig-zag book about natural resources and how to look 
after them. In the form of a role-play, create a news report about litter found nearby and what will happen to it. Children could present the report using a cut-out cardboard box for a screen, for example, or they could be recorded by an adult (with the relevant permissions).

Learning review
Children have consolidated their knowledge and learning on the topic of ‘Looking after our places’.

Differentiation
For children who need more support provide sentences, questions, and words, either on the board or in gapped texts. Pair them with more able children. They may look back at their work to help them, or produce less. Give them choices: e.g. ask: Would you prefer to speak or write for this activity? Challenge more able children to write and say more sentences, providing additional details and using new language and grammatical forms learned.

Teacher implementation: Year 2 Week 24, reflection session 3 of 5; session focus: Draw and label a local town and its natural resources.
