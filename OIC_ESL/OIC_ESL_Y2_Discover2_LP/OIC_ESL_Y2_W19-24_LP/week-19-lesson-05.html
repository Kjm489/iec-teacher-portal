Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 5

Measurable targets: Identify key ideas about table manners; practise please, thank you, before / after; independently create a zigzag table-manners book. Check understanding through a relevant oral or written response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: To revise the vocabulary from this week, play ‘Snowman’ with children. Choose a word (e.g. ‘worried’). Write a dash for each letter on the board. Invite children to take turns suggesting letters. If a suggested letter is in the word, you write it in the correct space. If not, you begin to draw a snowman (adapt as you wish: two circles for the body, one circle for the head, two sticks for arms, two eyes, a nose, a mouth, and a hat). Each incorrectly guessed letter results in part of a snowman being drawn. If children guess the word before you have finished drawing the snowman, they win!

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to table manners?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Explicitly model the first step of the original task: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.90. By thinking again about the vocabulary for rules, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning. Now elicit from children the vocabulary connected to eating a meal together, bearing in mind that rules in different homes might be slightly different and that’s OK. Encourage children to consider why we have these rules. Think about what it means to be polite, considerate, and respectful towards other people (often elders, such as parents and grandparents) at the dinner table. Take suggestions and write some rules on the board using have to, may, and must e.g. You must not eat unhealthy food. You must not talk with your mouth full. You have to use [a knife / a fork / a spoon / chopsticks] when eating food. You must say ‘please’ and ‘thank you’. You must pass food to other family members. You may ask for a glass of [water]. Tell children

Think aloud, point to source evidence and demonstrate how to say or write please, thank you, before / after accurately. Show one non-example and explain why it is incorrect. CCQs: “What makes this an example of table manners?” “Which word or detail changes the meaning?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

We do: Complete the next example from the original activity as a class. Display the relevant picture, text, role card or materials. Prompt learners to select vocabulary and justify their answer. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise please, thank you, before / after through table manners prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Find out how table manners are different in other countries (e.g. eating with your hands, leaving food on your plate at the end of the meal, eating with a knife and fork, etc.). Make up a role-play about a friend who comes to dinner, but who doesn’t know any of the rules. Make tablemats with the table manners written on. Laminate them and encourage children to use them at school meal times. Consider if table manners are different at home to in a restaurant.

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently create a zigzag table-manners book without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using please, thank you, before / after. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: To support less confident children, provide gapped sentences for them to complete. Challenge more confident children to suggest a reason for each of the table manners.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have written a zigzag book about table manners using the modal verbs have to, may, and must.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about table manners using please, thank you, before / after. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”
