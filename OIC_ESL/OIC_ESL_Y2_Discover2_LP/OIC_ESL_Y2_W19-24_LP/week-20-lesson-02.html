Measurable Learning Objectives

Measurable targets: Identify key ideas about making polite offers; practise Would you like …? Yes, please / No, thank you.; independently make and respond to offers. Check understanding through a relevant oral or written response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: As an example, ask a child to open the window, saying: Would you open the window, please? Ask for a few more things, always using ‘please’. Ask children: What word do I always use when I would like something? Elicit ‘please’. Encourage children to think about how they feel when a request is made using ‘Would you …?’ instead of beginning with the verb. Repeat with ‘thank you’. Talk with children about when to say ‘please’ and ‘thank you’. Establish that ‘please’ is a good word to use when you want something, and ‘thank you’ is important when you receive something or when somebody helps you.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to making polite offers?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Explicitly model the first step of the original task: Sit in a circle. Show children the parcel you have. Tell them that when the music stops, the child holding the parcel opens one layer of wrapping paper and says ‘thank you’ according to the situation given (see suggestions on the ‘Additional information’ page). Model an example of saying ‘thank you’ for something you love and ‘thank you’ for something you don’t like as much. Play the game. Let other children guess what is written on the slip of paper. Then imagine that you have a big bowl of fruit. Offer a piece of fruit to a child. Say: Would you like [an orange]? Ensure that the child uses ‘thank you’ when replying. Elicit from children that we use ‘Would you like …?’ when we are offering something, or asking someone if they want something. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise Would you like …? Yes, please / No, thank you. through making polite offers prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: make and respond to offers

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently make and respond to offers without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using Would you like …? Yes, please / No, thank you.. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: Allow less able children to swap only five pieces of fruit instead of ten. Remind confident children to clearly model new language for their less confident peers in the role-play activity. Challenge them to recall different fruit names. 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have learned new expressions for making polite offers. They have practised them in a role-play activity in which they swap different items.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about making polite offers using Would you like …? Yes, please / No, thank you.. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”
