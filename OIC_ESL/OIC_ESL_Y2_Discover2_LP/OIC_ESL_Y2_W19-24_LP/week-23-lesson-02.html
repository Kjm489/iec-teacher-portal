Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 2

Measurable targets: Identify key ideas about preventing litter; practise Please don’t litter! / Put it in the bin.; independently make polite anti-litter reminders. Check understanding through a relevant oral or written response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Show children a rubbish (trash) bin and elicit what it’s for. Ask children if they have more than one type of bin at home (e.g. one for everyday rubbish and one for recycling) and find out what they use them for.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to preventing litter?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Explicitly model the first step of the original task: Write the lesson’s vocabulary on the board in a scattered way (you could prepare this before class – see also ‘Additional information’). Ask: Do you know these words? Can you use one of them in a sentence? Go through the words one by one. Ask children if these words all work in the same way in a sentence (no). Elicit that some are nouns and others are verbs. Ask children how quickly they think they can sort the words into these two categories. Set a time limit and ask children to sort the words (in pairs or independently). Check answers as a class. Invite children to explain how they know that a word is a noun or a verb. ICQs: “Do we answer this one together?” “Where do we look for clues?” CCQs: “Does our answer fit the example?” “How can we prove it?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise Please don’t litter! / Put it in the bin. through preventing litter prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Ask children to draw themselves with a friend having a conversation about litter. They can use speech bubbles to indicate the dialogue. Hold a ‘litter pick’ every day this week, and allocate time to pick up as much litter as possible from a place (e.g. the school playground or dining hall). Make a poster reminding people not to drop litter. Make a list of the locations of all the rubbish bins in school, distinguishing between the recycling and non-recycling ones (if applicable).

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently make polite anti-litter reminders without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using Please don’t litter! / Put it in the bin.. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: For less able children, focus on the exchange: Where’s the bin? (It’s [behind] the [door].) As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to say more about which bin the litter should go into. Challenge more confident children to perform their dialogue without reading their notes or prompts on the board. Additional information: Week 23, Lesson 2 Nouns apple bin compost bin food litter plastic bag recycling bin rubbish Verbs drop endanger pick up pollute recycle reuse throw away

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have talked about what to do when they see someone dropping litter, and have created and practised a dialogue giving them directions to the rubbish bin. They have distinguished between nouns and verbs in a vocabulary set.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about preventing litter using Please don’t litter! / Put it in the bin.. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Partners place source examples into two overlapping categories and explain one similarity and one difference.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.
