PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Ask children to tell you whether they live in a city, town, or village. Elicit how these places are similar and different. Ask: Do you think towns are great? Take some answers and focus on the reasons why.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to places in a town?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Explicitly model the first step of the original task: Pre-teach or elicit the lesson’s vocabulary using the flashcards. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise library, castle, swimming pool, park through places in a town prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Write definitions for other places in your local city, town, or village. Draw or write about a favourite place in your local city, town, or village.

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently identify and define town places without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using library, castle, swimming pool, park. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original review aim: Children have learned new vocabulary to describe places in a town. They have practised using the words in pairwork.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about places in a town using library, castle, swimming pool, park. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Looking after our places’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills in pairs by practising new vocabulary for places in a town.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.


Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you whether they live in a city, town, or village. Elicit how these places are similar and different. Ask: Do you think towns are great? Take some answers and focus on the reasons why.

Main activity
Pre-teach or elicit the lesson’s vocabulary using the flashcards. Sound out the words carefully, paying close attention to children’s pronunciation. Play a flashcard game with the words. Ask children to close their eyes, then remove one of the cards from the board and see if children can guess which is missing. Then take a card yourself without looking at it, but that children can see, and try to guess which card it is. Ask: Am I at the cinema? Am I at the hospital? Children continue to play the game in pairs. Using the definitions provided, read a definition for a place. Encourage children to listen carefully and to tell you the name of the place. Continue with more definitions. Hand out the definitions or write them on the board. Children continue to play the game in pairs, with the guessing child looking away from the handout or the board. Alternatively, children could play this as a guessing game with ‘yes’ and ‘no’ answers. For instance, they could ask: Can I swim here? Yes, you can! Is it a swimming pool? Yes, it is!

Additional tasks
Write definitions for other places in your local city, town, or village. Draw or write about a favourite place in your local city, town, or village.

Learning review
Children have learned new vocabulary to describe places in a town. They have practised using the words in pairwork.

Differentiation
Provide support for the definition game by giving the first letter or by indicating the number of letters. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to say or write new definitions in their own words.
