Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 4

Measurable targets: Identify key ideas about feelings and appropriate responses; practise When I am happy/sad/angry, I …; independently discuss feelings and what people can do. Check understanding through a relevant oral or written response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Pre-teach or elicit vocabulary for different feelings and emotions using drawings or mime. Play a game. Invite a confident child to the front of the class to mime a feeling. The class has to guess what it is. Children continue to play this game in pairs.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to feelings and appropriate responses?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Explicitly model the first step of the original task: Ask children what they do when they feel happy. Elicit some responses, such as ‘smile’ or ‘skip’. Then ask children what they do when they are angry, or talk about what you do when you are angry to model correct behaviour. Continue to do this, covering all of the lesson’s vocabulary, as well as other suggestions from children. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise When I am happy/sad/angry, I … through feelings and appropriate responses prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Write a sentence for each feeling. Think of ways to remember the calming activities suggested when children need them.

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently discuss feelings and what people can do without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using When I am happy/sad/angry, I …. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, focus on just two or three of the emotions. 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have talked about their emotions and considered what they do (or can do) when they experience negative emotions.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about feelings and appropriate responses using When I am happy/sad/angry, I …. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”

1. Teacher Mistake Detective — When I’m …

Lesson-specific language focus: asking permission and sharing

Original teaching source: Teacher: Pre-teach or elicit vocabulary for different feelings and emotions using drawings or mime. Play a game. Invite a confident child to the front of the class to mime a feeling. The class has to guess what it is. Children continue to play this game in pairs.

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Teacher Mistake Detective”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Teacher deliberately gives a wrong source example. Learners signal the error and model the correct response.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.
