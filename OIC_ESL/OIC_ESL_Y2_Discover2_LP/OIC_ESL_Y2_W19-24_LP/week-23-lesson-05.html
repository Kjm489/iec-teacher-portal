Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 5

Measurable targets: Identify key ideas about writing about recycling; practise first / next / finally; recycle; independently plan, draft and revise recycling writing. Check understanding through a relevant oral or written response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Write three sentences on the board: one in the present simple, one in the present continuous, and one in the past simple. Ask children to identify which is which. If necessary, explain the function of and difference between each tense. Tell children that they are going to pay special attention to these tenses today.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to writing about recycling?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Explicitly model the first step of the original task: Elicit from children different coloured recycling bins you have in your local area, such as green for glass, yellow for paper. Write some items on the board and invite children to tell you which colour bin they go in. Now show children your recycled object and ask them to think about its journey from what it was before to what it is now. Do this orally, writing key phrases on the board. Focus on the tenses, referring back to the story in Week 23, Lesson 4 to remind them. Ask children to retell their imagined journey to their partner. Remind them to think carefully about verb tenses as they are doing this. At the end of each retelling, their partner should say what they enjoyed about the story, as well as kindly noting any mistakes made (if applicable). 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise first / next / finally; recycle through writing about recycling prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Complete ex.A in Oxford Discover WB 2 p.107. Write about another recycled object.

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently plan, draft and revise recycling writing without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using first / next / finally; recycle. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, write a short story together in an adult-led small group. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to use the internet to find out more about the journey of their object.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have imagined and written about the journey of a recycled object.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about writing about recycling using first / next / finally; recycle. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”

1. Picture Evidence Hunt — Writing about recycling

Lesson-specific language focus: past and present

Original teaching source: Teacher: Write three sentences on the board: one in the present simple, one in the present continuous, and one in the past simple. Ask children to identify which is which. If necessary, explain the function of and difference between each tense. Tell children that they are going to pay special attention to these tenses today.

Materials: past-and-present picture pairs and a timeline

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Picture Evidence Hunt”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “In the past, people ___. Now, they ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about past and present.

YOU DO — game procedure: Show four source pictures; learners locate a requested detail and explain what in the picture supports it.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is yesterday in the past or the future?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about past and present.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “In the past, people ___. Now, they ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of past and present.
