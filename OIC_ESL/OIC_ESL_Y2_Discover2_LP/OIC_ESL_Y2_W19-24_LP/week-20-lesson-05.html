Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 5

Measurable targets: Identify key ideas about cultural manners and expectations; practise Do / Don’t; In my country …; independently compare local manners with respectful explanations. Check understanding through a relevant oral or written response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Ask children to stand up. Create a movement routine with them, in line with the ability level of your class. Practise the routine and come up with a fun name for this sequence of actions. Tell children that whenever you say [‘choose a word!’], they must quietly stand up, do the actions, and then continue with their work. Make this fun!

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to cultural manners and expectations?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Explicitly model the first step of the original task: Tell children you have received a letter. Show them the envelope and open it, encouraging them to guess what is inside. Read the letter clearly and elicit from children what your friend, Yusuf, wants. Discuss together which would be a good festival to attend in your country, or a festival that takes place in your school. Ask children to think about some dos and don’ts (i.e. rules to ensure that everybody is safe and has a nice time) for the festival or context they have chosen. Mind map some ideas as a class, looking back over the past two weeks, and encourage children to think about good manners and how they should treat others in their country. Children expand on their ideas in small groups and write some sentences together. On the board, show the class how to address and sign off in a letter (‘Dear …’ / ‘From …’). 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise Do / Don’t; In my country … through cultural manners and expectations prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Draw a picture of the festival. Think of another situation when dos and don’ts are important, and write about it (e.g. when meeting an older person, or when visiting a hospital or a library).

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently compare local manners with respectful explanations without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using Do / Don’t; In my country …. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, provide a gapped text. As an extension task for more able children, they can write about other aspects of the festival, including what to wear, etc. Remind them to use a variety of modal verbs. Additional information: Week 20, Lesson 5 Model letter Dear [teacher’s name], How are you? I’m coming to your country for [xxx] festival. What can I do at your school? What can’t I do? What about at the dinner table? Can you tell me? I don’t want to be rude. I want to be thoughtful. Write to me soon! From Yusuf

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have written a short letter to an imaginary friend about dos and don’ts in their country.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about cultural manners and expectations using Do / Don’t; In my country …. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”

1. Venn Diagram Detectives — Dos and Don’ts in my country

Lesson-specific language focus: food and healthy choices

Original teaching source: Teacher: Ask children to stand up. Create a movement routine with them, in line with the ability level of your class. Practise the routine and come up with a fun name for this sequence of actions. Tell children that whenever you say [‘choose a word!’], they must quietly stand up, do the actions, and then continue with their work. Make this fun!

Materials: food picture cards and two labelled sorting mats

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Venn Diagram Detectives”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about food and healthy choices.

YOU DO — game procedure: Partners place source examples into two overlapping categories and explain one similarity and one difference.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is an apple a fruit?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about food and healthy choices.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of food and healthy choices.
