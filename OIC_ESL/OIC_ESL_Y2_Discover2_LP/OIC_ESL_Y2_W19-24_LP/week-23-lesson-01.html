PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 2 p.99. Write an acrostic poem about a natural resource.

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently identify resources and their uses without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using water / air / trees / soil. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text or produce a piece of writing in an adult-led small group. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to find out more details (e.g. which trees make the best paper, or if there are any initiatives to plant more trees in your local area).

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 2 Looking after our places
Week 23, Lesson 1: We need natural resources
Learning outcomes: 2.3g, 2.4a, 2.4b

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Looking after our places’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and writing skills by identifying the main information in a text, then use it as inspiration to write about a chosen natural resource.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.


Introductory activity
Write ‘natural resources’ on the board and challenge children to make as many words as they can from the two words (e.g. ‘on’, ‘an’, ‘our’, ‘are’, ‘rest’, ‘cat’, ‘run’, ‘ran’, ‘turn’, etc.). They can use letters from both words. Model a few examples first and set a time limit.

Main activity
Tell children that today’s lesson is a reading and writing one, which might feel challenging – but this is OK! They will need to think deeply to extend their understanding of natural resources and the language used to talk about them. If the class enjoy it, you could play some soothing music in the background to focus children’s attention. Elicit names for natural resources using the flashcards or projected pictures. Ask children to think about why we need these resources, and what we can make with them or use them for. Share as many ideas as possible. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 pp.110–111. Divide the class into four or five groups. Assign a different section of the text to each group (introduction and/or first paragraph, paragraph about smoke, two paragraphs about rubbish [trash], and the bulleted list). Children read their section in pairs, then share what they have learned with the rest of the class. Note down key points on the board. Encourage children to share their reactions to the text. Elicit why they feel that way. Tell children they are now going to write about a natural resource. Ask them to think of the type of information they can write about. Give them the opportunity to research online, ask adults questions, or use reference books. You may like to use the following questions to prompt them to plan and think about their writing: What is the natural resource? Where/What does it come from? Why do we need it? What can we make with it? Children write a first draft of their work. Once you have checked it (or a classmate), encourage them to present it in a creative way. For example, if they are writing about water, they could cut out a drop of water from blue paper.

Additional tasks
Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 2 p.99. Write an acrostic poem about a natural resource.

Learning review
Children have read and written about natural resources and how we use them.

Differentiation
To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text or produce a piece of writing in an adult-led small group. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to find out more details (e.g. which trees make the best paper, or if there are any initiatives to plant more trees in your local area).
