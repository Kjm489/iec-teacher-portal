PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Play a game with children revising the place names from the previous lesson (Week 22, Lesson 2). Challenge them to tell you as many different places in a town as they can remember. Remind them that they have to keep on practising new vocabulary in order to remember it! Tell the class you are now going to say something you might overhear at a particular place: e.g. The water is very cold! (swimming pool). Invite a confident child to say a sentence for their classmates to guess. Continue to play the game in small groups.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to locating town places?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise next to / between / opposite / near through locating town places prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: describe where places are on a town map

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently describe where places are on a town map without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using next to / between / opposite / near. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: Support less able children in a small adult-led group to check their understanding of prepositions of place. Continue to use the cardboard box and toy. Challenge more able children to use prepositional language in their questions: e.g. What is in front of the castle? The library! Additional information: Week 22, Lesson 2 [Draw a simple map on the board showing, for example, that the shop is behind the swimming pool and the park is next to the museum and the supermarket.]

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have practised asking and answering about the location of places in a town using prepositional language.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about locating town places using next to / between / opposite / near. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”

YEAR 2 Looking after our places
Week 22, Lesson 2: Where’s the swimming pool?
Learning outcomes: 2.2a, 2.2d, 2.2e

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Looking after our places’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by saying where the places in a town are located.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.


Introductory activity
Play a game with children revising the place names from the previous lesson (Week 22, Lesson 1). Challenge them to tell you as many different places in a town as they can remember. Remind them that they have to keep on practising new vocabulary in order to remember it! Tell the class you are now going to say something you might overhear at a particular place: e.g. The water is very cold! (swimming pool). Invite a confident child to say a sentence for their classmates to guess. Continue to play the game in small groups.

Main activity
Using a cardboard box and a small toy, pre-teach or elicit prepositions of place. Ask children to take out their pencil cases and a rubber. Say: Put the rubber in front of your pencil case. Children do the action. Repeat with other prepositions of place to check their understanding. Complete exs.D–E in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.113. Ask children about a place in your town: e.g. Where is / Where’s [the library]? Draw a simple map on the board including a variety of places (see the ‘Additional information’ page for a visual to adapt). Use this to elicit a short dialogue: Excuse me, where is [the hospital]? Look, it’s [next to the restaurant and opposite / across from the library]. Model an example with another adult in the classroom or a confident child. Put children into pairs and give them the letters A and B. A asks a question, B describes the location of that place, and then they swap. They can tick off the prepositions when they have used them, so they can see which ones they still need to use. Challenge them to give each other directions to a place they both know (without saying where it is) that isn’t on the map. The listening child has to follow in their head and say where it is. Children then swap roles. At the end of the activity, congratulate children for their effort in trying to use all the prepositions of place in their descriptions. Additional task Draw a map of your local city, town, or village with the different places labelled. Continue to play the preposition game using this map.

Learning review
Children have practised asking and answering about the location of places in a town using prepositional language.

Differentiation
Support less able children in a small adult-led group to check their understanding of prepositions of place. Continue to use the cardboard box and toy. Challenge more able children to use prepositional language in their questions: e.g. What is in front of the castle? The library! Additional information: Week 22, Lesson 2 [Draw a simple map on the board showing, for example, that the shop is behind the swimming pool and the park is next to the museum and the supermarket.]
