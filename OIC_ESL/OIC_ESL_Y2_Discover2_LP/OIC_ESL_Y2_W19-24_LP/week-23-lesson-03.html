Measurable targets: Identify key ideas about clean and polluted places; practise clean / polluted / dirty / healthy; independently compare and explain evidence of pollution. Check understanding through a relevant oral or written response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Begin the lesson with a mindfulness activity. Put children into groups of four or five. Give each group a balloon and ask them if they can keep it off the floor. This is a silent activity – their movements have to be slow and gentle. See how long children can keep the balloon off the ground. Now ask children to sit back down and to breathe in and out slowly. As they breathe out, tell them to imagine that they are releasing all of their worries into a big balloon, then letting it fly away into the sky. Ask children if they enjoyed the mindfulness activity, and if they now feel calmer and ready to work.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to clean and polluted places?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise clean / polluted / dirty / healthy through clean and polluted places prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 2 p.98. Display children’s ideas for dealing with pollution in a poster. Act out the dialogue or story, changing information to make it more realistic for your context.

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently compare and explain evidence of pollution without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using clean / polluted / dirty / healthy. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, let them follow the transcript. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to think about activities that might cause pollution and activities that are more environmentally friendly.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have listened to a dialogue or story about pollution and identified the key words. They have thought about how we can solve the pollution problem, focusing on their context.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about clean and polluted places using clean / polluted / dirty / healthy. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”
