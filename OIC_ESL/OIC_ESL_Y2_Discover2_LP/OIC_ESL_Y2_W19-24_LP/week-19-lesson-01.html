Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 1

Measurable targets: Identify key ideas about polite and rude actions; practise knock, help, wash, thoughtful, rude; independently sort actions into Do and Don’t. Check understanding through a relevant oral or written response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Tell children that the next two weeks are about being a ‘good person’. Ask children: What is a good person? Elicit a couple of ideas. It might be easier for children to think about what is not a good person, what this person does, or what this person doesn’t do. Put children into pairs. Once they are talking, ask them to think about some actions that ‘good people’ do.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to polite and rude actions?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Explicitly model the first step of the original task: Introduce the vocabulary by either miming or giving a definition of the word: e.g. ‘knock’ – Is it OK to just walk into class once it’s started? 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise knock, help, wash, thoughtful, rude through polite and rude actions prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Complete ex.B in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.96 and exs.A–C in WB 2 p.82. Encourage children to draw a picture of themselves doing one of the actions. They can write sentences saying when they do and don’t do the actions: e.g. I wash my hands before I eat. I don’t wash my hands before I go to bed.

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently sort actions into Do and Don’t without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using knock, help, wash, thoughtful, rude. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, put them in a group with more able children. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to add more words to illustrate what ‘thoughtful’ people do and what ‘rude’ people do.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have learned new vocabulary on the topic of good manners. They have practised listening and speaking during a running dictation.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about polite and rude actions using knock, help, wash, thoughtful, rude. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”
