Measurable Learning Objectives

Lesson: 2

Measurable targets: Identify key ideas about household and classroom chores; practise I sweep / tidy / wash / help; independently create and explain a chores chart. Check understanding through a relevant oral or written response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Teacher: Elicit what day of the week it is today, and ask children how to spell it. Play a spelling game based on the days of the week. Say the first letter: e.g. ‘T’, then the next child says either ‘h’ or ‘u’, followed by the next child saying the following letter (e.g. if the second child says ‘h’, then the next letter is ‘u’, spelling ‘Thursday’). Repeat a few times, ensuring the participation of all children. Write the days of the week on the board for reference.

Student action: Think, pair, share a lesson-related example before viewing the model. ICQs: “Are we sharing what we know or writing our final answer?” “Who speaks first?” CCQs: “Which action, picture or word connects to household and classroom chores?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Teacher demonstration: Explicitly model the first step of the original task: Show a picture of an untidy bedroom. Ask children what is wrong with this room (it needs tidying up!). Follow up by asking: Who should tidy this bedroom? Do you keep your bedroom tidy? Encourage children to consider how their parents or carers might feel having to ask them to tidy up or do chores, and how they might feel if they didn’t need to ask. Ask children: What other things do you do around the house to help? Use the Think-Pair-Share technique. Hold a whole-class discussion and write useful words and phrases on the board. Elicit from children a list of chores they do around the house to help out, such as ‘dry the dishes’, ‘put away the toys’, etc. Write: ‘I make my bed in my bedroom.’ Ask children: What is the thing (noun/object)? 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partner exchange: In pairs, learners practise I sweep / tidy / wash / help through household and classroom chores prompts. Partner A gives a description, question or example; Partner B responds and points to evidence. Swap roles and repeat with a different prompt.

ICQs: “Do both partners have a turn?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Was the response appropriate for this situation?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Compare chores in the countryside (e.g. feeding animals) and chores in the city. Create a whole-class song about the chores children have to do. Use a traditional melody or a simple beat. Ask children to draw a picture of themselves doing one of the chores and write a sentence describing the action.

Students complete their own response, drawing, map, chart, writing, dialogue or project artifact as the original lesson requires. Teacher checks whether each learner can independently create and explain a chores chart without copying the group model.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: picture/word cards, gesture, two-choice questions and a sentence starter using I sweep / tidy / wash / help. Developing: complete a full response with one relevant detail. Extending: explain why, compare two examples, or create a new scenario.

Original differentiation: To support less able children, provide a chart template including the days of the week. Encourage more confident children to write sentences about their chart.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Original review aim: Children have created a useful chart to encourage them to think about the chores they need to do at home to help out.

Exit ticket: Respond to a fresh prompt about household and classroom chores using I sweep / tidy / wash / help. Mark Independent / Prompted / Not yet and identify who needs follow-up practice. CCQ: “What did we learn and how do we show it?”
