Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.1j, 2.2e, 2.2f

Learning goal: Students will give an animal preference and reason. Language scaffold: My favourite animal is ___ because ___.. Materials: animal choice cards, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Show children a flashcard of an animal that usually lives in a group and ask: Do [lions] usually live alone or in a group? You could teach collective nouns: e.g. ‘flock’, ‘pride’, ‘herd’, etc. Ask children to name some animals that usually live alone, such as bears, rhinos, or leopards.

Show animal choice cards. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to give an animal preference and reason?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.15. Children watch the video (see Big Question 1), and note down the animals they see and what the animals can do. Ask children to think about their own favourite animal. Ask children to make a few notes about their favourite animal. They are going to describe it in three sentences. Give them an example, such as: My favourite animal eats meat. It doesn’t live in a group and it’s got spots. (leopard) Remind children to keep their animal secret.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: My favourite animal is ___ because ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses give an animal preference and reason?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Elicit ‘yes/no’ question forms. Ask: Does your animal eat meat? Does your animal live alone? Children are going to guess their classmates’ animals by asking ‘yes/no’ questions. Depending on the size of your class, put children into groups of six to eight. Children stand up and mingle in their group, but they can only ask one question before moving on to another person. At no point must anyone say what their animals are. Set a time limit (five to seven minutes). Ask everyone to sit down individually and in silence, then write down the favourite animals of their group: e.g. ‘Rebekah – leopard, Hueng – whale’, and so on.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: In their groups, they check their ideas. Remind children to speak one at a time and to listen to each other’s ideas. Encourage them to explain their choices: e.g. I think your favourite animal is a fish because you say it lives in water. Ask children to think about whether they prefer to be in groups or alone, and why. Give them the opportunity to Think-Pair-Share. Discuss the benefits of belonging to a group.

Pairs use animal choice cards; one asks or explains and the other responds with My favourite animal is ___ because ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to give an animal preference and reason using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: In their groups, they check their ideas. Remind children to speak one at a time and to listen to each other’s ideas. Encourage them to explain their choices: e.g. I think your favourite animal is a fish because you say it lives in water. Ask children to think about whether they prefer to be in groups or alone, and why. Give them the opportunity to Think-Pair-Share. Discuss the benefits of belonging to a group.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “My favourite animal is ___ because ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.9. Draw an animal and write some sentences to label it. Find out more about an animal. Repeat the activity, but with an animal group rather than one particular animal.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen give an animal preference and reason example. Ask students to explain their choice using My favourite animal is ___ because ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have participated in a mingle activity in which they have asked and answered questions about their favourite animal. Differentiation To support less confident children, write the questions on the board. Provide them with three gapped sentences to use if they like. Challenge more confident children by asking them to find out about an Australian animal or a jungle animal.

YEAR 2 Amazing animals
Week 2, Lesson 2: What is your favourite animal?
Learning outcomes: 2.1j, 2.2e, 2.2f

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Amazing animals’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills through a mingle activity with their classmates.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
