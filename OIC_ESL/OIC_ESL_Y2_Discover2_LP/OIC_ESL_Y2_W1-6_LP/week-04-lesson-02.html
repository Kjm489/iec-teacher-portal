LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 2
Theme: Things that change
Week: 4
Lesson: 2
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 2.3c, 2.3f

Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.3c, 2.3f

Learning goal: Students will predict or explain change. Language scaffold: If ___, then ___.. Materials: before-and-after picture sequences, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Play a spelling game to revise the words from Week 4, Lesson 1. Write the words on the board. In pairs, children ‘write’ the words letter by letter on each other’s backs (using their fingers) for their partner to guess the word.

Show before-and-after picture sequences. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to predict or explain change?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Write,draw on the board or tell children a story about a girl who drops her ice creamand then cries. Ask: Which action comes first – dropping the ice cream or crying? Then on the board, write ‘Cause’ and ‘Effect’. Under ‘Cause’, write or draw an ice cream on the ground, and under ‘Effect’, a crying girl. Be ready to accept the idea that perhaps the girl was already crying and then she dropped her ice cream! Give another example: e.g. My plants grow because I water them. Children identify the cause (watering the plants) and the effect (they grow).

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: If ___, then ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses predict or explain change?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Invite children to give you some of their own examples. Show children your graphic organizer and tell them that they are going to complete it with some examples from their course book. They might see a picture that shows a cause and an effect, or they might read something that makes them think of cause and effect. Put children into pairs for this activity. Set a time limit to find one cause and effect example in their course book. Between two and five minutes would be reasonable.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Hold a class feedback session. Ask if everyone agrees with each other’s examples, and check your instructions were understood properly. Give children ten minutes to find more examples, depending on how many you want them to find (see below). Children look through their course books to identify some situations of cause and effect. Do some examples together, looking at the following pages: Oxford Discover SB 2 p.29 (various examples, see ex.C). Be sure to praise children for identifying cause and effect situations and making an effort to explain their reasoning (even if it isn’t quite right) as you monitor.

Pairs use before-and-after picture sequences; one asks or explains and the other responds with If ___, then ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to predict or explain change using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Hold a class feedback session. Ask if everyone agrees with each other’s examples, and check your instructions were understood properly. Give children ten minutes to find more examples, depending on how many you want them to find (see below). Children look through their course books to identify some situations of cause and effect. Do some examples together, looking at the following pages: Oxford Discover SB 2 p.29 (various examples, see ex.C). Be sure to praise children for identifying cause and effect situations and making an effort to explain their reasoning (even if it isn’t quite right) as…

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “If ___, then ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.22. Find more examples of cause and effect in Oxford Discover WB 2. Illustrate other classmates’ examples, and then see how similar or different they are to the book.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen predict or explain change example. Ask students to explain their choice using If ___, then ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have learned about the concept of cause and effect, and completed a graphic organizer. Differentiation To support less confident children, ask them to find just two examples. They can complete the graphic organizer with drawings, rather than words or sentences. As an extension task for more confident children, ask them to think of more examples of cause and effect in their daily routine.

Materials: past-and-present picture pairs and a timeline

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Two Clues, One Answer”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “In the past, people ___. Now, they ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about past and present.

YOU DO — game procedure: Give two clues about a lesson concept; pairs predict, justify and compare with the original source.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is yesterday in the past or the future?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about past and present.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “In the past, people ___. Now, they ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of past and present.
