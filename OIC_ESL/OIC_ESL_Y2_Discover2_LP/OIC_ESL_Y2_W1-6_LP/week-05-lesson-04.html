Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.4a, 2.4b

Learning goal: Students will classify states of matter. Language scaffold: A ___ is a solid/liquid/gas because ___.. Materials: states-of-matter picture cards, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Everyone stands up. Put children into three groups – solids, liquids, and gases. Say an item (e.g. cheese) and the solid group has to jump up and down. Continue, gradually making it harder (e.g. lemonade with pulp), and everyone has to jump (solid, liquid, and gas). Everyone sits down. Now ask children to take out their paper/whiteboards and pens. You are going to do the same activity, but they do it individually, and they write ‘solid’, ‘liquid’, or ‘gas’ (or S, L, or G). You say some new items.

Show states-of-matter picture cards. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to classify states of matter?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Tell children that today’s activities will reinforce what they have learned over the past two weeks. Remind them of, or talk to them about, the concept ‘Fire together, wire together’. Ask children to look around and identify solids, liquids, and gases they can see. They might have to look out of the window, as well as at the weather. Say: Do we use ‘is’ or ‘was’ for the past? Do we use ‘are’ or ‘were’ for the past? For the past, we use ‘was’ and ‘were’. Children repeat. Ask them if they think they can remember this.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: A ___ is a solid/liquid/gas because ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses classify states of matter?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Children draw a picture and label the different parts of it with ‘solid’, ‘liquid’, or ‘gas’. They then write some sentences using was and were: e.g. Today, there are three solids on my desk, but yesterday, there were four. Hand out smaller pieces of paper and children draw a single image or write a word on each one.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Ask them to create some ‘odd one out’ activities for their partner. They place four of the images on the desk, and ask their partner to identify the odd one out: e.g. ‘cheese’, ‘yoghurt’, ‘desk’, ‘table’ (yoghurt is the odd one out because it is a liquid, and the others are solids). Then they write sentences about their partner’s game: e.g. In Mina’s game, the odd one out was yoghurt because it is a liquid. The other things are solids. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.44. Children read and listen to the story.

Pairs use states-of-matter picture cards; one asks or explains and the other responds with A ___ is a solid/liquid/gas because ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to classify states of matter using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Ask them to create some ‘odd one out’ activities for their partner. They place four of the images on the desk, and ask their partner to identify the odd one out: e.g. ‘cheese’, ‘yoghurt’, ‘desk’, ‘table’ (yoghurt is the odd one out because it is a liquid, and the others are solids). Then they write sentences about their partner’s game: e.g. In Mina’s game, the odd one out was yoghurt because it is a liquid. The other things are solids. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.44. Children read and listen to the story.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “A ___ is a solid/liquid/gas because ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.33. Find a picture and write a sentence about it for today and yesterday, using was and were. Write definitions of solids, liquids, and gases. Search online, if possible. Use own words (rather than copying). Free writing helps children to learn better.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen classify states of matter example. Ask students to explain their choice using A ___ is a solid/liquid/gas because ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have revised and reinforced their knowledge of solids, liquids, and gases. Differentiation To support less confident children, provide a picture for them to label which has clear examples in it. Then they can look for their own examples. As an extension task for more confident children, ask them to think of unusual places to find solids, liquids, and gases (e.g. gas – air in a bicycle tyre).

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Things that change’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by writing some short sentences to play some games. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
