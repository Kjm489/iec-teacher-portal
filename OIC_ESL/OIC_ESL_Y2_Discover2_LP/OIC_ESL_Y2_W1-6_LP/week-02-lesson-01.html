Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.3a, 2.4a

Learning goal: Students will describe animal body parts. Language scaffold: A ___ has ___.. Materials: labeled and unlabeled animal diagrams, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Play a spelling game with children. You say an animal, and the next child has to think of an animal beginning with the last letter of your animal: e.g. bird – dog – goat, and so on. Try not to repeat animals within the chain.

Show labeled and unlabeled animal diagrams. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to describe animal body parts?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Pre-teach or revise animal body parts. You might do this with flashcards of the body parts, or perhaps flashcards of different animals with the different body parts. Alternatively, use projected pictures of close-ups of animals, just showing the body parts. Agree on mimes for the various body parts with the class. Everyone stands up. You say a body part and children do the action.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: A ___ has ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses describe animal body parts?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.14. Play the listening in ex.A and children repeat the words. Then ask them to close their books. You say the word and they write it down. Then they open their books to see if they spelled the word correctly. Do ex.B. Children write a short paragraph describing an animal and its body parts. They must also add a body part which doesn’t belong to that animal.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Children swap their work and their partner has to identify which is the incorrect or illogical information. An example of illogical could be that animals don’t have three feet. Hold a whole-class feedback session. Ask children how they feel about today’s lesson, and encourage them to name their feelings (e.g. satisfied, happy, worried, etc.). Try to encourage them to link their feelings to the lesson: e.g. I feel worried because spelling is hard. I feel satisfied because I finished my work.

Pairs use labeled and unlabeled animal diagrams; one asks or explains and the other responds with A ___ has ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to describe animal body parts using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Children swap their work and their partner has to identify which is the incorrect or illogical information. An example of illogical could be that animals don’t have three feet. Hold a whole-class feedback session. Ask children how they feel about today’s lesson, and encourage them to name their feelings (e.g. satisfied, happy, worried, etc.). Try to encourage them to link their feelings to the lesson: e.g. I feel worried because spelling is hard. I feel satisfied because I finished my work.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “A ___ has ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.8. Write clues about an animal and play a guessing game in pairs. Repeat the activity as a team guessing game.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen describe animal body parts example. Ask students to explain their choice using A ___ has ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have developed their reading and writing skills by reading and writing short texts about animal body parts. Differentiation To support less confident children, provide a gapped text about an animal. You could also provide a choice of animals for them to write about. As an extension task for more confident children, they add information about why the animal has this body part: e.g. A kangaroo has a tail to help it jump.

Materials: animal picture cards, action-verb cards and the original animal examples

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Memory Tray”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “An animal can ___, but it cannot ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about animals and their abilities.

YOU DO — game procedure: Display six source picture cards for 30 seconds; hide one and ask learners to name and describe the missing item.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Can a whale swim? Can it fly?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about animals and their abilities.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “An animal can ___, but it cannot ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of animals and their abilities.

YEAR 2 Amazing animals
Week 2, Lesson 1: Animal parts
Learning outcomes: 2.3a, 2.4a 

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Amazing animals’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and writing skills by learning the words for animal body parts. They write about an animal, describing its body parts.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
