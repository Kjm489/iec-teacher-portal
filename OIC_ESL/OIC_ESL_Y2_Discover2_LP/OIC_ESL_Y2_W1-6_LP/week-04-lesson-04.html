Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.3b, 2.3f

Learning goal: Students will identify materials. Language scaffold: It is made of ___.. Materials: everyday material pictures, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Play a game with children. You say an item and children tell you if it is liquid, solid, or gas. If your class permits, you can make this an active game by asking children to run or walk to different parts of the class. Alternatively, take children outside to play in the playground. Then ask children to come up with a list of things to test the other groups.

Show everyday material pictures. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to identify materials?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Put the flashcards or projected pictures on the board. Ask children how water becomes ice, and write ‘freeze’ on the board. Then add a picture of water, ask how ice becomes water, and write ‘melt’. Finally, show the picture of steam and write ‘heat’. Make sentences with children. Ask children to identify the solid, liquid, and gas. Establish with children that things change their state when we make them hot or cold.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: It is made of ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses identify materials?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Give everyone a number – 1, 2, or 3, where 1 = solid, 2 = liquid, and 3 = gas. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.30. Say: You have a text in front of you about solids, liquids, and gases. You must remember your part of the text. You have two minutes. Ready? Go! Children read their texts, knowing that they must remember their part based on their number. Explain that remembering information in a short amount of time can be difficult and that children are in the ‘stretch zone’. A little discomfort will help them to learn and cope with pressure. When the time is up, ask them to close their books or turn over the page. They write down as much as they can remember.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Give them another minute to read, but not write. Then they can write or add more. Put all the 1s, 2s, and 3s together (or small groups of 1s, 2s, and 3s). Encourage children to share what they have written and see if they can now reconstruct the text together. Children open their books or turn over their papers, and compare their texts against the original. Ask: Have you remembered as much as you expected? Was it easy to remember? Why? Why not? Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.31. Children read and and answer the ‘Think’ questions in pairs (as a Think-Pair-Share).

Pairs use everyday material pictures; one asks or explains and the other responds with It is made of ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to identify materials using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Give them another minute to read, but not write. Then they can write or add more. Put all the 1s, 2s, and 3s together (or small groups of 1s, 2s, and 3s). Encourage children to share what they have written and see if they can now reconstruct the text together. Children open their books or turn over their papers, and compare their texts against the original. Ask: Have you remembered as much as you expected? Was it easy to remember? Why? Why not? Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.31. Children read and and answer the ‘Think’ questions in pairs (as a Think-Pair-Share).

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “It is made of ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.21. Think about changes that happen to chocolate, jelly, eggs, and bread, and write sentences or draw pictures: e.g. When you heat bread, you make toast.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen identify materials example. Ask students to explain their choice using It is made of ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have increased their knowledge and understanding of changes in matter. Differentiation To support less confident children, give them a gapped text, or ask them to remember only one sentence. As an extension task for more confident children, ask them to find out about changing states due to weather events, and write about them.

YEAR 2 Things that change
Week 4, Lesson 4: What is the world made of?

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Things that change’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by reading a short text.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
