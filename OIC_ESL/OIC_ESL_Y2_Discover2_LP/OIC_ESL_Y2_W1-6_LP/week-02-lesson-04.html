Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.1a, 2.2b

Learning goal: Students will explain how animals help people. Language scaffold: A ___ helps people by ___.. Materials: working-animal pictures, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Play a quick mime game with the verbs from Week 2, Lesson 3: ‘crawl’, ‘creep’, ‘escape’, ‘fight’, ‘hunt’, ‘jump’, ‘peck’, ‘sleep’, and ‘squawk’. You say a word and children mime it. Explain that they have to practise certain things over and over in order to master new language. Choose a more able child to call out a verb and everyone has to mime.

Show working-animal pictures. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to explain how animals help people?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Tell children that today they are going to think about how animals can help people.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: A ___ helps people by ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses explain how animals help people?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.17. Tell children they are going to read a story about animals. Ask them if cats and birds are friends. Then ask them to think of a situation in which they might help each other. Read the story of Leo and Lily (or listen to it) on pp.18–19. In pairs, children compare their answers. Ask them if they think that their stories could be true. Encourage children to think about fact and fiction, and how fiction can explain things or how we act (stories can have a ‘moral’).

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Read or tell children the story of The Hare and the Tortoise, or another story with animals which can help people understand situations. To do this, split your story into six parts. Children draw a picture after each part of the story. Then they retell the story together in pairs. Ask: Why do you think [the hare] behaved in this way? Conclude that these are some ways animals can help people understand their own different behaviours and situations.

Pairs use working-animal pictures; one asks or explains and the other responds with A ___ helps people by ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to explain how animals help people using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Read or tell children the story of The Hare and the Tortoise, or another story with animals which can help people understand situations. To do this, split your story into six parts. Children draw a picture after each part of the story. Then they retell the story together in pairs. Ask: Why do you think [the hare] behaved in this way? Conclude that these are some ways animals can help people understand their own different behaviours and situations.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “A ___ helps people by ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.12–13. Act out one of the stories: Lily and Leo, The Hare and the Tortoise. Hand out a gapped copy of the story of The Hare and the Tortoise or another animal story, and children complete it. Encourage them to read the story themselves. Alternatively, to save time preparing a handout, you could cover up words on a projected picture of the hard copy book or online text.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen explain how animals help people example. Ask students to explain their choice using A ___ helps people by ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have listened to and spoken about how animals in stories can help people. Differentiation To support less confident children, provide some pictures of key words. Challenge more confident children by asking them to create their own animal story which can help people (with a moral or lesson at the end).

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Sort and Explain”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “An animal can ___, but it cannot ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

YOU DO — game procedure: Teams sort six lesson cards into two meaningful groups and orally explain the rule.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “An animal can ___, but it cannot ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of animals and their abilities.
