Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.2e, 2.4a

Learning goal: Students will identify shared features. Language scaffold: Both animals have ___.. Materials: paired animal photographs, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Ask children if they can do star jumps. Demonstrate. Tell them at three points in the lesson, you are going to point to the star and they should stop what they are doing and take position ready for star jumps. Be mindful of children with limited mobility – how can they participate? Do an example with them first. Ask children to pretend to be busy, point to your star, and insist that everyone stands up very quietly. Then you count out loud and they do ten star jumps. They then sit down quietly and continue as though nothing had happened.

Show paired animal photographs. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to identify shared features?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Ask children to open their books to find out how animals are the same. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 pp.10–11. Ask children to read about birds and fish first on p.10. Hold a feedback session, then children read about amphibians and mammals. Put the list of similarities on the board. Point to the star. Children stand up and do ten star jumps. Sit down. Write these sentences on the board: ‘Birds lay eggs. Fish lay eggs too. Both birds and fish lay eggs.’ Ask children: Which words do we use when things are the same? Which word do we use when things are different? How many sentences are there with ‘too’? With ‘both’? With ‘but’? Are the words at the beginning, in the middle, or at the end of the sentences?

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: Both animals have ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses identify shared features?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Say two animals (e.g. frog and rabbit). Together, make sentences with too, both, and but. Ask children to write a sentence about two animals with too and both. Children display traffic light ‘help’ buttons. Point to the star. Children stand up and do ten star jumps. Sit down. Write on the board: ‘Birds can fly. They have got wings.’ Ask children what ‘they’ refers to (‘birds’). Elicit that we do this because we don’t want to repeat ‘birds’.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Ask children to write two sentences. The second sentence should begin with they. Randomly choose an animal. Children display traffic light help buttons. Point to the star. Children stand up and do ten star jumps. Sit down. Ask children to get into groups. They work as a team to write about two animals and their similarities. They should use both, too, and they (i.e. not repeating the noun). Ask children if they enjoyed the star jumps. Elicit how moving their bodies makes them feel.

Pairs use paired animal photographs; one asks or explains and the other responds with Both animals have ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to identify shared features using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Ask children to write two sentences. The second sentence should begin with they. Randomly choose an animal. Children display traffic light help buttons. Point to the star. Children stand up and do ten star jumps. Sit down. Ask children to get into groups. They work as a team to write about two animals and their similarities. They should use both, too, and they (i.e. not repeating the noun). Ask children if they enjoyed the star jumps. Elicit how moving their bodies makes them feel.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “Both animals have ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.5 exs.A–B. Write about two more animals using it or they.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen identify shared features example. Ask students to explain their choice using Both animals have ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have read, spoken, and written about similarities in animals using new language. Differentiation To support less confident children, provide a gapped text for them to complete. As an extension task for more confident children, they write about both similarities and differences.
