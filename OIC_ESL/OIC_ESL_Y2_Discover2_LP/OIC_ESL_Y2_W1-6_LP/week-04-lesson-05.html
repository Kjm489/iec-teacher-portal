Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.2b, 2.2e, 2.4a

Learning goal: Students will describe observable properties. Language scaffold: It looks ___ and feels ___.. Materials: mystery object picture cards, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Point to the ‘star jump time’ star you made in Week 1, Lesson 3. Tell children that today they will be doing lots of jumping. Talk with them about moving their bodies and why it is important. How much have they moved their bodies already today? Tell them that you are going to ask them to do ten star jumps every ten minutes. Set a timer to remind yourself. Be mindful of children with limited mobility – set an alternative joyful task to ensure their participation.

Show mystery object picture cards. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to describe observable properties?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.34. Play the listening in ex.A and ask children to identify solids, liquids, and gases. Review the words using realia, and discuss how states can change: e.g. An ice pop is a solid until it melts and becomes liquid. A kettle is a solid, but it turns liquids into steam. Children are going to make a game. Give an example clue: e.g. When people heat me, I change to steam. (water) Write the sentence on the board for reference, drawing children’s attention to the sentence construction. Ask children what it is (water). Say: This is a clue. I don’t say what it is. It’s a clue.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: It looks ___ and feels ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses describe observable properties?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Put children into small groups. Each group writes five clues. They will need to discuss what they are going to write about and how they are going to write it. Encourage them to try to do this in English as much as possible. Children can choose any items from the week’s topic (changing states). Remind children to set their traffic light ‘help’ buttons to green or red, indicating whether or not they need help.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Set your timer, and from now until the end of the lesson, every ten minutes, stop and do ten star jumps with children. When everyone has written their five clues, play the game. Groups earn points for correctly answering the other groups’ questions. Reflect with children if they enjoyed the game and whether they enjoyed jumping at regular intervals.

Pairs use mystery object picture cards; one asks or explains and the other responds with It looks ___ and feels ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to describe observable properties using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Set your timer, and from now until the end of the lesson, every ten minutes, stop and do ten star jumps with children. When everyone has written their five clues, play the game. Groups earn points for correctly answering the other groups’ questions. Reflect with children if they enjoyed the game and whether they enjoyed jumping at regular intervals.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “It looks ___ and feels ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.26 exs.A–B. Write down three favourite sentences from the game. Display on the wall. Choose a topic and say a clue. The other group has to guess what the word is.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen describe observable properties example. Ask students to explain their choice using It looks ___ and feels ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have made and played a guessing game about matter and changing states. Differentiation To support less confident children, provide some gapped sentences for them to complete or some words for them to define. Mix more able and less able children together in groups so they can help each other. As an extension task for more confident children, ask them to write more clues and create a simple crossword.

YEAR 2 Things that change
Week 4, Lesson 5: Guess what it is …
Learning outcomes: 2.2b, 2.2e, 2.4a

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Things that change’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing and speaking skills by writing clues for a guessing game, and then playing it.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
