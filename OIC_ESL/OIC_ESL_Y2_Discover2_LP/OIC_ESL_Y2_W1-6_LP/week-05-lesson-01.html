Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.2a, 2.2e

Learning goal: Students will explain temperature-related change. Language scaffold: When ___ gets warm, it ___.. Materials: sun and snow sequence cards, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Play ‘Snowman’ on the board. Choose a word from a previous lesson. Draw a blank line (dash) for each letter in the word. Children suggest letters, and the objective is to spell the word before the snowman is completed. If they guess a letter correctly, you write the letter. If not, you start drawing a snowman in nine steps (three circles for the snowman, two sticks for its arms, then a hat, two eyes, and a line for its mouth). These correspond to each wrong guess.

Show sun and snow sequence cards. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to explain temperature-related change?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.33. Tell children they are going to listen to a song about a snowman (ex.D). Ask them to note down all the body parts they hear. Play it again and ask children what happened to the snowman (it melted).

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: When ___ gets warm, it ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses explain temperature-related change?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Ask how it happened, and elicit that the sun melts snow. Elicit or write on the board: ‘The snowman was solid. Now the snowman is liquid.’ Elicit that was is the past. Repeat with: ‘The snowballs were hard. Now they are soft.’

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Put children in pairs to make sentences together (ex.E) using was and were to talk about the past. Now tell children they are going to make up a ‘chant’. Quickly elicit what a chant is, and emphasize the importance of the rhythm. Show children how to keep the rhythm by tapping a foot or a finger, or clapping. Put children into small groups to make up two sentences using is, are, was, and were. Give them time to think of their sentences and to practise saying them while keeping to a rhythm. They could clap or beat a pencil on the table. Ask the class to chant together. Encourage everyone to clap or tap a pencil. Then ask pairs to say their…

Pairs use sun and snow sequence cards; one asks or explains and the other responds with When ___ gets warm, it ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to explain temperature-related change using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “When ___ gets warm, it ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.24. Make a list of things that have changed or could change in the classroom.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen explain temperature-related change example. Ask students to explain their choice using When ___ gets warm, it ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have learned was and were to talk about the past, and have practised their speaking skills through a chant. Differentiation To support less confident children, put them in a pair with a more confident child. Ask them to draw pictures to illustrate the snowman, and reinforce was and were. As an extension task for more confident children, they can create a whole verse for the chant.

Week 5, Lesson 1: Sun and snow don’t go

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Things that change’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by making up a chant.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
