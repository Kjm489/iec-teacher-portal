Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.2a, 2.2b

Learning goal: Students will sequence and describe a change. Language scaffold: First ___, then ___, finally ___.. Materials: ice-cream process picture cards, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Remind children of the ‘stretch zone’. Hold a conversation about what is difficult, why something can be difficult, and what to do when something is difficult (ask for help, breathe, and don’t get flustered). Difficult activities can help to extend children’s understanding of a concept or topic.

Show ice-cream process picture cards. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to sequence and describe a change?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.41 and play the song in ex.D. Elicit was and were. Ask a few ‘yes/no’ questions. Ask: Was the freezer door open? Was the classroom door open? Were the books on the table? Were children inside the classroom? Write the questions, and postive and negative answers (e.g. ‘Yes, they were’; ‘No, it wasn’t’) on the board.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: First ___, then ___, finally ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses sequence and describe a change?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Quickly draw a tub of ice cream on a table on an individual whiteboard, but keep it secret from children. Ask: Was it on, under, or behind the table? Draw again using a different preposition (e.g. ice cream under the table) and repeat with a confident child. Put children into pairs. One draws and the other guesses. Then they swap. Tell children that if they are finding this hard, it is OK because they are in the stretch zone, so it is supposed to be harder. Check if there are any particular difficulties, such as remembering the word order, remembering prepositions, or using was or were.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Encourage children to ask for help if they need to do so. Put children into groups of three. They repeat the exercise. Tell them to draw things in, on, under, or behind a box. They can have fun with this by thinking of funny or unusual things! Hold a whole-class feedback session.

Pairs use ice-cream process picture cards; one asks or explains and the other responds with First ___, then ___, finally ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to sequence and describe a change using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Encourage children to ask for help if they need to do so. Put children into groups of three. They repeat the exercise. Tell them to draw things in, on, under, or behind a box. They can have fun with this by thinking of funny or unusual things! Hold a whole-class feedback session.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “First ___, then ___, finally ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.32 (if not done previously). Find a picture online and write some sentences about it, underlining that the picture is from yesterday (not today).

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen sequence and describe a change example. Ask students to explain their choice using First ___, then ___, finally ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have practised asking and answering questions using was and were and prepositions. Differentiation To support less confident children, provide some pictures. Write questions on the board. As an extension task for more confident children, recount what happened when they made ice cream.

Week 5, Lesson 3: What happened to the ice cream?
Learning outcomes: 2.2a, 2.2b 

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Things that change’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by asking and answering questions.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
