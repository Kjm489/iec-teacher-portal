LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 2
Theme: Amazing animals
Week: 1
Lesson: 2
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 2.3e, 2.3f

Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.3e, 2.3f

Learning goal: Students will compare animal features. Language scaffold: A ___ has ___, but a ___ has ___.. Materials: two contrasting animal photos, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Ask children to stand up. Say: Move like an elephant! Move like a dolphin! Children stamp their feet like an elephant, swim like a dolphin, or do the actions for any other animals named. Encourage a more confident child to call out the animal names. Remind children that moving their body regularly helps them to feel good and stay healthy. Ask children: Are all animals the same? What things are different? Elicit some differences: e.g. where they live, body parts, colours, what they can do, etc.

Show two contrasting animal photos. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to compare animal features?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Hold up two of the animal flashcards and ask children how the animals are different. Write some sentences on the board. Elicit from children different ways to say things that are different. For example, use the negative form: e.g. It has got legs. It hasn’t got legs. Alternatively, change the information to make it true: e.g. It lives on water. It lives on land. Say: This is comparing and contrasting. Write on the board: ‘Birds lay eggs, but lions don’t lay eggs.’ Ask children: Where does the contrasting word go? (in the middle of the two sentences).

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: A ___ has ___, but a ___ has ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses compare animal features?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Tell children but is a useful word to look for when you want to find things that are different. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.9. Read the text about mice and hamsters, and identify how they are different. Now ask children to use their notes to write two sentences using but. Hold a whole-class feedback session to check answers. Tell children they are going to work in pairs and compare and contrast two similar animals (Oxford Discover has mice and hamsters as an example). Children then write three sentences saying how the animals are different using but.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Children find out about the two animals. Encourage them to use the internet or reference books for their research. Children draw their two animals (taking up half of an A4 sheet of paper each). Hand out the ice lolly sticks and encourage children to work out for themselves how to stick the picture to the stick with the sticky tape. Ensure that you praise everyone on their efforts before the presentation stage. Children present their two animals to the class, holding up the animals appropriately.

Pairs use two contrasting animal photos; one asks or explains and the other responds with A ___ has ___, but a ___ has ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to compare animal features using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Children find out about the two animals. Encourage them to use the internet or reference books for their research. Children draw their two animals (taking up half of an A4 sheet of paper each). Hand out the ice lolly sticks and encourage children to work out for themselves how to stick the picture to the stick with the sticky tape. Ensure that you praise everyone on their efforts before the presentation stage. Children present their two animals to the class, holding up the animals appropriately.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “A ___ has ___, but a ___ has ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.4. Find out about two more animals (online or using reference books) and write about them. Illustrate them on paper and display them on the classroom walls.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen compare animal features example. Ask students to explain their choice using A ___ has ___, but a ___ has ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have read a short text, and researched and written about differences in animals. They have learned how to compare and contrast information using but. Differentiation To support less confident children, they find only one difference. When finding out about animals, pair them with a more confident child. As an extension task for more confident children, find out why there are differences, especially if they chose similar animals.
