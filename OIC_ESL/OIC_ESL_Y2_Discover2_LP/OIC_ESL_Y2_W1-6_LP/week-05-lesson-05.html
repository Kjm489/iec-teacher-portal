Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.4c, 2.4d, 2.4e

Learning goal: Students will describe mixing and changes. Language scaffold: We mix ___ with ___; then ___.. Materials: food mixture photographs, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Play a food game with children. Say a letter of the alphabet, and they say a food beginning with that letter. You can vary this by asking for a food beginning with the same letter as the first letter of their name. Put the words on the board. Ask children which of the foods are OK to eat every day, and which are not. Find out if they know why. Establish which foods are OK to eat every day and which are not.

Show food mixture photographs. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to describe mixing and changes?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Using the foods from the introductory activity, ask children which foods you can mix together. Use your flashcards or projected pictures to help pre-teach the words: ‘omelette’, ‘soup’, ‘smoothie’, ‘juice’, and ‘ice pops’.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: We mix ___ with ___; then ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses describe mixing and changes?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Tell children they are going to make a book about food mixtures. Write an example together with children on the board: e.g. A banana smoothie. A banana smoothie is a mixture. It’s a liquid. The bananas were solid and now they are liquid. They change. I like smoothies and I drink them for breakfast. Look at one example in Oxford Discover SB 2 p.45. Children are going to write individually, but put them together in small groups. Each group writes about the same mixture.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Ask children to plan their book together. Read out loud some questions for them to think about: What kind of book are you making: zigzag book, flap book, mini book, other? Are you writing on separate paper? Is the paper cut out in the form of a bowl/glass? How many lines are you writing? Where is the illustration? How will you make the illustrations show the change from solid to liquid? Who will check your work? Allow groups time to write, check, draw, and put their books together. Display them so that everyone can read each other’s books.

Pairs use food mixture photographs; one asks or explains and the other responds with We mix ___ with ___; then ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to describe mixing and changes using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Ask children to plan their book together. Read out loud some questions for them to think about: What kind of book are you making: zigzag book, flap book, mini book, other? Are you writing on separate paper? Is the paper cut out in the form of a bowl/glass? How many lines are you writing? Where is the illustration? How will you make the illustrations show the change from solid to liquid? Who will check your work? Allow groups time to write, check, draw, and put their books together. Display them so that everyone can read each other’s books.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “We mix ___ with ___; then ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.34–35.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen describe mixing and changes example. Ask students to explain their choice using We mix ___ with ___; then ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have participated in a group project, making a book about food mixtures. Differentiation To support less confident children, write the words and possible sentences on the board. Provide a gapped text, if possible. As an extension task for more confident children, they write out the precise recipe for the mixture, including the equipment needed and the ingredients.
