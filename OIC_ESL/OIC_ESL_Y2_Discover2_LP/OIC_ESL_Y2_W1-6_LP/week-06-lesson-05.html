Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: Reflection and review: Things that change

Learning goal: Students will review and demonstrate unit learning. Language scaffold: I learned ___; I can ___.. Materials: completed unit work and project cards, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: Retrieve vocabulary and skills from Things that change; students select two examples from the completed unit.

Show completed unit work and project cards. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to review and demonstrate unit learning?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Session 5 of five for the Things that change review project. Present the project, reflect on learning, and self-assess.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: I learned ___; I can ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses review and demonstrate unit learning?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Session 5 of five for the Things that change review project. Present the project, reflect on learning, and self-assess.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Session 5 of five for the Things that change review project. Present the project, reflect on learning, and self-assess.

Pairs use completed unit work and project cards; one asks or explains and the other responds with I learned ___; I can ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to review and demonstrate unit learning using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Session 5 of five for the Things that change review project. Present the project, reflect on learning, and self-assess.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “I learned ___; I can ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Independent reflection Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the unit ‘Things that change’. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection…

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen review and demonstrate unit learning example. Ask students to explain their choice using I learned ___; I can ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Collect project evidence and assess understanding of the unit.

SIX ACTIVITIES & GAMES — WEEK 6, LESSON 5

1. Memory Tray — Share, reflect and assess

Lesson-specific language focus: asking permission and sharing

Original teaching source: Show completed unit work and project cards. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to review and demonstrate unit learning?”

Materials: classroom object cards, please/thank-you dialogue strips and a puppet

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Memory Tray”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about asking permission and sharing.

YOU DO — game procedure: Display six source picture cards for 30 seconds; hide one and ask learners to name and describe the missing item.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Do we ask before taking someone else’s things?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about asking permission and sharing.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “May I borrow your ___? Yes, you may. / Sorry, I need it.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of asking permission and sharing.
