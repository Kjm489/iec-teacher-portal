Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.2b, 2.2e

Learning goal: Students will describe animals with evidence. Language scaffold: A ___ can ___, but it cannot ___.. Materials: animal picture cards, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: As a quick revision game of the week’s work, ask children to get into groups of four. You say two animals and the children have to come up with a sentence about similarities or differences. Start with easy ones (using examples you had earlier in the week), and then make it harder. You could stipulate if you want similarities or differences. Award points according to: firstly, vocabulary; secondly, grammatical accuracy; and thirdly, content. The team with the most points at the end wins.

Show animal picture cards. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to describe animals with evidence?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Ask children if they love animals. Most do! Tell them that they are going to talk about why they love animals and to think carefully about why. They can either choose an animal, a species, or a habitat (e.g. animals that live in caves), or other criteria. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.13. With books closed, children listen to the song (ex.D). They note down all the animals mentioned. Then play it again and ask them to say why the song is called ‘We Love Animals!’ (because they are all amazing in different ways). Elicit all the amazing things animals can do. Focus on the pronouns and review the grammar point quickly. Children should use these ideas when they talk about why they love animals.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: A ___ can ___, but it cannot ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses describe animals with evidence?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Children work in small groups to prepare a short talk called ‘We love animals’. As you walk around the classroom and monitor their progress, reinforce the importance of listening carefully to each other and making sure everybody is included. Ask: Why is this important? How does it make you feel? Remind children to use their traffic light ‘help’ buttons to indicate if they need help.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Children give their presentations to the class. At the end of the lesson, once everybody has presented, discuss mistakes with children, and help them to identify where they might do things differently next time.

Pairs use animal picture cards; one asks or explains and the other responds with A ___ can ___, but it cannot ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to describe animals with evidence using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Children give their presentations to the class. At the end of the lesson, once everybody has presented, discuss mistakes with children, and help them to identify where they might do things differently next time.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “A ___ can ___, but it cannot ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 pp.6–7. Write up the presentation and illustrate it. Then display it on the wall. Write up the presentation on a piece of paper and produce a class book.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen describe animals with evidence example. Ask students to explain their choice using A ___ can ___, but it cannot ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have reviewed the week’s work and have given a spoken presentation about why they love animals. They have demonstrated effective teamwork in small groups. Differentiation To support less confident children, provide them with some points to include. They could also talk about just one animal. As an extension task for more confident children, encourage them to think about why some people don’t like animals.

YEAR 2 Amazing animals
Week 1, Lesson 5: We love animals
Learning outcomes: 2.2b, 2.2e 

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Amazing animals’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by giving a short presentation.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
