LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 2

Measurable Learning Objectives

Source outcomes: 2.1d, 2.2c, 2.4b

Learning goal: Students will predict or explain change. Language scaffold: If ___, then ___.. Materials: before-and-after picture sequences, original Oxford Discover 2 Student Book exercises, mini-whiteboards.

Success: Students identify evidence, participate in an exchange and independently produce an accurate response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate prior knowledge: As an introduction to the next two weeks, ask children to tell you all the things they can think of that can change: e.g. the weather, food if you leave it out of the fridge, bubbles in a soda drink, hair, etc. Encourage children to make a KWL chart titled ‘Things that change’.

Show before-and-after picture sequences. Invite predictions before revealing answers. ICQs: “Do we predict before checking?” “Do both partners share?” CCQs: “Which image connects to predict or explain change?”

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explicit teacher model: Use the original lesson sequence: Ask children if they can hold water in their hands. Show them your hand holding the ice cube. Say: Look! I’m holding water! Elicit that it is water which has changed its form. Elicit the changes and how they happen (don’t focus on the verb tense – just the vocabulary for the moment). Pre-teach the words ‘flow’, ‘solid’, ‘liquid’, ‘gas’, ‘heat’, ‘steam’, ‘ice’, ‘freeze’, and ‘melt’ with flashcards or through any other form. You could project pictures of these onto the board.

Think aloud while demonstrating a full example: If ___, then ___.. Contrast a correct and incorrect example. CCQs: “What evidence supports the answer?” “Which part of the example expresses predict or explain change?”

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Scaffolded practice: Go through the words with children and elicit verbs, nouns, and adjectives. Play a game with the words. Tell children you are thinking of a word and they have to guess it by asking Is it …?. Ask children to give you an example of something that flows, something that is solid, and so on.

Model once, answer the second example together with a word bank, then hide part of the frame for a third example. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What proves this choice is correct?”

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Communicative pair task: Put children into groups. They write a list of things that flow, that are solid, etc. This can be done as a Think-Pair-Share. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.28. Children complete exs.A–B in pairs. As a wrap-up, tell children that it is important to know the difference between a fact and an opinion. This can often be signposted by ‘I think …’. Say different statements that are facts and opinions. Children stand still when you say a fact and hop on one leg when it’s an opinion: e.g. I think water is good for you. (opinion) Water is a liquid. (fact)

Pairs use before-and-after picture sequences; one asks or explains and the other responds with If ___, then ___.. Swap roles and fade sentence prompts. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you swap?” CCQ: “Does your response match the textbook evidence?”

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent application: Learners independently demonstrate how to predict or explain change using a new example, not the teacher’s model. For writing lessons, produce a short connected text; for speaking, deliver two meaningful sentences; for review sessions, complete the appropriate project stage.

Original lesson anchor: Put children into groups. They write a list of things that flow, that are solid, etc. This can be done as a Think-Pair-Share. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.28. Children complete exs.A–B in pairs. As a wrap-up, tell children that it is important to know the difference between a fact and an opinion. This can often be signposted by ‘I think …’. Say different statements that are facts and opinions. Children stand still when you say a fact and hop on one leg when it’s an opinion: e.g. I think water is good for you. (opinion) Water is a liquid. (fact)

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Emerging: Give picture choices, word bank, gestures and partial frame “If ___, then ___.”. Developing: Produce a full sentence with one accurate detail. Extending: Justify the answer, compare examples or ask a follow-up question. Original extension: Complete Oxford Discover WB 2 p.20. Assign each group a topic (e.g. food, music, growing things, or weather). They make a list of liquids, solids, and gases within that topic.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Show an unseen predict or explain change example. Ask students to explain their choice using If ___, then ___. without copying the board. Record independent / prompted / not yet. Original learning review: Children have learned that there are three different forms of matter and are able to identify them. They have understood the difference between fact and opinion. Differentiation To support less confident children, ask them to draw a picture to illustrate the words. As an extension task for more confident children, refer them to Oxford Discover SB 2 p.28, and ask them to write (correct) sentences with the incorrect words from ex.B.

Lesson-specific language focus: food and healthy choices

Materials: food picture cards and two labelled sorting mats

Teacher preparation: Use the exact vocabulary, pictures and examples from the original teaching source above. Prepare the cards or prompts required for “Venn Diagram Detectives”.

I DO — teacher models: Model one complete turn using a real example from the source. Say the target language aloud: “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” Point to the supporting picture or text.

WE DO — guided example: Complete a second example together. Ask students to identify the correct source clue, then elicit a full sentence about food and healthy choices.

YOU DO — game procedure: Partners place source examples into two overlapping categories and explain one similarity and one difference.

ICQ: Will you show your own answer? Do you need to use the lesson pictures or words?

CCQ: Is an apple a fruit?

Success evidence: Learner uses the lesson vocabulary accurately and completes the activity with one correct example about food and healthy choices.

Support: Show two visual choices; rehearse the frame “I like ___. It is ___ because ___.” before learners respond.

Challenge: Ask for a second example or an explanation using because, while remaining on the topic of food and healthy choices.
