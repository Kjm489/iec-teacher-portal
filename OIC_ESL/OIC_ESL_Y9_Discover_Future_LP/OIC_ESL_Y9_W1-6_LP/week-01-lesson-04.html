Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask students what they remember from the text ‘Where Words Fail, Music Speaks’ (see ODF SB 4 p.7) from the previous lesson. If necessary, use prompts to get the information flowing: What is the Golden Record? What is the origin of the word ‘music’? How do anthropologists explain our love of music? High-pitched and low-pitched sounds are associated with which emotions?
  • Write the words ‘music’, ‘science’, and ‘power’ on the board and see if students can think of any connections between them.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Divide the class into groups of four and give each group a card with one of the words ‘music’, ‘science’, ‘power’ on it. Tell students to work individually at first to write down as many words or phrases as they can which they associate with their group’s word.
  • In their groups, students compare their lists of words and nominate a group member to report back to the class their ten most common words.
  • Draw a word cloud on the board using the words from this lesson’s vocabulary list. Challenge each group to make as many sentences as they can, incorporating at least one of the words ‘music’, ‘science’, ‘power’ and any of the words in the cloud or from their own lists: e.g. Music has the power to make us feel more upbeat. / According to science, listening to slow, relaxing music 45 minutes before bedtime helps us to fall asleep.
  • Set a time limit for the activity. When the time is up, ask groups to nominate new group members to share their best sentences with the class.
  • Form new groups. Ask students to reflect on the questions:
  • Is there a connection between music and science? If so, what is it?
  • Is there a connection between music and power? If so, what is it?
  • Invite group members who haven’t spoken previously to summarize their group’s discussion, eventually feeding into a whole-class discussion on the topic.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.2d, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Encourage less confident but able students to report their group’s discussion back to the whole class, if they feel comfortable. Explain that doing something outside of their comfort zone isn’t always a bad thing; it will help them to grow in confidence and acquire an important real-world skill: public speaking and presenting.
  • Form the small groups in a way that you feel will benefit your students the most. You could mix up abilities in the first group activity, so more able students can support less able students to make sentences, whereas students might excel more in the reflection activity if they are grouped with students of similar ability. You can offer more support to less able groups during this discussion.
  • Ask students to individually consider the question: What is the science behind music? as a lead-in to the next lesson. They can research it online, using key words to narrow down search engine results.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have reflected on the relationship between music and science, and music and power. They have practised speaking on the topic in small groups, responding to challenging questions and taking turns to report their conclusions back to the whole class.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
