1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Show students pictures of three (local) charities: a food bank, a helpline, an upcycling scheme. You may wish to show them a few logos or advertisements as prompts, or a video clip of a TV advert.
  • Ask students to discuss in small groups what each charity offers. Invite groups to share their ideas with the class.
  • Tell groups to imagine that they have participated in a ‘fundraising’ activity and are deciding on one of the three charities. Ask them to discuss their preferences, outlining the reasons behind their decisions, and come to an agreement on which charity to donate to.
  • Invite a volunteer from each group to share their decision with the class. Remind them to give reasons for their decision, and explain if there has been any disagreement and why.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Turn to ODF WB 4 p.19 ex.2. Students are going to listen to three people making the same decision about whether to donate to Helpline, Upcycling Network, or Food for Free. Ask: How did they raise the money? (student snack bar) Which charity do they choose? (undecided) Play the audio once and discuss the answers.

  • Divide the class into two halves: A and B. Ask the As to make notes under ‘For’ and the Bs to make notes under ‘Against’. Play the audio again.
  • In A–B pairs, students exchange notes and complete their tables. Check answers as a class.
  • Ask students if they can remember how the speakers: 1. express an opinion, 2. politely disagree. If necessary, play the audio again and pause after each expression: I like the look of …; I hear what you’re saying, but …; I understand where you’re coming from, but …; That’s a good point, but …; I see what you mean, but in my opinion …
  • Ask: Can you think of other expressions to give an opinion and agree/disagree? (e.g. As far as I’m concerned, …; Do you see what I’m getting at?; I’d go along with that; That’s not the way I see it; I kind of agree with you).
  • In pairs, ask students to choose one of the situations in ODF WB 4 p.19 ex.7 and role-play a conversation in which they express opinions, agreement, and disagreement using the new vocabulary. If time allows, invite pairs to perform in front of the class.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1e, 9.1g, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Provide a listening framework for less able students to support them in the listening task.
  • Put more able students together for the final activity to stretch their thinking.
  • Students can write a script of their role-play, using a variety of new vocabulary learned.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have listened for pros and cons in a conversation about donating money. They have learned different expressions to agree/disagree, and reused them in a role-play.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
