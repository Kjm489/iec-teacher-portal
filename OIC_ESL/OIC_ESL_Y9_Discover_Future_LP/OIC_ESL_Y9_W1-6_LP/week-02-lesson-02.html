1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Draw a timeline on the board with ‘1925’ at one end and ‘2021’ at the other. Elicit from students different music players invented between these dates. Write them in a random order on the board, adding any remaining from the list below.
  • Show students pictures of different music players and challenge students to match them to the correct names. Check their answers without giving away too much information about their origins. Then ask students to try and arrange the pictures on the timeline, from oldest to most recent (answers: 1925 electric record player, 1948 LP ‘long play’ record, 1954 transistor radio, 1963 compact cassette, 1979 Walkman [portable cassette player], 1984 Discman [portable CD player], 1997 MP3 player, 2001 iPod, 2007 iPod Touch, 2007 iPhone).
  • Invite students to consider future ‘trends’ or behaviours which could change the industry.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Divide students into groups of four. Tell them to answer the following questions:
  • Which music player became popular in the 80s and what impact did this have on the sale of other music players? (CD – sales decreased/declined/dropped/fell)
  • What features did the CD have compared to the cassette? (light, more durable, cassette tape gets tangled and breaks easily)
  • Which of the two would be easier to manufacture? (CD – single piece)
  • What would be the benefits for the manufacturers of CDs rather than cassettes? (make good profits – easier and cheaper to produce so can sell more)
  • Why was an iPod more popular than CDs? (smaller, more portable, can choose and download music, can access a wider variety of music)
  • When the iPod came on the market, what happened to sales? (increased/went up/rose/jumped/rocketed)
  • What has happened to streaming services over the last ten years? (increased and improved)
  • How has the arrival of the smartphone affected the purchase of iPods? (fallen/dropped/declined)
  • Allow groups time to discuss their answers.
  • Draw five columns on the board, respectively labelled: ‘rise’, ‘fall’, ‘stay the same’, ‘change frequently’, ‘peak’. Draw arrows to illustrate each trend.
  • Check the answers to the questions as a whole class. Inform students that the answers to questions 1, 6, 7, 8 can be described more precisely. Randomly write the following verbs on the board and see if students can place them in the correct columns: ‘go up’, ‘plummet’, ‘plunge’, ‘fluctuate’, ‘reach a peak’, ‘level out’, ‘soar’, ‘go down’, ‘stabilize’.
  • In pairs, ask students to think of two or three sentences that describe changing trends in music players using appropriate verbs: e.g. In the 1970s the sale of LP records plummeted when Walkmans became available. Invite students to share their sentences with the class.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.2b, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Groups can include a mix of more able and less able students to provide/receive necessary support. Alternatively, group less able students together to allow them to work at their own speed. In case of the latter, ensure that they are monitored and helped.
  • Complete exs.1–2 in ODF SB 4 p.8.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have produced a timeline of music players to show changing trends. They have learned new verbs to describe changing trends across the decades.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
