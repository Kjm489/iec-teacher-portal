1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask students to close their eyes and silently reflect on their favourite song, thinking about the sound, the speed, the effect it has on them, when they listen to it the most, and why they like it. Allow sufficient time for this exercise (2–3 minutes).
  • Students describe their songs in pairs, focusing on the abovementioned points – you can write them on the board for students to refer to. Encourage students to identify any common interests or similarities between their and their partner’s song choices.
  • Invite a few students to share their songs with the class. Note down any useful vocabulary under the headings: ‘Sound’, ‘Speed’, and ‘Effect’.
  • Encourage students to add more words to the categories (e.g. Sound: loud, soft, smooth, staccato, textured, clear, rich, melodious, dark, flat, gentle, upbeat; Speed: fast, brisk, energetic, lively, frantic, slow; Effect: calm, relax, soothe, stimulate, frighten, stir, uplift, excite, heal).
  • Introduce the words ‘timbre’ (ˈtæmbə(r) – the quality of sound) and ‘tempo’ (the speed of a song).

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students that they are going to write a review of their favourite song. Establish that the purpose of the review is to promote a song and persuade readers to listen to it.
  • Divide students into groups of three and ask them to discuss what information they should include in the review.
  • Invite students to share their ideas with the class (e.g. title of song and name of artist; why you like the song; how you personally relate to the song; your favourite part; how it makes you feel; what you would change; observations – lyrics, mood, vocals, style, speed/sound quality; facts about the song’s success and how it was made; background information on the artist).
  • In preparation for writing their review, students participate in a free-writing exercise. Tell them to write down all their thoughts and feelings about their chosen song. They don’t need to worry about spelling or grammar, but write freely and honestly. Allow at least 15 minutes for this activity.
  • In newly formed pairs, allow students to discuss their chosen songs in more depth.
  • Explain to students that in the next lesson they will use their free-writing notes to write a more structured review.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4a, 9.4b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • For less able students, prepare a list of questions to guide them through the free-writing activity.
  • Pair more able students together in the final speaking activity to inspire ideas and allow them to practise using new language to describe music.
  • Students can research the artist/band/composer/producer of their favourite song, and consider if there is a deeper meaning in the lyrics or sound of the song.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have reflected on and described their favourite song to each other, and have learned new vocabulary to describe timbre, tempo, and the effect of music. In preparation for writing a review, they have participated in a free-writing activity to uncover their thoughts and feelings related to their chosen song.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
