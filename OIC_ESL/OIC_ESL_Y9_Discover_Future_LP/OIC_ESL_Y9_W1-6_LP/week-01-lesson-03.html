1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Dictate the words in the vocabulary list to students, then write them on the board. Tell them all the words are in the text they are going to read – can they predict what the text is about based on these words?
  • Encourage speculation. Ask: What makes you think that?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Hand out copies of the pre-prepared text (see ODF SB 4 p.7 for original), with the six paragraphs rearranged in a random order and this lesson’s key vocabulary omitted.
  • Set a time for students to work out where the words from the vocabulary list fit in the text. Tell them to work individually to begin with. Explain that this is a challenging activity, given the difficult vocabulary and the length of the text. It aims to stretch students and teach them how to use context and language to make logical guesses.
  • When the time is up, students check their answers in small groups before reviewing them as a whole class. Ask: What strategies did you use to work out where the words best fit? (context, verb endings, capitalization, plural nouns, etc.) Discuss these strategies as a class, highlighting specific examples: e.g. Some [anthropologists] think that … – it is a plural noun, indicated by the ‘s’, so it has to correspond with the ‘they’ form of the present tense (‘think’).
  • In the same groups, ask students to rearrange the text in the correct order and check their version with another group. Go through the correct order as a class.
  • Ask each group to prepare five questions on the text for another group to answer.
  • Pair groups up and allow them to ask and answer their questions, making sure that each group member has the opportunity to ask or answer at least one question. Remind students to scan the text to find the answers to questions related to specific facts, whereas questions eliciting an opinion will require a more critical and thoughtful response.
  • Bring the class back together and direct students to the three questions at the end of paragraph F. Engage in a whole-class discussion about the following:
  • What will the Golden Record tell intelligent life forms about our civilization?
  • Will they understand our message?
  • If words fail, will music speak?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3a, 9.3b, 9.3c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • The gap-fill activity allows students to progress at their own pace. You can choose simpler and fewer vocabulary items for less able students.
  • Ask more able students to define the key vocabulary in their own words for the benefit of less able students.
  • The group work allows every student to make a contribution, with more able students supporting less able students with explanations and question prompts.
  • Complete exs.1–3 in ODF WB 4 p.6.
  • Ask students to read the text again and highlight all the words related to emotions.
  • Students can consider what they would put on a Golden Record that reflects life in 2021.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have used their knowledge of text structure, and their problem-solving and reading comprehension skills, to complete a gap-fill exercise and a jigsaw reading of a text. They have worked together in groups, and practised asking and answering questions.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
