Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Explain to students that they are going to explore the school community through sound.
  • Ask them to close their eyes and listen to a natural environment soundscape (e.g. rainforest walk soundscape). Play the audio clip and stop it before students open their eyes.
  • Ask students to describe what they heard: Where do you think the soundscape was recorded? How did it make you feel?
  • Ask students to discuss in pairs: What sounds do you associate with school? Do you associate these sounds with specific places/times? How do the sounds make you feel?
  • Invite students to share their ideas. Discuss how sounds can define a place – for example, can they imagine school without these sounds?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Divide students into groups of three and hand out a recording device to each – this can be a dictaphone (voice recorder) or a tablet. Their creative task is to create a two-minute recording of the sounds in a specific part of school (e.g. classroom, playground, sports hall, art room, music room, nursery class, cafeteria, general office, computer room, school corridor, locker room).
  • Ask students to listen to their recordings and transcribe the sounds into ‘word maps’ on flipchart paper. Note: They must not write the name of the place. Assign each group a letter to write on their flipchart paper, then tell them to leave it on their desk.
  • Leave all recordings in a separate part of the room (e.g. on the teacher’s desk).
  • Tell students that they are now going to write a poem about the place in school they visited. They can use one of the following poetry formats, or make up their own:
  • Encourage students to display their poems around the room. See if they can match the poems to the recordings and flipchart word maps. Allow time for this to take place. Group members shouldn’t reveal the answers for their group’s recording/map/poems.
  • As a class, check the results of the activity to see if groups have identified the recordings correctly. Ask: How does it feel to think of your school in this way? What did you learn about your school you didn’t know before?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.2b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pair up less confident students with more confident students during the creative tasks. The first task is an opportunity for less able students to lead a group in their ESL lesson, since its focus is on sound and not on language production. Their strengths might lie in technology.
  • Students can recreate their own soundscapes of the place they visited by using their voice, bodies, and objects around them.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have explored how sound forms an important part of their school’s identity, giving it a ‘sense of place’. They have worked on two creative projects: creating a soundscape and writing a short poem.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
