1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • See if students can list the most charitable musicians in the world today – those who donate a lot of money to charities, foundations, and relief efforts. Elicit artists like Taylor Swift, Rihanna, Elton John, Alicia Keys, Drake, Beyoncé, Ariana Grande.
  • Then show the class a photo of Chance the Rapper and ask if they know who the artist is. Tell students they are going to listen to a nonfiction text about this artist – what information and key words do they expect to hear based on their existing knowledge?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Hand out True/False worksheets to pairs of students, with the following statements:
  • 1. Chance the Rapper is a rap artist from Boston, USA. F
  • 2. Chance has supported various charitable organizations. T
  • 3. Chance started recording rap after he left school. F
  • 4. His first mix tape was called Coloring Book. F
  • 5. Chance won three Grammy awards for Coloring Book. T
  • 6. He was involved in an anti-violence campaign. T
  • 7. He donated a lot of money to a science programme. F
  • 8. He has promised to help people all over the world. T
  • Turn to ODF SB 4 p.12 while students keep their books closed. Read the text on Chance the Rapper out loud. Ask students to check their T/F statements as they listen. They tick the true statements and correct the false statements. Check their answers as a class.
  • Read the text out loud again and ask students to identify how many paragraphs are in the text, and the main idea of each paragraph. In pairs, students compare their answers. Invite pairs to volunteer their ideas to the rest of the class.
  • Write the following paragraph headings on the board: ‘Para 1: Introduces the artist’; ‘Para 2: Gives background information’; ‘Para 3: Explains the artist’s contribution to society’; ‘Para 4: Summarizes why the writer chose the artist’.
  • Put students into groups of four. Give each group six ‘sequence connector’ cards, labelled ‘while’, ‘following this’, ‘at first’, ‘in the end’, ‘next’, ‘recently’. Ask students to arrange them in the correct order, then try to recall how they were used in the text.
  • Read the text again. In the same groups, each group member takes notes on one of the four paragraphs. Tell them to note down key words only.
  • Ask groups to try to reconstruct the whole text based on their notes. At the end of the lesson, groups read their versions to the class, followed by a brief feedback session. Praise students for working well in teams over the course of the lesson. Ask: What worked well? How could you improve your teamwork next time?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1d, 9.4c, 9.4d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Form groups of more able and less able students so that they can support each other.
  • Adjust the pace of your reading in line with your students’ abilities.
  • Students can plan an essay on another ‘role model’ artist who has contributed to society and give a short oral presentation based on their notes.
  • Complete exs.1–4 in ODF WB 4 p.10.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have made predictions about a charitable musician. They have listened to a text for specific information and have identified how a text is organized. They have practised using sequence connectors and worked collaboratively to reconstruct the text in writing.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
