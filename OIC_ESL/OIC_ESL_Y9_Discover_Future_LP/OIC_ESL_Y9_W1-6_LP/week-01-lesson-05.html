1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Before showing students the reading text, write the title ‘What Science Tells Us about the Power of Music’ on the board. Invite the class to predict what the text is about and what kind of information it will include. Ask: Why do you think that? Will it be a fiction or non fiction text? Why?
  • After students have shared some possible ideas, dictate and write on the board this week’s key vocabulary. Elicit the meaning of the words and expressions, if possible (e.g. Which emotions are typically connected to the colour blue? So what do you think ‘have the blues’ means?), and explain any unfamiliar ones.
  • Ask: Do these words help you get a clearer idea of what might be in the text?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Divide the class into groups of five. Give each group member one of the cut-up paragraphs from the text, and place a ‘question box’ containing five to ten pre-prepared reading comprehension questions in the middle of each group’s table. Explain that the task has two parts:
  • For part 1, students read their paragraph independently, then summarize it in their own words to their group. After, they work as a group to decide on the correct order of paragraphs. Make clear that they shouldn’t read their paragraph out loud to the others in the group, nor read anybody else’s paragraph – the activity is based on students’ ability to clearly communicate the information in their paragraph.
  • For part 2, students take a question from the question box. Depending on the paragraph to which the question refers, group members take turns to answer the questions, referring to the text for information.
  • When the groups have completed both tasks, bring the whole class back together and check their answers.
  • Ask each group to reflect on how they approached the task. Ask: What did you do well as a team? What didn’t quite work? Encourage students to identify what they might do differently next time, during a similar team-building activity.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3a, 9.3c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • The introductory activity allows all students to express an opinion, regardless of their language ability. Support less able students with simple question prompts.
  • Less able students can work in pairs (with another less able student or a more able student) during the summary exercise.
  • Write the questions to match students’ general ability, or include questions of varying difficulty levels.
  • Students read through the whole text in ODF WB 4 p.5 and complete exs.1–5 on p.4.
  • Students can prepare a short oral or written summary of the main points of the reading text, or of a time when music has helped them.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have reviewed the structure of a nonfiction text and worked in groups to logically reconstruct its cut-up sections. In turn, they have read and orally summarized a paragraph to their peers, and consolidated their understanding of the topic by asking and answering each other reading comprehension questions. They have reflected on their strengths and weaknesses in a team.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
