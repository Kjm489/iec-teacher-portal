1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Introduce the topic of money by asking: What is the currency in your country? Can you think of other examples of currencies from around the world?
  • Hand out a currencies worksheet to students. Give them a few minutes to match the currencies to their country of origin.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • To establish that money has not always been a form of payment, give students a list of unusual forms of payment (salt, squirrel pelts, dolphin teeth, arrowheads, cowry shells; and some distractors, such as pebbles, sand, leaves, rope). Ask them to decide which items are, or have been, means of payment.
  • Turn to ODF SB 4 p.15. Play the Factflix video and tell students to note down all the types of currency mentioned and any other information they can gather. Round up what information students have gathered.
  • Play the video a second time. Ask students to share their answers, first in pairs and then in small groups.
  • Elicit the full list of forms of payment and ask students to work together, in their pairs, to order them chronologically. Review the answers as a class.
  • Play the video a third time. Now ask students to listen specifically for how the ‘barter system’ works, and what is replacing notes and bills as forms of payment.
  • Use the answers to draw students’ attention to the last line of the video: ‘money itself is here to stay’. Discuss as a class: What is the future of money? Reflect on how technological advances may have an impact on how we pay for things.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1d, 9.1e, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pair and group work allows more able students to help less able students by sharing information after each listening phase. You can provide a simple gap-fill exercise for less able students to facilitate their listening.
  • The open discussion allows all students to participate.
  • Complete exs.1–3 in ODF WB 4 p.16.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have extracted specific information from a video about currency and how it has evolved over the years. They have worked collaboratively by sharing information and ideas, and discussing the future of money.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
