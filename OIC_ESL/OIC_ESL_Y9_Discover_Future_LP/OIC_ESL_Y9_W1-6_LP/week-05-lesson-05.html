Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Play the class a video clip of other students participating in a community service project, or display quotations around the classroom: e.g. ‘One of the greatest gifts you can give is your time’, ‘Volunteering is work of heart’, ‘I always wondered why somebody doesn’t do something about that, then I realized that I was somebody’.
  • Invite students to comment on the video clip/quotations – consider again the question of whether community service should be an important part of learning in secondary school.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Remind students that in today’s lesson they are going to write a first draft of their opinion essay, using the outlines they made in the previous lesson. Allow students enough time to complete their first draft (approximately 20 minutes).
  • Before they start writing, explain that extended writing is difficult and can feel intimidating. Advise them to focus their attention by taking a few deep breaths.
  • In pairs, students exchange their first drafts with each other. Ask them to edit their partner’s draft using the writing checklist provided in ODF WB 4 p.21. They shouldn’t correct spelling and grammar mistakes, but underline them and indicate the error with a code.
  • Suggested code: Sp = spelling, T = tense, WO = word order is wrong, ᴧ = missing word, prep = preposition, WW = wrong word
  • When pairs have finished peer-editing, tell them to discuss their mistakes and comment on each other’s arguments. Encourage them to give positive feedback, as well as suggestions for improvement. Remind students of the value of feedback – it will help them to identify how to improve their work, so they can continue excelling at learning English.
  • If time allows, students can write their final draft based on their partner’s feedback.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4d, 9.4e, 9.4f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • For less able students, consider providing a writing framework to guide them through the paragraphs.
  • Encourage more able students to peer-edit the work of less able students, giving clear explanations for their corrections. This will help to reinforce their own understanding of language.
  • Students can read the opinion essay in ODF WB 4 p.20 and complete exs.1–4.
  • Students can write another essay using one of the ideas from ODF WB 4 p.21 or ODF SB 4 p.22.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have written the first draft of their opinion essay, and have peer-edited their partner’s essay using a correction code and writing checklist. They have provided constructive feedback tactfully, suggesting areas for development, then written a final draft based on the feedback received.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
