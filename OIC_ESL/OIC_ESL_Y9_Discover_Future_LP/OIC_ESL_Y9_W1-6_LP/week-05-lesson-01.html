Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Review the purpose and structure of a decision map (see Week 4, Lesson 5). Ask: Can you remember the steps for creating a decision map? Write these steps on the board in the correct order.
  • Pre-teach and drill some useful topic vocabulary in preparation for today’s listening lesson.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Refer students to the leaflets supporting four good causes in ODF SB 4 p.21. Ask them to classify the charities according to their area (helping criminals to reform, health, social enterprise, environment).
  • They are now going to listen to two people discussing which good cause to support. Ask them to complete the grid in ODF SB 4 p.21 as they listen for the advantages and disadvantages of each.
  • Play the audio twice, allowing students to compare their answers in pairs after each play.
  • As a class, round up the answers and check that all the information has been gathered. Encourage students to evaluate the arguments each speaker makes – do they agree or disagree?
  • Put students in groups, then refer to the steps for creating a decision map on the board.
  • Set students the task of making a decision map based on the information they have gathered from the listening. Give them the choice of making either a simple map using one or two of the charities, or a more complex one using three or all four of the charities.
  • Tell students to test their decision maps. Ask: Does the decision map help you to make responsible and ethical choices?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1d, 9.1e, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • The creative aspect of the lesson will challenge all students regardless of linguistic level.
  • The decision map activity allows students to opt for a simple map or a more complex one, depending on their level of confidence and their linguistic ability.
  • Students can design a leaflet in support of the charity of their choice, outlining the reasons why people should donate to this particular charity.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have listened to a discussion on four charitable causes, gathering information about the advantages and disadvantages of supporting each one. They have practised speaking during a creative task (designing a decision map) by discussing options, negotiating, and making difficult decisions as team members.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
