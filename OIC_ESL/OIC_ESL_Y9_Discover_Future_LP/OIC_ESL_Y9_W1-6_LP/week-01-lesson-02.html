Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students that in the previous lesson you played them a piece of music that is important to you. Can they remember your reasons for selecting this track?
  • Invite one or two students to talk about the tracks that are important to them, and what emotions they evoke. Ask: Is there a reason behind your emotional connection to this song/piece of music?
  • Allow students some time to reflect on three to five songs/pieces of music that are important to them and why. Ask them to note down their choices, explaining the reasons behind their decisions.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • In pairs, students take turns to explain their choices and why these particular songs/pieces of music are important to them (e.g. the song reminds them of a particular event or memorable holiday, it cheered them up when they were feeling sad, it’s their parents’ favourite song, etc.).
  • Bring the class back together and explain the main activity – students are going to role-play an interview with their partner on the songs/pieces of music that are important to them. It can be in the form of a radio, TV, or YouTube interview for broadcasting.
  • On the board, work with students to list the type of questions an interviewer might ask to elicit interesting answers from the interviewee, and the follow-up questions they could ask after the initial response. For example:
  • How does this song make you feel? Do you always feel this way when you listen to the song, or does it depend on your mood?
  • It’s an upbeat dance track. Why did you add it to your ‘important song’ list?
  • How often do you listen to music/these songs in particular? When do you listen to music/these songs in particular?
  • Can you tell me more about (this memory)?
  • In their pairs, students draft an interview script with their preferred questions. Remind them that the interview will also need an introduction (welcome, names) and a closure (summary of events, thanks).
  • Students take turns to play the role of the interviewer and interviewee. Change pairs as often as time allows so that students get to speak to as many of their classmates as possible.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2d, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • All students can participate in choosing their favourite songs, regardless of ability. The open speaking activity with no ‘right’ answer invites students to produce language in a free way, which will encourage them to participate to the best of their ability. Try not to intervene too much in their role-plays, unless less able students require a few question prompts – you can recap common mistakes at the end of the lesson.
  • Students can make an audio or video recording of the interview when they are satisfied with the form and content.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have explored their own and others’ reasons for selecting specific songs/pieces of music that are important to them. They have practised their speaking and improvisational skills in a role-play, asking a variety of questions to elicit and clarify their partner’s responses.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
