1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students that they are going to write a review of their favourite song.
  • Establish that the purpose of a review is to express an opinion about something in a way that will persuade the reader to act (e.g. buy a record) or adopt the same opinion.
  • Direct the class to the ‘writing strategy’ notes in ODF SB 4 p.12 about writing persuasively. Tell them to reread the text on Chance the Rapper and complete ex.3.
  • As a class, discuss the effect of rhetorical questions and evaluative adverbs.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • In pairs, students discuss how they can organize their ideas from the free-writing activity into a structured review. Consider the number of paragraphs and the main idea of each.
  • Bring the class back together and share ideas. For example:
  • Para 1: Introduction – general overview, song title and artist, one interesting fact about the song’s success, how it was made;
  • Para 2: Body – why you like the song, some detailed observations about the lyrics, vocals, instruments, timbre, tempo, and production;
  • Para 3: Body – if there is a deeper message behind the song, how it makes you feel, your favourite part, a part you would like to change (if any);
  • Para 4: Conclusion – a summary of why the song is important.
  • Tell students to organize their ideas into paragraphs. Remind them to make use of evaluative adverbs (e.g. impressively, clearly, incredibly) and rhetorical questions (e.g. How many artists can say that?).
  • Allow enough time for students to write a first draft of their review. Before they start writing, explain that extended writing is difficult and can feel intimidating. Advise them to focus their attention by taking a few deep breaths.
  • In pairs, students peer-edit each other’s reviews. They shouldn’t correct spelling and grammar mistakes, but underline them and indicate the error with a code.
  • Suggested code: Sp = spelling, T = tense, WO = word order is wrong, ᴧ = missing word, prep = preposition, WW = wrong word
  • Allow time for students to peer-edit and return the drafts to the author.
  • Authors study the corrections and ask for clarification if required. Remind students that everyone makes mistakes and that it is good to share and learn from them.
  • If time allows, students can write their final draft.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4d, 9.4e, 9.4f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Prepare a writing frame to guide less able students through the writing process. It can include various sentences starters: ‘The mood of the music is … ’, ‘The singer’s voice is … ’, ‘What I like about the song is … ’.
  • More able students can work together during the peer-editing stage.
  • Display the final drafts around the classroom. Provide sticky notes for students to provide constructive feedback – one thing that they like about the essay and one thing that the author could improve.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have learned how to use evaluative adverbs and rhetorical questions in persuasive writing. They have discussed how to organize a review and prepared an outline, before writing their first draft and participating in a peer-editing activity.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
