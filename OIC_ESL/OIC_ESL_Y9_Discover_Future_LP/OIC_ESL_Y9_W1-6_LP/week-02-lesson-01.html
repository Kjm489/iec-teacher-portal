Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask students if they make connections between colours and specific emotions (e.g. red and anger). Elicit a few examples and establish if students make the same or different connections – a difference in opinion can often lead to an interesting discussion, so encourage students to explain their reasoning behind their choice of colours.
  • Tell students you are going to play some of your favourite songs or pieces of music (between three and five). Play approximately 15 seconds of each and write down their names/titles.
  • As you play each piece, encourage students to close their eyes and focus on the music, blocking out any background noise. How does each piece make them feel?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Divide the class into small groups and give each group some sticky notes and marker pens. You are going to play the same music again. When students listen to each piece, they should work individually and write on separate sticky notes:
  • the colour it makes them think of,
  • the emotion(s) it evokes,
  • the time of day it reminds them of.
  • After the second listening, tell students to write down which piece they enjoyed the most on another sticky note.
  • Hand out a sheet of flipchart paper to each group. Each sheet should be divided into a grid with the titles of each piece of music down the left-hand side, and four columns (labelled: ‘Colour’, ‘Emotion’, ‘Time of day’, ‘Favourite piece’). Students can appoint a group leader to create the chart, ensure everybody’s involvement, and lead their peers through the three stages of the activity (see below point).
  • Ask students to add their sticky notes to the appropriate category on the chart, discuss the results in their groups, and work together to summarize them. They nominate one group member to present their group’s findings to the rest of the class.
  • Focus on the ‘time of day’ column and ask for volunteers to explain why they think a particular piece of music corresponds to a particular time of day.
  • Remind students of circadian rhythms (see Week 1, Lesson 1 when they were first introduced to this concept). Discuss as a class: Do you think your response to the music I have played is related to your own circadian rhythm? How and why does music affect an individual’s circadian rhythm? Encourage students to refer to their own experiences when sharing ideas.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Group work encourages students of varying abilities to collaborate and give/receive support. You can appoint more able students to lead the research task and ensure that the data has been correctly compiled in the chart.
  • Students can work individually to design their own ‘circadian rhythm profile’, choosing the songs or pieces of music that they would listen to at different times of the day and explaining why. Let students present and design their profile in their preferred way.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have reflected on the colours, emotions, and times of day that music can evoke. In groups, they have compiled and analysed their group’s data and presented their findings orally. They have looked again at circadian rhythms and considered the effect of music on their body clock.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
