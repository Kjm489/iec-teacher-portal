1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students of the article about Jacob from the previous lesson. Then write the following sentences on the board for students to complete:
  • Next year, I … (got – future perfect) my nursing degree and I … (make – future continuous) a difference in the lives of many patients like Mum. By then, my high school friends … (work – future perfect continuous) in their careers for years.
  • Tell students that the grammar activity is meant to stretch them and extend their learning – it is not easy to study three different future tenses with three specific uses.
  • Invite students to share their answers as a class, then discuss the use and meaning of the three tenses. You can refer to ODF SB 4 p.19 ex.6 and the final paragraph of the text on p.18, where students were first introduced to these tenses.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell the class that you are going to ask a number of questions. Students must respond by writing predictions about themselves, based on their life goals. Encourage them to write detailed responses, considering all aspects of their life (e.g. home, money, qualifications, job, plans).
  • Ask: Where will you be living in 20 years? What will you be doing by the time you’re 30? When you turn 18, what will you have done already? Then tell students to put their answers to one side.
  • Put students into pairs. Tell them that this time they must write predictions about their partner, based on what they already know about their classmate’s strengths and interests.
  • Students read each other’s predictions and compare them to the ones they made about themselves. Have a short class discussion about the results.
  • Elicit from students what aspects of the working and financial world they think will change in the year 2050 (e.g. workplace/working hours, local communities, job types, money, commuting/travel, communication, technology, shopping).
  • In small groups, ask students to list as many questions as they can about the working and financial world in 2050 using future tense forms (e.g. By the year 2050, will people still be working a 9–5 hour day? Will they have swapped working in an office for working at home? Will bankers be travelling to work in self-driving cars? Will we have been paying for items using a microchip in our arms for almost ten years?).
  • Tell groups to exchange their questions with another group. Invite them to discuss the questions altogether, focusing on whether these changes will be positive or negative.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2b, 9.2e, 9.4d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Less able students will need further support with future tense work. See ODF SB 4 p.115.
  • For the prediction activity in pairs, pair less able students together so you can monitor them closely and guide them through the different tenses. You can mix more able students with less able students for the final group activity.
  • Complete exs.8–10 in ODF WB 4 pp.17–18.
  • Students can write their predictions for 2050 based on their questions, then display them around the classroom for other students to read.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have learned about and practised using the future perfect, future continuous, and future perfect continuous tenses. They have made predictions about themselves and their partner, and asked questions about the working and financial world in 2050.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
