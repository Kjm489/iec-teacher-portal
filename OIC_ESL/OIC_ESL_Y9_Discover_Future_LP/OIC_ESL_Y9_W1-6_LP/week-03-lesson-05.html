1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask students to close their eyes and listen to a song that you are going to play.
  • Play the performance version of ‘A Great Big World’ by Rockstar, then ask students to discuss it in pairs, focusing on the mood of the song and the images that it evokes.
  • Invite a few students to volunteer their thoughts to the class. Then ask them to think of a song title and explain why it is appropriate.
  • [Note: Teachers can carry out the same lesson with a song of their choice. Look for songs with a strong storyline/message, a melody that doesn’t drown out the lyrics, and one that is age-appropriate.]

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • The aim of this lesson is to focus on listening skills and the meaning of lyrics. You can choose one or more of the following activity ideas:
  • Create a gap-fill worksheet of song lyrics with specific words omitted. Students first try to predict the missing words from the context, then listen to the audio again and try to fill in the gaps.
  • Create a worksheet with the lines/verses of a song rearranged. Students listen to the audio again and try to put them in the correct order.
  • Students listen to one verse and write down as many words as they can. Then ask them to try to write the whole verse using the words they have written, their memory of the song, and logic skills.
  • Students complete one or more of the listening activities, then review their answers in pairs and as a whole class.
  • Now that students have the song lyrics (from the worksheets), ask them to discuss the meaning of the song in small groups. Focus on the line ‘Take a ticket and get off the line’.
  • Invite groups to share their interpretations of the song.
  • Ask students to stand up. Play the karaoke version of the song and sing it as a whole class, encouraging students to loosen up and move their bodies to the music. As a variation, you could ask the boys to sing the boys’ part and the girls to sing the girls’ part, or swap roles. Encourage all students to participate and not worry about whether they have good singing voices or not. Highlight that the activity will help to improve their pronunciation in English!

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.2b, 9.2f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • The first two listening activity options (gap-fill and reordering lyrics) will be the most accessible to less able students. You can choose to omit easier words for these students.
  • The third listening activity option will challenge more able students.
  • Students can record their own version of the song with the instrumental version as a backing track.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have developed their listening and problem-solving skills by listening to a popular song and completing a range of activities. They have practised singing the song to improve their general pronunciation. They have discussed the possible meaning of the song by studying its sound and lyrics.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
