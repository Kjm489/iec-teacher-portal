1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • See if students can recall the previous lesson – they looked at changing trends in the way we listen to music. Divide the board into two halves and the class into two teams. Instruct team members to go to the board, in turn, and write a verb for describing movement (e.g. rise, soar, rocket, fall, drop, plummet). The team with the most correct verbs after two minutes wins!
  • To consolidate students’ understanding, review the difference between ‘rise’ and ‘soar’ and ‘fall’ and ‘plummet’ – ‘rise’ and ‘fall’ are steady changes over time, whereas ‘soar’ and ‘plummet’ indicate sudden, dramatic changes.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Explain how adverbs can modify verbs by explaining how a change happened:
  • Sales rose dramatically/substantially/considerably.
  • Sales dropped steadily/gradually.
  • Allow enough time for pre-teaching the use of adverbs in this context.
  • Then explain to students that an ‘adjective + noun’ construction can be used to express the same information:
  • There was a dramatic (A) rise (N) in CD sales in the 1980s.
  • In pairs, students try to replace some examples of ‘verb + adverb’ constructions with ‘adjective + noun’ constructions (e.g. a considerable rise; a slight drop; a steady rise). Invite a few pairs to share their examples.
  • Write ‘hip-hop, rock, and jazz’ on the board. Ask: Do you know anything about the popularity of these three genres of music?
  • Divide the class into pairs and allocate them a letter A, B, or C.
  • Give each pair a graph (see ODF WB 4 p.7) showing the changing popularity of different music genres. Pair A = hip-hop, pair B = rock, pair C = jazz. Ask them to write a description of their graph using the new language learned.
  • Allow time for students to write their descriptions. Then bring pairs A, B, and C together to form groups of six students. Tell them to put their graphs on the table but keep their descriptions apart. Ask each pair to read out their description. The other group members try to identify which graph it corresponds to.
  • Students turn to ODF WB 4 p.7 and compare their descriptions.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.2e, 9.4d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pair up less able students in the writing activity. Provide writing frames to help them complete the graph descriptions.
  • Ask more able students to write several graph descriptions using a range of ‘verb + adverb’ and ‘adjective + noun’ constructions.
  • Complete ex.6 on p.7 and ex.7 on p.8 in ODF WB 4.
  • In pairs, students can create their own graphs to represent the popularity of a music genre of their choice and give it to another pair to write a description.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have built on their existing knowledge of describing graphs by learning about ‘verb + adverb’ and ‘adjective + noun’ constructions. They have practised using this new language when describing graphs.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
