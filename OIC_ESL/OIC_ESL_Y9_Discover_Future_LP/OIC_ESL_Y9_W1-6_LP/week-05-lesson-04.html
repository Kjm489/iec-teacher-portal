1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Show photos of students taking part in community service activities and ask the class to describe what they are doing and why.
  • Write the word ‘community service’ on the board and elicit different community service activities from the class (e.g. cleaning snow from elderly neighbours’ drives; helping senior citizens use computers in a nursing home, or volunteering as a regular companion; organizing clean-up days or food drives for homeless people).
  • Invite students who have done community service to describe their experience.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students that they are going to write an opinion essay on: ‘Community service should be an essential part of the school experience.’
  • In pairs, ask students to list the pros and cons of community service in school.
  • Now form groups of six students to exchange ideas – adding any new ideas to their notes. Invite groups to share their ideas with the class and make notes on the board, introducing new vocabulary where necessary.
  • Ask students whether they agree/disagree with the opinions and invite them to enter into a short debate, making sure to support their opinion(s) with reasons.
  • Present the organization of an opinion essay using the following graphic organizer:
  • Using the pro and con arguments on the board, discuss linking ideas with sequencers:
  • Ask students to prepare an outline using the above graphic organizer.
  • Encourage students to share their outlines with their neighbour and talk through their arguments, giving constructive feedback and kindly suggesting ways to expand on ideas. Monitor pairwork closely and praise students when they demonstrate effective teamwork and solid effort in discussing and improving their essay plans.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4a, 9.4b, 9.4c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • For less able students, guide them through the model essay in ODF SB 4 p.22.
  • For group work, consider keeping more able students together so they can stretch each other.
  • Study the essay in ODF SB 4 p.22 and complete exs.2–3.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have discussed the pros and cons of community service in secondary school. They have looked at the organization of an opinion essay and learned linking words/expressions for specific purposes. They have written an outline for an opinion essay.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
