1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • In small groups, students discuss the following questions in the context of education:
  • What type of content stands out for you?
  • What type of content engages you?
  • What type of content and information do you recall the most?
  • Invite students to share their ideas with the class. Establish that visual data is important in learning – it helps us to understand, process, and retain information.
  • Ask: How often do you see infographics? Why are people using infographics more and more in the world today?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Students are going to listen to a podcast about infographics (track 1.08). Give out the listening handout and tell students to read and complete Activity 1 on the first listen.
  • Order the topics discussed:
  • Maps (4), Economic activity (6), Age of big data (1), Processing images (3), Cost of bread (5), Pie charts (7), Checking sources (8), Social media apps (2)
  • Answers in parentheses – remove from students’ handouts.
  • Play the podcast once, or twice if necessary. Allow students to discuss their answers with their neighbour and try to recall what was said about each. Then review answers as a class.
  • Tell students to read and complete Activity 2 on the second listen. Explain that listening for and understanding different numbers is an important but difficult skill, so they will need to practise lots. Students are in the stretch zone and it’s OK if they don’t hear or understand every statistic or date.
  • What do the numbers refer to?
  • 80% / 60,000 / seven seconds / 1960 / 8,000 / 18th / 1700 / 19th
  • Play the podcast once more, or twice more if necessary. Allow students to discuss their answers with their neighbour. Then review answers as a class (see transcript).
  • Refer students to ODF SB 4 p.33 and ask them to complete ex.4.
  • Show students the video clip about the history of infographics. Ask them to note down any new information that they didn’t learn from the podcast.
  • Discuss the history of infographics as a class. Consider how this could be represented in an infographic (e.g. a timeline).

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1a, 9.1c, 9.1d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • For Activity 2, convert the activity into a ‘match the number to the information’ exercise for less confident students. For more confident students, mix up the order of the numbers.
  • Students can create an infographics timeline to illustrate its history.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have discussed the importance of visual data in education. They have listened to a podcast about the history of infographics, listening first for gist and then for specific information (numbers).

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
