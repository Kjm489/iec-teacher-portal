1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • In small groups, challenge students to list as many features of an infographic that they can think of, based on their learning from previous lessons. By thinking again about infographics and what they include, students are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning. Set a time limit for groups to complete their lists.
  • Ask all groups to stand up. Encourage students to call out their answers. When all of a group’s answers have been called out, they sit down. The final group standing, with the most correct answers, wins the game.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Remind students of the listening activity from the previous lesson. Today, they are going to listen to the second part of the conversation between designers.
  • Divide the class into two groups, A and B. Tell Group A to note down any numbers, including dates, which they hear. Tell Group B to note down any facts which they hear.
  • Play track 3.02 twice. Pause the audio between plays so students have the opportunity to check their answers within their groups. Then form A–B pairs so students can share what they have learned. Review answers as a class and make sure that all students understand the gist of the designers’ conversation.
  • Return to the same groups from Week 10, Lesson 1. Hand out their infographics. Today’s activity is to create a final draft of their infographic, including the key dates and statistics from the second part of the listening activity.
  • Allow students time to plan their final draft, suggest improvements, and decide where to add the new information. Encourage them to prioritize tasks and to play to each other’s strengths (illustrations or IT skills, working with numbers, writing and spelling, organization) so they can complete their infographic within the time limit.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1e, 9.2d, 9.4b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • You can create a worksheet to support less confident students in the listening activity. Ask them to match the numbers to the correct information.
  • Ensure that less able students play an active role in the group activity – even if they find learning English challenging, they might excel at design work or working on the computer.
  • Challenge more able students to include complex topic vocabulary learned in the unit on their infographic.
  • Students reflect on how well their team performed in Week 10, Lessons 1 and 2. Encourage them to think about what went well and what could be improved for next time.
  • Complete exs.3–6 in ODF WB 4 p.29.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have listened for important numbers and dates in a listening activity to rework their infographic from Week 10, Lesson 1. They have worked together in teams to complete the creative task, setting goals and tasks.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
