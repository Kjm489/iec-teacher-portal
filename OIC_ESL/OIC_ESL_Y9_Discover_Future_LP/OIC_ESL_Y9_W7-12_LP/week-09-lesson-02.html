Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Write the following statement on the board: ‘A picture is worth a thousand words.’ Ask students to discuss in pairs what they understand by this, and whether they agree or disgree with the statement.
  • Invite students to share their ideas with the class.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Hand out or write on the board some comprehension questions on the text ‘A Thousand Words’ in ODF WB 4 p.25. For example, you could include the following types of question:
  • Match the quote to the correct speaker.
  • Give three reasons why photos can be powerful.
  • What three tips does the text suggest? Write a summary in your own words.
  • Alternatively, students can complete exs.1–3 on p.24.
  • First, students work individually on their assigned task. After, allow them time to share and discuss answers in pairs or small groups. Then feed back as a class.
  • Project or display a large colour picture showing lots of activity (e.g. a photo of a street scene, a striking or emotive photo of an event, or a famous artwork like ‘The Persistence of Memory’ by Salvador Dalí). Give students one minute to look at and memorize as much of the picture as possible without taking notes.
  • Remove the picture and ask: Can you describe the picture from memory?
  • Pair up students and form groups of two pairs. Give each pair a different picture. Pairs study their picture and create five to eight questions on what they can see or understand by it. Following the same model, Pair A gives Pair B two minutes to look at the picture, before they remove it and follow up with their questions. They then swap roles.
  • At the end of the group activity, challenge each pair to write a creative caption for their picture on coloured card. Collect the captions and display them randomly around the classroom, while students display their pictures elsewhere.
  • Students move around the classroom and try to match the captions to the pictures.
  • Review answers. Encourage each pair to explain why they decided on a particular caption for their picture. Does the rest of the class agree with their interpretation?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.3a, 9.3c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Allow less confident students to answer the comprehension questions in pairs, or look at the text in a small group guided by an adult.
  • You can vary the difficulty level of the memory game by choosing simple or more complex pictures.
  • The caption writing activity gives scope for all students to demonstrate creativity regardless of language level.
  • Hand out more pictures for students to study, decide on a caption, and write a reflective paragraph explaining their reaction to the picture and the reason for their caption.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have read, understood, and answered comprehension questions on an information text about storytelling through photos. They have scrutinized and explored the story behind a range of pictures in a memory game, and created suitable captions.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
