1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask a few confident children to remind you what today’s task is – to produce a poster, slideshow, or short video about the benefits of reading for pleasure. The aim is to promote reading for pleasure throughout the school.
  • Elicit from students the qualities of a good team member (listening to each other attentively and respectively, communicating ideas and decisions clearly, delegating tasks fairly, playing to each other’s strengths, ensuring that everybody is able to participate, peer support and motivation, kindness, hard work). Remind them to try and reflect what they have learned in their own behaviour, today and in future lessons. Being able to work well in a team is an important life skill!

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Explain the task to students as follows:
  • Work as a team to design a poster, slideshow, or short video to present to the class. It must highlight the benefits of reading for pleasure every day.
  • The final product should include a minimum of eight benefits for health and study, with a brief explanation of why each is beneficial.
  • The end-of-lesson presentation should last no longer than three minutes. All team members must actively participate in the final presentation.
  • Remind students that they need to decide on the structure of their team in order to complete the task successfully. Will there be a team leader? How will they divide the work? How will they prioritize and order tasks? Who has design skills? Who enjoys using technology? Who has good writing skills?
  • Set a time limit, depending on the length of your lesson.
  • Teams present their creative work to the rest of the class for questions, comments, and appreciation.
  • Regardless of the success of the final products, praise students for the strong teamwork and creative thinking they displayed in the lesson. They worked together to achieve a goal with minimum teacher input, which has stretched them and helped to consolidate their learning on the topic.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.4a, 9.4b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Encourage less confident but able students to take the role of team leader. Reassure them that stepping outside of their comfort zone can feel scary, but the classroom is a safe and supportive place where they can stretch themselves and grow in confidence.
  • Group work encourages students of varying abilities to collaborate and give/receive support. Invite more able students to act as a mentor for less able students, making sure that they understand the task and are able to participate in line with their interests and abilities.
  • Ask students to write a short reflection on the success of the group task, what went well, and what could be improved for similar future activities.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have practised their productive skills in group work. Understanding the key to successful teamwork, they have worked together to produce a piece of creative work to present to their classmates. They have promoted and understood the benefits of regularly reading for pleasure.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
