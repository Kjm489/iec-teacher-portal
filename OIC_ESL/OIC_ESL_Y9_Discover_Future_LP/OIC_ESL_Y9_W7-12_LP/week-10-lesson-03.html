1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask students to make two circles, with the inner circle facing the outer circle.
  • Give them two minutes to discuss a given topic with the classmate opposite them. Then ask students in the outer circle to move around clock-wise until you clap your hands. Ensure that they are facing a new classmate. Give them a second topic to discuss, using the language they have learned from the unit. Repeat the activity three to four times.
  • When was the last time you read a good book? What was it about?
  • Which do you prefer, reading a paper book or an e-book? Why / Why not?
  • What do you think of libraries?
  • Do you think listening to a story is the same as reading one? Which do you prefer? Why?
  • Ask students to return to their seats. Invite volunteers to share their answers and discuss.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students that they are going to read a text discussing the pros and cons of audio books. In pairs, ask students to predict how the text will be organized. For example:
  • Introduction: Present the topic, showing the direction that the essay will take (the author’s initial opinion);
  • Body (two paragraphs):
  • Argument 1 and counter-argument (consider an opposing view, but then show why it isn’t a strong or relevant argument);
  • Argument 2 and counter-argument (consider an opposing view, but then show why it isn’t a strong or relevant argument);
  • Conclusion: A summary of the arguments, with a final opinion.
  • Turn to ODF WB 4 p.30. Ask students to read the text ‘What are the arguments for and against audio books?’ and identify the writer’s opinion.
  • Highlight the use of linking words which connect sentences together, or relate ideas to one another. Ask for examples from the text (e.g. ‘That said, …’ ‘As a result, …’ ‘therefore’ ).
  • Give each student a graphic organizer. Tell students to ‘deconstruct’ the essay (breaking it down into its different parts) to show how the writer has organized their arguments.
  • Allow students time to compare their graphic organizer with their neighbour’s.
  • Ask students to look closely at the structure of the second and third paragraphs:
  • e.g. Paragraph 2: Topic sentence introduces argument in favour; ‘That said …’ introduces argument against; ‘As a result, …’ summarizes the outcome.
  • In small groups, ask students to think about other arguments in favour of audio books (e.g. they help people to relax and feel less stressed [mindfulness], good for people with learning difficulties – dyslexia, some people are auditory learners, less strain on the eyes).
  • In pairs, ask students to write a paragraph in support of audio books.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.3d, 9.4b, 9.4c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • For the writing task, pair up similar abilities so students can challenge each other appropriately and work at their own speed.
  • Challenge more confident students to compile a list of useful linking words.
  • Additional information: Week 10, Lesson 3
  • Graphic organizer: ‘For and against’ essay
  • Complete exs.1–4 in ODF WB 4 p.30.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have deconstructed a ‘for and against’ essay to better understand its structure.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
