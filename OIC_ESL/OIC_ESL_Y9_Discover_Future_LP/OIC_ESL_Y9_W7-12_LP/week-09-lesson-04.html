Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Write ‘infographic’ on the board. In small groups, ask students to discuss its meaning. (If the word is new, encourage students to divide the word into two INFO/GRAPHIC to help them work out the meaning.) Share ideas as a class.
  • Give each group a different infographic on reading habits. Ask them to discuss the following questions:
  • Why do people use infographics to present information?
  • What are the different elements of an infographic? (e.g. facts and statistics, colour, font size, pie charts, tables, line charts, bar graphs, illustrations)
  • How would you define ‘infographic’ in your own words?
  • Share ideas as a class. Take in the infographics to be used later.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Turn to ODF SB 4 p.32 and read the information under the heading ‘Presenting data with infographics’. Then tell students to study the infographic ‘Reading Habits of Australian Teens’. Ask: What is it about?
  • Individually or in pairs, complete exs.1–2. Review answers as a class.
  • In pairs, ask students to think about the types of question asked to gather the information needed to produce the infographic. Encourage them to answer the questions themselves.
  • For example: Which do you prefer: paper or e-books? What do you think of e-books? How often do you read for pleasure on a weekly basis? Do you have a dedicated e-reader? How often do you use it? Do you have a smartphone? How often do you use it to read?
  • Invite students to report back their questions and answers. Ask: If we were to create an infographic about the reading habits of this class/country, would it be similar or different from the infographic about the preferences of Australian teens?
  • Encourage students to take a few breaths before they respond to the question. This will give them time to reflect on their answer.
  • Put students into small groups. Give each group an infographic about reading habits from the introductory activity. Instruct students to use questions 1–6 from ex.1 on p.32 to help them interpret the infographic, then summarize the information in writing.
  • If the visuals are big enough, invite each group to present their infographic summary to the class. If not, sit two groups together so they can present their findings to each other.
  • Bring the class back together. Ask: What are the pros and cons of infographics? (e.g. Pros: clear and quick way of communicating information, easier to digest complex information, easier to retain facts, easy to share with others, eye-catching design; Cons: they take time to produce – a lot of work goes into data collection and design, information can be misinterpreted, difficult to read on a small screen, data overload, similar designs may lead to information confusion)
  • Encourage students to take a few breaths before they respond to the question. This will give them time to reflect on their answer.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.3b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Mix more able students with less able students in group work so support can be provided.
  • Complete exs.10–11 in ODF WB 4 p.28.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have learned about the layout and purpose of infographics. They have interpreted a selection of infographics and considered their pros and cons.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
