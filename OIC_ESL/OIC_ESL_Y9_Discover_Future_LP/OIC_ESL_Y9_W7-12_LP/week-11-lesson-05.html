1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Describe a (real/imaginary) stressful moment in your life (e.g. driving test, job interview). Talk about the strategies you used to help you cope.
  • Then invite a few volunteers to either share a personal example, or suggest more general examples of stressful situations. Write a list on the board.
  • Elicit a variety of coping strategies for stress (e.g. exercise/being active; connecting with others [a ‘support network’]; having some ‘me time’ to relax [e.g. listening to music or a podcast]; setting goals and challenges to build confidence and emotional resilience; prioritizing important tasks rather than overworking; avoiding unhealthy habits, like drinking too much caffeine; trying to have a positive mindset and appreciating the good things in your life).

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students that they are going to read a text which recommends six coping strategies for stress. In pairs, they note down what they think the strategies might be.
  • Then write the six headings from the text ‘Stay Calm under Pressure’ (see ODF SB 4 p.42) on the board in anagram form. Number them 1 to 6. Challenge students to rearrange the letters to get the correct headings, then compare them with their own suggestions.
  • In small groups, encourage students to think about what advice they would find under each heading. Discuss as a class.
  • Display the large copies of the different coping strategies around the classroom. In their groups, encourage students to circulate, read each strategy, and take notes.
  • Tell students to return to their seats and feed back as a class.
  • Introduce a role-play activity. In pairs, each student chooses a stressful situation (either one that is personal to them, or an example from the lesson). They engage in a dialogue in which Student A explains why they are feeling stressed, and Student B listens carefully and offers a few coping strategies. They then swap roles.
  • Reinforce that in order to help a friend and give advice, we need to show empathy and imagine putting ourselves in their position. It’s important to try and understand how they are feeling, and remember when we experienced similar emotions.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2d, 9.2e, 9.3c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • You can vary the difficulty level of the anagram activity by jumbling up all the words in the headings, or just one word in each heading: e.g. ‘Rename your AEFR’ (fear).
  • Pair up similar abilities in the role-play activity so that students can work at their own pace and use vocabulary suitable for their language level.
  • Students can write about a stressful experience they have faced, including the coping strategies they used. The situation can be fictional if students don’t want to relive a personal experience.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • In pairs and small groups, students have thought about coping strategies for stress and read an information text offering helpful advice. They have practised their speaking skills and learned to cultivate empathy in a role-play scenario, drawing upon their own stressful experiences.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
