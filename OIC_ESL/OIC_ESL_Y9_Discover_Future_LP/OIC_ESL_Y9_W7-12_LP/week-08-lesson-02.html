1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask students to think again about the benefits of reading and call out what they can remember from the previous lesson. In turn, invite students to the board to list their ideas.
  • If necessary, guide students towards other benefits that they have not thought of (e.g. mental stimulation, improved memory, analytical thinking skills, focus and concentration, reduced levels of stress, increased calm, knowledge, expanded vocabulary in their own language and in English, escapism, imagination, interesting conversation starters with friends and family who also enjoy reading) until you have compiled a list of 10–15 benefits. This activity will stretch students as they consider how reading could help them in all aspects of their life (at school, socially, improved personal wellbeing).

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Instruct students to draw two columns with the headings ‘Health’ and ‘Study’. Individually, they sort each benefit on the board into the column they think it fits best. Encourage them to note down why they made particular decisions.
  • Form groups of four or five students. Ask groups to compare their answers and clearly explain the reasoning behind their choices. Invite volunteers to share their group’s answers, and encourage the rest of the class to comment, agree, or disagree respectfully.
  • Switch the focus of the activity by asking students to reflect on how they worked together as a team. Ask: Did you find it easy to communicate? What went well? Why? What could have gone better? Encourage students to highlight each other’s strengths in the group activity, but also to identify what they could do and improve on personally to ensure successful teamwork.
  • In the same groups, students write a definition of a successful team, reflecting on the qualities of team members.
  • Listen to each other’s definitions as a class. You may need to guide the discussion, prompt students with questions, and make suggestions to fine-tune their definitions.
  • Explain to students that in the next lesson, they will work in a team to produce either a poster, a slideshow, or a short video on the benefits of reading.
  • Decide on the groups. Allow them enough time at the end of the lesson to plan how they will achieve their goal. Ask: What format will you choose? What information will you include? How will you divide up the work? What roles will the different members of your group take?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.4a, 9.4b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Group work encourages students of varying abilities to collaborate and give/receive support. Invite more able students to act as a mentor for less able students, making sure that they understand the task and are able to participate.
  • Allow less confident students to play to their strengths. They might be artistic or have excellent digital skills, which they will be able to contribute to the group task.
  • Students can use any additional time to research and plan their creative task in preparation for Week 8, Lesson 3.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have thought more deeply about the benefits of reading. After engaging in a small-group discussion, they have reflected on what makes a successful team. They have begun planning a creative task together, applying what they have learned about teamwork to their actions and behaviour.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
