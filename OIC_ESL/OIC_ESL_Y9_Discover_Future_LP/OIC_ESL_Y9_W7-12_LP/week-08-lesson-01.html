Year 9 · Week 8 Lesson 1 · What are the benefits of reading?

Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Play the game ‘Last person standing’: Tell students to stand up. Ask them to sit down if they have read no books ‘for pleasure’ (not for study) this year. Then ask them to sit down if they have read one book for pleasure this year. Repeat the question, adding one more book each time until there is only one student or a small group left standing. Introduce the term ‘bookworm’ – somebody who loves to read.
  • Encourage students to discuss their reading habits. Ask: Who has an e-reader? Who prefers to read printed books? How often do you read on your phone?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Ask: What are the benefits of reading? Write students’ suggestions on the board.
  • Students are going to watch a video about the benefits of reading (see Factflix video, Unit 3). Set up the listening activity by pairing up students and labelling each pair A, B, or C. Then give each student an information sharing grid (a list of the below questions with spaces for students’ answers).
  • Assign each pair one of the following questions:
  • A pairs: What are the advantages of printed books and e-readers?
  • B pairs: What are the benefits of reading?
  • C pairs: Is reading for pleasure more or less popular than it was 10 years ago? Why?
  • Play the Factflix video twice. Pause after each play so pairs can compare answers.
  • Form new groups consisting of one A, one B, and one C student. Invite each group member to share their information with their new group to complete the information grid.
  • Discuss the answers as a class. Highlight the benefits of reading for wellbeing and study, as discussed in the video (helps to improve concentration and memory, builds vocabulary, more challenging than watching TV, reading for pleasure before bed helps with relaxation). Encourage students to reconsider their own reading habits and what they could do to improve them, if necessary.
  • Tell students to imagine they are discussing reading with a public figure (a favourite actor, sports star, singer, author, activist, etc.). Elicit some examples of questions they could ask about their reading habits: e.g. How often do you read? Do you use any reading devices? What’s your favourite book/genre?
  • Ask students to prepare 5–10 questions individually, then consider how their chosen public figure would respond.
  • Introduce a role-play activity in pairs. Student A finds out who their partner’s public figure is, then asks their prepared questions. Student B replies in the role of that person. They switch roles for a new role-play. If there is time, students can form new pairs.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1d, 9.1e, 9.2f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Role-play activities give all students an opportunity to exercise their creativity regardless of their proficiency in English. Demonstrate a role-play with a confident student first.
  • Students can create illustrated posters about the benefits of reading for pleasure. Display them around the classroom to remind and motivate them.
  • Encourage students to set themselves one or two reading goals. For example, they might choose to read 15–20 minutes before bed every day, or explore an unfamiliar genre.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have listened to a conversation about the benefits of reading and considered their own reading habits. They have applied their learning to a role-play activity.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
