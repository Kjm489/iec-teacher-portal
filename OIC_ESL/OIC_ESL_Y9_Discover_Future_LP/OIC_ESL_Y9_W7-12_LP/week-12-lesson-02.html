Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students of their learning from Week 11, Lesson 5. By thinking again about stress-management techniques, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning, which will serve them in real life. Ask them to recall:
  • the stressful situations discussed,
  • the coping strategies recommended,
  • which coping strategy they think is the best.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • In groups, tell students to look at the pictures in ODF SB 4 p.43 and decide if the scenarios they show are stressful. Why / Why not?
  • Ask students to stand up if they think Picture 1 looks stressful. Invite two or three volunteers to say why they think so. Allow other students to disagree and explain why. Remind them that there is no ‘right’ answer and we might feel stressed in different circumstances. Repeat the process for the other three pictures.
  • Students are now going to listen to short extracts that accompany each of the pictures. Only play the audio that corresponds to Pictures 1 and 2. Tell students to note down the coping strategies the speakers discuss.
  • Play the audio twice (track 1.11) with a pause after each play, so students can compare their answers in pairs.
  • Repeat the process for Pictures 3 and 4. Tell students to note down the advice given.
  • Lead an all-class round-up of the answers to the questions.
  • In groups, ask students to consider the following questions:
  • For Pictures 1 and 2, would you use the strategies mentioned? If not, how would you cope in these situations?
  • For Pictures 3 and 4, do you agree with the advice and how it was given? What alternative advice would you give and how?
  • Bring the class back together. Invite a spokesperson from each group to sum up their group’s conclusions.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1d, 9.1e, 9.2d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Provide a gapped text to assist less confident students with the listening activity.
  • As an extension task for more able students, ask them to write up their responses to the discussion questions.
  • Encourage students to make a list of stressful situations which they have encountered (or might encounter) in school and note down the coping strategies they have used (or would use) to deal with them. Alternatively, set the task up as a speaking activity in pairs.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have built on their previous learning on coping strategies for stress. In listening and speaking activities, they have considered their own emotions and empathized with others to evaluate the appropriacy of advice given in stressful situations.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
