Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Invite students to enter the classroom, sit down quietly, close their eyes, and take a few deep breaths. If available, play some soothing music.
  • Ask them to open their eyes. Elicit how they are feeling now – hopefully, they now feel relaxed, focused, and ready to work hard!
  • Remind students that in today’s lesson they are going to write their ‘for and against’ essay. If fast finishers completed their first draft in the previous lesson, give them the option to plan and write another essay on the motion: ‘Graphic novels aren’t proper literature.’ Are they in agreement or disagreement with the motion?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Hand out students’ essay plans from the previous lesson. They read their plans and remind themselves of the different arguments they wish to include, as well as the organization of an effective ‘for and against’ essay.
  • Give students enough time to write their first draft (approximately 20–30 minutes). After 10 minutes, ask students to put their pens down and take a few deep breaths. Encourage them to reflect on their work so far before starting again.
  • In pairs, students exchange their first drafts with each other. Ask them to edit their partner’s draft using the writing checklist provided in ODF WB 4 p.31. They shouldn’t correct spelling and grammar mistakes, but underline them and indicate the error with a code.
  • Suggested code: Sp = spelling, T = tense, WO = word order is wrong, ᴧ = missing word, prep = preposition, WW = wrong word
  • When pairs have finished peer-editing, tell them to discuss their mistakes and comment on each other’s arguments. Encourage them to give positive feedback, as well as suggestions for improvement. Remind students of the value of feedback – it will help them to identify how to improve their work, so they can continue excelling at learning English.
  • If time allows, students can write their final draft based on their partner’s feedback.
  • Praise students for their effort and perseverance shown in today’s lesson. Writing an essay can sometimes feel stressful, but they stretched themselves and produced what they could in line with their ability.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4d, 9.4e, 9.4f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Allow fast finishers to move to the peer-editing stage if other students are still writing.
  • Remind less confident students to focus on the quality of their work, rather than the amount of words / the number of paragraphs. You are more interested in what they can produce within the time limit and the effort they put into their work. Gently encourage them and provide a writing frame, if necessary.
  • Create a display in the classroom or in another part of the school in support of public libraries where students can exhibit the final draft of their essays. Encourage artistic students to think about how they can make the display eye-catching.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have followed their plans to write a ‘for and against’ essay in support of public libraries. They have consolidated their understanding of the topic and new language learned by reading and peer-editing a partner’s essay, and understood the value of constructive feedback.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
