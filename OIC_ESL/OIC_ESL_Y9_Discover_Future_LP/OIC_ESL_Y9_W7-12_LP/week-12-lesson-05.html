Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask students to recall the organization of a report (see Week 12, Lessons 3 and 4).
  • Introduction: Purpose of report
  • Body: Survey group – a description of the people surveyed, survey results, data analysis
  • Conclusion: Suggestions based on your analysis
  • Discuss the importance of using a formal style in the report. Ask: What does a formal style mean? Help students to understand that the report must include clear headings, formal vocabulary (evidence verbs – see the lesson’s vocabulary), no contractions (e.g. ‘do not’ instead of ‘don’t’), and clear data analysis (using % if possible).

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Encourage students to plan an outline of their report under the abovementioned headings, referring to the results of their survey.
  • Now allocate students enough time to write the first draft of their report. Remind them to organize their report correctly and use a variety of evidence verbs.
  • In pairs, students peer-edit each other’s first drafts, using the writing checklist provided in ODF WB 4 p.41. Remind them that they shouldn’t correct spelling and grammar mistakes, but underline them and indicate the error with a code.
  • Suggested code: Sp = spelling, T = tense, WO = word order is wrong, ᴧ = missing word, prep = preposition, WW = wrong word
  • When pairs have finished peer-editing, tell them to discuss their mistakes and comment on how clearly they have analysed the data and drawn a conclusion. Encourage them to give positive feedback, as well as suggestions for improvement. Remind students of the value of feedback – it will help them to identify how to improve their work, so they can develop their research skills and continue excelling at learning English.
  • Praise students for their effort and perseverance shown in today’s lesson. Presenting and analysing survey results in a report is challenging, but they stretched themselves and produced what they could in line with their ability.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4c, 9.4d, 9.4e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Allow fast finishers to move to the peer-editing stage if other students are still writing.
  • Remind less confident students to focus on the quality of their work, rather than the amount of words / the number of paragraphs. You are more interested in what they can produce within the time limit and the effort they put into their work. Gently encourage them and provide a writing frame, if necessary.
  • Students can write their final draft based on their partner’s feedback.
  • For more writing activities, see exs.5–8 in ODF WB 4 p.41.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have planned and written the first draft of a report, based on their survey results from Week 12, Lesson 4. They have used a variety of evidence verbs to present their data clearly and draw sensible conclusions. In pairs, they have peer-edited their partner’s work and provided constructive feedback.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
