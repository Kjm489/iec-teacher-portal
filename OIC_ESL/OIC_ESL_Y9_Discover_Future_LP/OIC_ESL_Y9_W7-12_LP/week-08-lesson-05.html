1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Put the title ‘The Raven’ on the board and tell students that it is the title of a Tlingit ‘folktale’ – a traditional story passed down from generation to generation. Display the Native American illustrations of the raven ‘stealing’ the sun to accompany the title.
  • In small groups, students predict what the folktale is about based on the title and illustration only. Encourage them to be as imaginative as possible. If students have already researched ‘The Raven’ as an extension task, tell them not to reveal the answer but prompt their peers with questions.
  • Share ideas as a class.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Ask students to read ‘The Raven’ in ODF SB 4 p.31 and identify the different tenses used (narrative tenses: simple past, past continuous, past perfect simple, and past perfect continuous).
  • In pairs, students discuss the function of each tense. Then bring the class back together and ask students to share their ideas.
  • See ODF SB 4 p.117 to review the grammar explanation and complete ex.2.
  • Divide the class into two groups: A and B. Form pairs of students within each group. Give Group A pairs the cut-up story ‘The fisherman and the golden rope’ and Group B pairs the cut-up story ‘The broken promise’.
  • In their pairs, challenge students to put the story into the correct order, then practise retelling the story in their own words. The cut-up sentences only give the bare structure of the story (without examples of the past perfect simple and past perfect continuous tenses), so they will need to add detail and vary the use of narrative tenses. The oral retelling is designed to be challenging because students will have to draw upon their knowledge of vocabulary and grammatical forms to communicate the story clearly. Remind them that they are in the stretch zone.
  • Form new pairs consisting of one Group A student and one Group B student. They orally retell each other their stories. Encourage them to think about how they can capture the interest of their audience: add extra details, gestures, and enthusiasm. If possible, record students’ oral performances.
  • Allow time at the end of the lesson for students to write up their version of the folktale.
  • Discuss the ‘moral’ of each story – a useful life lesson that we can apply to our own lives.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.2b, 9.4d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pair up students of similar ability so they can work at their own pace and challenge each other at an appropriate level.
  • Allow less able students to focus only on the simple past and past continuous tenses.
  • To consolidate their understanding of narrative past tenses, encourage more able students to explain their function to less able students.
  • Additional information: Week 8, Lesson 5
  • The fisherman and the golden rope (Group A):
  • Long ago, there was an old man who loved catching fish.
  • One evening, the man caught a golden fish.
  • The fish spoke to him. ‘Let me go,’ it said, ‘and I will give you a rope made of gold.’
  • ‘How can I trust you?’ asked the man.
  • ‘Put your fishing line in the lake again, and you will catch a golden rope.’
  • The man pulled a heavy golden rope into the boat, but he couldn’t see the end of it.
  • The man wanted to keep the fish and the rope.
  • The man kept pulling the rope and soon the boat became so heavy it sank.
  • The greedy fisherman swam to the shore with no boat, no fish, and no rope.
  • The broken promise (Group B):
  • Long ago, a young man was walking along a beach.
  • He saw a group of children poking a turtle with sticks and throwing stones.
  • The young man chased the children away.
  • The turtle wanted to thank the man for his kindness and invited him to his underwater castle.
  • The turtle ordered a feast for the young man and he enjoyed the tasty meal.
  • When it was time to leave, the turtle gave the young man two boxes.
  • The turtle made him promise that he would only open one box.
  • Back on land, the young man opened one box and found it was full of gold.
  • He believed the other box also had gold in it, so decided to open it.
  • All his hair turned white, his face was full of wrinkles, and he looked like an old man for the rest of his life.
  • Replay the recordings for error analysis, focusing specifically on the narrative tenses.
  • Complete exs.7–9 in ODF WB 4 pp.27–28.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have reconstructed a traditional folktale and retold the story in their own words, using a range of narrative past tenses.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
