Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • In groups of three, students discuss the following questions: What is a story and how is it told? Who tells a story? What makes a good story? Invite groups to share their ideas.
  • As a class, discuss: When are stories spoken and not written? Do you know any stories that are spoken or recorded? Who tells them and who are they for?
  • Show students a video clip about the Tlingit people of southeast Alaska. Ask: How do the Tlingit preserve their history and why?
  • Elicit what it might be like to have no written or electronic way to save a story. How would you make it last?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Turn to ODF SB 4 p.30. First, tell students to cover up the grammar explanation. Then ask them to read the short article about the Tlingit people and note down exactly what is said about: grandma/traditional stories, early societies/stories, historical events/stories, written accounts of past events/nowadays, memorizing stories.
  • Tell students to look at the sentences from the text and, in pairs, discuss the function of the grammatical forms used to, would, get used to, and be used to. They might have already studied this grammar in Year 8.
  • Highlight how teamwork and looking at the context of the sentences are effective strategies for understanding complex grammar.
  • Tell students to uncover the grammar explanation ‘Talking about past habits and changing states’ on p.30. Compare and discuss students’ answers, then complete exs.3–4.
  • Now challenge students to write True/False sentences about themselves and their reading habits using used to, would, get used to, and be used to.
  • Give students a handout with prompts to help them create their sentences, or write them on the board.
  • Ideas for prompts: childhood bedtime stories, reading stories on an e-reader, reading more than one book at a time, reading newspapers/graphic novels/poetry, returning an unread library book to the library, favourite snack when reading, favourite language to read in, writing in the margins of books, bending back the corners of pages as bookmarks, money spent on books, longest time spent without reading.
  • In pairs, students take turns reading out their statements. After each statement, their partner asks questions to work out if it is true or false. Example statement: When I was younger, I would often return books to the library unread. Example questions: Why did you do that? Did you ever read any of the books? Encourage students to ‘think on their feet’ when answering the questions to convince their partner that a false statement is true.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2b, 9.4d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • In a small, adult-led group, provide extra grammar support to less able students.
  • Ask more able students to research the story of the folktale ‘The Raven’ for next lesson.
  • Complete ODF WB 4 p.27 exs.5–6 and review the grammar point in ODF SB 4 p.117.
  • Students can write a short comparison of their reading habits when they were a young child and now using used to, would, get used to, and be used to.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have learned how to talk about past habits and changing states in the context of oral storytelling traditions and their own reading habits.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
