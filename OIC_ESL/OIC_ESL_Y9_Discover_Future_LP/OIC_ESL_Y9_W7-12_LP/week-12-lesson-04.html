1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Write on the board: ‘How competitive are we as a year group?’
  • Ask students to list activities that involve competition both in and out of school. Share ideas as a class.
  • Explain that the title on the board is the subject of a survey they are going to conduct.
  • Elicit from students the research steps they must follow.
  • Conduct a survey
  • Analyse the data
  • (optional) Present the data graphically (e.g. infographic, bar graph, line chart, pie chart)
  • Prepare an outline for a report
  • Write a first draft of the report, revise and edit, write a final report

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Pair up students. Give each pair a copy of the survey questions. Tell them to interview 10 students. (If possible, carry this out with other classes in the same year group.)
  • 1. How competitive are you? Rate yourself on a scale of 1–6. 1 = not competitive; 6 = very competitive.
  • 2. Do you play competitive sports? Yes/No. If yes, which do you play most?
  • 3. Do you play video games? Yes/No. If yes, how many hours a week do you play?
  • 4. Do you like entering competitions? Yes/No. What was the subject of the last competition you entered?
  • 5. How do you feel about competition in your life? Explain your answer.
  • In pairs, students collate their data and analyse it. Tell them to use % where possible to show results (e.g. 10 = total number of students asked; 7 = students who play competitive football; 7/10 = 7 divided by 10 = 0.7 x 100 = 70%)
  • Ask students to think about what conclusion they can draw from the results. How could this be reflected in their report? Could they make any recommendations? Highlight that this activity is designed to stretch students and extend their understanding.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.4a, 9.4b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pair up mixed abilities for the research task so more able students can take the lead and demonstrate what to do at each stage. Encourage them to explain the data collection and analysis process clearly and ensure that their partner actively participates.
  • Challenge more able students to prepare their own survey questions.
  • Fast finishers can begin preparing an outline for next lesson’s report writing task.
  • Encourage students to design an infographic to illustrate their findings (see Week 10, Lessons 1 and 2). The infographic can accompany the report they write in the next lesson to visually illustrate their findings.
  • Complete exs.3–4 in ODF WB 4 p.40 on evidence verbs.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have conducted a survey and analysed the results, in preparation for next lesson’s report writing task. They have established how competitive students are in their year group and identified common trends.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
