1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask students to stand in a circle. Blindfold a more confident student and ask them to stand in the centre.
  • Silently, choose another confident student to stand in the centre and say three things about themselves using a funny voice – for instance, when they were born, where they were born, and how many brothers or sisters they have or if they are an only child.
  • Encourage the blindfolded student to listen carefully and to repeat what they heard (to reinforce the language used), before guessing who the speaker is. Repeat.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Elicit the meaning and purpose of a biography (the story of a person’s life to inform and inspire readers).
  • Explain to the class that they are first going to read a short biography of a famous artist, and then plan their own biography of an artist of choice. Elicit three questions that a biography usually answers and write them on the board.
  • Put students into groups of three. Hand out the paragraphs. Tell them to skim the paragraphs for gist, and then to work together to put them into a logical order. Review answers as a class. Find out if any of students’ questions were answered.
  • Write the following questions on the board. Check understanding of expressions for describing a life (in bold). Then ask groups to read the text again to find the answers.
  • What was a turning point in the artist’s career?
  • What did the artist produce over the course of his career?
  • What was he best known for?
  • What happened in his later years?
  • What happened after his death?
  • Put students into pairs and ask them to choose an artist (to be the focus of their biography text). Hand out the biography timeline templates and sticky notes.
  • Next, give pairs time to research their chosen artist. Tell them to write facts about their early life, career, and legacy on sticky notes. They can stick these to their timeline. Remind them to include supporting examples and to focus on factual information from reliable sources. They should be aware of opinions and biases.
  • Once pairs have finished, encourage them to read through their sticky notes, reordering the facts where necessary to make sure they are presented logically.
  • Encourage students to reflect on how they approached their research, and whether they used key words to narrow down search results and identified reliable sources of information. What would they do differently next time?
  • Collect students’ plans and keep them safe for the next lesson.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3c, 9.4a, 9.4b, 9.4c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • To support less able students, study the input text in an adult-led small group.
  • Consider grouping together more able students so they can stretch each other.
  • Complete ex.5 in ODF SB 4 p.111 and exs.1–4 in ODF WB 4 p.100.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have studied the structure of a biography in a reading activity and learned expressions for describing somebody’s life. They have researched an artist of their choice and planned a biography using a timeline.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
