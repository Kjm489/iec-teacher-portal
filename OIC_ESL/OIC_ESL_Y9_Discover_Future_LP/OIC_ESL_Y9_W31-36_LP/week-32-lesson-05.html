1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Elicit what students can remember about the Mexican community in Pachuca whose neighbourhood was transformed by street art (see Week 32, Lesson 4). Ask: What issues did the community have? How/Why did the murals help?
  • Then put students into pairs. See if they can remember the grammar the interview text introduced – auxiliaries for emphasis and reply questions. Challenge them to write down three examples of each without looking at their notes. Remind students that by thinking again about new grammar, they are building it into their long-term memory.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Elicit that ‘community’ can either mean people who have interests and values in common; or a group of people who live closely together. Being part of a community gives us a sense of belonging. Put students into groups of five to write on their whiteboards the different communities they belong to (e.g. their neighbourhood, their school, clubs or shared interest groups, online forums, pen pals, religious or cultural groups, etc.).
  • Bring the class back together. Hold a quick discussion, encouraging groups to explain what members of each community might have in common. If not already shared, suggest that your school is also a community.
  • Tell groups that they are a project team. They have been commissioned to transform the school with murals. First, they will need to think of issues the project could address (e.g. uninspiring buildings, different year groups don’t feel part of the same community, dark classrooms, etc.). Encourage them to consider how a mural project might help overcome these issues, and to explain the reasoning behind their ideas.
  • Now give groups time to plan their mural transformation project. Explain that this is a stretch zone activity because planning a project is challenging. It requires good teamwork and deep thinking to consider multiple elements, such as:
  • specific area of focus, based on the issues identified;
  • design ideas;
  • health and safety;
  • challenges and how they will be overcome;
  • breaking tasks down into steps – project timeline;
  • roles and responsibilities of the project team.
  • Invite groups to present their plan to the class. Students may ask and answer questions to elicit further details of each project.
  • Hold a whole-class vote on the most inspiring project plan.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1e, 9.2a, 9.2e, 9.2f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Form mixed-ability groups so peer-support can be provided.
  • Consider choosing less confident but able students to be project leaders, and support them to stretch themselves.
  • Less confident groups may be more comfortable presenting to another group of five, while more confident groups should be challenged to present to the class and summarize their project’s aims.
  • Write an opinion essay on the question: How can street art improve a school community?

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have worked together to plan a mural transformation project for their school.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
