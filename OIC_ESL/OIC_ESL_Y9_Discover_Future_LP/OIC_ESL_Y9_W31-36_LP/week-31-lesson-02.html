1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Divide the class into small groups to discuss the following questions:
  • Why is it important that school children are taught about road safety?
  • Whose responsibility is it to teach school children about road safety? The school? Parents and carers? Siblings? Friends and classmates? The government? Why?
  • What is the approach to road safety in other countries? If you’ve travelled abroad, have you noticed any differences between countries?
  • Bring the class back together to hold a whole-class discussion.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Introduce the idea of a Road Safety Week to students. Elicit what they know about this kind of initiative. Have they had a Road Safety Week at school?
  • Put students into pairs to think of ways to raise awareness about road safety at their school, during a Road Safety Week or otherwise.
  • Invite pairs to share their ideas and to explain why they think they would be effective. If songs and poetry have not been mentioned, suggest them now. Reinforce that raising awareness about an issue in this way can help the audience to engage with the message.
  • Explain the creative task. Students are going to write a haiku – a very short Japanese poem – to raise awareness about road safety. A haiku has just three lines and 17 syllables, with the syllable pattern 5–7–5. Check that the class understands the meaning of a ‘syllable’. A haiku does not need to rhyme and is often focused on a brief moment in time or theme.
  • Write your example haiku on the board. You may use the one below. Look at its form together and allow students to ask any questions before they begin.
  • Why won’t you slow down (five syllables)
  • so your family are safe? (seven syllables)
  • Choose life over death (five syllables)
  • Ask students to pause and quietly reflect on the poem’s message and impact.
  • Now put students into pairs to write their own haiku on the theme of speed, with the aim of raising awareness about road safety. They can choose their target audience (e.g. younger school children, parents, or teachers) and adapt their message accordingly. Encourage them to be as creative as possible, and to give their haiku a title.
  • Suggest that pairs refer back to their learning in the unit to find useful vocabulary. Also provide dictionaries and thesauruses. Remind them that it is OK to take risks with their ideas, and the words and messages they choose. Being experimental with language can lead to interesting and unexpected results!
  • Invite volunteer pairs to read their haiku to the class at the end of the lesson. Praise them for their creativity demonstrated in today’s lesson.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3d, 9.4a, 9.4e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Encourage less confident students by monitoring and offering praise. Remind them that they do not need a wide vocabulary to write a haiku well.
  • Challenge fast finishers to write a second haiku independently.
  • Create a poster version of each haiku for display.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have considered ways to increase road safety awareness at their school. They have studied the form of a haiku, then written their own road safety haiku in pairs.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
