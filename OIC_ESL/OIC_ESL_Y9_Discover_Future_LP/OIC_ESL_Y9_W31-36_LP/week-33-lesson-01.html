Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Display a picture of street art. Ask: Is this really art? Tell students to move to one side of the classroom if they think ‘Yes’, and to the other side if they think ‘No’.
  • Invite students to give reasons to support their opinion, reminding them to politely acknowledge their classmate’s argument before offering their own point of view.
  • Take the opportunity to recall auxiliaries for emphasis (see Week 32, Lesson 4): e.g. I do understand your argument, but I don’t think it’s as black and white as you think.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Introduce the word ‘graffiti’. Display the pictures of street art and grafitti side by side. Ask: What is the difference between street art and graffiti? Put students into pairs to discuss, giving reasons and referring to specific examples to support their opinions.
  • Tell the class that they are going to listen to two people giving their opinions about street art. Before they listen, ask them to predict in pairs what opinion(s) they will hear. Invite them to write their ideas on the board.
  • Play the audio (see ODF SB 4 p.107 ex.4, track 2.11) and ask students to listen for gist. Were their predictions correct?
  • On the second listen, ask students to note down the reasons the speakers give to support their views. Reassure them that they do not need to understand every word they hear.
  • Give students time to compare their notes in pairs before checking answers as a class.
  • Then hand out the listening transcripts with the replaced words. On the third listen, challenge students to replace the words with those they hear.
  • Give students time to compare their words in pairs before checking answers as a class.
  • While students are working, write the following questions on the board. Then put them into groups to discuss.
  • Which speaker do you tend to agree with? Why?
  • How might murals convey a powerful message? Give examples.
  • Would you go on a guided graffiti tour? Why / Why not?
  • Do you agree that artwork should only be displayed in art galleries?
  • Bring the class back together and ask a spokesperson from each group to share their ideas. Then set up an informal class debate on the motion: People who paint on walls and buildings without permission should always be punished. Use this as an opportunity to discuss with students if, in their opinion, it is ever OK to break the law (e.g. to ‘cheer up’ a ‘boring’ neighbourhood by painting a mural illegally).

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1b, 9.1f, 9.2d, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pre-teach new vocabulary, including phrasal verbs, from the listening text to support less able students.
  • Challenge more confident students to summarize the conversation in their own words.
  • Encourage less confident students to offer at least one opinion in the group discussion.
  • Encourage students to reflect on the whole-class debate and to summarize their thoughts and opinions in a short paragraph.
  • Put students into pairs to research a well-known graffiti or street artist and to present their findings to another pair.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have listened to a conversation about street art for gist and detail. In response, they have debated the ethics of graffiti and street art as a class.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
