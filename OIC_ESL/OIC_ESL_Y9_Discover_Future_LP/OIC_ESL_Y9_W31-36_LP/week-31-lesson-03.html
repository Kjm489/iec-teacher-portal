1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Display the picture of the skier. Establish if anybody in the class has ever been skiing. If so, invite them to share their experiences. If not, ask: Would you like to try it? Why / Why not?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Explain to the class that when they write or talk about an experience, they should try to make it interesting to engage their audience. To illustrate, they are going to read a description of a skier’s experience. In pairs, ask students to think of words or phrases the author might use to grab the reader’s attention. Elicit ideas and write them on the board.
  • Distribute the handouts (one for each student) for students to skim the text quickly. Are their words or phrases included?
  • Write the ‘more interesting’ gapped phrases you replaced on the board. Challenge pairs to replace the underlined words and phrases in the text with the more interesting phrases. Review answers and check students’ understanding of new vocabulary.
  • Now ask pairs to identify in the text:
  • the opening sentence that grabs the reader’s attention;
  • examples of exciting vocabulary;
  • descriptions of the body’s sensations, so the reader can feel what is being described;
  • examples of short and long sentences.
  • While students are working, write the following on the board:
  • A time when fear rushed through me
  • An experience when my heart thumped nervously in my chest
  • A time when I felt a great sense of relief
  • Something I did that made my muscles burn
  • A time when I thought I would lose control
  • Something I did that made me feel amazing
  • Ask students to stand up and get into pairs – standing will encourage them to use their hands and talk more animatedly. Indicate to the prompts on the board and explain the task. Give them two minutes (one minute each) to take turns describing a scenario in which they felt a particular way, using exciting language to help their partner visualize what happened. After two minutes, they move to a different partner and the next discussion prompt.
  • Thinking on their feet and using recently learned language in a speaking task is challenging. Remind students that they are in the stretch zone.
  • Bring the class back together. Discuss as a class the meaning of the quote: ‘Go fast enough to get there, but slow enough to see.’ –Jimmy Buffett.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1e, 9.2e, 9.3a, 9.3d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • To support less confident students, pre-teach difficult vocabulary from the input text.
  • Challenge more confident students to stand with their back to the board and use the interesting phrases from memory.
  • Complete exs.1–4 in ODF WB 4 p.90.
  • Put students into pairs to write three sentences using less exciting language, then challenge another pair to rewrite the sentences using more exciting language.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have learned ways to relate experiences using more exciting language. They have practised discussing their own experiences using new vocabulary.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
