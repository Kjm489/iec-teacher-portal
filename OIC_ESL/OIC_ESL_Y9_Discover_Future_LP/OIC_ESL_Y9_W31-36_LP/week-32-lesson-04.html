1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Put students into pairs. Ask them to describe the street or neighbourhood where they live.
  • Invite students to share their descriptions with the class. Follow up by asking:
  • Does your neighbourhood have street art? If yes, can you describe it?
  • Do you like/dislike street art? Why? / Why not?
  • When eliciting answers in a whole-class discussion, take the opportunity to explore any cultural differences with regard to students’ perceptions of street art.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Show the class the street art pictures you have prepared in a collage. Give students time to examine them carefully and then discuss the questions in pairs: Which of these houses/neighbourhoods would you most/least like to live in and why?
  • Display the title of the interview text ‘Painting a Brighter Future’ (see ODF SB 4 p.106). Encourage students to predict what the text will be about. Elicit and discuss ideas.
  • Put students into groups of three and give each group a set of interview questions removed from the text. Explain that a community resident will answer these questions in an interview. Give them time to read the questions and think about possible answers.
  • Then hand out the reading text with the gapped questions. Tell students to work in their groups to match the questions to the paragraphs in a logical way. Allow groups to compare ideas before eliciting and checking the correct answers.
  • Ask: What did you find most/least surprising about the text? Can you think of other examples of this kind of street art transformation?
  • Now focus on the highlighted examples of auxiliaries for emphasis and reply questions. Read them out in turn and drill the stress pattern: e.g. We did enjoy … / Didn’t you?
  • Then write ‘Are you?’, ‘Have they?’, and ‘Doesn’t he/she?’ on the board. In pairs, ask students to say the correct reply questions using the correct word stress to the following:
  • My teacher doesn’t like street art.
  • They’ve invited street artists to design a mural.
  • We’re going to paint the school.
  • Write ‘neighbourhood’, ‘design of buildings’, and ‘city centre facilities’ on the board. Give students two minutes to think of something interesting to say on these topics. Then ask them to stand up and mingle with their classmates. In paired discussions, encourage them to use one or two auxiliaries for emphasis. Their partner must respond with a reply question: e.g. A: It does look bright and colourful, but it isn’t the right place to have street art. B: Isn’t it? I can’t think of a better place.
  • Monitor students’ discussions discreetly and note down good and incorrect examples of the grammar point for review at the end of the lesson.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2b, 9.2c, 9.3a, 9.3b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Use ODF SB 4 p.131 to support less able students with the new grammar, while fast finishers can use this page to create their own revision handout for future reference.
  • Complete exs.1–2 in ODF SB 4 p.131 and exs.5–7 in ODF WB 4 p.97.
  • Use the internet to research another community that has embraced street art. Ask students to note down five interesting facts to share with a partner.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have completed a matching exercise in a reading text about street art. They have practised using auxiliaries for emphasis and reply questions in a mingle activity.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
