1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Elicit what students can remember about Aboriginal art (see Week 32, Lesson 2). Ask: What can you remember about how art has broken down cultural prejudices?
  • Put students into small groups. If possible, group them so there is a mix of cultures in each. Ask them to discuss how art represents their culture.
  • Invite students to share their ideas with the class.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Introduce the term ‘self-portrait’ and elicit its meaning (an artistic portrayal of yourself). Ask: How can a self-portrait convey (or show) cultural and personal identity?
  • Put students into groups of three. Invite somebody from each group to come to the front of the class and choose one of the self-portrait print-outs.
  • Tell groups to examine and discuss their chosen self-portrait and, using it as inspiration, to consider the questions: Who am I? What is important to me? What is my life like?
  • As students share their ideas in their groups, they can use the following frame, or adapt it accordingly, to write a mini presentation:
  • I am ___. I come from ___. On a typical day, I___. I’m grateful because___. My life is sometimes challenging because ___. I’m proud of ___. I like/dislike ___.
  • If they feel comfortable, invite a few volunteers to share their mini presentations.
  • Explain to students that they are now going to make an identity mind map, answering the question: What makes you you? Give them sub-topics to consider:
  • Interests and Hobbies: experiences
  • Beliefs and Values: family, social, personal, school
  • Family: traditions, relationships, history
  • Appearance: what family see, what friends see, what teachers see, what you see
  • Give students time to create their mind maps and to share their ideas with their peers.
  • Hand out A3 paper and coloured pencils/pens for students to draw themselves, using their mind map as a prompt. Highlight that this will help them to express who they are in a creative way. Their ideas can be as abstract as possible so they won’t require mirrors to copy exact details – explain that you are more interested in the representation of who they are as an individual, and not of physical features.
  • Collect the self-portraits and display them anonymously. Encourage students to guess the artist of each based on its characteristics. Praise students for their thoughtful work in expressing who they are.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2d, 9.2e, 9.4e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Encourage those students who are less able linguistically to express themselves in a creative way. Highlight their strengths in this area. Prompt them with simple questions and key words so they can articulate their cultural identity in their own words.
  • Challenge more able students to expand on the main points in their mini presentation with supporting details.
  • Invite students to prepare a monologue about themselves and their self-portrait to present in small groups.
  • Complete the reading comprehension activity in ODF WB 4 pp.94–95.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have examined a self-portrait and thought about their own cultural and personal identity. They have produced an identity mind map to inspire their own self-portrait.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
