Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • In turn, invite students to the board to write as many expressions as they can remember for describing a life. Remind them that by thinking again about these useful expressions, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.
  • Review and correct (if required) the expressions together.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Remind students that in today’s lesson they are going to write the first draft of a biography of their chosen artist in pairs, using the timeline plans they created in the previous lesson.
  • Hand out the plans. Allow pairs time to check that they are happy with the order in which they will present the factual information and events.
  • Before students start writing, explain that extended writing is challenging and can feel intimidating. Encourage them to focus their attention by taking a few deep breaths.
  • Give pairs enough time to complete their first draft.
  • Remind them to take a few more deep breaths at regular intervals.
  • Ask pairs to exchange their first draft with another pair, and to peer-edit their biography. They can refer to the relevant timeline template to assist them, as well as the writing checklist in ODF WB 4 p.101 ex.9. They shouldn’t correct spelling and grammar mistakes, but underline them and indicate the error with a code.
  • Suggested code: Sp = spelling, T = tense, WO = word order is wrong, ᴧ = missing word, prep = preposition, WW = wrong word
  • When pairs have finished peer-editing, give them time to highlight what their classmates did well and to discuss any mistakes or unclear information. Encourage them to give positive feedback, as well as suggestions for improvement. Continue to remind the class of the value of feedback – it will help them to identify how to improve their work, so they can continue excelling at learning English.
  • If time allows, pairs can revise their work and write their final draft based on the feedback received.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4d, 9.4e, 9.4f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • For less able students, provide a writing framework to guide them through the paragraphs.
  • Encourage more able students to peer-edit the work of less able students, giving clear explanations for their corrections. This will help to reinforce their own understanding of language.
  • Students can present their chosen artist to the class or in small groups.
  • In pairs, students can role-play an interview with their chosen artist in which they talk about their life’s work.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • In pairs, students have written a biography of their chosen artist.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
