1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Play the Factflix video in ODF SB 4 p.59 ex.3 to find out how other people describe ‘home’.
  • Ask students to note down key words and phrases they hear that people associate with home. Allow them time to check their answers in pairs.
  • Play the video one more time and check answers as a class.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Show the class pictures of different types of home, including gers in Mongolia which were mentioned in the Factflix video. Encourage students to comment on the homes and what they notice that is interesting.
  • Ask: Are there any similarities to your home? Which place is the most different from your home?
  • Choose the picture of that place and follow up by asking:
  • Can you imagine living here?
  • Would you be comfortable? Why / Why not?
  • What do you think is the most important part of a home, to you and to the people whose homes are in the pictures?
  • To answer the final question above, refer students to the photo in ODF SB 4 pp.58–59. Ask: What do you think of this home? Are the people who live there happy? Why do you think that?
  • In pairs, students choose one of the pictures showing a different kind of home. Ask them to inspect the picture more closely and write five sentences about how it is different or similar to their own home.
  • Then ask pairs to write two or three sentences comparing their feelings about their own homes with how the people that live in these homes might feel. Encourage them to talk about their own feelings and experiences between themselves, if they are comfortable. Be aware of the fact that some students might not have a stable or happy home life, so you might need to alter the wording or activity slightly.
  • Invite pairs to read aloud their favourite sentence(s) to the rest of the class.
  • Ask: What are the key things that make all these different places a home?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1a, 9.1b, 9.1c, 9.4d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • To support less confident students in the listening activity, pre-teach new topic vocabulary and/or provide a gapped text for them to follow.
  • Form mixed-ability pairs in the main activity so more able students can support less confident students to express their ideas in writing.
  • To expand and consolidate students’ understanding of vocabulary related to houses and homes, they can complete exs.1–2 in ODF SB 4 p.122.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Through listening, discussion, and writing activities, students have explored different homes around the world and compared them to their own. They have reviewed what makes a house a home in all (familiar and unfamiliar) contexts.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
