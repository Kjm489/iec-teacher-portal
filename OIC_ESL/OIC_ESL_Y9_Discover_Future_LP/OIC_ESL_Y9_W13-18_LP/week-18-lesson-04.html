1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • If suitable for your context, play students the first 36 seconds of the Nomadland (2020) film trailer. This is a realistic portrayal of a nomadic lifestyle and will help to set the scene and inspire thinking for today’s lesson.
  • Then show students a picture of a camper van. Ask: Have you ever been on a trip in a camper van? Would you like to? Why / Why not? How would you feel about living in a camper van permanently?
  • Students discuss their answers in pairs or small groups first, then share their ideas with the whole class. Encourage them to give reasons for their answers.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Explain that many people globally live in mobile homes, like camper vans or houseboats. They work and travel from these homes instead of living in a permanent house.
  • People who live nomadic lifestyles don’t often have a lot of personal possessions.They usually have the ‘bare essentials’ – items of clothing, books, cooking utensils, and maybe a computer to stay in contact with the rest of the world.
  • Turn to ODF WB 4 p.56 and ask students to complete ex.1. Check answers as a class.
  • Now ask students to imagine that they are going to travel the world in a camper van and live a nomadic lifestyle. Consider the following questions:
  • What would you do with all your personal possessions? What would you take with you?
  • Would you feel happy and safe in the camper van, or would you prefer to live in a permanent home?
  • How would you feel – excited or scared, or both?
  • Is having a permanent home important to you? Why / Why not?
  • Remind students to give reasons for their opinions and to listen respectfully to each other’s ideas. Their classmates might have different but interesting perspectives.
  • In pairs or independently, instruct students to write a monologue about nomadic lifestyles. In it, they should compare living in a camper van and travelling the world with living in a permanent home. What are the advantages and disadvantages of each lifestyle? Which of the two is better and why? Explain that they have to give two reasons as to which of the two is better.
  • Remind students to check their work for accurate spelling, punctuation, and use of language. Have they come to a decisive conclusion and explained why?
  • Allocate enough time for students to rehearse their monologues.
  • Students present their monologues in small groups.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.2f, 9.2g, 9.4c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pair up mixed abilities for the monologue activity so more able students can guide less confident students through the task and support them with language choices.
  • Encourage more confident students to present their monologues using notes only, not just reading aloud their text.
  • With the necessary permissions, encourage students to record their monologues so they can watch back and evaluate their presentation skills, use of language, and correct pronunciation.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have prepared and performed a monologue in which they think critically about nomadic lifestyles, taking into account the advantages and disadvantages.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
