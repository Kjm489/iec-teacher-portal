1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Turn to ODF SB 4 p.61. Ask students to quietly read Poem B.
  • Ask: What is the poem about? (homesickness / missing home) How does it make you feel?
  • Refer students to the words ‘mud house’ in the first line. Ask: What does it tell you about the poet’s circumstances? (They come from a humble background.)
  • Then ask: Do you think the poet likes their mud house? How do we know this? (Yes – they use a positive adjective: ‘the fresh smell of clay’.)
  • Encourage students to find more descriptive language that paints the poet’s home in a positive light (e.g. ‘walls standing tall’, ‘looming with calming familiarity’, ‘the sky flowered’). Help them to understand difficult vocabulary, using the context to give hints.
  • Ask: Is the poem happy, sad, or a mixture of both? (a mixture of both)
  • Write the word ‘nostalgia’ on the board and elicit its meaning – a ‘yearning’ or desire to return to a moment in the past. Highlight that it is an abstract noun. The adjective to describe the feeling is ‘nostalgic’, which is what the poet is feeling.
  • Finally, ask: Which senses does this poem appeal to? (sight, sound, smell, touch)

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Set students the task of writing their own poem independently.
  • Ask them to close their eyes and to think about their childhood home. What good memories do they have? What type of things do they remember? Encourage them to think about the five senses. Give an example: e.g. My family home always smelled of warm, buttery biscuits because my grandmother used to make them for me.
  • They can also reflect on their home’s natural surroundings. Ask: What can you remember about the colour of the sky? The flowers or trees in the garden? Did you play outside?
  • If some students have never moved house, tell them to imagine that they have moved to a different country and are feeling homesick.
  • Look at the structure of Poem B together. Elicit that there are three stanzas, made up of four or five short lines. Each stanza focuses on a different memory.
  • Elicit that there are no rhyming words in the poem – not all poems have to rhyme!
  • Tell students to write two verses, with each verse revealing something different about their home. Encourage them to be as creative as possible as they use descriptive language to explore their past memories.
  • Remind them to consider the poem’s tone. Will it be happy, sad, hopeful, or a mixture?
  • Finally, encourage students to give their poem a title.
  • Invite a few volunteers to read their poems aloud to the class.
  • Praise students for their effort and creative use of language in today’s lesson, no matter what the final result.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4a, 9.4b. 9.4e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Less confident students can write one verse only, or write their ideas in their first language initially before conveying them in English.
  • Encourage more confident students to allude to all five senses in their poem.
  • Ask students to read Poem A, then compare both the poems in their own words. See exs.3–4 for vocabulary to describe poetry in ODF SB 4 p.122.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have written a poem describing the nostalgia of ‘home’.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
