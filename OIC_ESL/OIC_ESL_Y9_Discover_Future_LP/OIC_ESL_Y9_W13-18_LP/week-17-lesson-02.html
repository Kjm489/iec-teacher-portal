1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Explain to the class that in today’s lesson they are going to think about the recent past, and how we can learn from our past mistakes.
  • Turn to ex.1 in ODF SB 4 p.54. Ask students to read the ‘What’s your problem?’ section.
  • Hold a whole-class discussion about whether students agree or disagree with the typical teenage problems. Prompt them to expand on their answers by asking: Why do you think that? Have you experienced this problem? What did you do to solve the problem?
  • Remind students that the classroom is a safe space for these kinds of discussions, but it is also OK if they don’t feel comfortable talking about personal experiences.
  • Show the class examples of advice columns and problem pages. Ask: Would you write to an advice column if you had a problem? Why / Why not? Help students to understand that there are lots of different ways of solving a problem and different methods work for different people. Advice columns can show us that we all have similar problems and experience similar emotions at times.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students that they are going to write a letter to an advice column, and then practise problem-solving and showing empathy by responding to somebody else’s letter.
  • As a class, expand on the list on p.54 by eliciting more problems that teenagers might or are likely to face. Write ideas on the board.
  • Form small groups of three to four students. Ask them to choose one problem and imagine a scenario that led to the problem.
  • Give a large sheet of paper to each group. Instruct them to fold the paper in half and write their letter on the top half. They can use the example problem pages and experience of writing a letter in Week 17, Lesson 1 to help them. Remind them to listen carefully to each other’s ideas and ensure that everybody is involved and is participating.
  • Let groups know when they have five minutes left to complete the task.
  • Collect in all the letters and redistribute them to different groups. Tell them to read their classmates’ letter and, between themselves, decide on a good piece of advice. Tell students to think about how they would solve the problem if they were in the writer’s shoes – this is key to understanding a problem and developing empathy.
  • Allow groups enough time to write a response on the bottom half of the paper.
  • Bring the class back together. Ask students to nominate one group member to read out the problem and another group member to read out their piece of advice.
  • Ask the rest of the class if they think the advice is helpful and realistic. Invite volunteers to propose other solutions.
  • Say: Before the class ends today, I would like you to reflect on a difficult experience you had in the past. You don’t need to share this experience, but what did you learn from it?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.4b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Form mixed-ability groups so peer-support can be provided. If necessary, allow less confident students to make suggestions in their first language so they can participate in a meaningful way. Encourage more able students to help their peers communicate what they want to say in English.
  • Students can complete exs.8–10 in ODF WB 4 p.48.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have practised soliciting and giving advice in a collaborative letter-writing activity.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
