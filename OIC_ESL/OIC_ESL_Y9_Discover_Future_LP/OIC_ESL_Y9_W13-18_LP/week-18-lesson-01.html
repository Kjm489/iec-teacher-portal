Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Turn to ODF SB 4 p.59. Look at the two quotations in ex.5 and write them on the board.
  • Hold a whole-class discussion about what students understand by the word ‘home’. Try to elicit that it doesn’t necessarily mean our physical house and what’s inside, but it has more of an ‘abstract’ meaning – for instance, it could be where our family and friends are at any one time; a feeling of community in our town; or where we feel safe, warm, and comfortable.
  • Suggest that people’s home might not be the place where they grew up. If appropriate, you could ask a student whose country of origin is different from where they are living now what they understand by ‘home’ and if they feel at home in both places.
  • Use this discussion as an opportunity to celebrate different cultures and homes, and (if relevant) how lucky you are to meet people from lots of different cultures at school.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • In small groups, students are going to research a ‘success story’ of a public figure or migrant who has made their home in another country. Before beginning the task, elicit how they can work together effectively as a team.
  • Groups will need to decide the best method for their research. They might already have a public figure in mind (e.g. Dua Lipa, Malala Yousafzai, Freddie Mercury, Isabel Allende, Gloria Estefan) or might be aware of a member of the local community who has achieved success in their new country.
  • Encourage them to find key facts about their chosen person and to compile a short biography, including what their life was like before, where they live now (if still alive), and how they achieved success despite initial difficulties. If they can find a quotation about what their understanding of home is, even better.
  • Remind students to use key search terms to find relevant information on suitable websites, and to try to distinguish between facts and speculation. Monitor them actively.
  • When students have found the information they need, they will need to decide as a group how to present the information – they could choose a slideshow, a poster, a role-play, or a purely oral presentation with notes.
  • Invite groups to present their successful migrant to the class. At the end of all the presentations, hold a whole-class vote on the most interesting story.
  • Encourage students to imagine that they have to leave their country and live in another one. Ask:
  • What would be the biggest challenge about moving to a new ‘home’? (e.g. language, meeting new people, integration, education, work, money, trying new kinds of food)
  • How do you think migrants feel when they first arrive?
  • What can we do to make migrants feel comfortable and at home?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.3f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Form mixed-ability groups so peer-support can be provided. Encourage able but less confident students to stretch themselves and take a leadership role.
  • Ask students to use the key phrases in ODF SB 4 p.59 ex.5 to write a short paragraph about what ‘home’ means to them.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have participated in a collaborative activity, researching a migrant success story and presenting their findings. They have empathized with the situations of others.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
