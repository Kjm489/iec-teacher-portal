1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Give students an example of a past mistake, such as: A few years ago, my friend asked me to help him move house. He had helped me when I had moved, so I should have returned the favour. However, I wanted to go to the beach. I made an excuse, saying I had to visit a family member in hospital. On my way to the beach, my friend saw me waiting for the bus with my beach bag. I felt ashamed because he was very upset. I made a big mistake!
  • Ask: How should I have dealt with the situation? Try to elicit from students that it is much better to be honest, to accept responsibility for your mistake, and to apologize when you have hurt somebody’s feelings.
  • Follow up by asking: How do you feel when you make a mistake? (e.g. ashamed, defensive, embarrassed, angry, upset, worried)
  • Encourage students to give examples of difficult experiences they have had, if they are comfortable. What did they learn from them?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students they are going to listen to someone giving advice on what to do if you make a mistake. Play the audio (track 1.14). Ask them to note down answers to the questions:
  • What kinds of mistakes do people make? (worrying too much about what friends think, not taking school seriously enough, not asking for help)
  • Is it possible to avoid mistakes? (no – but you can learn and grow from them)
  • What do people often do or feel when they make a mistake? (they might feel defensive or embarrassed, they might try to cover up the error because they are worried about the consequences, they might think they are a failure)
  • What advice does the presenter give? (try not to feel embarrassed, be honest and admit the mistake, apologize if your actions have affected another person, accept responsibility, think about how you can avoid the same situation in the future, don’t obsess about what could have been, ask for help)
  • Tell students to share their answers with a partner, then play the audio once more. Review answers as a class and check that students understand any new vocabulary.
  • Play the audio a third and final time now that students know what to listen out for.
  • Tell students to choose their favourite piece of advice from the listening task and to design a poster for younger students in their school, offering them advice on how to deal with making a mistake.
  • Hand out coloured card and other creative materials for students to make their poster.
  • Allow students enough time to complete the task. As they work, prompt them to explain why they have chosen a particular piece of advice. Praise them for their thoughtful and sensible answers, and encourage them to apply this advice to their own lives.
  • Display the posters around the classroom for students to read and enjoy.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1a, 9.1c, 9.1f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • To support less confident students, pre-teach new vocabulary before the listening activity. Elicit definitions from more confident students. You can also provide a gapped text for them to follow and complete.
  • Students can complete ex.3 in ODF SB 4 p.54 and exs.1–7 in ODF WB 4 p.49.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have learned about the best way to deal with a mistake in a listening activity, then applied their learning to a creative task.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
