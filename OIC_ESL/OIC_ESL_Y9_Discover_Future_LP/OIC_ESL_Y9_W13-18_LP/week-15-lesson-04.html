1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Refer students to the reading strategy in ODF SB 4 p.50 which outlines the structure of an opinion piece. Elicit this structure from students and write on the board:
  • Introduction – introduces the debate and provides the author’s initial opinion;
  • Paragraph 1 – argument 1 in support of the author’s opinion, accompanied by evidence and/or examples;
  • Paragraph 2 – argument 2 in support of the author’s opinion, accompanied by evidence and/or examples;
  • Conclusion – a summary of the two arguments and concluding statement to convince the reader of the author’s point of view.
  • Then turn to pp.44–45 in ODF WB 4. Ask students to scan the article and complete ex.3 in pairs to consolidate their understanding of how a good opinion piece is presented.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • In today’s lesson, students are going to write an opinion piece on what makes a good museum, in follow-up to the ideas they generated in the previous lesson.
  • Form the same groups of students from Week 15, Lesson 3.
  • Write the three main ideas the class mutually agreed on on the board and hand out the group mind maps. Allow students time to remind themselves of their arguments through quiet reflection and discussion.
  • Before beginning the writing task, elicit some useful expressions for introducing each argument: e.g. ‘Firstly, I believe … because …’; ‘Secondly, …’; ‘Furthermore …’; ‘One argument is that …’; ‘Another argument is that …’.
  • Repeat for concluding expressions: e.g. ‘All in all, …’; ‘Finally, …’; ‘To conclude, …’.
  • In pairs (from the same group), students work together to write the first draft of their opinion piece. They can refer back to the articles in ODF SB 4 p.51 and WB 4 p.45.
  • Remind students to regularly review their main points to make sure that they are logical and unbiased. Help them to understand that their arguments will be more persuasive if they use strong supporting reasons and examples.
  • Explain that presenting and justifying their ideas in writing is an important life skill to have, but it can feel challenging. They are in the stretch zone as they gradually build on their confidence and ability to write persuasively.
  • When students have completed their first draft, encourage them to take a short break and then read through their work with fresh eyes. Have they organized their arguments logically, with evidence and examples? Have they used varied and persuasive language to capture the attention of the reader? Is the grammar, spelling, and punctuation correct? Allow them time to discuss their work in pairs and make suggestions for improvement.
  • Invite a few pairs to read aloud their work.
  • Praise students for their effort in today’s lesson and for supporting and encouraging each other in their pairs to produce an opinion piece.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4c, 9.4d, 9.4e, 9.4f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Less confident students can focus on writing just one body paragraph, while more confident students can present and evaluate the validity of counter arguments.
  • Students can write a final draft of their opinion piece and put it on display.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • In pairs, students have written a persuasive opinion piece and organized it correctly.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
