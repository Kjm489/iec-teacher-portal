1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Play a quick vocabulary game to test students’ memory and understanding of recently learned topic words. Divide the class into mixed-ability teams.
  • Say: I am thinking of an ancient item that is exhibited in a museum. The first person to call out the correct word (‘artefact’) wins a point for their team. Write the word on the board.
  • Repeat with a range of words, such as ‘museum’, ‘exhibition’, ‘display’, ‘collection’, ‘archaeologist’, ‘cultural’, ‘hieroglyphics’, ‘value’, ‘preserve’, ‘fascinating’, ‘civilization’, ‘loot’, ‘treasure’, ‘caryatid’, ‘precious’, ‘country of origin’, ‘statue’, and ‘excavate’. Start with easier words and increase the difficulty level if students are doing well.
  • At the end of the game, congratulate the team with the most points. Then ask students to look at all the topic words on the board. In pairs, ask them to categorize them under the labels ‘Adjective’, ‘Noun’, and ‘Verb’ on their whiteboards. Review as a class.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • In today’s lesson, students are going to participate in a role-play set in a museum. They will either be a tour guide or a member of a tour group visiting the museum. Divide the class into groups of four to five.
  • First, ask students to choose one or two artefacts researched previously and make a list of facts that a tour guide would need to present the item(s) (e.g. its name, where it is from, how old it is, what it is made of, what it shows, why it is important). Alternatively, hand out pre-prepared artefact ‘fact cards’ with brief notes and pictures.
  • Ask groups to nominate one student to be the tour guide.
  • Allow groups time to write down a list of questions that a tour group would ask.
  • Invite a (more confident) group to the front of the class as an example. Tell them to put their picture(s) of the artefact(s) on display. Using their notes, the tour guide presents the artefact(s) to the tour group members, who listen carefully to the information provided and ask relevant questions as a follow-up. The tour guide then uses their existing knowledge of the artefact or their imagination to answer the visitors’ questions.
  • Now instruct all tour guides to stand in various places around the classroom with their artefact(s). The remaining students pretend they are in a museum and walk around the class, asking questions about the artefacts on display.
  • Encourage all students to try to use the topic words revised at the beginning of the lesson.
  • Elicit from students what spoken register their characters would use in a real-life context – tour guide (friendly and enthusiastic but polite, not too formal, professional); tour group members (informal but polite and respectful when asking questions). Tell them to apply this register to their own characters.
  • Hold a whole-class discussion about the success of the task. What worked well? What could be improved? Did students stretch themselves by using difficult topic vocabulary?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2b, 9.2c, 9.2f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Group work encourages students of varying abilities to collaborate and give and receive support. You can appoint more able students to lead the group task. Help less confident students with preparing and practising their questions.
  • Students can write a final draft of their role-play script.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have prepared and participated in a role-play activity in small groups, using newly learned topic vocabulary and an appropriate spoken register to suit the museum context.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
