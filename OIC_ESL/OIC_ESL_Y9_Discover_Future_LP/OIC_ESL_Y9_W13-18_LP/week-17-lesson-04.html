1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Turn to p.56 of ODF SB 4 and look at the article ‘Memorial to an Educator’. Ask students to cover up the text with a piece of paper and focus on the title, photo, and caption.
  • In pairs, students discuss what they think the statue represents, based on all the information they have. Then invite them to share their ideas with the class. Give praise for their critical thinking and originality.
  • Now ask them to read the first paragraph only. They check their initial predictions and explain who Mary Mcleod Bethune was, in their own words, to their partner.
  • Write ‘LEGACY’ on the board. Elicit what the word means from the context – it is the way in which people remember you, thanks to what you’ve achieved or done in your lifetime, and it can be either positive or negative. Ask: Was Mary’s legacy positive or negative?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Students complete their reading of the article. Ask them to note down the features of the text, in addition to the title, photo, and caption:
  • Paragraph 1 – provides context by describing the statue, and explaining where it is located and who/what it represents;
  • Body paragraphs (2 & 3) – offers background information, outlining the person’s legacy;
  • Paragraph 4 – summarizes the importance of the person depicted in the statue and why they should be remembered, including a direct quotation.
  • Help them to understand the purpose of an article further by asking:
  • What is the register of the text? (formal)
  • Are there for and against arguments? (no – it is a presentation of facts in celebration of Mary’s achievements and legacy)
  • Does the writer include their opinion? (not explicitly, but some of the language used indicates that they view Mary in a positive light – see below question)
  • Can you identify any form of bias on the writer’s part? Why do you think they use emotive language? (to elicit an emotional response from the reader – ‘passionate’, ‘determined’, ‘selflessly dedicated’, ‘generously supported’, ‘inspired’)
  • Hand out A3 sheets of paper. In small groups, ask students to create a mind map showing how Mary demonstrated good leadership. Encourage them to put themselves in Mary’s shoes and think about all the challenges she had to overcome.
  • Invite groups to share their mind maps in turn and write their ideas on the board.
  • As a class, talk about any commemorative statues/monuments in your local area which could be the focus of students’ articles in the next lesson.
  • Students can begin researching their chosen statue/monument using a variety of methods (e.g. the internet, local history books/tourist brochures, asking adults questions).

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3b, 9.3d, 9.3e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pre-teach the lesson’s vocabulary before looking at the article in-depth. Less confident students could be guided through the text in an adult-led small group.
  • Encourage more able students to consider how the writer expresses their appreciation of the subject in a nuanced way, maintaining the formal register of the article.
  • Read the article in ODF WB 4 p.50 and complete exs.1–4.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have read and identified the features and purpose of an article about a commemorative statue. They have started planning their own article.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
