1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Introduce the lesson by saying an adapted version of the following: I had to move house a few years ago. I went from a big house to a much smaller one. I had so much stuff at the old house: books, CDs, gadgets that I never used, recipe books, wardrobes full of clothes, even old toys. Many things had sentimental value. You know, a bag that my best friend gave me as a gift, and a bread maker from my brother which I never use. I decided to donate a lot of it to charity. It was overwhelming at first and I felt very anxious knowing I had to downsize. But I did it. I only chose a few meaningful possessions to take with me. Can you guess what I took with me?
  • Elicit answers but don’t tell students if they are correct or not. They are likely to mention electronic devices, so hint that you took two things with you which they wouldn’t expect (e.g. a book about how to garden, an old toy giraffe, or a photo album).
  • Ask: Were you surprised by my choices? Gauge students’ reactions. Say: Everyone is different, so what is important to one person may not be important to another. Who is more correct here? What I think is important or what you think is important? (Neither – different things are meaningful to different people and that is OK.)
  • Encourage students to think about their own homes and families. Ask: Can you think of one thing that is very important to you but perhaps not to others? How would you feel if it went missing? Can you think of one thing important to a family member but not to you? Discuss first in pairs, then as a class.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Show students pictures of tiny homes around the world. Tell them to imagine that they are moving there with their family. Explain that they can only take three things with them because there is very little space.
  • Elicit the meaning of ‘sentimental value’ – when something is important to you because of the story behind it or because it reminds you of a special person. It doesn’t have to cost a lot of money – in your opinion, it is worth more than expensive items!
  • Allow students enough time to decide on and note down their three possessions.
  • In pairs, students share their final list and explain the reasoning behind their decisions (i.e. why the item has sentimental value). Prompt students further by asking: How would you feel if you lost or couldn’t take this item with you?
  • Making decisions, even when hypothetical, can feel stressful. Tell students to stop, take a few deep breaths, and think of something calming if the activity is making them feel anxious. If necessary, demonstrate a few grounding techniques.
  • Invite students to share their ideas with the class, as well as an interesting or thoughtful personal possession that their partner suggested.
  • Establish if students have chosen the same kinds of items or if they are all different.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2d, 9.2e, 9.2g.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Less confident students can focus on just one meaningful item instead of three.
  • Challenge more confident students to explain their selection process and why they chose a certain item over other items in the running.
  • Complete exs.8–9 in ODF WB 4 p.58.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have decided on their three most important personal possessions and explained their significance to their partner.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
