1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Show students pictures of the British Museum and the Acropolis Museum. Elicit what they know about these two famous museums. (The British Museum in London is the world’s oldest public museum and is free to visit. It has over six million visitors a year and exhibits artefacts from civilizations all over the world, including ancient China, Egypt, and Greece. The Acropolis Museum holds artefacts from one of the world’s most important archaeological sites, where it is located – the ancient Acropolis in Athens, Greece.)
  • Ask: Which of these two museums would you like to visit and why? If possible, let them find the museum websites to help them with their decision.
  • Then show the class a picture of a smaller museum. Ask: Would you like to visit this museum? Why / Why not?
  • Then encourage students to think more critically: Why are smaller museums important? Do you think they have less value than bigger museums? Why / Why not?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • In small groups of three or four, students are going to discuss what makes a good museum and present their ideas to the class. At the end of the lesson, you will select the best ideas from each group’s presentation to make a whole-class list.
  • First, encourage groups to create a mind map of ideas.
  • When their mind map is complete, ask students to agree on their two strongest ideas and to work together to explain their thinking behind their decision.
  • Allow groups enough time to complete this task. All students must have a speaking role in the presentation.
  • Remind students that group work allows them to transfer knowledge and to learn from each other. Each group member might have a different museum-related experience to share, or they might have a keen interest in a specific civilization or era of history, so it is important to listen and engage.
  • As students prepare, write a few useful presentation expressions on the board, such as ‘I think …’, ‘In my opinion …’, ‘I strongly believe …’, ‘I expect a good museum to have …’, ‘However, …’, ‘Although some people say … I think …’, ‘On the [one/other] hand …’. See also the key phrases in ex.5 ODF SB 4 p.49.
  • Invite students to come forward and present their ideas, using their mind map to support them. Write their ideas on the board in note form.
  • After all groups have presented their ideas, review each one and ask students to raise their hands if they support it. Give them three votes each. For each hand raised, allocate one point to each idea. Add up the points to find the three best ideas.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • More confident groups can select and expand on three ideas instead of two. Encourage them to think of a possible argument against museums.
  • Less confident students can use the mind map or their own notes to support them during the presentation.
  • Produce a final draft of the three most popular ideas for display.
  • Students can begin planning an opinion piece on the value of museums and what makes them worth visiting in preparation for Week 15, Lesson 4.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have discussed and presented their views on what makes a good museum.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
