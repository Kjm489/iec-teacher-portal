Main activity

Explain that this week, students will work in small groups on a project of their choice. They can extend their learning into an area of particular interest to them, and practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way that they could apply to a real-world setting.

Students will focus on a project related to the topics ‘Why do we read?’ or ‘Why do we compete?’. They should work in small groups with shared areas of interest.

Students can choose their own project, linked to either topic, or they can develop a project from one of the options below.

Encourage students to use the IDEAS to Action framework, which they will be familiar with from their Global Skills Projects lessons. Remind them that this is a smaller-scale project, so they may need to adapt and modify it to suit their needs.

Students will be responsible for the planning and management of their project. This should involve:

suggesting and allocating specific tasks

researching and drafting creative work

reviewing drafts and contributing constructive ideas and suggestions

joint presentation (the following week)

formative self-assessment and peer-assessment (the following week).

Students can present their work in a way that best suits their project, depending on resources and time available. See how creative they can be with their presentations. Here is a non-exhaustive list of different kinds of presentation:

A speech or oral retelling (perhaps supported by visual resources, such as a PowerPoint presentation, photographs, a campaign poster, a model, artefacts, graphs or charts, etc.)

A pitch (to sell a product or service)

A skills demonstration

A debate

An art/photography exhibition or display

An interview with an expert in the field of enquiry

A short film or documentary

A creative performance

Remind students that they have five days to complete the planning, research, and execution of their project. At the end of Day 1, teachers could look over each group’s plan to ensure that they have allocated tasks appropriately and know what to accomplish each day.

Topic 1 (Why do we read?)

Reading week: Plan a reading week to promote reading in your school. Consider:

A theme: This will help to connect the week’s activities and will give the event a ‘catchy’ title: e.g. ‘Plant a seed and read!’ or ‘Read around the world’.

Activities to promote reading: This might involve reading challenges, games, and competitions to encourage students to read for pleasure. You could create a way to monitor how many books are read over the week and show students’ progress, organize live or virtual author/illustrator talks or a second-hand book fair, set up a book display, give a school assembly to celebrate reading, share a story with younger students, or arrange a book club.

Promoting the event: Think about how you will promote the event. You could design posters, send letters/flyers home to parents or other students, or give an announcement.

Organizers/Helpers: Who can help you? (e.g. the school librarian, teachers, other students, parents, local bookshops or writers)

Initially, this project is a presentation of an idea to show how you would organize a reading week. Perhaps it is something that you could really organize at a later date, but for this week’s lessons you are creating a detailed plan to present to the class.

Creative writing: Write a story in your preferred format.

Consider how you will present your story. Will it be a short story, a graphic novel, a play script, an audio story, a short video, or a traditional oral retelling?

Remember the elements of a good story: interesting characters, setting, theme, a plot with a conflict and a resolution.

The writing process still applies whatever medium you decide to use: list your ideas using a mind map, select two or three and develop them a little further before deciding which one you like best, plan your ideas and organize them in a coherent way, write a first draft, revise and edit, then write the second draft.

Discuss your ideas with other students. Their constructive feedback and suggestions will help you to develop your story and produce a final draft.

Decide how you will present it to the class when it is complete.

Topic 2 (Why do we compete?)

Design a reading competition: For this project, you could work collaboratively with a student who is working on the ‘Reading week’ project, or you could create some reading challenges for younger grades and help out their teachers. Consider:

The age group and aim of the competition or challenge (e.g. to read more, to encourage reading for pleasure, to develop reading skills).

Activities: Ensure that the activities you organize are engaging and accessible.

Prizes/Awards: Consider how students will be rewarded for their achievement and/or effort (e.g. certificates, book mark prizes).

Research your ideas: Who can help you with your ideas, in addition to the internet? (e.g. the school librarian, reading specialists at school, school teachers)

Try out your ideas: Testing your ideas on your peers will allow the opportunity to receive feedback and improve them.

When you present your project to the class, describe the different competitions you have designed, comment on the feedback you received from the people you tested your ideas on, and explain how you aim to improve your ideas based on their feedback.

Design a board game: For this project, you will need to follow these steps:

Research: What makes a good game? Investigate existing board games and consider the aim of the games, the challenges, the storyline, the rules, and components.

Create a prototype: Select your theme, sketch your idea, identify the different parts of the game, and create a playable paper prototype. Invite a small focus group of classmates or teachers to play the game and give you feedback.

Final design: You might want to create a larger or digital version of the game.

Advertise your game: Create a pitch to advertise your game, perhaps accompanied by a poster.

Rate the game: Create a feedback sheet so that students can provide feedback and rate the game when they have played it.
