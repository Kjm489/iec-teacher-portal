1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Elicit from students what they already know about modal verbs. Write their suggestions on the board. They might suggest ‘can’, ‘could’, ‘may’, ‘might’, ‘must’, ‘shall’, ‘should’, ‘will’, and ‘would’.
  • Ask for a few suggestions in the present or future tense to warm up their thinking.
  • Then help students to understand that we can use modal verbs to speculate about the past. Provide a few examples:
  • This statue might have come from ancient Greece.
  • These hieroglyphics must have been hidden away for centuries.
  • Ask students to look at the sentences carefully and identify the modal verb. Elicit that the modal verb occupies the first position in the verb phrase, before the auxiliary verb have and the past participle. See ODF SB 4 p.121 for additional grammar support and practice.
  • Then show the class a picture of an eight-track cartridge (a type of CD from the past, containing eight songs which you could play on a special machine). In pairs, encourage students to speculate about what it was used for using modals in the past: e.g. It may have been a kind of record player. People wouldn’t have carried it around!

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • In today’s lesson, students are going to make their own time capsule.
  • Form groups of three to four students. Explain that they need to agree on three items from the past to put in their time capsule. Then they will look for pictures of their chosen items on the internet.
  • Encourage them to include objects that we no longer use and might not be familiar to younger generations.
  • Instruct students to print out their three pictures, stick them on a sheet of A3 paper, and label them ‘A’, ‘B’, and ‘C’. Then ask them to fold the paper in half.
  • Remind students to keep information about their time capsule items in a separate place.
  • Tell groups to exchange their capsules. They must work together to speculate about what their classmates’ time capsule items are, and what they were used for in the past.
  • Tell them to write their guesses on the back of the sheet of paper.
  • Remind them to use modals in their sentences. Monitor and assist where necessary.
  • When students have finished speculating, invite groups to tell each other what their capsule items are and how they were used in the past.
  • Hold a whole-class feedback session following the activity. How many time capsule items did students guess correctly? How did they find using modals in their sentences? What present-day items would students use in a new time capsule for the year 2200?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2b, 9.3f, 9.4d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • To support less able students, spend more time working through the examples in ODF SB 4 p.121.
  • Form mixed-ability groups so more confident students can show less confident students how to form speculative sentences.
  • Students can read the article about time capsules in ODF SB 4 p.52 and complete exs.1–2. They can also complete exs.6–7 in ODF WB 4 p.47.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have built on their existing knowledge of modal verbs to speculate about how different time capsule items were used in the past.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
