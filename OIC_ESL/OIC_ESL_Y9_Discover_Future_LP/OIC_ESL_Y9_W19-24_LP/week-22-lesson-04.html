1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students that in Week 22, Lesson 1 they listened to a Factflix video in which Ryan and Ava discussed the meanings of colours in different cultures. Can they remember the cultures and colours mentioned?
  • Ask students to think of a colour that has meaning in their culture.
  • Students discuss their ideas first in pairs before sharing their thoughts with the class.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • In small groups, give students a few minutes to make a list of different cultures around the world. Invite a representative from each group to write their ideas on the board.
  • Introduce the term ‘cultural diversity’ and explain what it means. See if students can think of examples of societies or places that are culturally diverse.
  • Elicit examples of different cultural customs and beliefs (e.g. meanings of colours, language, enjoyment of specific sports and activities, special foods). Briefly discuss why cultural identity is important.
  • Put students into pairs. Tell them to choose a culture from the list on the board and use the internet to find out facts about the meanings of colours in that culture. They should also look for visual evidence (e.g. photographs or video clips, if possible) to back up what they have learned.
  • Remind students that when researching on the internet they should use appropriate search terms to find relevant search engine results, check that the websites they use are reliable (e.g. not biased in favour of a particular opinion), and keep a note of the web address to use as a reference.
  • Praise pairs who are using effective research strategies to narrow down search engine results and find the information they need.
  • Allow students enough time to do their research and organize their findings.
  • Form groups of two pairs of students. Tell one pair to present their findings without saying the name of the culture for the other pair to guess, then swap roles.
  • Once both pairs have presented, encourage students to reflect on why different cultures might view the meanings of colours differently.
  • Invite pairs to present the most interesting facts they found to the class.
  • As a class discuss: What meanings of colours were most surprising for you? Why do you think this was?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3a, 9.3c, 9.3f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Students with limited language skills might enjoy looking for photographs online while those who are more able will be challenged by reading online texts.
  • Confident students can take a leading role linguistically, demonstating key topic vocabulary and instructional language.
  • To support less able students, prepare a note-taking frame or omit note-taking altogether.
  • Give pairs of students paper and coloured pens/pencils. Tell them to choose one meaning of one colour and to create a design to represent their chosen meaning.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have researched the meanings of colours in different cultures on the internet and presented their findings. They have considered why meanings might differ. They have discussed cultural diversity and why cultural identity is important.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
