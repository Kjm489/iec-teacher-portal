Year 9 · Week 22 Lesson 3 · What do colours mean to me?

Original Oxford outcome(s): 9.2d, 9.2e, 9.3a

Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Say: Imagine you are sitting in your favourite room in your home. How do you feel? What colours are in the room? Elicit a few answers from the class.
  • Then say: Now imagine that everything in that room is white (or black, orange, etc.). How does this change the way you feel? Elicit answers from the class.
  • Discuss as a class: How much can colours influence mood and enhance or reduce emotions?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Ask students to look around the classroom. Say: What colours can you see? Introduce the term ‘colour scheme’. Tell groups they are designers who have been asked to recommend a new colour scheme for one part of the school. Make clear that they can choose any colours they want, however, their choices should encourage a positive learning and teaching experience.
  • Put students into groups of four. Tell half of the groups to think of five adjectives to describe good students (e.g. ‘attentive’, ‘cooperative’, ‘hard-working’). Tell the other half to think of five adjectives to describe good teachers (e.g. ‘supportive’, ‘knowledgeable’, ‘fair’, ‘calm’). Invite representatives to share their adjectives with the class and write them on the board.
  • Then give each group paper and coloured pens to make a poster of their ideas, as well as one part of the school to focus on. Select from the following list:
  • classroom(s)
  • sports hall
  • canteen
  • staff room
  • headteacher’s office
  • shared spaces (e.g. playground)
  • Allow students about 30 minutes to make their posters. They can use the adjectives from the last activity as inspiration for the colours they choose.
  • Display the posters around the classroom. Encourage students to walk around, look at each other’s posters, and see what original ideas their classmates have come up with.
  • Finally, invite groups to present their ideas, giving reasons for their choices, before the whole class votes on the best design.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2d, 9.2e, 9.3a.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • The group work allows every student to make a contribution. Form groups in the way you think will benefit students the most. You could mix levels of confidence so that more confident students can support those who are less confident.
  • Students can write a short letter to the headteacher proposing a new colour scheme for their classroom, sports hall, or canteen. Remind them to give reasons to support their recommendations.
  • Put students into groups. Ask: What colour food do you most and least prefer to eat and why? Do you ever choose not to eat something because of its colour?

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have considered the relationship between colour and emotions. They have worked in groups, agreeing a new colour scheme for part of the school. They have made a poster and presented their recommendations to the class.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
