Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students that last lesson they discussed the environmental impact of manual toothbrushes. Ask: Can you remember the infographic statistics?
  • Ask pairs of students to note down other common colourful products that might have an impact on the environment.
  • Tell pairs to swap their list with another pair and add to the other pair’s list of items. Do this once more with a new pair. Then bring the class back together to review the items and discuss their potential environmental impact.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Form groups of four students, consisting of Pair A and Pair B. Tell students they are going to read sections of a text entitled ‘The Cost of Colour’ (see ODF SB 4 p.76).
  • Give each pair one of the subheadings: ‘Beauty Products’ or ‘Pens and Ink’. Ask pairs to predict what their section will be about based on the title only. They then share their ideas in groups.
  • Distribute six sentences (three from each text, including Green Tip sections) to each group of four. Challenge students to work out which sentences go under which subheading, and to say why.
  • Review answers as a class.
  • Now give each group cut-up copies of both texts. They work together to reconstruct them, drawing upon their logic and knowledge of text structure, and check if their sentence predictions were correct.
  • Ask Pair A to read the full text ‘Beauty Products’ and ask Pair B to read the full text ‘Pens and Ink’. Tell them to prepare a minimum of three comprehension questions on their text.
  • Pairs swap texts. Give them two minutes to skim-read before turning the texts face-down.
  • Pair A and Pair B take turns asking and answering each other’s questions, based on their understanding of the text.
  • Bring the class back together and discuss what they have learned.
  • Ask students if the texts they read will have any effect on their behaviour. Will they think of the environmental impact when they decide what products to buy and use in the future?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2c, 9.3b, 9.3c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • As these are relatively short texts, they should be accessible to all students. However, remind less confident students that they do not need to understand every word to understand the main idea of a text. Alternatively, pre-teach vocabulary.
  • Challenge more confident students to close their books and summarize in their own words what they have learned about the environmental impact of everyday products.
  • Complete exs.9–10 in ODF WB 4 p.68.
  • Students can read the whole text ‘The Cost of Colour’ and identify any new vocabulary. Working in pairs, they divide the new words between them and check the meanings using a dictionary. They take turns explaining the meanings to their partner.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have practised their speaking and reading skills while considering the environmental impact of commonly used products.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
