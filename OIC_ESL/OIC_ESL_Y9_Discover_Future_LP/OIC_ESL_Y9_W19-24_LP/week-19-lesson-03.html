1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Refer students to ODF WB 4 p.58 ex.9. Allow them time to complete it in pairs.
  • Read through the completed paragraph together. Elicit all the phrasal verbs related to being tidy: ‘put in order’, ‘put away’, ‘clear out’, ‘get rid of’, ‘sort out’, ‘clean up’, ‘tidy up’, ‘throw out’, ‘give away’, and ‘pass on’. Write them on the board and confirm students’ understanding of their meanings.
  • Decide which ones are synonyms or similar in meaning (e.g. ‘put in order’ / ‘sort out’; ‘tidy up’ / ‘clean up’ / ‘put away’; ‘clear out’ / ‘throw out’ / ‘throw away’ / ‘get rid of’; ‘give away’ / ‘pass on’). You could model the nuances between meanings using classroom items.
  • Establish if the class is familiar with Marie Kondo’s work – if not, explain that she is a ‘home expert’ and well known for showing people how to keep their home neat and tidy. If possible, show them a short video clip of her giving advice.
  • Discuss: Do you think it’s a good idea to focus on the things you want to keep, rather on the things you want to give away? Why / Why not? Would this strategy work for you?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Pair up students. Explain that in today’s lesson they are going to role-play a conversation between a teenager with a messy bedroom and a home expert (like Marie Kondo). The teenager will explain the problem and the impact it is having on their life, and the home expert will give advice on how to tidy up their living space. Encourage students to practise using a variety of new vocabulary and phrasal verbs from the unit in their conversation.
  • Model a short exchange with another adult or a confident student first.
  • Refer them to ODF SB 4 p.65 ex.7 and ODF WB 4 p.58 ex.10 for useful role-play language.
  • First, allow students time to decide on their roles and note down what they want to say. Encourage them to think of three main points each – for instance, in the case of the messy teenager, they could mention that:
  • they struggle to concentrate when doing homework because their desk is always disorganized and they can never find their notes;
  • they want to sort out their possessions, but aren’t sure where to begin;
  • they think they have too many pairs of trainers.
  • After the preparation stage, allow students time to practise their role-plays. Remind them of the importance of ‘active listening’ – to pay attention to what their partner is saying and to respond directly to the point(s) they make, and to use body language (e.g. eye contact and gestures) to show they’re engaged and interested.
  • Invite a few volunteer pairs to perform their role-play to the class. Elicit from the rest of the class what they did well.
  • Ask: Do you think Marie Kondo’s work is meaningful? Why? / Why not? Have a class discussion about what meaningful work students would like to do in the future.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2b, 9.2c, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Encourage more confident students to take on the role of the home expert, so they can demonstrate to their partner how to use new language (e.g. phrasal verbs) in context.
  • Less confident students can reduce their ideas to one or two main points and use their notes during the role-play.
  • Students can write a to-do list of things they can do to organize their personal space.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have participated in a role-play activity about organizing their personal space.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
