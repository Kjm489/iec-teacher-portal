Year 9 · Week 19 Lesson 1 · Are you organized or disorganized?

Original Oxford outcome(s): 9.1a, 9.1c, 9.1e, 9.2c

Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • In small groups, ask students to write a list of adjectives to describe their bedrooms and a list of adjectives to describe their personalities. Allow them enough time to do this, then share answers as a class.
  • Write students’ adjective ideas on the board in two columns, under the headings ‘My bedroom’ and ‘My personality’.
  • Encourage students to read the words on the board and quietly reflect on how their bedroom matches their personality. Is it a relaxing space to sleep, spend social time, and do their homework in?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Explain to students that they are going to listen to four short monologues describing other teenagers’ bedrooms (see ODF SB 4 p.64 ex.2).
  • On the first play (track 1.17), students listen carefully for gist and take notes under A–D.
  • On the second play, students note down if the speaker believes being messy (disorganized) is a positive or negative thing, and why.
  • Allow students time to compare their answers in pairs, before playing the audio for a final time (if necessary). Note: Don’t check answers as a whole class yet.
  • Form groups of three or four students. Instruct each group to make questions based on what they have heard in the listening activity. They are going to test another group’s understanding of the four arguments for or against messy rooms.
  • Before students begin, remind them that it is a team activity. Elicit that working well together will mean sharing and comparing their notes, suggesting and listening to each other’s questions, and agreeing on their best four or five questions. The aim is that every student contributes at least one question – this might require some prompting from the teacher or from more confident students.
  • This activity will rely on the quality of students’ notes, their ability to identify main points in a listening text, and their memory. Explain that despite the discomfort they may feel, they shouldn't give up as the activity is designed to stretch their thinking.
  • Remind students to also make a list of answers that they expect to receive.
  • Pair up groups. They take turns asking and answering their questions, ensuring that all group members have the opportunity to ask and answer once.
  • Bring the class back together. Hand out the listening transcript. Review each monologue together to check students’ understanding of each speaker’s main points.
  • Ask: What did you understand well? What didn’t you get quite right? Why do you think you misunderstood? What has today’s group activity taught you?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1a, 9.1c, 9.1e, 9.2c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pre-teach or revise key words from the listening text first, so less confident students will have concrete vocabulary to listen out for.
  • Encourage more confident students to summarize what they have understood from the listening activity in their own words.
  • Complete exs.3–4 in ODF SB 4 p.64.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have listened to four monologues about the advantages and disadvantages of having a messy bedroom. They have listened for gist, then consolidated their understanding of the main points in group work.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
