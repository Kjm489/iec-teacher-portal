1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Students decide what their favourite colour is.
  • When they have decided, see if they can find two other people with a different favourite colour. They must do this by mingling and saying: My favourite colour’s X. What’s yours? Give students a few minutes to do this before asking them to sit down.
  • In small groups, students discuss why they like their favourite colour. If they are struggling for ideas, tell them your favourite colour and why you like it. For example: My favourite colour’s white because it reminds me of snowflakes on a cold winter’s day. Encourage students to listen carefully and to show an interest in each other’s opinions.
  • Invite volunteers to share the reason(s) behind their favourite colour with the class.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Ask: Do colours have a meaning? Write students’ suggestions on the board. Some ideas may be similar to the reasons given in the introductory activity (e.g. ‘white means winter’).
  • Turn to ODF SB 4 p.71. Tell students they are going to watch a Factflix video in which Ryan and Ava discuss whether colours have a meaning. Ask them to write down the colours they hear as they watch – purple, green, red, white.
  • Play the video once. Check answers as a class.
  • Divide the class in half, labelling students A or B. Tell them they are going to watch the video again. Instruct A students to write down the meanings they hear for the colours purple and red, and B students to do the same for green and white.
  • Play the video again. In pairs (AA and BB), students compare their notes briefly.
  • Then form small groups, making sure there are A and B students in each one. Allow them enough time to share information before taking answers from the whole class.
  • Draw students’ attention to the last sentence in the video – ‘we shouldn’t make assumptions about the meanings of colours in different cultures’. Discuss as a class why it is important not to make assumptions.
  • Play the video for a third time. Challenge students to write down phrases which include colour (colour idioms), for example, ‘a bit green’.
  • Elicit the phrases and explain the meaning of any which are unclear. Then discuss as a class: When was the last time you were green with envy? What makes you see red? Reflect on why people feel envious of others, why people get angry, and how they might control these negative emotions.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1a, 9.1d, 9.1e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Listening activities allow students to gather different levels of information in line with their ability. The subsequent pair and group work stages will help to fill in any gaps in their understanding. For less able students, you may wish to provide a gapped text (the transcript with key words and phrases removed) to complete.
  • Challenge more confident students to define key word phrases in their own words for the benefit of less able students.
  • Complete exs.1–2 in ODF WB 4 p.66.
  • Ask students to research more colour idioms online and create a whole-class list.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have extracted specific information from a video about colours and their different meanings. They have worked collaboratively by sharing information and ideas.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
