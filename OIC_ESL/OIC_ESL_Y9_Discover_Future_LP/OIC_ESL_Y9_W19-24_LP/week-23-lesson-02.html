1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students that last lesson they read about Holi – a colourful celebration. Ask: What can you remember about this festival? Elicit any information that students remember.
  • Encourage students to give you examples of local cultural festivals.
  • Ask: Why are cultural festivals important? Students discuss their ideas first in pairs.
  • Share ideas as a class. They might suggest: cultural festivals help us to remember an important event from the past, they engage people in their cultural heritage, and they bring people together.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students they are going to research more colourful festivals around the world.
  • Elicit what questions they could ask to find out interesting information: e.g. What colours are associated with the festival? What are its origins? What is celebrated? What do people do there? When does it take place? Where does it take place? Write them on the board for students to copy down.
  • Form groups of three. Give each group the name of a colourful festival from the list you prepared before the lesson.
  • Instruct students to use the internet to find out facts about the festival. Remind them that they are a team and should work together fairly, dividing the research questions between themselves according to their preferences and interests. Encourage more able students to support their group members with extracting main points from websites and understanding difficult vocabulary.
  • Allow students enough time to complete their research.
  • Explain to groups that they are going to present their festival to the class. Give them time to decide how they are going to do this in the most effective way. For example, they might decide that two team members will speak, while the other team member will show photographs or write key points on the whiteboard. Alternatively, they might take turns presenting different facts, in line with their individual interests and ability. Encourage them to consider the strengths of each person and work together to agree what works best for the team. This activity is designed to stretch students and extend their understanding of how to present effectively.
  • Allow students enough time to produce and practise their presentation.
  • As groups present their research, encourage the rest of the class to make notes about what they hear. Pause briefly after each presentation to elicit what was understood.
  • Lead a whole-class discussion about some of the similarities and differences between the festivals and encourage students to consider the importance of colour for festivals.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.2f, 9.3e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Form mixed-ability groups so peer-support can be provided.
  • Allow less confident students to read their notes in the presentation, while more confident students can try to present their findings in their own words without notes.
  • Students can write a short article about the festival they researched.
  • Groups can write down five new vocabulary items learned in the research task. Pool ideas to create a whole-class list.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have worked in small groups to research a colourful festival. They have presented their findings, having considered the strengths and preferences of the team.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
