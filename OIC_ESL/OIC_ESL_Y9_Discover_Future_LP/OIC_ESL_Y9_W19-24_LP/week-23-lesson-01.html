Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask: What is your favourite celebration? What colours are traditionally associated with this celebration?
  • As a class, encourage students to discuss colour associations for celebrations in their own and different cultures. For example, see if they know the colour of wedding dresses around the world.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Show students a selection of pictures of colourful celebrations around the world. Ask: Do you know the name of any of these celebrations? Elicit answers before telling them the names. Don’t go into detail as students will research colourful festivals in the next lesson.
  • Tell students they are going to read an article about one of these colourful celebrations. Encourage them to predict which celebration the article will be about.
  • Divide the class in half and then into pairs (AA, BB).
  • Give A pairs the first half of the text in ODF SB 4 p.74. They read the text and note down what the celebration is, its origins, and when it is celebrated.
  • Give B pairs the second half of the text. They read the text and note down what the celebration involves, where it is, and what the text says about elephants.
  • Allow students time to work individually before sharing their answers with their partner. If they have different answers, encourage them to reread the text and find the correct answer together.
  • Now form new pairs (AB). In turn, students communicate what they have learned to their partner so both students have information on the whole text. Highlight the value of learning from each other to complete a reading task.
  • Lead a whole-class round-up of the information they discovered about Holi. Ask: Would you like to go to this festival? Why / Why not? If any of your students have been to Holi, encourage them to share their experiences.
  • Discuss as a class: Why are colours important for celebrations? Invite volunteers to share their ideas.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2d, 9.2f, 9.3c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Pairwork allows more able students to support those who are less confident.
  • Pre-teach any unknown vocabulary before the reading activity to prepare for the task.
  • Allow less confident students to discuss the final questions in small groups. This should help build their confidence.
  • Complete exs.5–6 in ODF WB 4 p.67.
  • Write a paragraph in response to the class discussion on why colours are important for celebrations.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have read a part of a text about Holi celebrations and shared new information with a partner. They have engaged in a class discussion about the importance of colour in cultural festivals.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
