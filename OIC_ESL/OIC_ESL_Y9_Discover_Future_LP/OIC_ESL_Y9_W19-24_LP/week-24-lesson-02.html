Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students that last lesson they learned some presentation signposting phrases. Elicit the topic of the presentation and the key points (see ODF TG 4 pp.135–136 ex.4, track 2.03 for transcript).
  • In pairs, students write down the phrases they remember (e.g. ‘First of all, let’s look at …’; ‘Next I’m going to talk about …’; ‘I’d like to leave you with one clear message: …’). By thinking again about signposting language and presentation skills, students are building their long-term memory of this learning.
  • Put pairs into groups of four to share their lists, before eliciting from the class. Write the phrases on the board, categorizing them into their function.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Introduce the term ‘eco-friendly’. In today’s lesson, students are going to present an eco-friendly product. Elicit examples of eco-friendly products they, or their family, use.
  • Place slips of paper with the names of eco-friendly products and photographs (if you chose to use them) at the front of the class. Invite students to come and choose a product.
  • Hand out the presentation templates. Tell students to research their chosen product on the internet and make notes on the template. Set a time limit.
  • Remind students to add some presentation signposting phrases to their notes (they can use the ones you wrote on the board). Allow time for students to practise their presentation, speaking quietly to themselves.
  • Form AB pairs. Ask students to stand up (they can take their notes) and form two lines on opposite sides of the classroom – As on one side, Bs on the other facing As.
  • Pairs take turns presenting their product to each other. Since they are presenting to someone on the opposite side of the room, they will need to speak loudly and clearly.
  • This activity will be noisy as half of the class will be speaking at the same time. However, students should find the activity fun and non-threatening – as the rest of the class will be engaged in their own presentations – which will improve their speaking confidence.
  • Change pairs so that students can present their product more than once, before bringing the class back together.
  • Praise students for speaking clearly and confidently.
  • Invite a few volunteers to present their product to the whole class.
  • Discuss as a class: What are the benefits of eco-friendly products? Which products would you use? Why? Which products would you not use? Why?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2a, 9.2b, 9.2f, 9.2g.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Monitor and support less confident students during the research, note-taking, and practice phases of the lesson. Pair up similar abilities so students can work at their own pace and help to stretch each other.
  • Challenge more confident students to present without using their notes.
  • Students can write an opinion piece about the necessity of eco-friendly products.
  • Work in groups of four and debate the following question: Are eco-products too expensive?

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have used the internet to find out information about an eco-friendly product. Using their notes, they have practised presenting it to a partner in a fun way.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
