Year 9 · Week 22 Lesson 2 · Colours and personality

Original Oxford outcome(s): 9.1c, 9.2a, 9.4d, 9.4f

Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Write ‘red’, ‘blue’, ‘yellow’, and ‘green’ on the board.
  • Put students into small groups. Give each group four sticky notes (one for each colour). Say ‘red’ and give students 30 seconds to think of one personality adjective they associate with the colour. To support, elicit examples such as ‘strong’ or ‘ambitious’.
  • Tell each group to write their word on a sticky note and put it on the board next to ‘red’.
  • Repeat this for the remaining colours. Set a time limit of five minutes.
  • Read the adjectives with the class, checking meaning and correcting spelling mistakes.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • If students did not include the following adjectives, write them on sticky notes: ‘optimistic’, ‘warm’, ‘aggressive’, ‘timid’, ‘creative’, ‘energetic’, ‘stubborn’, ‘reliable’, ‘serious’, ‘rebellious’, and ‘confident’.
  • Ask: Which colour do you associate with someone who is [optimistic]? Briefly discuss with the class and introduce the term ‘character/personality traits’.
  • Dictate three headings: ‘positive’, ‘negative’, and ‘neutral’. Put students into pairs and ask them to categorize the adjectives on the board under these headings.
  • Elicit the answers. Explain the meaning of new words.
  • Encourage students to think of famous people who have clear personality traits. Write their names on the board.
  • Now ask students to write a paragraph describing the personality of a chosen famous person without saying their name. They should start: This person’s personality is the colour [x]. I’d describe their character traits as … because …
  • In groups of three, students to take turns reading aloud their paragraphs. The rest of the group has to guess which famous person is being described. Did the colour the writer chose help them to guess correctly or not?
  • Discuss as a class: Is there a relationship between colour preferences and personality? See if students think that people with different personality traits might be attracted to different colours.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1c, 9.2a, 9.4d, 9.4f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Encourage less confident students to play an active role in guessing the famous person, even if they are not sure. A correct guess might improve their confidence.
  • Allow less confident students to work together and mind map the personality adjectives of the same famous person before writing a paragraph independently.
  • Challenge more able students to include more complex personality adjectives and phrases.
  • Collect the adjectives from the board. Give one to each student and say: This is your personality. Put students into pairs and ask them to start a conversation. They must behave like the adjective until their partner guesses what adjective they were given.
  • Challenge students to make a noun from the adjectives on the board: e.g. ‘warm’, ‘serious’, and ‘creative’ = ‘warmth’, ‘seriousness’, and ‘creativity’. Explain that these suffixes can be used to make other nouns. See exs.3–4 in ODF SB 4 p.124 for more vocabulary practice.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have considered the relationship between colour and personality. They have written a paragraph describing the personality of a famous person and reflected on how people with different personality traits might be attracted to different colours.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
