1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask or dictate: What was the last item of clothing you bought, or that someone bought for you? Why did you/they buy it? What colour was it? In pairs, students briefly discuss their answers before sharing them with the rest of the class.
  • Ask: What do you do with clothes you no longer want or need? Allow students time to think about their answer individually, then lead a class discussion.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Introduce the idea of ‘signposting language’. Explain why it is important when giving a presentation – it orientates the listener, helping them to follow the speaker. Elicit examples from the class, such as ‘Today I’m going to talk about ...’.
  • Pair up students. Hand out the pre-prepared gapped phrases/sentences to each student and instruct them to discuss what word(s) they think complete the phrases.
  • e.g. ‘First of all, let’s look at the history of [colourful] clothing.’
  • Bring the class back together and review answers.
  • Tell students they are going to listen to a short presentation (see ODF SB 4 p.77 ex.4, track 2.03). Explain that the first time they listen, they should try to answer the following gist questions:
  • What is the topic of the presentation? (the environmental impact of the fashion industry)
  • What are the three sub-topics the speaker is going to talk about? (1. history, 2. what is wrong with the industry, 3. suggestions for improvement)
  • Students compare their answers in pairs, then check answers as a whole class.
  • Play the audio a second time. Tell students to listen and complete the phrases/sentences from the first activity. They compare their answers in pairs, then check answers as a class.
  • Write a list of signposting functions on the board, such as ‘introducing the topic’, ‘providing an overview’, ‘starting a new topic’, and ‘summarizing’.
  • In groups of three, ask students to work together to categorize the signposting phrases/sentences. Then invite a student from each group to write the phrases on the board under the correct function headings.
  • Allow students time to peer-correct miscategorized language before reviewing the answers as a whole class. This will help them to support and learn from each other.
  • Hold a debate: How far do you agree that the fashion industry is polluting the planet?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1a, 9.1e, 9.1g, 9.2b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • There is a lot of challenging vocabulary in the listening activity. To support less able students, you could allow them to read the transcript while listening, reduce the number of words they are listening for in the gapped text activity, or pre-teach key vocabulary. Remind them that they do not need to understand every word to get the main idea.
  • Challenge more confident students to write down the linking phrases they hear without giving them the gapped sentences.
  • Complete exs.1–3 in ODF WB 4 p.69.
  • Students can look for more examples of signposting language for presentations on the internet and add them to the whole-class list.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have listened to a presentation about the fashion industry. They have answered gist questions and listened for detail. They have categorized presentation signposting language and debated the impact the fashion industry has on the planet.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
