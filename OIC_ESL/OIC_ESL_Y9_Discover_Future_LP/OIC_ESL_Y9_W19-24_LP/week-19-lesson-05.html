1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Describe a favourite room of your own to students. Organize your description to mirror the organization of the model texts from Week 19, Lesson 4, using strong sensory language.
  • Ask students to listen to your description with their eyes closed and try to visualize your favourite room.
  • When you have finished, invite volunteers to share their image of the room you described orally, or to draw it on the board. Elicit or bring to their attention any new language.
  • Invite students to ask you more questions about your favourite room to get a better understanding of what it is like and why it is special.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Explain to the class that in today’s lesson they are going to use their plans to write a description of their favourite room. Encourage them to refer back to the two model texts from the previous lesson to remind them of the structure of a description.
  • Students should spend most of the lesson writing the first draft of their description.
  • In pairs, students exchange their first drafts. Tell them to read through and peer-edit each other’s work. Refer them to the following writing checklist:
  • Content
  • Do they introduce their favourite room and describe what they notice first when they walk through the door? Do they include how this makes them feel, and why?
  • Do they describe what else they can feel, hear, see, smell, and taste?
  • Do they include any memories of joyful or comforting moments?
  • Do they conclude by explaining what makes the room special?
  • Language
  • Do they use sensory language and varied adjectives?
  • Is their spelling correct?
  • Is their grammar correct?
  • Remind students that they shouldn’t correct spelling and grammar mistakes, but underline them and indicate the error with a code.
  • Suggested code: Sp = spelling, T = tense, WO = word order is wrong, ᴧ = missing word, prep = preposition, WW = wrong word
  • Allow time for students to discuss their mistakes and recommended revisions with their partner. Remind them that identifying and making mistakes is part of the learning process – they will continue improving with practice.
  • Students revise their work based on the feedback received.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4c, 9.4e, 9.4f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Encourage more able students to peer-edit less able students’ work, to offer support, and to carefully explain their mistakes.
  • Students can form small groups and read their descriptions to each other. Encourage listeners to be respectful, close their eyes, and try to visualize the room. After each reading, listeners can ask questions to learn more about their classmate’s favourite room.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have written a description of their favourite room using sensory language. They have peer-edited each other’s work, providing helpful and constructive feedback, before revising their text accordingly.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
