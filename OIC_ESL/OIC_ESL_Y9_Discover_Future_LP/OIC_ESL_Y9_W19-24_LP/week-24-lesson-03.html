Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Display the pictures of chameleons. Ask: What animal is this? Why does it change colour? Elicit ‘chameleon’ and that they can change colour to reflect their mood, to warm up (turning darker absorbs more heat), to cool down, and to communicate.
  • Ask: What other animals change colour? Encourage students to think of animals that change colour for various reasons, such as seasonal change (e.g. snow rabbit).

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Pair up students. Give each pair a set of the vocabulary items: ‘animals’, ‘camouflage’, ‘visible’, ‘predators’, ‘prey’, ‘plants’, ‘disguise’, ‘stripes’, ‘survival strategy’. Pre-teach new vocabulary.
  • Students are going to read a paragraph from an essay called ‘How do animals use colour?’. Tell them to try to predict the content of the paragraph from the title and the vocabulary. Allow them time to discuss with their partner, then form groups of four to share ideas.
  • Hand out a gapped text to each student. Instruct them to scan it quickly to see if their predictions were correct or close to correct. Review as a class.
  • Give each group of four the slips of paper with the sentence parts, underlining the linking phrases which are used to give examples:
  • ‘such as big cats’
  • ‘for example mice’
  • ‘examples include lions’
  • ‘a leopard’s spots are typical of’
  • Still in groups of four, tell students to read the text again and complete the paragraph with the phrases. Remind them to read the words on either side of the gap to help them to understand the context.
  • Bring the class back together. Check and discuss answers in a whole-class feedback.
  • Elicit that the animals in the paragraph use colour for survival (predators and prey). Ask: In what other ways do animals use colour? (e.g. to attract a mate, as a warning sign to other animals, or to communicate)

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3b, 9.3c, 9.3f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Group work allows every student to make a contribution. Form groups in the way you think will benefit students the most. You could mix levels of confidence so that more confident students can support those who are less confident.
  • Complete exs.4–5 in ODF WB 4 pp.70–71.
  • Make a whole-class list of linking phrases to give examples.
  • Students can think of three facts about their class or school, as well as one example to support each fact. They take turns saying their facts and examples in pairs.
  • If students have access to the internet, they can find out facts about an animal that changes colour (e.g. a chameleon or an octopus) and take turns presenting what they find.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have predicted the content of a paragraph using vocabulary items and completed a group task focusing on giving examples in essay writing. They have discussed the reasons why animals change colour.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
