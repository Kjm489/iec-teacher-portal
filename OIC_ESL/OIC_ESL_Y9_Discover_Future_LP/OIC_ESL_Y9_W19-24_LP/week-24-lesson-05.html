1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students that last lesson they wrote an essay plan for a factual essay and considered different planning methods. Elicit the different methods quickly.
  • Then elicit the structure of a factual essay and write it on the board (introduction, main body paragraphs, conclusion).
  • In groups of three, tell students to discuss what needs to be included in each paragraph:
  • Introduction – introduces the topic and explains what you are going to say about it;
  • Main body paragraph(s) – starts with a topic sentence and has one or more supporting sentences, with examples;
  • Conclusion – summarizes the main points from the body paragraph(s) and answers the essay question.
  • Review answers as a class. Add notes to the board under the paragraph headings.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students that in today’s lesson they are going to use their plans to write an essay collaboratively. Give them the essay title: ‘Why is colour important in the natural world?’.
  • Allow students time to review and revise their plans accordingly, in line with the recommended structure of a factual essay.
  • Give each student a blank piece of paper:
  • Tell them to write their name and the essay title at the top of the page.
  • Say: Choose an essay plan and use the notes to write an introduction for your essay. Set a short time limit.
  • Say: Crumple up the paper and throw it somewhere else in the room.
  • Say: Retrieve one of the ‘snowballs’ and smooth out the paper. Read the introduction and continue writing the first main body paragraph. Set a short time limit.
  • Repeat the process of snowballing, retrieving, and continuing the essay until conclusions have been written and the essays are complete.
  • When all essays are complete, return the papers to the original introduction writers. They should read the essay and review it against a writing checklist. (If students created a checklist in the last lesson they could use it now, or they can use ex.11 in ODF WB 4 p.71.)
  • Put students into groups of four. Using the checklists, tell them to identify three things they did well and one thing to improve. Then review common mistakes as a class.
  • Praise students for their concentration and perseverance during the lengthy writing process. Highlight that by working collaboratively they were able to complete the task of writing well-planned essays. Ask: Did you enjoy writing an essay together?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4d, 9.4e, 9.4f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • You could divide the class into smaller groups to complete the snowball exercise, so more able students can work together at their own pace and less confident students can work more slowly with additional teacher support.
  • When the first writer receives their original essay, they could edit it and produce a final draft.
  • Consolidate learning by completing exs.2–4 in ODF WB 4 p.70.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have used their essay plans to write a collaborative factual essay. They have reviewed their essays against a checklist and considered what they did well and what they could improve.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
