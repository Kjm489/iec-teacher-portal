Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Invite students to settle down, take a deep breath to focus their attention, and close their eyes. Tell them they are going to think about their favourite room. This can be in their own home or somewhere else (e.g. a favourite room at school, a public space, a room at the house of a family member, or a room in a holiday home, etc.). With a calming voice, slowly read out the five bullet points in ODF SB 4 p.66 ex.2. On the first read-through, encourage students to quietly reflect on the questions and the senses that they evoke.
  • When you read through a second time, ask students to open their eyes and note down their ideas after each question. Pause at the end of each so they can do this.
  • If they are comfortable, invite a few volunteers to share their ideas about their favourite room.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Divide the class into two halves, A and B. Tell A students to read the description in ODF SB 4 p.66, and B students the description in ODF WB 4 p.60. To begin with, students read their text independently.
  • Draw a five-column table on the board with the headings ‘Feel’, ‘Hear’, ‘See’, ‘Smell’, and ‘Taste’. Instruct students to copy the table down and write examples from the text under the correct headings: e.g. Taste – a delicious cheese sandwich (ODF WB 4 p.60).
  • In AA and BB pairs, students compare their answers.
  • Then form AB pairs. Students take turns summarizing the description to their partner, using their notes to describe the different senses that the writer mentions.
  • Bring the class back together. Elicit why sensory language is used in descriptions – to bring a place to life in the reader’s head.
  • Now ask students to think back to their own favourite room from the introductory activity. How does it make them feel? Ask them to complete another sensory table with their own memories of the room, and why these different senses make them feel comfortable/happy/safe.
  • Hand out dictionaries and thesauruses to support students in selecting descriptive language that accurately describes their chosen space. Explain that the aim of their description is to transport the reader to the room they are describing.
  • Finally, encourage students to think about why their room is special. Refer them back to the conclusions in the two texts to inspire their thinking.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3d, 9.3e, 9.4a.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • For the reading activity, less able students could be supported in an adult-led small group, focusing on just one of the texts.
  • Encourage more confident students to find synonyms for the adjectives in the model texts and create a whole-class list. This will be helpful for the writing task in Week 19, Lesson 5.
  • Invite students to read each other’s plans and offer feedback, highlighting one or two aspects that their partner did really well (e.g. a strong adjective to describe one of the senses), and one or two aspects that they could develop.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have learned how sensory language helps to bring a description to life. They have planned their own description of a favourite room, using the model text(s) as a basis for their own ideas.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
