1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Show or hand out pictures of St Patrick’s Day. Establish if students are familiar with the celebration. Teach them what a ‘shamrock’ is using the pictures as a support.
  • Ask: What do you know about St Patrick’s Day? Where in the world is it celebrated? (Ireland) What colour is it associated with? (green) Invite students to share their ideas.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Encourage students to imagine that they are hosting a St Patrick’s Day party at school. Put them into groups of four (A, B, C, D) and tell them to note down items they will need for the celebrations.
  • Hand out a set of four pictures to each group. Ask them to compare what they can see with their lists. Did they come up with the same or different ideas?
  • Students are going to listen to Ana and Josh talking about their St Patrick’s Day preparations. Tell them to put the pictures in the order they hear them. Play the audio (track 2.02). Allow groups time to discuss their answers, then review as a class (1. cake, 2. balloons, 3. put up decorations, 4. face painting).
  • For the second listening, each group member should listen for who is going to do the different tasks: Student A – who is going to make the cake (Brandon); Student B – who is going to blow up the balloons (Jonah); Student C – who is going to do the face painting (Charlie); Student D – who is going to put up the decorations (Lucy).
  • Students compare their answers in their groups, then check as a class. Ask: Which of these tasks would you most and least like to do? Why?
  • Introduce the structure have/get something done. See ODF SB 4 p.125. Hand out copies of the listening transcript and ask students to find examples of this structure. If they need support, help them with the first example: ‘had a cake made’. Review as a class.
  • In the same groups of four, students continue to discuss preparations for their St Patrick’s Day party. Dictate the following questions: What have you got to get done? What do you have to have made? What do you need to do yourselves? Encourage students to use the target language. Remind them that by repeating the target structure regularly and by explaining it to others they will remember it more easily.
  • Bring the class back together and discuss each other’s parties. Who has the most work to do? Whose party sounds the most fun?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1a, 9.1c, 9.1g, 9.2b.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Provide less able students with the listening transcript at the beginning of the listening activity, or you may wish to provide a gapped text (the transcript with key words and phrases removed) to complete.
  • Challenge confident students to take a leading role by modelling key vocabulary and supporting their peers to use have/get something done correctly.
  • Complete exs.7–8 in ODF WB 4 pp.67–68.
  • In pairs, students can practise asking and answering questions using the structure have/get something done (e.g. Have you ever had a cake made? Have you ever had your face painted?).

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have participated in a jigsaw listening activity. They have used the transcript to identify target language and practised using the language in a group discussion.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
