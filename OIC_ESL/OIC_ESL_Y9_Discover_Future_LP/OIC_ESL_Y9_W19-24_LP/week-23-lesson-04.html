1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Show students pictures of brightly coloured manual toothbrushes. Ask: Do you use a manual or electric toothbrush at home?
  • In small groups, give students a few minutes to think of the advantages and disadvantages of using a manual toothbrush.
  • Write ‘Advantages’ and ‘Disadvantages’ on the board. Invite a volunteer from each group to come to the front of the class and write their ideas under these headings.
  • In a whole-class feedback, encourage students to give reasons to support their ideas. If the environmental impact of using a manual toothbrush is not mentioned, elicit it now along with two or three examples.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students they are going to explore the environmental impact of manual toothbrushes. First, they must estimate some key statistics on the topic. Dictate your prepared infographic questions and check the meaning of any new vocabulary.
  • In pairs, students try to estimate the answers. Elicit ideas from the class, but do not give them the correct answers yet.
  • Write the key statistics from the infographic on the board, plus three additional statistics. Say: Here are the correct answers to the questions. Three are not needed.
  • Form new pairs of students. See if they can work out which statistics answer the questions and which are not needed. Ask: Are any of the statistics close to your earlier guesses?
  • Display the infographic so students can check their predictions first, before discussing the results in small groups.
  • Check the meaning of any unknown vocabulary before reviewing answers as a class.
  • As a class, discuss: Which statistics were the most or least surprising? Encourage students to reflect on how the items we use every day can impact the environment.
  • Introduce a debate. Divide the class in half. One half must argue for the continued manufacture and use of manual toothbrushes, while the other half must argue against it. Allow students time to prepare their arguments before debating in groups of six (three for and three against the use of manual toothbrushes). This activity will help to consolidate their understanding of the infographic, provide an opportunity to practise giving reasons and examples to support their ideas, and encourage them to consider both sides of an argument and how their own actions might impact the environment.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2e, 9.2g, 9.3b, 9.3d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • To support less able students, write the infographic questions on the board.
  • More able students can explain the meaning of new vocabulary to less able students.
  • Encourage more confident students to lead the debate activity and elicit ideas from their peers.
  • Students can write a persuasive letter to a toothbrush manufacturer, explaining the impact that manual toothbrushes have on the environment and making suggestions for change.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have considered the environmental impact of manual toothbrushes. They have predicted statistics in an infographic and debated the continued manufacture and use of manual toothbrushes.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
