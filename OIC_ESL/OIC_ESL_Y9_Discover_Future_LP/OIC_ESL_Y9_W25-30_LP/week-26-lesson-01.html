1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students that last lesson they read a blog post about IJburg, Amsterdam. Elicit what they can remember. Then ask: Do you know any other floating villages, towns, or cities? Elicit one or two examples (e.g. Venice).
  • Divide the class into small groups. Tell half the groups to mind map the advantages of living in a floating community, and the other half to mind map the disadvantages.
  • Invite groups to share their lists and have a brief discussion. Hold a whole-class vote on: Does living in a floating community have more advantages or disadvantages?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Pair up students (AB) and tell them they are going to research a floating community from around the world. Put the slips of paper at the front of the class. Invite one student from each pair to come up and select a community.
  • Tell pairs to research their chosen floating community online. Remind them to narrow down their search engine results by using suitable key words, and to assess the reliability of the websites they use. In their notes, they should include:
  • its name and location,
  • how big or small it is,
  • two interesting facts about it.
  • Give students enough time to complete their research. When they are ready, instruct them to form two lines of chairs with pairs facing each other (i.e. a line of A students facing a line of B students). Tell Student A to sit down. Tell Student B to move one place to the left to form new AB pairs.
  • Ask Student A to present their community, using their notes, to Student B. Student B must listen and make notes about what they hear. Set a timer for 30 seconds.
  • When the timer sounds, Student B moves one place to the left. Repeat the above point.
  • At the halfway point, encourage Student A to put their notes down and to try to present their research from memory. Explain that they are in the stretch zone and this will help to consolidate their understanding of their learning. Repeat the process until the original AB pair are facing each other.
  • In the original AB pair, ask Student B to present a summary of all the communities they have learned about to Student A. Challenge them to present the key points they remember without notes, reminding them that this is a stretch zone activity. Afterwards, they can check their notes to see if they missed anything. Student A must listen and make notes about what they hear.
  • At the end of the speaking activity, praise students for their perseverance – it wasn’t easy and they likely didn’t remember all the points, but they embraced the challenge.
  • As a class, discuss which floating community students would most like to live in.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1f, 9.2a, 9.2b, 9.2f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Instead of Student A presenting their community multiple times while Student B takes notes and presents the final summary at the end, you could alternate roles each time students change pairs to take the initial pressure off Student A.
  • You could pre-prepare a list of suitable websites to assist students in the research task.
  • Complete exs.6–7 in ODF SB 4 p.85.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have researched a floating community in pairs. They have presented their findings in a speed dating-style speaking activity, using their notes and their memory.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
