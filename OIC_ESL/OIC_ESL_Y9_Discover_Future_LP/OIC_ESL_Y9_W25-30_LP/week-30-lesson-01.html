Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • To introduce the idea of what makes things go faster, display pictures of two 100-metre world record holders along with their times. Ask: Are people faster and stronger now than in the past, or are they just better prepared? Put students into small groups to discuss.
  • Invite groups to share their ideas, encouraging them to give reasons and examples.
  • Ask: What other sports or activities have become faster? Elicit answers and possible reasons (e.g. new techniques in swimming, new equipment to change tyres in Formula 1).

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Tell students they are going to read a blog with the title ‘Greenpower Car Club’. Ask: What do you think ‘Greenpower’ suggests?
  • Display the car diagram with the labels gapped. Write the missing words on the board – ‘battery’, ‘motor’, ‘seat’, ‘steering wheel’, ‘tyre’ (US Eng. ‘tire’), ‘wheel’. Put students into pairs to complete the labels. Check answers as a whole class.
  • Put pairs together to make groups of four. Instruct them to use the picture and the title to predict what the blog will be about. Invite groups to share their predictions. Hand out the gapped texts and ask students to quickly skim-read. Ask: Were your predictions correct?
  • In the same groups, tell students to read the text again. Ask: What things made the bloggers’ car go faster than their competitors’? Encourage students to summarize their findings in their own words. This will help you to check their understanding of new vocabulary. Allow students to find and record the meaning of new vocabulary as needed.
  • Focus students’ attention on reduced relative clauses (see ODF SB 4 p.129 for grammar explanation). Then dictate the four sentence parts that were removed from the text (i.e. ‘chosen’, ‘needed’, ‘driving on’, ‘competing’). Tell students to complete the blog with the missing words, then to compare their answers in groups. Check answers as a class.
  • As students are working, write on the board:
  • The students who were waiting to race looked anxious. [A: The students waiting to race looked anxious.]
  • The students who we know trained the hardest ran faster. [cannot reduce]
  • Students can compete in the 100-metre race, which is held on Sport’s Day. [A: Students can compete in the 100-metre race held on Sport’s Day].
  • Teachers are thankful to the students who help organize the race. [A: Teachers are thankful to the students organizing the race.]
  • Tell students to work in pairs to reduce the relative clauses, if possible. For those that cannot be reduced, they should discuss why not. Check answers as a class.
  • Challenge pairs to write three reduced relative clause sentences using information from the text. Then give them time to compare their sentences with another pair, encouraging peer-correction. Remind them of the benefits of learning together.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3a, 9.4d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Challenge more able students to explain to less confident students what relative clauses and relative pronouns are, including when and how they are used.
  • Complete ex.1 in ODF SB 4 p.129.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have learned key vocabulary for parts of a car. They have predicted the content of a blog post, then read the text for gist and detail. They have identified examples of reduced relative clauses, then practised writing their own.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
