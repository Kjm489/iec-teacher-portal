1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Dictate the following questions for the class to note down, or write them on the board:
  • Do you leave the water running while you brush your teeth?
  • Are your clothes washed after every time you wear them?
  • Do you take baths or showers?
  • Are your plants or garden watered with drinking water?
  • Give students a few minutes to discuss their responses in pairs, then invite volunteers to share their ideas.
  • Introduce the idea of ‘conserving’ (saving) water. Ask: How can we conserve instead of waste water? Encourage students to refer back to the questions and suggest ways water consumption could be reduced (e.g. by turning the tap off while brushing their teeth).

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Introduce the term ‘water footprint’ – a measure of how much water we use as individuals. Students might relate this to the term ‘carbon footprint’. Ask: Why is it important to reduce our water footprint? Write suggestions on the board. Highlight that this is a global issue, but what we do at a local level will contribute to the overall impact.
  • Tell students they are going to listen to a podcast about water consumption. Put them into pairs to predict what the presenter will talk about.
  • While students are discussing their ideas, write the following list on the board: ‘light bulb’, ‘washing machine’, ‘smartphone’, ‘burger’, ‘laptop’, ‘coffee’, ‘cow’s milk’, and ‘salmon’. Ask: Which of these words do you think the presenter will mention? Ask them to write down their predictions.
  • Play the audio (see ODF SB 4 p.87, track 2.05). Tell students to listen carefully and to tick the items they hear. Give them time to compare their answers in pairs, then check answers as a class (‘laptop’ and ‘salmon’ are not mentioned).
  • Hand out the gapped transcripts. Play the audio a second time. Ask students to complete the gaps with the information they hear. Give them time to compare their answers in pairs first, before checking them as a class.
  • Discuss as a class which piece of information they found most surprising.
  • In pairs or individually, encourage students to write down three personal goals for reducing their water footprint (e.g. eating less red meat).

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1a, 9.1d, 9.1e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • To support less confident students, pre-teach complex topic-based vocabulary from the listening activity before playing the audio. You may wish to gap out these words instead of the numerical data, depending on the ability level of your class.
  • Pause the audio after every point the presenter makes to check students’ understanding. Encourage more able students to explain each issue presented to their less able peers.
  • Complete ex.9 in ODF WB 4 p.78.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have listened to a podcast for specific information about wasting and saving water. They have considered their personal consumption of water.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
