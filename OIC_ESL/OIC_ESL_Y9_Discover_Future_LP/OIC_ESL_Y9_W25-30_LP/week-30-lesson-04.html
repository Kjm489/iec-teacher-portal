1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Ask students to stand up. Tell those who don’t ride a bicycle to sit down. Then ask those who cycle only once a week to sit down. Then, twice a week. Continue increasing the time until you discover who cycles the most in your class. Encourage them to explain what they enjoy about cycling.
  • Put students into groups to discuss what they like/dislike about cycling. If students are from different countries, encourage them to compare national attitudes to cycling.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Display the picture of a bicycle. In the same groups, challenge students to name the different parts in English (‘brakes’, ‘chain’, ‘gears’, ‘handlebars’, ‘pedals’, ‘saddle’, ‘spoke’, and ‘tyre’). It is unlikely they will know this technical vocabulary, so explain that they can use ‘circumlocution’ to help their classmates understand what they mean. For example: A: It’s the thing that you sit on. B: You mean the pedal? C: No, I think it’s the saddle!
  • Check answers as a whole class. Then invite volunteers to share how they described words they didn’t know in English in a different way. Praise them for the strategies used.
  • Put students into pairs (AB). Ask pairs to put their chairs back to back. Give Text A to Student A and Text B to Student B. Here is an example which you can use:
  • Text A: ___ close behind another rider. As the ___ in front of you pedals himself forward, the ___ spins behind him and ___ you with him. The rider ___ can also shelter you from the wind.
  • Text B: Ride close behind another ___. As the rider in front of you ___ himself forward, the air ___ behind him and pulls you with him. The rider ahead can also ___ you from the ___.
  • Students A and B have different words gapped. They need to help each other to complete the text, taking it in turns to read their version. The listening student fills in the missing words. They cannot turn to face each other, nor share their texts. They therefore need to speak clearly and confidently, paying attention to correct pronunciation. They can also use spelling and circumlocution to help their partner to understand the meaning (e.g. ‘“rider” is spelled with –er because it is a person’).
  • After a set time limit, allow pairs to compare their notes and their texts, making corrections where necessary.
  • Allow students time to reflect on their performance. Did they speak clearly and confidently? Did they allow nerves to get the better of them? How did circumlocution strategies help them?

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1b, 9.2c, 9.2d.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Give less able students a text with fewer gaps.
  • Encourage less confident students to focus on clear pronunciation rather than fluency.
  • Challenge more confident students to take a leading role in the back-to-back activity, offering encouragement and support to their less confident peers. Invite them to model good circumlocution strategies.
  • Complete ex.8 in ODF SB 4 p.97.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have developed circumlocution strategies when naming different parts of a bicycle. They have completed a gapped text in a back-to-back reading activity, continuing to use circumlocution where necessary.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
