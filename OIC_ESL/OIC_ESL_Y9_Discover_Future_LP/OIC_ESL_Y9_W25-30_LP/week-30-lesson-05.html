1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students that last lesson they focused on bicycles, including the different parts of a bicycle and tips for competing in a bike race. Elicit from the class any key words that they can remember. Write them on the board.
  • Reinforce that cycling is much more environmentally friendly than travelling by car. What’s more, it is great exercise. However, it is important to know how to stay safe on roads.
  • Ask: In what ways can cyclists stay safe on busy roads? Put students in pairs to discuss their ideas, before inviting volunteers to write them on the board.
  • If not included, add: use separate cycle lanes (‘bike path’ used in listening exercise, but ‘cycle lane’ used in UK Eng.), wear a helmet, obey road rules, always stop at red lights, and wear high-visibility clothing.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Ask pairs to discuss the following questions. Encourage them to think of reasons to support their thinking, as they are going to debate with another pair afterwards:
  • Are speed limits too slow?
  • Do speed cameras encourage drivers to obey the speed limit?
  • How effective is wearing high-visibility clothing?
  • Are speed bumps useful?
  • Form groups made up of two pairs. Ask them to discuss and debate each question, showing agreement and disagreement in a respectful way.
  • Tell students they are now going to listen to a radio show about a road safety campaign (track 2.09; see ODF SB 4 p.99 ex.4). On the first listen, ask them to write down how the interviewees Alice and Omar met. Give students time to discuss their answers in pairs first, before sharing ideas as a whole class.
  • On the second listen, ask students to take notes of the key points. This is an important life skill – the ability to identify relevant information and condense it into note form. They shouldn’t write down every single word, but focus on the main nouns and verbs. Give students time to discuss their answers in pairs first, before sharing ideas as a whole class.
  • On the third listen, ask students to note down the response to the following question: How did Alice and Omar feel about what happened?
  • Take answers from the class (e.g. Alice felt guilty/ashamed – she hit Omar and often went over the speed limit when late; Omar felt he was mostly to blame – his phone distracted him; they now feel motivated to learn from their experience and make a difference).
  • Ask: Have your opinions on speed limits, speed cameras, and speed bumps changed after listening to the radio show? Why / Why not? Discuss as a class.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1a, 9.1c, 9.1f, 9.2e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Provide the listening transcript for less able students to follow as they listen.
  • Consider pre-teaching vocabulary, as necessary, as part of the introductory activity.
  • Encourage pairs to discuss the reflection questions in ex.9 in ODF SB 4 p.99.
  • Then ask pairs to find information about successful road safety campaigns in your local area. Tell them to take notes and present their findings to another pair or the class.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have discussed different road safety measures. They have listened to a radio show on the topic, then considered whether their opinion on road safety measures has changed.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
