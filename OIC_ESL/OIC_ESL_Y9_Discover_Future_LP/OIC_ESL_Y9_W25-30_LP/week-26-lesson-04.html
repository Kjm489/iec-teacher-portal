Year 9 · Week 26 Lesson 4 · Planning a cause and effect essay

Original Oxford outcome(s): 9.4a, 9.4b, 9.4c

Section indexYear 9 libraryPrevious lessonNext lesson
Individual Oxford lesson
Week / lesson

26 / 4

Duration

90 minutes (2 × 45)

Oxford learning outcomes

9.4a, 9.4b, 9.4c

Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Hand out the pre-prepared sentence halves (one half for each student). Tell students to mingle and find the classmate who has the other half of their sentence.
  • Give students enough time to complete the activity, then go through the answers.
  • Check students’ understanding of any difficult vocabulary in the sentences (e.g. ‘drain’, ‘evaporate’, or ‘reservoir’). Invite volunteers to explain their meaning.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Write ‘CAUSE’ and ‘EFFECT’ on the board and one sentence from the introductory activity.
  • In pairs, ask students to decide which part of the sentence is the cause and which the effect. Invite volunteers to write the sentence parts under the correct headings. Elicit which word or phrase is used to show cause or effect and circle it.
  • Encourage pairs to think of other words or phrases that show cause and effect (e.g. ‘lead to’, ‘make’, ‘result in’, and ‘contribute to’).
  • Divide the class into half, asking some pairs to turn to ‘The causes and effects of coastal erosion’ (see ODF WB 4 p.80); and the remaining pairs to turn to ‘The causes and effects of floods’ (ODF SB 4 p.88). Tell them to scan their text quickly to find the causes and effects. Set a time limit of five minutes.
  • Check answers as a class. Briefly talk about what students learned from their respective articles. Explain difficult or unfamiliar vocabulary.
  • Dictate the essay title: ‘How can our actions affect water?’. Elicit that ‘our actions’ is the cause. In small groups, encourage students to mind map the causes and effects in response to the essay title.
  • Share ideas as a whole class. Ask: What is a good way to structure a cause and effect essay? Ensure that students mention 1. the introduction, 2. a paragraph for causes, 3. a paragraph for effects, 4. the conclusion. Elicit the purpose of a ‘topic sentence’. (Refer to the model texts as you go through each feature.)
  • Give each group a sheet of flipchart paper to write a plan for their cause and effect essay, using their favourite causes and effects from their list. Encourage them to work together to decide which causes and effects they should use/will be the most engaging.
  • Display the plans around the classroom. Invite groups to present their plan in turn, outlining briefly what they will include in their introduction, main body paragraphs, and conclusion. Keep the flipchart plans for the next lesson.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.4a, 9.4b, 9.4c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Give less confident students the SB text where the cause and effect verbs have already been highlighted.
  • Challenge more confident students to include complex topic vocabulary learned in the unit in their essay plans.
  • Complete exs.3–4 in ODF WB 4 p.80 and/or ex.2 in ODF SB 4 p.89.
  • Encourage students to reflect on how well they worked together in their group when planning their essay. What did they do when they did not agree?

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have related their learning of cause and effect to how their actions affect water. They have used their ideas to plan a cause and effect essay in groups.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
