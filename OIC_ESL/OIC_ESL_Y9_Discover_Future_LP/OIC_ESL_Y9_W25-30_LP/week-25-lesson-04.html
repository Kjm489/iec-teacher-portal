Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Put students into groups of four. Blindfold one member from each group and tell them to stand at the back of the class. Instruct the other members of the group to shout directions to guide their blindfolded classmate to the front of the class.
  • Bring the class back together and discuss the experience. Ask: What obstacles did you face? (e.g. chair, desk, wall)
  • Draw students’ attention to the fact that there are hidden dangers, even in a familiar place like a classroom.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Display your chosen photo of a waterway. Say: Imagine it’s a beautiful sunny day during the holidays. Would you like to spend time and swim here? The water looks clean. Do you think it’s safe to drink? Elicit one or two answers from the class. Remind students to explain their reasoning.
  • Put students into groups of three and give each group a large piece of paper. Remind them of the ‘hidden dangers’ in the introductory activity.
  • Refer them back to the photo and tell them they have one minute to think of as many hidden dangers as they can. To start students off, introduce the word ‘hypothermia’ (i.e. a dangerous drop in body temperature caused, for example, by being in freezing water).
  • After a minute, tell groups to pass their paper to another group. Give students another minute to add their ideas before passing the paper to a third group. Continue until the paper has returned to its original group.
  • Invite representatives to write items from their lists on the board. Encourage students to categorize the dangers under headings (e.g. Dangers underwater: strong currents, hidden objects, a sudden drop-off; Dangers at the water’s edge: fishing rods, runners, cyclists).
  • Form new groups. Hand out coloured pens and paper. Ask groups to work together to create an attractive poster highlighting the hidden dangers of water. The aim is to encourage other students in school to avoid these dangers.
  • Collect the posters and display them around the classroom. Invite each group to present their poster orally to the class, using topic vocabulary.
  • Praise students for their creative efforts and teamwork.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.2f, 9.2g, 9.4b, 9.4f.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • The group work allows every student to make a contribution. Form groups in the way you think will benefit them the most. You could mix levels of confidence so that more confident students support those who are less confident.
  • Dictate the following questions to debate: Would you jump off a bridge because your friend did? Would you wade into a river to get your ball back? Would you swim across a river because of a dare? If you were really thirsty, would you drink river water that looked clean?
  • Provide bowls of warm and freezing cold water. Ask students to plunge their hand into warm water, then plunge it into freezing cold water for as long as they can. Discuss: How did it feel? How long could you keep your hand in freezing water?

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have considered the hidden dangers of water. They have worked in groups to create a poster on the topic, then presented it to the class.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
