Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Check that students know what the phrase ‘queue up for something’ (stand in a line) means. Ask: What do you normally queue up for? Students mind map their ideas in pairs.
  • Invite pairs to write their ideas on the board to create a whole-class list. If students did not mention water, introduce it now.
  • Encourage students to reflect on whether they take the immediate availability of clean water for granted.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Write the heading ‘The cost of water’ on the board. Divide students into groups of five. Ask them to discuss in their groups what the costs of providing clean drinking water are.
  • Explain that they are going to read an article with this heading. Based on their discussion, can they work together to predict what the text will be about?
  • Invite each group to present their ideas to the class, giving reasons to support their ideas. For example: We think the text will be about … and we think it will include … because … Encourage listening groups to comment, agree, or disagree.
  • Refer students to the text ‘The cost of water’ in ODF SB 4 p.83. Tell them to skim through the article (read quickly) and check how well they had predicted the content.
  • Give half the groups the first three paragraphs of the text to read, and the other half the final three paragraphs.
  • Ask each group to work together to write three questions based on their understanding of their part of the text. Encourage them to focus on factual information.
  • Instruct groups to swap their questions with each other. Challenge them to find the answers as quickly as they can.
  • Bring the class back together. Hold a class debate about what, if anything, should happen to gangs who steal and sell water illegally.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.3b, 9.3d, 9.3e.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Group work allows more able students to support less able students during the discussion and reading activities.
  • You might need to explain difficult concepts or vocabulary, such as ‘illegal gangs’ and ‘slums’ to less able students, or challenge more able students to give definitions in their own words.
  • To stretch less confident students, encourage them to feed back their group’s ideas to the rest of the class. They can use notes if they prefer.
  • Students can research the advantages and disadvantages of ‘cloud-catching’ and prepare a presention or a fact file on the topic.
  • In groups, encourage students to discuss how their lives would be affected if they had to walk several miles to collect water from a well before or after school every day.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have predicted the content of a reading text by thinking critically about its heading. They have learned about how communities are working together to provide drinking water. They have created questions on the text for their peers to answer.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
