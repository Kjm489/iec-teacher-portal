Source integrity: Oxford activities are reproduced below in source order; IEC scaffolds are labeled separately.

1. Warm-up & activate prior knowledge · 8 min

  • Remind students of the listening activity from the previous lesson in which they learned about how to look after the world’s water supply.
  • Give students two to three minutes to discuss what they can remember in small groups.
  • Share ideas as a whole class, writing key points on the board.
  • Invite volunteers to respond to the questions: What have you done to save water at home or at school since learning about its impact? What is your role as a ‘global citizen’?

ICQ (IEC scaffold): What is the first step and how will you respond?

2. Teacher modeling — I DO · 12 min

  • Teacher demonstrates one example from the Oxford main activity using its original instructions.

Teacher move (IEC scaffold): Think aloud through the first relevant example before asking students to try.

3. Guided practice — WE DO · 15 min

  • Introduce the idea of stressing words for emphasis. Ask: What is word stress? Why is word stress important? Elicit ideas from the class.
  • Offer an example by saying the same sentence three times with different word stress.
  • I want a glass of water.
  • I want a glass of water.
  • I want a glass of water.
  • Dictate or write on the board:
  • In which sentence have I been given a bottle of water? (second)
  • In which sentence have I been given a glass of milk? (third)
  • In which sentence was someone else given a glass of water? (first)
  • Say the sentences again before eliciting the answers. For further practice, play the listening exercise again and ask students to complete ex.6 in ODF SB 4 p.87 in pairs. Go through the answers as a whole class, eliciting students’ observations. Establish that word stress in a presentation/monologue can help to engage the audience.
  • Ask students to look again at the listening transcript. Give them a few minutes to remind themselves of the facts and figures shared.
  • Form small groups of three to four students. Explain that they are going to work together to produce an infographic about reducing our water usage. They can use some of the facts and figures from the listening activity to help them to make their points.
  • Quickly talk about the purpose and layout of an infographic, and why it is effective.
  • Write the following sentence stems on the board for students to use: ‘You should/shouldn’t …’; ‘Try not to …’; ‘It’s important to avoid …’; ‘It’s best (not) to …’; ‘Why don’t you …?’. Ask them to make five sentences in their groups to add to their infographic.
  • Give students time to make their infographic. Remind them that tasks should be equally distributed between group members and that they should offer support to each other where required. When it is complete, encourage them to practise presenting their infographic to each other, paying attention to word stress for emphasis.
  • Encourage students to sensitively peer-correct each other’s attempts at word stress, highlighting mistakes and modelling where the stress should be applied.

CCQ (IEC scaffold): Which detail in the Oxford text or task supports that answer?

4. Supported pair/group practice · 10 min

  • Rehearse a response to the Oxford main activity with a partner, taking turns and using a sentence frame where helpful.

ICQ (IEC scaffold): Will each partner explain their reasoning?

5. Independent practice — YOU DO · 25 min

  • Individually complete a response or product demonstrating the original learning outcomes: 9.1c, 9.1g, 9.2c.

Evidence (IEC scaffold): Collect a student-generated response rather than a copied group answer.

6. Extension & differentiation · 10 min

  • Monitor and support less confident students by modelling and drilling examples of word stress. Invite more confident students to offer their own examples.
  • Hold a short debate about the best way to save water. Encourage students to respectfully agree and disagree with each other, using appropriate word stress.

7. Review, assessment & exit ticket · 10 min

  • Students have understood how to stress particular words in a sentence for emphasis. They have produced an infographic about saving water.

Exit ticket (IEC scaffold): Provide one independent example of what you learned.
