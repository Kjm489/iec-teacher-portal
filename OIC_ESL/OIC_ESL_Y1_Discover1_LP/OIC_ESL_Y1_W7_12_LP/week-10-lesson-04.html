Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “A farm story” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Elicit from children some farm animals. Check that all the animals on the vocabulary list are covered, and pre-teach any remaining ones. Children stand in a line and face you. If you say an animal that you normally find on a farm, they jump to the left; if you say an animal that you normally have as a pet, they jump to the right. If they think it’s both, they stay in the middle. Tell them that for some animals, there are no 100% correct answers, as many animals can be both!

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The farmer” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

You can use the text ‘The farmer’s in the barn’ – if you would like to turn it into a song, you can search online for the melody to ‘The farmer in the dell’. Alternatively, source a different reading text about farm animals. Read the text first as a class, and then independently or in pairs. Praise children for their work so far. Encourage children who are less confident to participate and help them to feel supported. Ask children about the colours of the animals: What colour are chickens? What colour are cows? Elicit the answer: They are = They’re … brown. Drill as a class. Now say the colour of an animal, and children have to guess: They’re black and white. Children will say: They’re cows! Drill the question and the answer. Set up a pairwork activity. One child says the colour of the animal and the other says what the animal is. Allow five minutes. Say: Your stories take place on a farm with farm animals. Now you’re going to write a short farm story. Think of some ideas and work with your classmate.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The farmer in the dell” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “A farm story” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “A farm story” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “A farm story” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The farmer” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Create a mind map of some story ideas together on the board: an animal gets lost, a new animal joins the farm, etc. In pairs, children write their short stories (only a few sentences). Monitor actively, checking spelling and punctuation. Allow children to illustrate their stories. Praise children for writing their own stories. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “A farm story” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The farmer in the dell” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “A farm story” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have read a text and written a short story about farm animals. Differentiation To support less able children, provide a gapped text for the written work. Put children in small groups to work together. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The farmer” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
