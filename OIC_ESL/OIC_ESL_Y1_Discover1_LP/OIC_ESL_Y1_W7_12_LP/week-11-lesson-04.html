Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “Animal pictures” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Start to spell out the name of an animal. If someone thinks they know the animal, they say the next letter (not the animal). Children should raise their hands, not call out. Alternatively, say an animal habitat and children tell you some animals that live there.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “in” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Draw a tree on the board and take one of your animal flashcards. Put the animal ‘in’ the tree. Elicit: The animal is in the tree. Continue with other prepositions (‘behind’, ‘on’, and ‘under’). You can be creative with the locations you use to illustrate the prepositions! Add another animal and elicit the plural form: They’re in the tree. See if children notice the difference between ‘they’ and ‘it’. Ask: When do we use ‘they’? When do we use ‘it’? Elicit the question: Where is/are the …? and the answers. Drill. Then write on the board: ‘Where is the eagle? Where are the eagles?’ Ask children to tell you when we use ‘is’ and when we use ‘are’. (For one eagle, we use ‘is’; for more than one eagle, we use ‘are’.) Go to Oxford Discover SB 1 and complete p.53 ex.D. Put children into pairs. They ask and answer about the animals on the coursebook page using Where is/are the …? and the prepositions. Monitor actively and help where necessary.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “behind” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Animal pictures” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “on” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Animal pictures” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “under” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Hold a whole-class feedback session and praise children. Ask children to secretly add another animal to the picture. They tell their partner the animal, but not where it is hidden. Their partner has to guess using: Is the [name of animal] in the tree? Behind the tree? Under the tree? Hold a whole-class feedback session and praise children. Sing the song in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.53 ex.C. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Animal pictures” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “they” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “it” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have talked about a picture(s) with animals in different places. They have practised using prepositions to describe position. Differentiation To support less able children, they can focus on the preposition only, rather than the whole sentence. Pair them with a more able child. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “in” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
