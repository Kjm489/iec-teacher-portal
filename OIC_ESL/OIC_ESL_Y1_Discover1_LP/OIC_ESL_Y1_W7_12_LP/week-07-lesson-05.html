Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “Having fun with friends” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Pre-teach ‘share’ and ‘have fun’. Play a TPR (Total Physical Response) game with children. Agree an action for ‘share’: e.g. in pairs, they both hold a book; and for ‘have fun’: e.g. they dance together in pairs holding hands. Read the following sentences: My family and I share the same interests. We have fun together. We go to the beach and look for shells. At the beach we have fun. My brother and I love playing ball together. We share the ball. When children hear the words ‘share’ and ‘have fun’, they do the actions.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Show children the enlarged frame from your story. Ask them to tell you what they can see. Say: This is part of a story. What do you think happens in the story? Pre-teach/elicit: ‘friend’, ‘brother’, and ‘go places’. Think-Pair-Share: Children compare their story ideas. Children read the stories in their books and check their ideas. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.24. Were their ideas correct? Ask children if they liked the story. Read the story to the class. They follow along in their books.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: When friends have fun together, are they enjoying the activity or feeling bored?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Having fun with friends” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: Can two friends share one game? What would they do?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Having fun with friends” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Children read the story aloud to each other in pairs. Check that they know who is going to read each part before they start. Both stories are about friendship. Ask children what is important in a friend. Elicit ‘sharing’ and ‘having fun’. Ask children to think of a title for the story in their books. Children write out their favourite word from the story. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Having fun with friends” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: When friends have fun together, are they enjoying the activity or feeling bored?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: Can two friends share one game? What would they do?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have predicted the topic and then confirmed their ideas by reading a cartoon story about friends. They have reflected on the qualities of a good friend. Differentiation To support less able children, do a matching activity first (picture to word) with the key words – ‘lizard’, ‘small’, ‘go places’, and ‘friend’. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

Model → Guide → Practise

1. Warm-up & Objective Discussion · 10 min

  1. Pre-teach ‘share’ and ‘have fun’.
  2. Play a TPR (Total Physical Response) game with children. Agree an action for ‘share’: e.g. in pairs, they both hold a book; and for ‘have fun’: e.g. they dance together in pairs holding hands.
  3. Read the following sentences: My family and I share the same interests. We have fun together. We go to the beach and look for shells. At the beach we have fun. My brother and I love playing ball together. We share the ball. When children hear the words ‘share’ and ‘have fun’, they do the actions.

Scaffolding & checks

Use picture cues, gestures and a model answer. ICQ: “Do we listen first or start?” CCQ: “Which picture shows our topic?”

2. Instruct & Model · 20 min

  1. Show children the enlarged frame from your story. Ask them to tell you what they can see.
  2. Say: This is part of a story. What do you think happens in the story?
  3. Pre-teach/elicit: ‘friend’, ‘brother’, and ‘go places’.
  4. Think-Pair-Share: Children compare their story ideas.
  5. Children read the stories in their books and check their ideas. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.24. Were their ideas correct?
  6. Ask children if they liked the story.
  7. Read the story to the class. They follow along in their books.

TESOL approach

I do → We do → You do. Pause to check meaning with pointing, choosing, acting or explaining.

3. Guided Practice · 15 min

  1. Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class.
  2. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Having fun with friends” using visual prompts.
  3. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

Communicate → Assess

4. Independent Practice & Communication · 30 min

  1. Children read the story aloud to each other in pairs. Check that they know who is going to read each part before they start.
  2. Both stories are about friendship. Ask children what is important in a friend. Elicit ‘sharing’ and ‘having fun’.
  3. Ask children to think of a title for the story in their books.
  4. Children write out their favourite word from the story.
  5. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Having fun with friends” during play.

5. Assessment & Closure · 15 min

  1. Children have predicted the topic and then confirmed their ideas by reading a cartoon story about friends. They have reflected on the qualities of a good friend.
  2. Differentiation
  3. To support less able children, do a matching activity first (picture to word) with the key words – ‘lizard’, ‘small’, ‘go places’, and ‘friend’.
  4. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated.
  5. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

1. Gallery Walk

Show or describe a having fun with friends picture. Children identify it, then say a word or short sentence. Give a model before they take turns.

2. Dice and Describe

Pairs take turns asking and answering about having fun with friends using picture or word cards. Switch partners after two turns.

3. Partner Puzzle

Set out four labelled or pictured choices related to having fun with friends. Say a clue; children move or point to the matching choice and explain.

4. Target Toss

Hide a topic picture or object. Give three clues; teams guess, check the picture, and repeat the target expression.

5. Action Stations

Give a short instruction linked to having fun with friends. Learners listen, act, and say what they did; let a child lead the next round.

6. True or False Steps

In small groups, match images, words, or actions for having fun with friends. Each child must explain one match before the group finishes.
