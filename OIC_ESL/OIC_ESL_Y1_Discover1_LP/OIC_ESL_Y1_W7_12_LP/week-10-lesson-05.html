Measurable Learning Objectives

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Everyone stands up. Say something about a farm or pet animal, but don’t say the animal: e.g. They’re black and white. Children move to the left if they know the animal, and to the right if they don’t. They must not say what it is yet! Then children who think they know say something else about the animal: e.g. It’s got four legs. Children who think they know move to the left. Continue until (hopefully) everyone thinks they know. Make it fun, and be careful to include all children so that nobody is left out. You will find that some children may be thinking of a different animal, and if the description still applies, that’s fine. Elicit from children that being included in a game feels nice, so it is important to make sure everybody is invited to take part and joins in.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Talking about farm animals” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Tell children that they are going to make their own animal cards. Give instructions, giving your own demonstration with a piece of paper at the same time: Fold your piece of paper/card in half, and then in half again. Unfold the paper. Cut along the folded edges so you have four smaller pieces of paper which are all the same size. Draw four of your favourite farm or pet animals – one animal on each small piece of paper. Assign farm animals to one half of the class and pets to the other half. Revise animal vocabulary before beginning the activity. Put sentences on the board and elicit from children what they can talk about: the colour of their animals, where they live, what they give us, what they can/can’t do, why they like them, etc. Children don’t have to say all of these aspects, but it helps to give them a structure. When everybody has prepared their four cards, demonstrate the activity. Ask a child to select a card (these should be face down). Choose a confident child to say some sentences about the animal they turn over: e.g. They’re black and white. They live in the country. We drink their milk. They can run. They can’t hop. I like them. They have beautiful eyes. Put children in pairs (one farm animal with one pet). Children play with their game cards and talk to each other. They take turns. Hold a whole-class feedback session and reflect on the activity with children.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Talking about farm animals” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Talking about farm animals” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Talking about farm animals” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Talking about farm animals” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Talking about farm animals” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Introduce talking about farm animals with visual examples and student responses. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Talking about farm animals” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Talking about farm animals” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Talking about farm animals” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have made a set of personalized game cards about either pets or farm animals and said some sentences identifying the animals. Differentiation To support less able children, they can say only two sentences. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Talking about farm animals” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Mini Interview

Pairs take turns asking and answering about talking about farm animals using picture or word cards. Switch partners after two turns.

3. Sound and Point

Set out four labelled or pictured choices related to talking about farm animals. Say a clue; children move or point to the matching choice and explain.

4. Clue Card Challenge

Hide a topic picture or object. Give three clues; teams guess, check the picture, and repeat the target expression.

5. Stand Up If

Give a short instruction linked to talking about farm animals. Learners listen, act, and say what they did; let a child lead the next round.

6. Create a Poster

In small groups, match images, words, or actions for talking about farm animals. Each child must explain one match before the group finishes.
