Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)

Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “An animal story” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Show children a picture of the main animal in the story you have chosen. Ask them what it is, and elicit some words and sentences about the animal. Ask: How can humans take care of this animal?

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “kind” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Pre-teach/Elicit the vocabulary: ‘kind’, ‘take care of’, ‘food’, and ‘water’. Tell children: I have a story for you. It’s about … (choose three or four key words from the story you’ve chosen). Put children into small groups to discuss what the story might be about. Show children some pictures from the story and ask them to revise their ideas. Hand out the story sentences and say: This is the story. It’s all mixed up. Can you put it in the correct order? Monitor actively. Read the story to children. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 pp.58–59 and play the audio. Ask children to follow the story in their books. They don’t need to understand every word, but ensure that they understand the gist of the story. You can use the illustrations and children’s native language at first, if necessary.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “take care of” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “An animal story” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “food” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “An animal story” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “water” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Ask children to think about the person’s motivation for helping the animal: Why does X help the (animal)? Ask children to think about why Dian works at the reserve. Ask children if they have ever helped an animal, and if they would like to help animals. Encourage children to think about how they can help animals. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “An animal story” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “An animal story” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “kind” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have read a story in which a person helps an animal and considered their motivations. Differentiation To support less able children, put them in a group with more able children when putting the story in order. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “take care of” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
