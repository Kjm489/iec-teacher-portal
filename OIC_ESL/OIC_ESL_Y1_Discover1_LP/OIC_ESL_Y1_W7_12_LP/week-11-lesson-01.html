LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Animals
Week: 11
Lesson: 1
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.2a, 1.2b

Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “Talking about wild animals” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Show a flashcard or a picture of a wild animal. Ask: Is it a farm animal? Is it a pet? Why not? Elicit that the animal lives ‘in the wild’ and that ‘it is a wild animal’. Ask children to tell you the names of some wild animals that they know. Encourage everyone to participate. Inspire children to name some wild animals from your country. Then challenge them to think of wild animals from different countries – perhaps ones with different habitats or climates. Establish that some countries have very different animals to others.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “in the wild” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Tell children that they are going to watch a video about wild animals. Ask how many wild animals they think they’ll see. Play the video. Play the ‘Big Question 3’ video (Oxford Discover SB 1 pp.46–47). Children watch the video and confirm their predictions. Play it a second time, and stop the video at each animal. Teach/Elicit the name of the animal. Ask children which wild animal from the video they liked best and why. Ask children to think about where wild animals live. Teach/Elicit ‘grass’, ‘rainforest’, and ‘ocean’. Children look at the picture on Oxford Discover SB 1 pp.46–47 and identify the wild animal. They answer the questions and complete the ‘Big Question Chart’ on p.47. Tell children that they are going to learn a new song. Teach some animal noises that correspond to the animals you have: e.g. a lion (‘roar’), a snake (‘sss’), or a frog (‘ribbit’). If you are familiar with the original song, play the tune of ‘The Wheels on the Bus’ and adapt the lyrics using the animal noises practised:

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “it is a wild animal” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Talking about wild animals” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Big Question 3” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Talking about wild animals” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “grass” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

The snakes in the grass go ‘sss, sss, sss’. The whales in the ocean go ‘sinnnnnnngggggg’. The monkeys in the jungle go ‘ooo, ooo, ooo’. Sing the song a few times. Put children into small groups. They think of a wild animal, where it lives, and the noise it makes. They create a new verse. Groups then sing their verse for the class. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Talking about wild animals” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “rainforest” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “ocean” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned the names of some wild animals by watching a video, and sung a song about them. Differentiation To support less able children, give them a gapped text of the song. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “in the wild” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
