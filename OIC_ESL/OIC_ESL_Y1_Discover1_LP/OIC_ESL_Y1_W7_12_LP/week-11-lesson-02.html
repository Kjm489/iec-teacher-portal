Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “Animal homes” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Play a game. Divide your classroom into two areas – land and ocean. Say an animal, and children hop to the place where the animal lives. For example, if you say ‘whales’ children hop to the designated ‘ocean’ area. Ask children to suggest different forms of movement: e.g. jump, walk, skip, and swim.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “whales” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Pre-teach/Elicit the vocabulary words: ‘pond’, ‘field’, ‘woods’, ‘tree’, and ‘ground’ with flashcards or pictures. Show children some animal pictures and ask: Where do rabbits live? Some ideas are provided below; however, please adapt accordingly for animals and their homes in your country (e.g. rainforest, desert, savannah). Rabbits – in the ground Squirrels – in a tree Frogs – in a pond (or in water) Mice – in a field Birds – in the woods

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “ocean” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Animal homes” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “pond” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Animal homes” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “field” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Do the listening activity in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.54 ex.A. Children cover the words, and listen and point. Encourage them to repeat the words. Then do ex.B. Write the animal homes on the board and children copy them down. Assign small groups one animal home, and ask them to create a mind map of more animals that live there. Set a time limit (e.g. ten minutes). Hold a whole-class feedback session to compare and check answers. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Animal homes” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “woods” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “tree” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have created a mind map of different places where animals live. Differentiation To support less able children, give them some animals that they have to put into their mind map. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “whales” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
