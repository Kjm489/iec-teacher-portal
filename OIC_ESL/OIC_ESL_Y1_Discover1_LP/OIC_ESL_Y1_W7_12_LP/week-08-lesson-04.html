Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “Making a game” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Start counting and see if children can join in with you. Play some simple counting games – count backwards, count with only odd numbers or only even numbers, play ‘Bingo’, etc. Children need to be able to count confidently from 1 to 20 for this lesson.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Show your maths symbols flashcards and pre-teach/elicit ‘plus’, ‘minus’, and ‘equals’. Put a sum up on the board (e.g. ‘4 + 2 = 6’) and help children to read it out with you: Four plus two equals six. Using a highlighter or different colour, indicate that ‘4 + 2’ is the ‘problem’ and ‘6’ is the ‘answer’. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 pp.90–91. Ask children to find the following words on the pages: ‘plus’, ‘equals’, ‘answer’, and ‘problem’. Ask them to read out the ‘seashells’ and ‘crabs’ problems on p.91. Draw a racetrack on the board and tell children they are going to make a game. Show children, on the board, how they can add questions, and that some spaces could be ‘Miss a turn’, ‘Go forward one’, or ‘Go back one’. Some spaces could also be purely decorative. Put children into groups of three and hand out the racetrack sheets. Encourage children to either write in pencil or to write in their notebooks first. This is a group activity, and so children need to listen to others’ ideas and share their own ideas. When the games are ready, children play. Hand out the dice or spinners. Set out your own rules for playing (e.g. play quietly, roll the dice carefully, etc.). Remind children about taking turns and introduce/elicit ‘my turn’ and ‘your turn’.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: When friends have fun together, are they enjoying the activity or feeling bored?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Making a game” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: Can two friends share one game? What would they do?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Making a game” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Introduce making a game with visual examples and student responses. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Making a game” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: When friends have fun together, are they enjoying the activity or feeling bored?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: Can two friends share one game? What would they do?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have designed and played a game with numbers. Differentiation To support less able children, encourage them to make sums with numbers 1–10. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

EVIDENCE & TEACHER REFLECTION

Record: independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. What worked? Which vocabulary or skill needs reteaching?

1. Board Race

Show or describe a making a game picture. Children identify it, then say a word or short sentence. Give a model before they take turns.

2. Guess My Card

Pairs take turns asking and answering about making a game using picture or word cards. Switch partners after two turns.

3. Memory Tray

Set out four labelled or pictured choices related to making a game. Say a clue; children move or point to the matching choice and explain.

4. Spin and Speak

Hide a topic picture or object. Give three clues; teams guess, check the picture, and repeat the target expression.

5. Picture Hunt

Give a short instruction linked to making a game. Learners listen, act, and say what they did; let a child lead the next round.

6. Team Charades

In small groups, match images, words, or actions for making a game. Each child must explain one match before the group finishes.
