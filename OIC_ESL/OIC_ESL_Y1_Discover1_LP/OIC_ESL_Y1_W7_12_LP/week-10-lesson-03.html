Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “Animals I like and don’t like” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Children stand up. They think of an animal and keep it secret. Tell them that you are going to say something about animals and, if it’s true for their animal, they sit down: e.g. It’s big. – anyone thinking of an elephant, for example, sits down. This activity works well with colours and features: e.g. It’s brown. It’s got four legs. It hasn’t got legs. Tell children that today’s lesson is mainly sitting down. When you call out Time to stretch!, they stop what they are doing, quietly stand up, and stretch as tall as a giraffe. Afterwards, they sit down and continue their work. Practise a few times.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Animals I like and don” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Ask children: Do you like snakes? Why? Why not? Do you like birds? Why? Why not? Children look at the animals in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.22. Ask them to work in pairs to find out who likes which animals. Elicit the question: Do you like hamsters? Encourage children to explain why: I like hamsters. They are small and soft. Do an example with a confident child first. On the board, write: ‘This is a dog. These are dogs.’ Elicit the differences in the sentences by asking some questions: Which sentence talks about many dogs? Which talks about one? How do you know? Tell children they are going to write a short text about animals they like and don’t like. Write a sentence on the board without any capital letters, and ask children which letters should be in capitals. Tell them that you want them to think about this when they write their sentences. Encourage them to think about one animal or animals in general (singular/plural). Refer to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.23 ex.F. Let children work on their own and at their own pace. During the writing time, ask them to stand up and stretch every five minutes. Encourage children to begin to think about managing their time and tasks. Tell them that it’s important to finish this task during class time. For some, they might only write one or two sentences. Stress that this is OK. Monitor actively.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Animals I like and don” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Animals I like and don’t like” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Animals I like and don” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Animals I like and don’t like” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Animals I like and don” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Hold a whole-class feedback session. Who managed to complete the task? Who managed to concentrate for the whole time? Did they find the stretching helpful? Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Animals I like and don’t like” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Animals I like and don” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Animals I like and don” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have read a text and written sentences about animals they like and don’t like. Differentiation To support less able children, provide a gapped text for them to complete. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Animals I like and don” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

1. Sorting Challenge

Show or describe a animals i like and don’t like picture. Children identify it, then say a word or short sentence. Give a model before they take turns.

2. Secret Word Whisper

Pairs take turns asking and answering about animals i like and don’t like using picture or word cards. Switch partners after two turns.

3. Gallery Walk

Set out four labelled or pictured choices related to animals i like and don’t like. Say a clue; children move or point to the matching choice and explain.

4. Dice and Describe

Hide a topic picture or object. Give three clues; teams guess, check the picture, and repeat the target expression.

5. Partner Puzzle

Give a short instruction linked to animals i like and don’t like. Learners listen, act, and say what they did; let a child lead the next round.

6. Target Toss

In small groups, match images, words, or actions for animals i like and don’t like. Each child must explain one match before the group finishes.
