OIC outcomes: Unit summary

Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “Games I play — reflection and review — Session 1” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used during Weeks 7–8. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, and Writing. Lead a discussion with children about these skills. For example: Tell me an activity when we practised speaking in English. When we [played XX game], what skills did we use? Did we speak? Did we listen? What did you find easy/difficult? Why? Help children to assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children who may find this self-reflective process quite challenging. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Explain that this week, children will participate in one or more extended activities in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas: Children make a class or group book about toys. Each child produces a page. Children need to work together so that no toy is repeated. Hold a quiz created by children about maths problems. In teams, they write (or record) questions: e.g. What is 2 + 4? Award points for correct answers and fast answers. Children make spinners to revise vocabulary and activities from the unit. Alternatively, half the class creates spinners revising Week 7, and the other half of the class focuses on Week 8. Then pair a Week 7 child with a Week 8 child to play together. If you do not have materials to make a spinner, ask children to create wordsnakes. Children make a zigzag book of activities they can and can’t do. Children make up a song about games and toys. This could include a dance or movement routine to encourage physical activity.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: When friends have fun together, are they enjoying the activity or feeling bored?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Games I play — reflection and review — Session 1” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: Can two friends share one game? What would they do?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Games I play — reflection and review — Session 1” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Children make a colour wheel with the three primary and three secondary colours. Give each group of six an A3 sheet of paper with a circle divided into six equal segments. Each group works together to paint it. Encourage them to say the primary and secondary colours out loud. In groups, children make a list of all the fun activities they could do if they had a party in a park. Children write out invitations for the pretend party, and make a list of who they want to invite and what they need to bring. Create a game using the coursebooks. Children can cover up pictures on various pages and ask their group: What’s this? Additional task Encourage children to find and read books about toys and games. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Games I play — reflection and review — Session 1” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: When friends have fun together, are they enjoying the activity or feeling bored?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: Can two friends share one game? What would they do?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have consolidated their knowledge and learning on the topic of ‘Games I play’. Differentiation Encourage children who need more support to answer simple questions with tick/cross cards. Provide language and gapped texts, and put them into groups with more able children. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?
