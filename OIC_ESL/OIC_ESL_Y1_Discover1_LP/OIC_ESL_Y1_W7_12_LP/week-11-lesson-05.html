LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Animals
Week: 11
Lesson: 5
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.4a, 1.4d

Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “An animal fact sheet” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Show children an example of a completed fact sheet. Say: This is a fact sheet. It’s about … Look puzzled and ask: What is it about? Elicit that it’s about an animal. Look at the different sections on your sheet and go through them with children. Appearance – what the animal looks like (say: Look – my animal has a [tail]!). Food – what it eats (say: Look – my animal eats [meat]!). Habitat – where it lives (say: Look – my animal lives in the [wild]! It lives in [Africa]. It’s a [wild] animal.).

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “animal fact sheet children” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Tell children that throughout today’s lesson, you are all going to take some deep breaths together. Take some deep breaths and say: Sometimes we have to stop and breathe to make us feel calm. Do this at appropriate moments throughout the class. Tell children to think of the animal they want to write about. Ask them to make a list of words to use for appearance, habitat, food, and a WOW fact. Let them work individually, in pairs, or in small groups. Look at the animal facts in Oxford Discover SB 1 pp.50–51. Monitor actively by checking the information and helping as necessary. Hand out the fact sheet template to each child and instruct them to copy their information neatly onto the fact sheet. For the fact sheet, search online: ‘animal fact sheet children’. Alternatively, create your own based on the sections listed in the introductory activity. Children check their work (or each other’s work) for neat writing and correct spelling. Children draw a picture of their animal.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “An animal fact sheet” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “An animal fact sheet” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “animal fact sheet children” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “An animal fact sheet” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “An animal fact sheet” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

If you can, display the work and let children comment on each other’s work. Hold a whole-class feedback session to see if they found it easy/difficult and why. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “An animal fact sheet” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “animal fact sheet children” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “An animal fact sheet” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have produced a fact sheet about an animal. Differentiation To support less able children, provide a word pool with the words they need. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “animal fact sheet children” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
