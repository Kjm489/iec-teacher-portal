Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “Trading toys” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Play ‘Tic-tac-toe’ (also known as ‘Noughts and crosses’) with the toy flashcards. Choose nine cards. Stick them to the board face down in a 3x3 grid. Number them. Put children into two groups. Tell one group they are ‘crosses’ (X), and the other group they are ‘noughts’ (O). Ask the ‘crosses’ group which number card they want. Turn it over and show them. If they can name the toy or game on their card, they get a cross for their team. The objective is to get three in a row (horizontally, vertically, or diagonally). If they don’t know the word, you turn the card over again and stick it back on the board in its place.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Take three of the toy flashcards and hand them to a confident child, deliberately not looking at them. This is a dramatic way to show that you don’t know what the cards are. Ask the child: Have you got a robot? Then elicit the answer: Yes, I have. / No, I haven’t. Repeat this a few times so that everyone has the opportunity to ask and answer. Ask children to draw five toys that they have got and five toys that haven’t got, but want. Teach ‘swap’ or ‘trade’ toys. Ask: Why do children swap (trade) toys? Elicit the answer and suggestions for how you could do this. Elicit/Teach: I’ve got a doll, but I want a toy boat. You’ve got a toy boat, but you want a doll. Let’s swap/trade! Children complete Oxford Discover SB 1 p.114 exs.C–D. Demonstrate the activity with a confident child. Show your own list of ‘have got’ toys and ‘want’ toys. Ask a question and try to trade one of your toys with the child. The objective is to trade. Children then carry out this activity. They will need to get up and move around. Point out that they can adjust their wants as they go round. They might see something better! Encourage children to communicate their ideas freely, but to also listen carefully to others.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: When friends have fun together, are they enjoying the activity or feeling bored?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Trading toys” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: Can two friends share one game? What would they do?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “Trading toys” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Children draw a red circle around their original toys when they swap them, and a blue circle around the new toys they get from someone. Monitor closely and ensure children are trading, rather than just saying ‘yes’ or ‘no’. Hold a whole-class feedback session to find out how many children managed to trade all their toys. Perhaps they got toys that were better than they actually wanted! Allow children time to reflect on their emotional reaction to the activity. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “Trading toys” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: When friends have fun together, are they enjoying the activity or feeling bored?

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: Can two friends share one game? What would they do?

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have practised in context the question Have you got? and the answers Yes, I have. and No, I haven’t. Differentiation To support less able children, encourage them to swap just one toy. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: If we share a ball, do we both get a turn or does one person keep it?
