Measurable Learning Objectives

Identify or demonstrate the key ideas in “What’s your favourite animal?” in 3 of 4 supported checks. Use lesson vocabulary in at least two partner or group speaking turns. Complete a topic-related task and share one thing learned.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Tell children that the topic for the next two weeks is animals. Ask: Do you like animals? They will probably say that they do. Ask more questions and encourage them to think about why we like animals. Most children will say that they’re cute. Elicit that there are also animals children perhaps don’t like, and find out why: e.g. they’re scary, dangerous, they can bite, etc.

TESOL scaffolding: Show the first picture or action; ask students to identify the topic. ICQ: Do we start by watching or speaking? CCQ: Which example matches today’s topic?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Make a list of all the animals children know in English. Show children the animal pictures on the flashcards and elicit their names. Drill the different names using the flashcards – you could also play a matching game (matching words to images). Then open books and complete Oxford Discover SB 1 p.22 ex.A. Ask a confident child: Which is your favourite animal? Elicit: My favourite animal is a/an ... Drill the question and the answer. Children think of their favourite animal. They stand up and do a mingle activity to find someone who has the same favourite animal. There might be more than one other child with the same favourite animal, so they can form a group. Then everyone sits back down. By quickly drawing a bar chart on the board, show children how information can be displayed (number of people along the vertical axis / animals on the horizontal axis). Ask for a show of hands. Say: If your favourite animal is a cat, put your hand up. Put the animals and numbers on the board. Do this until everyone has put up their hand. Count the number of children for each animal.

TESOL scaffolding: Model a source example aloud with a picture/gesture before learners respond. ICQ: Do you watch my example first? CCQ: Which action or picture matches the model?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “What’s your favourite animal?” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Complete one original source exercise together; fade from full prompt to picture cue. ICQ: Do we answer together first? CCQ: Which response fits the example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Model a source activity with a volunteer, then repeat with the class. Partners practise the target vocabulary or expression for “What’s your favourite animal?” using visual prompts. Circulate and provide supportive correction; note learners needing extra help.

TESOL scaffolding: Use pairs to rehearse the source target; provide a sentence frame and swap roles. ICQ: Does each partner take a turn? CCQ: Does the answer match the picture or action?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Hand out A3 paper and ask children to put the information into their bar chart. Display charts and check that everyone has the same. Ask children if they like seeing information in this way. Choose one or two of the six optional games below; require children to speak about “What’s your favourite animal?” during play.

TESOL scaffolding: Students complete an original source task individually; record who needs prompting. ICQ: Are you working on your own now? CCQ: Can you show the answer without my model?

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support: picture choices, gestures, partner model, short sentence frames. Challenge: ask learners to add a reason, detail or follow-up question.

TESOL scaffolding: Offer picture/word bank for support; require a reason or extra detail for extension. ICQ: Can you use the word bank if needed? CCQ: Can you explain one extra detail?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned the words for animals and transferred class information about favourite animals into a bar chart. Differentiation To support less able children, put them in a group with more able children when doing the bar chart. Check four learners or groups for understanding; record independent / prompted / not yet demonstrated. Exit task: each learner points, says, draws or demonstrates one target item.

TESOL scaffolding: Use a short independent demonstration or exit response from the source goal. ICQ: Do you show your own answer? CCQ: Which example demonstrates today’s learning?

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

1. Memory Tray

Show or describe a what’s your favourite animal? picture. Children identify it, then say a word or short sentence. Give a model before they take turns.

2. Spin and Speak

Pairs take turns asking and answering about what’s your favourite animal? using picture or word cards. Switch partners after two turns.

3. Picture Hunt

Set out four labelled or pictured choices related to what’s your favourite animal?. Say a clue; children move or point to the matching choice and explain.

4. Team Charades

Hide a topic picture or object. Give three clues; teams guess, check the picture, and repeat the target expression.

5. Draw and Guess

Give a short instruction linked to what’s your favourite animal?. Learners listen, act, and say what they did; let a child lead the next round.

6. Hot Potato Questions

In small groups, match images, words, or actions for what’s your favourite animal?. Each child must explain one match before the group finishes.
