Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.1a, 1.2c) in “How can we make music?” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Play some music when children are entering the classroom and settling down. Try to play short tracks from a variety of different music genres to spark their interest in the new unit.

Ask the class: Do you listen to music? Do you like music? When/Where do you listen to it?

Talk about any musical activities or clubs at school (e.g. music lessons or a school choir). Do they participate in these activities? Are they fun? If they don’t, perhaps a sibling does.

ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “How can we make music?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Ask children to tell you any more musical instruments they know the words for in English. If you have flashcards, stick them on the board. If not, quickly draw the instruments or write the words. Do a few drilling exercises – you say the word while indicating to the card, and children repeat. Then try saying them in different ways (e.g. slowly, quickly, lazily, in a very happy voice, in a sad voice, etc.).

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.147 ex.A. Play the video and ask children to identify the musical activities (a parade, singing) and musical instruments (a violin, a clarinet, drums, a guitar, a saxophone – they might not know the names for all these instruments, so encourage them to respond in mime, then give them the correct word).

Model the task step by step. Listen and point → teacher rhythm model → echo and clap → partner rehearsal → independent performance.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “How can we make music?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Now display flashcards or pictures of a car, a house, a shop, a lift, a child’s bedroom, and a garden. Elicit from children what kinds of music we hear in these places.

Encourage them to think of the last place they heard some music. Did they like it? Was it loud, soft, slow, or fast?

Put children into small groups. Play the video again, or the music from the beginning of the lesson. Tell groups to decide together whether they like the music or not, and where they might listen to this kind of music.

CCQ: Does fast music have a quick beat or a slow beat? How can you tell?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “How can we make music?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Support children complete the Big Question Chart in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.147 ex.D.

Pairs practise a response to “How can we make music?” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “How can we make music?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Complete the Big Question section in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.128.

Hand out sheets of paper. Play the music from the beginning of the lesson again and encourage children to respond creatively to what they hear in a free-drawing activity. Reassure children that what they draw is personal and they don’t need to share it with the rest of the class if they don’t want to.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “How can we make music?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, spend more time drilling the new vocabulary and playing flashcard games.

Encourage more able children to explain their music preferences by asking: How does this music make you feel? Is it too loud/soft, too fast/slow, or just right?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “How can we make music?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have thought about where and when we listen to music, and how we can make music. They have learned some musical instrument words and musical activities.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “How can we make music?” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “How can we make music?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 31, Lesson 1: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
