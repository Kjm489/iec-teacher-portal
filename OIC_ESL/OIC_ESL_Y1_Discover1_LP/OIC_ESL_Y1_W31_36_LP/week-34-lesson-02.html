Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.3a, 1.4c) in “Are they living or non-living things?” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Show children the picture of the rose and ask them how they can smell it (with their nose). Invite them to breathe in the rose scent slowly with you.

Now show them the picture of the candle and ask them how they can blow it out (with their mouth). Invite them to blow it out in one long breath.

Repeat so that children breathe in smelling the rose and out blowing out the candle.

ICQ: Do you look at the object before you choose a category? Where will you put your answer?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “breathe” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Show children the flashcards or pictures one by one. Introduce the lesson’s vocabulary, modelling pronunciation and encouraging children to repeat. Stick the cards on the board, then do a fast drill and a slow drill (you say the words quickly/slowly and children repeat quickly/slowly).

Together with the class, make up some actions to illustrate each word (e.g. heavy breathing for ‘breathe’).

Model the task step by step. Real objects → two-category teacher model → guided sorting → partner explanations → individual classification.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “changes” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Now say a word and invite children to do the action. Make up a routine by asking them to do two lots of deep breaths, three ‘changes’, and one ‘plant’ (or any of the other target words). Remind children of their previous learning in the ‘Music’ unit by helping them keep to the beat.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.168 ex.A. Ask children to identify all the living things on the page.

Then show the plant flashcard or picture again and ask: Is this living or non-living? The aim is to elicit further information: e.g. It moves, it grows, it breathes, and it needs food. Repeat with other living things by asking questions: e.g. Does it move? Does it grow?

CCQ: Is a toy animal living? Why does looking like an animal not make it alive?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “plant” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Ask children to complete Oxford Discover exs.B–C in SB 1 p.168.

Ask children how they can remember the characteristics of living things. Accept all reasonable ideas.

Pairs practise a response to “Are they living or non-living things?” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you look at the object before you choose a category? Where will you put your answer?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Music” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Explain that they are going to write an acrostic poem to help them remember the characteristics. They must use the action words (verbs), but they can also use other words as well. They can also change the order of the verbs. Challenge them to include the word ‘living’ in their work. Put children into pairs to create their acrostic poem.

Monitor actively, supporting and eliciting ideas where necessary.

When children have completed their first draft, tell them to swap it with another pair to check for spelling.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “living” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, ask them to write about a living thing, describing what it can do. For further support, give them a choice of some living things.

Challenge fast finishers to write another acrostic poem for non-living things.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “breathe” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have defined and learned the characteristics of living things. They have created an acrostic poem as an aide-mémoire.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “Are they living or non-living things?” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “changes” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 34, Lesson 2: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
