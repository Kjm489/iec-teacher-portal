Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.2b, 1.2d, 1.2e) in “I’m playing the drums!” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Set a timer for 30 seconds. Begin to draw one of the percussion instruments learned this week. Encourage children to call out the correct name before the time runs out.

Put children into small groups to continue to play the game and practise musical instrument vocabulary. Remind them that the person drawing can’t speak!

ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “–ing” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Mime playing the drums. Ask children: What am I doing? Elicit: You’re playing the drums. Do this with a couple more instruments.

Write the sentence on the board. Focus children’s attention on the ‘–ing’ form and the verb ‘to be’. Explain that we use this tense to say what we are doing right now.

Model the task step by step. Listen and point → teacher rhythm model → echo and clap → partner rehearsal → independent performance.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “to be” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Remind children of their previous learning by asking what they are wearing (see Week 16, Lessons 4 and 5). By thinking again about this grammatical form, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

Repeat the musical instrument exercise and encourage children to repeat clearly, focusing on the ‘–ing’ form.

Then mime playing the triangle and ask: Am I playing the tambourine? Model: I’m not playing the tambourine. Either shake your head or wag your finger as you say the sentence to indicate the negative form.

CCQ: Does fast music have a quick beat or a slow beat? How can you tell?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “You” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Repeat with the ‘You’ form, emphasizing the ‘aren’t’: e.g. You aren’t playing the drums.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.153 and play the song in ex.D (track 3.05). Ask children to listen first and to tell you what Gus and Layla are playing (the xylophone and the cymbals). Then encourage children to sing along.

Pairs practise a response to “I’m playing the drums!” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “aren” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Now tell children to imagine the following situation: You’re at home playing a musical instrument. Your friend calls and wants to know what you’re playing. How can they find out what you’re playing? Elicit the question: Are you playing …?

See the model dialogue provided. Elicit or adapt the dialogue, reminding children of phrases to use when they don’t understand: e.g. Can you say that again, please?

Tell children to write down or to draw a musical instrument in secret. Then ask them to move their chairs so pairs are sitting back to back (and can’t see each other). Instruct them to try to guess each other’s musical instrument by practising the dialogue rehearsed.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “–ing” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, practise the model dialogue in an adult-led small group.

As an extension task for more able children, ask them to guess other things in the dialogue. For example, they could ask: Is your mum watching TV? Is your brother wearing red shorts?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “to be” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned the present continuous tense. They have played a speaking game to find out which musical instrument their partner is playing.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “I’m playing the drums!” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “You” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
