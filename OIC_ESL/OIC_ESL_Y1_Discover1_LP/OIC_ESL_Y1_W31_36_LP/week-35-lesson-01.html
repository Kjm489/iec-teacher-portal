Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.2a, 1.2b, 1.2f) in “I spy with my little eye …” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Put children into groups of four or five to make letters with their bodies.

Choose two or three random letters (e.g. ‘f’, ‘t’, and ‘l’). Set a time limit for children to make the letters. Then ask them to write the letters on their whiteboard.

Divide the class into two. On one side, ask them to write down as many living things as they can beginning with the letters from the previous activity, while the other side does the same for non-living things. You could do this letter by letter, or all in one go.

ICQ: Do you look at the object before you choose a category? Where will you put your answer?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “move” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Ask children to recall their learning about living and non-living things from Week 34. Elicit relevant topic vocabulary (e.g. the verbs ‘move’, ‘breathe’, ‘grow’, ‘change’, and ‘feed’).

Ask a confident child to look around the classroom and to give you an example of a living thing. Then ask: Why is [it/he/she] a living thing? Elicit the characteristics using the vocabulary: e.g. [It/He/She] can breathe.

Model the task step by step. Real objects → two-category teacher model → guided sorting → partner explanations → individual classification.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “breathe” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Repeat with a non-living thing and encourage children to use the negative form of the present tense: e.g. It can’t breathe. Write sentences on the board with can and can’t.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.173 ex.E. Ask the class to look at the picture and elicit some sentences using can and can’t. Write them on the board. If necessary, use the Learn Grammar box to support teaching.

Play a game of ‘I spy …’. You might already be familiar with the rules. First, teach the rhyme: I spy with my little eye something beginning with … [p]. The aim is that children look for something around the classroom beginning with the letter [p], calling out or raising their hand to make suggestions. In this version of the game, explain that they shouldn’t just say the word (e.g. ‘pen’), but ask questions: e.g. Can it breathe? Yes, it can breathe. / No, it can’t breathe. Elicit a few question forms and write them on the board.

CCQ: Is a toy animal living? Why does looking like an animal not make it alive?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “grow” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Do an example as a whole class first, then put children into pairs to play the game. Make sure they have learned the rhyme, or write it on the board to refresh their memory.

Monitor actively and praise children for working well together in their pairs. If you hear an error, prompt them to think about where they might have gone wrong and to try to self-correct after the round of the game they were playing.

Pairs practise a response to “I spy with my little eye …” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you look at the object before you choose a category? Where will you put your answer?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “change” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Hold a whole-class discussion. Talk about the most interesting words (living and non-living things) children found in the classroom, and whether the activity was easy or difficult.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “feed” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, write some question starters on the board.

As an extension task for more able children, put them into a group together and allow them to choose an item which they can’t see.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “I spy …” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned how to use can and can’t in sentences. They have practised asking questions about living and non-living things while playing a game of ‘I spy …’.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “I spy with my little eye …” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “move” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 35, Lesson 1: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
