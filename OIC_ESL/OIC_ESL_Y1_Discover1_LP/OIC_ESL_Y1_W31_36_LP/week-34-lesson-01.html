Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.1c, 1.2c) in “Naming living and non-living things” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Do a quick warm-up. Ask children to sit in two lines on the floor, facing each other.

Explain that you are going to play some music and, in pairs (one from each row), they are going to dance/move up the middle like a soul train.

Do a quick example yourself. Some children may feel shy about dancing. Instead, they can run, walk slowly, or pretend to do a sport. Put them into pairs.

ICQ: Do you look at the object before you choose a category? Where will you put your answer?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “living things grow” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Show the flashcard of a soft animal toy or hold up a real one. Ask: Is this an animal or a toy? Then ask questioningly: How do we know? Elicit some ideas.

Continue to teach the lesson’s vocabulary, modelling correct pronunciation for children to copy and drill a few times.

Model the task step by step. Real objects → two-category teacher model → guided sorting → partner explanations → individual classification.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Naming living and non-living things” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Tell the class that they are going to watch a short video. The videos are different, but the steps are the same. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.167 ex.A.

Tell children the video is about living and non-living things. Encourage them to guess what they will see based on their existing knowledge. Play the video to find out if their ideas were correct.

Instruct children to take out a pencil and dictate seven words for them to write. Four of these words should appear in the video (as visuals or in speech/text) and three should not. Tell them to underline the words they saw or heard in the video.

CCQ: Is a toy animal living? Why does looking like an animal not make it alive?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What are living things?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Watch the video again for children to check their answers. Review as a class.

Ask the class: What is one difference between living and non-living things? Accept all reasonable answers. Focus on ‘living things grow’.

Pairs practise a response to “Naming living and non-living things” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you look at the object before you choose a category? Where will you put your answer?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “living thing” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Put children into small groups of three or four and ask them to make a list of things that grow. After a few minutes, take ideas. Praise the group who worked together well to find the most answers, as well as those who offered the most interesting or unusual answers.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.167 ex.D. Give children time to complete the Big Question Chart on the question ‘What are living things?’.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “non-living thing” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, give them a list of key words or write them on the board for them to copy (rather than dictating). Alternatively, give them the words with some letters missing.

As an extension task for more confident children, ask them to write a definition each for ‘living thing’ and ‘non-living thing’ in their own words.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “living things grow” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have watched a short video to introduce the topic. They have learned about living and non-living things, beginning to establish the difference between them.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “Naming living and non-living things” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Naming living and non-living things” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 34, Lesson 1: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
