Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.4c, 1.4d) in “Writing about music” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Using the flashcards or pictures of the vocabulary from the unit, play a quick-fire revision game.

Put children into pairs. Tell them that you are going to show five flashcards or pictures (or more/less depending on the ability level of your class). Give them ten seconds to look at each card and a further ten seconds to write the word on their whiteboard.

Count to three and ask everyone to hold up their whiteboard.

ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “I am” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Ask children whether they preferred learning about instruments or musical performances. Elicit a few reasons and hold a class discussion.

On the board, write ‘I am’ and, with your fingers, show the contracted form: push your fingers together and elicit ‘I’m’.

Model the task step by step. Listen and point → teacher rhythm model → echo and clap → partner rehearsal → independent performance.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Writing about music” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.163 and ask children to complete ex.F.

Tell children that they are going to write about either a musical performance or a musical instrument of their choice. Remind them to look through their work from the past two weeks for ideas and words to use. Put them into pairs and give them some time to think and plan their ideas in a mind map. Hand out or display the writing guidance to inspire their thinking.

Now ask pairs to work together to write their first draft. After a set time limit, encourage them to discuss their writing and to check it for correct spelling and punctuation. Can they notice any instances where they can contract two words?

CCQ: Does fast music have a quick beat or a slow beat? How can you tell?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “quality not quantity” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Monitor actively and encourage self-correction if you notice any mistakes.

Wrap up the lesson by talking together about what children enjoyed the most about the unit, and what they have learned.

Pairs practise a response to “Writing about music” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “I am” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Complete Oxford Discover WB 1 p.143 ex.A.

Hand out display paper for children to write up their final draft neatly.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Writing about music” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less confident pairs, provide them with a gapped text and/or some sentence starters. Remind them about ‘quality not quantity’ – it is better to work hard to write a few clear sentences for you to check.

Encourage more able pairs to use a range of vocabulary, exciting adjectives, and both the present simple and present continuous tense in their writing.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “quality not quantity” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have recognized contractions and understood what they mean. They have written a paragraph describing an aspect of music in pairs.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “Writing about music” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “I am” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
