Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.3a, 1.3b) in “Making a musical instrument” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Mime playing a percussion instrument. Encourage children to call out the name. Elicit if you shake it or strike it.

Put children into small groups to continue the activity.

ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “pulses” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Tell children that you have made a shaker and that you are going to play it. Next, explain to them that you want them to listen carefully and to tell you what they think is inside it.

Ask children to close their eyes. Play the shaker and elicit ‘pulses’ (or whatever you have put inside your shaker – e.g. ‘lentils’, ‘peas’, or ‘beans’).

Model the task step by step. Listen and point → teacher rhythm model → echo and clap → partner rehearsal → independent performance.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “lentils” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Tell them that in today’s lesson they are going to make their own shaker. Pass your pre-prepared one around the class for children to look at it more closely.

Pre-teach/Elicit the lesson’s vocabulary. Write the words on the board for children to read.

Open the lid of your shaker and replace the pulses. Encourage children to experiment with different sounds when they make their own shaker.

CCQ: Does fast music have a quick beat or a slow beat? How can you tell?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “peas” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Put children into groups to discuss how they want to make their shaker. Explain that you are going to give them some instructions and the materials they need. Hand these out. Children can take turns saying an instruction and then everyone does each part together with their own set of materials.

Monitor actively as children make their shakers. Only intervene when necessary, allowing children some time to problem-solve in their groups if they get stuck.

Pairs practise a response to “Making a musical instrument” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “beans” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Play some music and let children experiment with their shakers. Encourage them to stand up while they do this and to move their bodies.

Ask everyone to shake in time. Mark the rhythm and ensure children are playing along.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Making a musical instrument” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, pair them with a more confident child to help them make their shaker.

Challenge more confident children to work out how to make the shaker without looking at the instructions. You could challenge them further by asking them to write out the instructions, which they can then compare with yours.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “pulses” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have followed simple instructions to make a percussion instrument.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “Making a musical instrument” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “lentils” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
