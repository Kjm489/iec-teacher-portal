LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1

Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
Oic Outcomes: 1.3b, 1.3f, 1.4b

Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.3b, 1.3f, 1.4b) in “Contrasting things” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask children to stand up. Revise the verb characteristics of living things from earlier in the week by saying a verb and then a living thing. Encourage children to do the corresponding action (e.g. ‘move like an elephant’ or ‘grow like a plant’). By thinking again about these characteristics, children are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

Put children into groups of six or seven to continue the game.

ICQ: Do you look at the object before you choose a category? Where will you put your answer?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “move like an elephant” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Instruct children to sit back down. Ask them to quietly look around the classroom and see if they can notice things that move or change. Keep everyone quiet so that everyone has a chance to look and observe, then put children into pairs to share their ideas.

Tell children they are going to read about two different classes or lessons. Say what these are, and encourage them to think about the differences between them.

Model the task step by step. Real objects → two-category teacher model → guided sorting → partner explanations → individual classification.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “grow like a plant” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.169. Guide them through the Learn box and then ask them to complete ex.D question 1.

Give children time to read the two short texts quickly and check their ideas in pairs.

Ask them to read the text again and complete the chart. Review answers as a class.

CCQ: Is a toy animal living? Why does looking like an animal not make it alive?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Contrasting things” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Children can now complete ex.D question 2. Review answers as a class.

Tell children that they are now going to write their own texts to show the differences between living and non-living things. Similar to the examples they have read, these should only be a few sentences. First, put children into small groups to think of a variety of different things they could write about. Once they know what they will write about, give them the opportunity to decide how they are going to work (individually, in pairs, or in groups). Make sure everyone is writing about different things.

Pairs practise a response to “Contrasting things” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you look at the object before you choose a category? Where will you put your answer?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “move like an elephant” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Give children enough time to mind map their ideas and then write their sentences.

Monitor actively and praise children for their interesting ideas and effort in distinguishing between living and non-living things.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “grow like a plant” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, provide a gapped text. Guide them through the reading activity in an adult-led small group.

As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to write more detailed sentences about their chosen items, using interesting adjectives.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Contrasting things” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned about contrast in a reading activity. They have described the differences between a living thing and a non-living thing.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “Contrasting things” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “move like an elephant” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
