PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Using the flashcards or pictures from the previous lesson, quickly revise the musical performance words. Show a card and ask children to call out the word.

ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “asking for help” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Pick up lots of things from your desk, and keep picking them up and piling them into your hands. Say to children: I need help! What can I say? Elicit: Can you help me, please?

Ask children what they could say in response and elicit: Sure / Of course / Let’s do it together! / I can help you. Then model your final utterance: Thank you.

Model the task step by step. Listen and point → teacher rhythm model → echo and clap → partner rehearsal → independent performance.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Asking for help” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Drill each of the phrases in a fun way. You can say the sentences slowly or quickly, loudly or softly, with children repeating in the same way. To challenge them, if you say the phrase slowly, they have to say it quickly. This works particularly well with loud and soft.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.155 ex.E and read the model dialogue together.

Ask: Why do we ask for help? Accept all reasonable answers, guiding children towards the verbal response: We ask for help when we have a problem, or when we are finding something difficult. Widen the conversation by asking children if they like asking for help, and elicit some situations when they do ask for help. Support children to understand that offering help shows kindness – it is nice when other people help them, so they should remember to do the same.

CCQ: Does fast music have a quick beat or a slow beat? How can you tell?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “asking for help” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Form groups of three or four. Tell children that they are now going to create a role-play in which one person has to ask for help. They can come up with their own ideas, or you may like to provide them with the following ‘asking for help’ role-play cards. Model a dialogue with a confident child or another adult in the classroom first.

You are carrying some very heavy books.

Pairs practise a response to “Asking for help” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Asking for help” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

You don’t understand your homework.

You have to clear the table.

You don’t know how to play a game.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “asking for help” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Spend more time helping less confident groups, modelling the dialogue a few times to support them. Form similar-ability groups so more able children can work together independently while you help their peers.

Encourage more confident groups to extend their role-play with extra details. Remind them to try to use the present continuous in their dialogue.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Asking for help” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have practised asking for and giving help in a role-play.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “Asking for help” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “asking for help” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

You don’t know how to play a game.

You can’t find your pen or pencil.

Give children enough time to plan and rehearse their role-play.

Invite volunteer groups to perform their role-play to the rest of the class.

Ask listening children to raise their hands every time they hear a good ‘asking for help’ expression. At the end of the role-plays, praise children for their effort in applying their learning to their performance. Encourage self-correction if you heard any mistakes.

Remind children it is always OK to ask for help.
