Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.1g ,1.3a) in “Percussion instruments (1)” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Explain to the class that they are going to make some sounds using their bodies. Clap your hands three times and ask children to repeat.

Now pat your thighs three times, pat your tummy three times, pat your head three times, and clap three times. Pause after each body part for children to repeat.

Repeat until they can ‘play’ the rhythm unaccompanied. This is called ‘body percussion’.

ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “play” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Using the flashcards or musical instrument realia, present the lesson’s vocabulary. Show the card or percussion instrument, say the word, and invite children to repeat. Then stick the cards on the board or place the percussion instruments in front of you so that everyone can see. As you present the words in turn, either play the instrument or an audio clip (you can source these online) of the sound this instrument makes. This will help to familiarize children with what percussion instruments sound like.

Then ask children to close their eyes. Play the instruments or audio clips again and challenge children to call out the name of the instrument.

Model the task step by step. Listen and point → teacher rhythm model → echo and clap → partner rehearsal → independent performance.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “body percussion” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.148. Continue to familiarize children with the names of percussion instruments by playing the audio in ex.A (track 3.02).

Say the words together. Encourage children to look at the spellings and to identify any difficult ones with letter-sound relationships that aren’t easily identifiable (e.g. the ‘xy’ in ‘xylophone’ is pronounced /zaɪ/). Check that children are feeling more confident with the pronunciation of these words, offering additional support where required.

Using two of the instruments, model ‘shake’ and ‘strike’. Go through the instruments one by one and ask: Is this a ‘strike’ instrument or a ‘shake’ instrument? Elicit the answers.

CCQ: Does fast music have a quick beat or a slow beat? How can you tell?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “xy” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Place the flashcards or word cards randomly around the room. Invite a small group of children to stand up. Say one of the musical instrument words and tell children to walk to the correct card, then read the word aloud. Continue with different groups.

Put the word cards on the board in one column and the musical instrument flashcards or drawings in another column, but in the wrong order. Invite children who weren’t involved in the previous activity to match them together by drawing lines on the board.

Pairs practise a response to “Percussion instruments (1)” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “xylophone” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Stand children in a circle. Assign an instrument to each group of four (adapt according to the number of children in your class). Tell groups to decide together how to play the instrument and the sound it makes (e.g. a triangle – ‘ting ting’). Then stand in the middle of the circle as the ‘conductor’. Encourage groups to make their noises and to mime playing their instrument. Use conductor gestures to bring sections of your orchestra in and out.

Praise children for their listening and watching skills demonstrated in today’s lesson.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “shake” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, place the picture flashcards next to the word cards.

Invite more confident children to be the orchestra ‘conductor’ in the final activity.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “strike” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned about percussion instruments and the sounds they make. They have followed simple instructions to complete a range of activities.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “Percussion instruments (1)” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “play” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
