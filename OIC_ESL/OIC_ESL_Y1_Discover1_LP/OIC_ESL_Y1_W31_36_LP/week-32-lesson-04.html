Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.2a, 1.2b) in “What are they doing?” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Revise musical performance words with children (see Week 32, Lesson 2).

Put them into groups and tell them they are going to make letters with their bodies.

Spell out: b-a-l-l-e-t.

ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “–ing” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Show children one of the pictures and ask for a sentence: e.g. They are watching the ballet. He is dancing.

Remind children that they have already done this (i.e. using the ‘–ing’ form / present continuous tense) and today they are going to revise it to help them remember the grammar!

Model the task step by step. Listen and point → teacher rhythm model → echo and clap → partner rehearsal → independent performance.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What are they doing?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.162 and ask children to complete ex.B in pairs.

Hand out one picture with a musical theme to each pair of children. Explain that you want them to make three or four sentences to describe it orally. Give them enough time to plan and rehearse their sentences out loud.

Take back the pictures. Put children into groups of ten (= five different pictures in each group). Instruct pairs to put their pictures in the middle of the table and to mix them up.

CCQ: Does fast music have a quick beat or a slow beat? How can you tell?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “–ing” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In turn, tell each pair to say their prepared sentences to their classmates, who must work together to guess which picture is theirs.

As a round-up, ask children which picture they like best and least. Encourage them to give reasons why.

Pairs practise a response to “What are they doing?” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What are they doing?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Complete Oxford Discover WB 1 pp.132–133.

Look around the classroom for one minute. Invite each child to say a sentence about something happening. Challenge the class to do this without repetition.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “–ing” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Allocate some time to review the present continuous tense with less confident children, then form mixed-ability pairs so more confident children can model the ‘–ing’ form to their less confident peers.

As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to give more details or to widen their choice of verbs in their sentences.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What are they doing?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have practised describing a picture with a musical theme using the present continuous tense.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “What are they doing?” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “–ing” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

Teacher models — I DO: Show the original lesson example for “–ing”. Think aloud while selecting a matching picture, action or spoken response. Ask pupils to explain or demonstrate why it matches.
