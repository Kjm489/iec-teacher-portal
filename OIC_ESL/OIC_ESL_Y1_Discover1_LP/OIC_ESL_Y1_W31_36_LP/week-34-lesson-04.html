Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.3d, 1.3f) in “Reading about living and non-living things” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask children to stand up.

Using the lesson’s vocabulary, say a word and ask children to do a gesture/mime to represent that word. Do this with all the words.

Play ‘Simon says’ (or change the name to suit your context). If you precede the word with ‘Simon says [move]’, children must do the action. If you don’t say ‘Simon says …’, children shouldn’t do the action. Do a practice round first. Afterwards, if they do the action at the wrong time, they must sit down. Speed up the activity until you find a winner!

ICQ: Do you predict before reading or after reading? Which picture gives you a clue?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Simon says” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Divide the class into groups of ten (if you have 32 children, for example, have three groups with two children sharing).

Give each child one of the sentences provided, which they must keep secret. Explain that they must remember the words in their sentence.

Model the task step by step. Picture walk → model a prediction → sequence cards together → pair retelling → independent story response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Simon says [move]” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Within their groups, children must divide themselves into two smaller groups, ‘Living things’ and ‘Non-living things’. Encourage groups to work out how to categorize themselves first, but if they need help, give them a clue: e.g. Five sentences describe living things and five sentences describe non-living things.

Test children’s understanding by asking them what they think the things in sentences 1, 5, 6, 7, 9, and 10 might be. Accept all reasonable answers.

Show children flashcards or pictures of a plant and a pair of trainers/sneakers. Explain that they are going to read about these items.

CCQ: Which event happened first in the story? What detail tells you?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Simon says …” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Put them into pairs to think of three things they think the text will be about. Prompt if necessary by saying ‘living and non-living things’. Share ideas as a whole class.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 pp.170–171 and read the text together, inviting confident children to read aloud sections.

Pairs practise a response to “Reading about living and non-living things” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you predict before reading or after reading? Which picture gives you a clue?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Living things” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Work through the comprehension activities on Oxford Discover SB 1 p.172.

To wrap up, ask children to name one living thing and one non-living thing that hasn’t yet been mentioned.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Non-living things” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

If less confident children find it difficult to remember the words in their sentence, let them hold onto their sentence, but encourage them not to look at it unless necessary.

Allocate sentences 2, 3, 4, and 8 to less confident children.

As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think of ways to take care of living and non-living things.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “living and non-living things” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have categorized information in a group reading activity. They have deepened their understanding of living and non-living things.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “Reading about living and non-living things” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Simon says” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
