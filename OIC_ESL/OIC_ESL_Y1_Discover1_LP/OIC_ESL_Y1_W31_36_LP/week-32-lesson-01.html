Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.1a, 1.2b) in “This music is fast!” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask children to close their eyes.

Tell them to take a long, deep breath and then to let the breath out as slowly as they can.

Repeat this a couple of times, each time trying to breathe in more slowly and breathe out more slowly.

ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “This music is fast!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Introduce the lesson’s vocabulary by playing the audio clips selected, or by using your own voice in different ways.

After each audio clip or demonstration, ask children to repeat the words.

Model the task step by step. Listen and point → teacher rhythm model → echo and clap → partner rehearsal → independent performance.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “This music is fast!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Now play some music that illustrates each of the adjectives. Encourage children to tell you the adjective that they think describes the music best. Discuss if you have the same ideas.

Ask children which music they liked best, and why: e.g. It’s fun and fast. I like fast music! It makes me want to dance.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.154 and guide children through exs.A–D.

CCQ: Does fast music have a quick beat or a slow beat? How can you tell?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “This music is fast!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Ask children what instrument they would choose if they had to think of music for a lion. Accept all ideas, encouraging children to give reasons and to choose an adjective. Repeat with a snake.

Put children into pairs to find a picture of an animal in their coursebooks and to think of some music or instruments that could accompany this animal (e.g. if it was in a film, a cartoon, or a documentary). Hand out a selection of percussion instruments for them to experiment with different sounds. Remind them of the shaker they made last week (see Week 31, Lesson 4) – could they use this to make a good sound for their animal?

Pairs practise a response to “This music is fast!” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “This music is fast!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Invite pairs to share their ideas with the rest of the class.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “This music is fast!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Form mixed-ability pairs so more confident children can encourage less confident children in the music-making activity, modelling new vocabulary.

As an extension task for fast finishers, ask them to share their favourite rhymes or songs which their parents or carers sing to/with them. How does this music make them feel? Is the music loud or soft? Why?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “This music is fast!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned adjectives to describe different sounds. They have created and played some music to represent an animal.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “This music is fast!” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “This music is fast!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
