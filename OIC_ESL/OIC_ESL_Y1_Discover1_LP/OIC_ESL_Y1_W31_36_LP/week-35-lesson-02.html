LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1

Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)

Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.1d, 1.3a, 1.3b, 1.3f) in “The gingerbread man (1)” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Pre-teach/Elicit the verbs ‘run’, ‘chase’, ‘catch’, ‘stop’, ‘cross (a river)’, and ‘bake’ with the flashcards or pictures.

Do an action to represent each word – for instance, running on the spot to represent ‘run’, and running on the spot with your arms outstretched to represent ‘chase’.

Ask children to stand up and to move to one side of the classroom. Say a word and encourage them to do the corresponding action. Do a couple of practice rounds first. After, anyone doing the action incorrectly must move to the other side of the classroom.

ICQ: Do you predict before reading or after reading? Which picture gives you a clue?

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “run” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Now introduce the following nouns with the flashcards or pictures: ‘cow’, ‘fox’, ‘old lady’, ‘old man’, ‘chicken’, ‘horse’, and ‘gingerbread man’.

Ask children which are living and which are non-living. Encourage them to say why: e.g. The animals are living because they can breathe and move.

Model the task step by step. Picture walk → model a prediction → sequence cards together → pair retelling → independent story response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “chase” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Find out if children have ever made or eaten a gingerbread person. They are special biscuits which originated in Europe many centuries ago!

Explain that in today’s lesson they are going to read a story about a gingerbread man. Ask: Do you think the gingerbread man in the story is living or non-living?

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.178. You can play the audio (track 3.26) or read the story aloud while children follow in their books. Read up to the end of p.178.

CCQ: Which event happened first in the story? What detail tells you?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “catch” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Talk about how the gingerbread man starts as non-living, but becomes living. Recap the main events of the story so far as a whole class.

Teach children the gingerbread man’s words: Run, run as fast as you can! You can’t catch me, I’m the gingerbread man!

Pairs practise a response to “The gingerbread man (1)” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you predict before reading or after reading? Which picture gives you a clue?

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “stop” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Hand out gingerbread man templates. Ask children to listen carefully. Do a colour dictation exercise. Say: Colour in your gingerbread man. His eyes are brown and his nose is pink. He’s got a red mouth. He’s got three blue buttons on his jacket.

Discuss children’s favourite part of the story so far.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “cross (a river)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, pair them with more able children who will support them to retell the story by modelling key words.

Allow more confident children to read the story independently, at their own pace.

Fast finishers can write sentences describing what the gingerbread man can and can’t do: e.g. The gingerbread man can run, but he can’t eat food!

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “bake” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have read the first part of a traditional story about a gingerbread man. They have listened carefully to instructions to complete a colour dictation activity.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “The gingerbread man (1)” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “run” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 35, Lesson 2: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
