Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate the original OIC learning outcomes (1.3a, 1.3d, 1.3f) in “Percussion instruments (2)” and independently explain or perform a relevant example.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Sit in a circle with children. Start clapping a slow beat (e.g. 1, 2, 3, 4).

Now introduce half beats and create a rhythm with a mix of full and half beats. Encourage children to copy you.

Then invite a volunteer to create a rhythm for the class to follow.

ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Percussion instruments (2)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Tell children that today they are going to read about the percussion instruments they learned about last lesson (see Week 31, Lesson 2).

Create an anchor chart together, eliciting and noting down the type of information the text might have. Do an example with drums. Ask a child to describe or mime how to play them.

Model the task step by step. Listen and point → teacher rhythm model → echo and clap → partner rehearsal → independent performance.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Percussion instruments (2)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Turn to the text in Oxford Discover SB 1 pp.150–151. Explore and read it together as a class. Ask children to compare their ideas on the board to the text in the book. Are there any similarities or differences? Ask: What is the same/different? Then work together to complete the activities on p.152.

The above reading comprehension activities will stretch children and extend their understanding of percussion instruments and the associated vocabulary.

Ask children which instrument they think is easiest to play and which is hardest, and why.

CCQ: Does fast music have a quick beat or a slow beat? How can you tell?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Percussion instruments (2)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

If you have available some musical instruments to show children (e.g. cymbals, drums, a tambourine, a triangle, and a xylophone), give children time to explore them. Remind them to treat them with care and to share them with their classmates. Repeat the question: How easy or difficult is it to play [a tambourine]?

Play a tune on one of the instruments and challenge children to keep to the beat on their instruments. If you don’t have enough instruments for each child, let everyone else do body percussion (see introductory activity in Week 31, Lesson 2).

Pairs practise a response to “Percussion instruments (2)” with a visual or language frame. ICQ: Do you listen first or play first? Show me when you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Percussion instruments (2)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Put children into pairs to mime playing one of the percussion instruments they have learned about. Their partner guesses what it is. They can also draw an instrument on their whiteboard and challenge their partner to write the word. After a few minutes, remind children to swap roles.

Collect one independent response or product as evidence of learning.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Percussion instruments (2)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children during the reading comprehension tasks, provide two choices for the answers. Put key words on the board to help them.

Invite more confident readers to read the text independently or in pairs.

As an extension task for fast finishers, ask them to find out about a traditional percussion instrument from their country or culture.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Percussion instruments (2)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have read about percussion instruments and how they are played.

Exit ticket: Demonstrate one correct example from “Percussion instruments (2)” without teacher prompting.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Percussion instruments (2)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
