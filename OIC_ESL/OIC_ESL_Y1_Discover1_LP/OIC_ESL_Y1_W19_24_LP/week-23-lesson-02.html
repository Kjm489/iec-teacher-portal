Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about numbers and number relationships using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Motivate children to do star jumps, hops, jumps, stretches, and claps. (You can adapt these actions to suit the physical capabilities of your class.) As a class, decide together on a routine including all these movements (e.g. two star jumps, one hop, turn around, stretch, clap hands, and two jumps). Practise together. Tell children that when you put your hand up during the lesson, you want them to stop what they are doing, get up, and do the…

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Numbers every day (2)”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Check meaning: If we add one more object, does the number stay the same? Show how you know.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Numbers every day (2)”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Using the punctuation flashcards, elicit what the punctuation marks are called in English. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.103 ex.F. Tell children that they are going to write some sentences with the punctuation marks. Choose the full stop first. Let each child choose a number word card. Their sentence must include the number. Repeat this with the other punctuation marks. Children choose a new number each time. Children should now have three…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Are we counting the objects or guessing? How can we check the answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Numbers every day (2)”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Numbers every day (2)”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate numbers and number relationships vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Numbers every day (2)”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, allow them to draw some parts of their story, or just give them one of the three elements to incorporate. Challenge more confident children to add some more details to their story.

Extension: Explain a number pattern or check a classmate’s calculation with objects.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Numbers every day (2)”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have used their imagination to write a short story using numbers and a range of punctuation marks.

Exit ticket: Solve or represent a new number example and explain the answer.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Numbers every day (2)”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

YEAR 1 Working with numbers
Week 23, Lesson 2: Numbers every day (2)
Learning outcomes: 1.4a, 1.4b
Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Working with numbers’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by writing a short story about numbers.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Motivate children to do star jumps, hops, jumps, stretches, and claps. (You can adapt these actions to suit the physical capabilities of your class.)
As a class, decide together on a routine including all these movements (e.g. two star jumps, one hop, turn around, stretch, clap hands, and two jumps). Practise together. Tell children that when you put your hand up during the lesson, you want them to stop what they are doing, get up, and do the routine together, but very quietly (and with no giggling!).
Main activity
Using the punctuation flashcards, elicit what the punctuation marks are called in English. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.103 ex.F.
Tell children that they are going to write some sentences with the punctuation marks. Choose the full stop first.
Let each child choose a number word card. Their sentence must include the number.
Repeat this with the other punctuation marks. Children choose a new number each time.
Children should now have three phrases (one question, one exclamation, and a sentence).
Give each child or group of children a character, a place, and an object – for instance, a superhero, the town where you live, and a pencil.
Challenge children to write a short story using their number phrases and these three elements. It can be as silly and fun as they like!
Allow children enough time to write their short stories. Remind them to check their work carefully for correct punctuation.
Invite children to share their stories with the rest of the class.
Praise children for their effort in trying to incorporate all the requested elements in their stories. It wasn’t an easy task!
Additional tasks
Illustrate the stories. Display the work, or create a class book of short stories.
Encourage children to find example sentences with punctuation marks in their coursebooks or around the classroom. They can copy these down.
Learning review
Children have used their imagination to write a short story using numbers and a range of punctuation marks.
Differentiation
To support less able children, allow them to draw some parts of their story, or just give them one of the three elements to incorporate.
Challenge more confident children to add some more details to their story.
