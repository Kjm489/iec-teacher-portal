Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Seasons
Week: 21
Lesson: 2
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: Unit summary

Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about seasons and weather using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Activate topic knowledge with pictures and the opening task in the original OIC plan.

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Seasons” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Check meaning: If a picture shows snow, is it more likely winter or summer? What clue helps?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Seasons” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Explain that this week, children will participate in one or more extended activities in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas: Children make an accordion flower to represent spring. Children create a seasons journal, identifying what happens in their life in the different seasons. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.85. Design a…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Do all places have the same weather in the same season? What do we see in this example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Seasons” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Seasons” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate seasons and weather vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Seasons” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Encourage children who need more support to answer simple questions with check/cross cards. Provide language and gapped texts, and put them into groups with more able children. For more able children, encourage them to do more. For example, they could write questions or true/false statements for the game.

Extension: Compare two seasonal scenes and justify one difference.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Seasons” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have consolidated their knowledge and learning on the topic of ‘Seasons’.

Exit ticket: Identify a season from a new picture and give one reason.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Seasons” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Reflection and review
Week 21, Lessons 1–5: Reflection on ‘Seasons’
Learning outcomes: Unit summary
Context
This week, children will reflect on their individual learning from Weeks 19–20.
Children will also participate in an extended activity, practising the skills they have learned and focusing their work on areas of particular interest.
Lessons this week will vary, depending on the scope of the chosen activity. Break up the activity into manageable stages to fit into the ESL lessons this week.
Independent reflection
Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used during Weeks 19–20.
Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, and Writing. Lead a discussion with children about these skills. For example: Tell me an activity when we practised speaking in English. When we [played XX game], what skills did we use? Did we speak? Did we listen? What did you find easy/difficult? Why?
Help children to assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children who may find this self-reflective process quite challenging.
Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.
Main activity
Explain that this week, children will participate in one or more extended activities in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.
Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas:
Children make an accordion flower to represent spring.
Children create a seasons journal, identifying what happens in their life in the different seasons. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.85.
Design a racetrack game. Children can design items to count. Alternatively, they can write words. They prepare the game for another group to play.
Children create a seasons fact book. Encourage them to ask ‘Why?’ and then find the answer. Provide age-appropriate reference books or websites to assist them.
Children write a shape poem about your country’s national flower or plant.
Children create a role-play set in one of the seasons.
Additional tasks
Complete the KWL chart from Week 19, Lesson 1.
Children read some picture books about weather and seasons.
Learning review
Children have consolidated their knowledge and learning on the topic of ‘Seasons’.
Differentiation
Encourage children who need more support to answer simple questions with check/cross cards. Provide language and gapped texts, and put them into groups with more able children.
For more able children, encourage them to do more. For example, they could write questions or true/false statements for the game.
