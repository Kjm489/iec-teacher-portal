Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about seasons and weather using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Quickly revise the months of the year. Mouth a month and invite children to say the word. Tell children which month and season you were born in: e.g. My birthday is in July. I’m a summer baby. Put children into pairs to practise telling their partner their birth month and season.

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “hot” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Check meaning: If a picture shows snow, is it more likely winter or summer? What clue helps?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “warm” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Show children the thermometer and elicit what it is and what it does. Ask children where they might see one – perhaps someone in their family has one? Elicit/Pre-teach ‘hot’, ‘warm’, ‘cool’, and ‘cold’. Ask children how they feel at these temperatures. Which do they like best? Invite a child to read the temperature on the thermometer and tell you if the day is hot, warm, cool, or cold. Support them to understand how it works. Then pass the…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Do all places have the same weather in the same season? What do we see in this example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “cool” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “cold” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate seasons and weather vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “hot” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Form mixed-ability pairs in the flashcard guessing game so more confident children can support less confident children with the learning of new topic vocabulary. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to make a list of / draw places where we might find a thermometer (e.g. in a car, in a hospital, an oven, etc.).

Extension: Compare two seasonal scenes and justify one difference.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “warm” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have understood how we can measure temperature. They have learned new vocabulary to describe the weather and have talked about how it might change in different seasons, depending on their local context.

Exit ticket: Identify a season from a new picture and give one reason.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “cool” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 19, Lesson 2: Talking about seasons
Learning outcomes: 1.1a, 1.2c
Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by learning and categorizing topic vocabulary.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Quickly revise the months of the year. Mouth a month and invite children to say the word.
Tell children which month and season you were born in: e.g. My birthday is in July. I’m a summer baby.
Put children into pairs to practise telling their partner their birth month and season.
Main activity
Show children the thermometer and elicit what it is and what it does. Ask children where they might see one – perhaps someone in their family has one?
Elicit/Pre-teach ‘hot’, ‘warm’, ‘cool’, and ‘cold’. Ask children how they feel at these temperatures. Which do they like best?
Invite a child to read the temperature on the thermometer and tell you if the day is hot, warm, cool, or cold. Support them to understand how it works. Then pass the thermometer carefully around the class for children to look at.
Hand out the thermometer print-outs for children to colour in today’s temperature.
Using the flashcards or mime, pre-teach or elicit the remaining vocabulary items.
Look out of the classroom window and ask: What’s the weather like today? Elicit the answer using ‘It’s … [rainy]’. Prompt children to repeat individually and in chorus.
Play some flashcard guessing games. Remove all the cards and shuffle them. Take one and ask children to guess using ‘Is it …?’.
Using adhesive putty, attach the cards face down on the board and number them.
In pairs, children play a game. Child A chooses a card and writes down the number secretly and Child B guesses by asking ‘Is it …?’ questions: e.g. Is it sunny? Is it snowy?
Monitor actively and praise children for working together supportively.
Say some months of the summer season and ask children: Does it get hot in summer here?
Using the flashcards, ask children to tell you about the seasons in your country using ‘It gets … [cold]’ and ‘It doesn’t get … [warm]’.
In small groups, children play a game. One child thinks of a season and the others have to guess what it is by asking questions about the season: e.g. Does it get cool?
Hold a whole-class feedback session and praise children for their group work.
Additional tasks
Complete exs.A–C in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.68 and exs.A–E in Oxford Discover WB 1 pp.56–57.
Write a sentence about today’s weather: e.g. It’s June. It gets hot in summer in my country. Today it’s hot.
Learning review
Children have understood how we can measure temperature. They have learned new vocabulary to describe the weather and have talked about how it might change in different seasons, depending on their local context.
Differentiation
Form mixed-ability pairs in the flashcard guessing game so more confident children can support less confident children with the learning of new topic vocabulary.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to make a list of / draw places where we might find a thermometer (e.g. in a car, in a hospital, an oven, etc.).
