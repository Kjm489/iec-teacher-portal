LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Working with Numbers
Week: 23
Lesson: 3
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.1e, 1.1f

Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about numbers and number relationships using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask: How many children are in class today? Is it an odd or an even number? Is it possible to count you all in twos? Depending on the class size, decide to test out a number: e.g. Let’s see if I can count you all in fives. Repeat with a different number.

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “thumb” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Check meaning: If we add one more object, does the number stay the same? Show how you know.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “shoe” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Show children the flashcard or picture of the ant. Elicit the word, then explain that ants are usually in groups. Ask: Do you think ants march in twos or threes? Play the first verse of the song. Children listen for how the ants march (one by one). Write on the board and support children to sound out the following words from the song: ‘thumb’, ‘shoe’, ‘tree’, ‘door’, ‘dive’, ‘sticks’, ‘roller skate’, ‘time’, and ‘the end’. Ask children to listen…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Are we counting the objects or guessing? How can we check the answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “tree” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “door” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate numbers and number relationships vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “dive” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, put them in a group with more confident children. Challenge more able children to make a new verse for another number.

Extension: Explain a number pattern or check a classmate’s calculation with objects.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “sticks” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have sung a song with numbers and learned about rhyming words.

Exit ticket: Solve or represent a new number example and explain the answer.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “thumb” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

Preparation: Prepare visual or word cards for thumb, shoe, tree, door. Check they appear in the original lesson; do not introduce unrelated content.

Teacher models — I DO: Show the original lesson example for “thumb”. Think aloud while selecting a matching picture, action or spoken response. Ask pupils to explain or demonstrate why it matches.

Game instructions: Give commands using target verbs and phrases. Pupils act only when preceded by “Teacher says”; discuss the meaning after each round.

Guided round — WE DO: Complete one round together; elicit a response from several pupils and correct any confusion before pairs begin.

ICQ: Are you going to look or listen before giving your answer?

CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “thumb” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

Scaffolding: Offer a two-choice visual prompt and the frame “It is ___” or “I can ___” as appropriate for Song time!.

Challenge: Ask learners to give a second example or explain a difference using because.

Assessment: Check two pupils individually: can each demonstrate or explain the meaning of the lesson target for “Song time!” without copying? Record independent / prompted / not yet.
