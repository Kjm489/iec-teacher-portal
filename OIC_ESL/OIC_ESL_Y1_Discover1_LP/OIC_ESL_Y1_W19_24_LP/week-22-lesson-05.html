LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Working with Numbers
Week: 22
Lesson: 5
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.3e, 1.4b

Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about numbers and number relationships using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Play a game. Place a long piece of string or rope on the classroom or playground floor. Instruct children to stand on it in a line, one behind the other. Tell them that this is not a race and that everyone must wait three seconds before jumping. When you say an odd number, children jump to the left; when you say an even number, children jump to the right. Say numbers in a random order. Confirm children’s understanding of left and right before…

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “one” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Check meaning: If we add one more object, does the number stay the same? Show how you know.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “one” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Hold up a ‘one’ word card and ask a child to read the number. Do this with the rest of the numbers, but not in order. As children read the words, stick them on the board (not in order). Practise reading the words with the class. Tell children to copy the words down as you drill, writing the numeral next to each. On the board, write ‘I have six pens and you have two pens. How many pens do we have?’. If necessary, hold up six pens and ask a child…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Are we counting the objects or guessing? How can we check the answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “one” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “one” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate numbers and number relationships vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “one” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, ask them to copy down the words for 1 to 10 only. As an extension task for more able children, teach them numbers 20 to 30. Additional information: Week 22, Lesson 5 Read and answer. Gabrielle has four pencils and I have three. How many do we have? Ming has seven buckets and Li has five. How many do they have? Suki has eight orange flowers and I have three red flowers. How many do we have? They have seven red…

Extension: Explain a number pattern or check a classmate’s calculation with objects.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “one” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned the written form of numbers 1 to 20. They have put their learning into practice by reading, solving, and creating some addition problems.

Exit ticket: Solve or represent a new number example and explain the answer.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “one” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
