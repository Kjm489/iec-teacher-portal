LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Working with Numbers
Week: 23
Lesson: 4
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.2b, 1.2e, 1.2f

Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about numbers and number relationships using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Throw a dice, or spin the spinner, and write the number on the board. Repeat. Convert the two numbers into an addition problem: e.g. 5 + 3 = ?. Elicit ‘eight’ from children. Ask: What other two numbers can I throw/spin to get number 8 again? (e.g. 4 + 4 or 2 + 6). Repeat with other numbers. Invite a couple of volunteers to the front to lead the activity.

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “eight” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Check meaning: If we add one more object, does the number stay the same? Show how you know.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “eight” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Ask children to think about which numbers they have heard, said, or thought about so far today (e.g. the page number in another lesson’s coursebook). Turn to ex.E in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.95. Play the audio (track 2.08). Ask: How old are the two boys? (seven years old) Elicit the question(s) and answer(s) from the audio: How old are you? I’m (five). How about you? I’m (five), too. Encourage children to think about other occasions when we might…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Are we counting the objects or guessing? How can we check the answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “eight” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “eight” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate numbers and number relationships vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “eight” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Provide less confident children with the full questions or question starters. Challenge more confident children to think of other questions whose answers include numbers, such as the number of toys they have. Additional information: Week 23, Lesson 4 Write your information here. Name You Age Birthday number (e.g. 10 06 for 10th June) House number Phone number Pets? How many …? Ask your friend questions and write their answers here. Name Your…

Extension: Explain a number pattern or check a classmate’s calculation with objects.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “eight” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have practised asking and answering personal questions with numbers.

Exit ticket: Solve or represent a new number example and explain the answer.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “eight” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
