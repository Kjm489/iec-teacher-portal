Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about seasons and weather using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Elicit the four seasons and the twelve months of the year from children. Establish which are months and which are seasons. Label each corner of the room as a different season. Say different months in a random order and children move to the correct corner, in line with the seasonal patterns in your context.

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Weather and seasons”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Check meaning: If a picture shows snow, is it more likely winter or summer? What clue helps?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Weather and seasons”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Look out of your classroom window and ask inquisitively: What’s the weather like today? Elicit responses. Show the flashcards and introduce/recall weather vocabulary. Play some flashcard games to help familiarize children with the words. For example, put the flashcards on the board and ask children to repeat the words. Then remove the cards from the board one by one. Challenge children to remember the words and say them in the correct order. If…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Do all places have the same weather in the same season? What do we see in this example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Weather and seasons”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Weather and seasons”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate seasons and weather vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Weather and seasons”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less confident children, play the game in an adult-led small group instead of in pairs. Ensure that they have grasped the new vocabulary first. Challenge more able children to make sentences including the weather and the temperature: Today is Thursday and the weather is warm and sunny.

Extension: Compare two seasonal scenes and justify one difference.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Weather and seasons”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have practised weather and days of the week vocabulary across different activities.

Exit ticket: Identify a season from a new picture and give one reason.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “Weather and seasons”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 20, Lesson 1: Weather and seasons
Learning outcomes: 1.1a, 1.2b
Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their vocabulary through listening and speaking activities.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Elicit the four seasons and the twelve months of the year from children. Establish which are months and which are seasons.
Label each corner of the room as a different season. Say different months in a random order and children move to the correct corner, in line with the seasonal patterns in your context.
Main activity
Look out of your classroom window and ask inquisitively: What’s the weather like today? Elicit responses.
Show the flashcards and introduce/recall weather vocabulary. Play some flashcard games to help familiarize children with the words. For example, put the flashcards on the board and ask children to repeat the words. Then remove the cards from the board one by one. Challenge children to remember the words and say them in the correct order.
If not done previously, put the flashcards on the board in a random order. Play the audio in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.74 ex.A. Tell children to listen and to number the weather flashcards.
In pairs, children work together to remember which was number 1: e.g. Number 1 is ‘It’s rainy’.
Then put days of the week word cards on the board. Make sentences using a day of the week and a weather type. Demonstrate an example orally: e.g. It’s Monday and it’s snowy.
Children repeat the activity in pairs. Tell them to draw their own weather cards and to secretly write a different day of the week underneath each one. They can say sentences as a dictation for their partner, or they can play a guessing game: e.g. Is it rainy on Monday? Yes, it is. / No, it isn’t.
To end the lesson, ask children to write a sentence describing today’s weather: e.g. Today is Thursday and the weather is sunny.
Additional tasks
Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.62.
Create a weather chart with the days of the week along the top for children to complete with words and pictures.
Learning review
Children have practised weather and days of the week vocabulary across different activities.
Differentiation
To support less confident children, play the game in an adult-led small group instead of in pairs. Ensure that they have grasped the new vocabulary first.
Challenge more able children to make sentences including the weather and the temperature: Today is Thursday and the weather is warm and sunny.
