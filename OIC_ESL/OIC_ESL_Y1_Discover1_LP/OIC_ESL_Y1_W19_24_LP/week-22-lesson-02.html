LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Working with Numbers
Week: 22
Lesson: 2
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.1a, 1.2c

Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about numbers and number relationships using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Put children into small groups and ask them to spell out some words with their bodies. Spell out ‘six’. Then ask children to write the letters on their whiteboards and to tell you what the number is – number 6. Challenge children to find six of something in the classroom. Repeat with a few more numbers.

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “six” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Check meaning: If we add one more object, does the number stay the same? Show how you know.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “six” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Using the flashcards, elicit or pre-teach the vocabulary. Play the audio in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.88 ex.A (track 2.02). Ask children to listen and repeat. Play some flashcard games. Ask children to close their eyes, then remove a card from the board. Ask them to open their eyes and tell you which one is missing. Continue with a few more. Invite a confident child to come to the front and to do the same. Children play a similar game in pairs.…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Are we counting the objects or guessing? How can we check the answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “six” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “six” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate numbers and number relationships vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “six” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, provide them with some counters to use when counting. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write out an addition problem and to label all the parts using the new vocabulary learned.

Extension: Explain a number pattern or check a classmate’s calculation with objects.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “six” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have consolidated their understanding of number words in number games.

Exit ticket: Solve or represent a new number example and explain the answer.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “six” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
