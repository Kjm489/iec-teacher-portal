PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask children to tell you some different activities, eliciting vocabulary learned earlier in the week or in previous units. Remind them that they have to practise saying new English words and phrases over and over in order to remember them. Play a mime game with these words – mime the activity in a humorous way and children guess the word or phrase. Then invite a volunteer to the front to do a mime. Put children into pairs. Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “What do we do in each season?”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Check meaning: If a picture shows snow, is it more likely winter or summer? What clue helps?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “What do we do in each season?”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Ask children what activities they do in winter, and what activities they do in summer. This could be a pairwork activity. Make it competitive by asking children to think of as many activities as possible that they can only do in each season. Play the audio for exs.C–D in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.82. Elicit answers to the question: What do you do in [spring/summer/autumn/winter]? Support children to allocate themselves two seasons. On the board,…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Do all places have the same weather in the same season? What do we see in this example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “What do we do in each season?”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “What do we do in each season?”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate seasons and weather vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “What do we do in each season?”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Less confident children can focus on just one season in the question and answer activity. Challenge more able children to explain why they (don’t) like doing different seasonal activities using because: e.g. I don’t like picking apples in autumn because my arms hurt.

Extension: Compare two seasonal scenes and justify one difference.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “What do we do in each season?”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have identified different activities in a listening activity. They have asked and answered questions about what they do in two different seasons.

Exit ticket: Identify a season from a new picture and give one reason.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: Using the lesson example for “What do we do in each season?”, which picture, action or response shows the target meaning? Explain or demonstrate.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 20, Lesson 4: What do we do in each season?
Learning outcomes: 1.1a, 1.2b, 1.2e
Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by learning about what others do in different seasons, then asking and answering questions.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you some different activities, eliciting vocabulary learned earlier in the week or in previous units. Remind them that they have to practise saying new English words and phrases over and over in order to remember them.
Play a mime game with these words – mime the activity in a humorous way and children guess the word or phrase. Then invite a volunteer to the front to do a mime.
Put children into pairs. Child A mimes, Child B guesses, and then they swap.
Main activity
Ask children what activities they do in winter, and what activities they do in summer. This could be a pairwork activity. Make it competitive by asking children to think of as many activities as possible that they can only do in each season.
Play the audio for exs.C–D in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.82.
Elicit answers to the question: What do you do in [spring/summer/autumn/winter]?
Support children to allocate themselves two seasons. On the board, number the four seasons 1–4. Then children are going to roll a dice twice. If they get 1–4, the season is as per the board. If they roll a 5, someone chooses for them. If they roll a 6, they choose.
Explain that they are going to ask a partner questions about these two seasons.
Encourage children to find a partner who has different seasons to the ones they have been allocated (as far as possible). Children ask their partner the ‘What do you do in …?’ question and note down or sketch a picture of their answers.
Put children into small groups. They share what they have learned about their partner’s seasonal activities in turn.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Find out which was the most popular activity for each season. Repeat with the least popular activity.
Additional tasks
Complete exs.C–D in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.69 and exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.70.
Record the questionnaire results in sentences or pictorially.
Draw a picture of their classmate doing their chosen seasonal activity.
Learning review
Children have identified different activities in a listening activity. They have asked and answered questions about what they do in two different seasons.
Differentiation
Less confident children can focus on just one season in the question and answer activity.
Challenge more able children to explain why they (don’t) like doing different seasonal activities using because: e.g. I don’t like picking apples in autumn because my arms hurt.
