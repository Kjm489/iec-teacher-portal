Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about seasons and weather using the original OIC lesson tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask children to close their eyes and to breathe in and then to make a long breath out. Continue this for a few breaths. Children open their eyes. Ask them how they are feeling. Refer to the feelings clouds from the previous lesson. Tell children that today’s activities might feel a little frustrating. Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “fall” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Check meaning: If a picture shows snow, is it more likely winter or summer? What clue helps?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “fall” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Pretend to the class that you know absolutely nothing about autumn (or ‘fall’ – US Eng.) and winter. Ask children to give you some words or phrases about these seasons. Write or draw their ideas on the board. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.71. Tell children to read the text about winter and autumn/fall and see if any of their ideas are in the text. Allow them to compare their ideas in pairs. Explain to children that in today’s lesson they are…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Do all places have the same weather in the same season? What do we see in this example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “fall” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “fall” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate seasons and weather vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “fall” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Pair up less confident children with more able children so the latter can guide the former through the instructions and demonstrate what the imperative verbs mean. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to make more snowflakes with different patterns. Extension: Compare two seasonal scenes and justify one difference.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “fall” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have followed a short set of instructions to complete a seasons-related craft activity.

Exit ticket: Identify a season from a new picture and give one reason.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “fall” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 19, Lesson 4: Autumn and winter
Learning outcomes: 1.3d, 1.3f
Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by completing a craft activity.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to close their eyes and to breathe in and then to make a long breath out.
Continue this for a few breaths. Children open their eyes.
Ask them how they are feeling. Refer to the feelings clouds from the previous lesson.
Tell children that today’s activities might feel a little frustrating. At any time during the lesson when they feel stretched or think they can’t do something, remind them to breathe deeply. This should focus their attention, calm their nerves, and enable them to continue.
Main activity
Pretend to the class that you know absolutely nothing about autumn (or ‘fall’ – US Eng.) and winter. Ask children to give you some words or phrases about these seasons. Write or draw their ideas on the board.
Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.71. Tell children to read the text about winter and autumn/fall and see if any of their ideas are in the text. Allow them to compare their ideas in pairs.
Explain to children that in today’s lesson they are going to get creative! First, pre-teach some words for the craft activities, such as ‘cut’, ‘fold’, ‘rub’, ‘wax crayon’.
Show children your pre-prepared snowflake and leaf rubbing. Ask them which one they would like to make the most. Say that you are not going to tell them or show them how to do it, but they are going to follow some written instructions. This activity will stretch children as they try to understand the meaning of the instructions with minimal teacher input, drawing upon their existing knowledge of English.
Remind children to breathe deeply if they are feeling anxious or unsure.
Divide the class into two groups – snowflakes and leaves.
Hand out the instructions and craft equipment.
Individually or in pairs, children follow the instructions to make their snowflakes or leaf rubbings.
Monitor actively, praising and helping children as they work through the instructions.
Children display their snowflakes and leaves around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete exs.A–D in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.59.
Complete the second craft task.
Draw a picture of a tree in autumn or winter.
In pairs, children can play a game. One child says a sentence or a word and the other says which season it belongs to.
Learning review
Children have followed a short set of instructions to complete a seasons-related craft activity.
Differentiation
Pair up less confident children with more able children so the latter can guide the former through the instructions and demonstrate what the imperative verbs mean.
As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to make more snowflakes with different patterns.
Additional information: Week 19, Lesson 4
How to make a snowflake
You need:
A square piece of paper
Instructions:
Fold the paper in half.
Keep folding it in half. Take your scissors and cut shapes along the folds.
Unfold it carefully.
How to make a leaf rubbing
You need:
A big leaf
Lightweight paper
A wax crayon
Instructions:
Place the leaf face down.
Put the paper over it.
Rub the side of the wax crayon gently over the paper.
