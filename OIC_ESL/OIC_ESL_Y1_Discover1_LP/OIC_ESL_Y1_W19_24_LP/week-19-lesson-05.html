LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Seasons
Week: 19
Lesson: 5
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.4a, 1.4b

Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about seasons and weather using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Show the class the pictures you have for each season. Encourage children to describe what they can see and to say which season it represents. If you have a playground, you could take children outside for this lesson.

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The Season” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Check meaning: If a picture shows snow, is it more likely winter or summer? What clue helps?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The Season” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Explain that some people like to write poems about the seasons. Ask if anyone has ever read a poem about a season. Look at ‘The Season’s of Arnold’s Apple Tree’ on p.78 of Oxford Discover SB 1, or use the short, simple poems on p.13, or source your own poem. Choose one of them to write on the board. Elicit its features. Allow children a few minutes to choose a season to write about in their own poem. In pairs, children note down any key…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Do all places have the same weather in the same season? What do we see in this example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The Season” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The Season” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate seasons and weather vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The Season” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, provide them with something tangible for inspiration (e.g. autumn leaves), as well as showing them some more pictures or playing a video of some seasonal sounds (e.g. bees buzzing, lambs bleating, spring showers, etc.). As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to write another verse for their poem, or another poem about a different aspect of a season. Additional information: Week 19, Lesson 5…

Extension: Compare two seasonal scenes and justify one difference.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The Season” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have recalled their learning from the week and written a poem about their favourite season.

Exit ticket: Identify a season from a new picture and give one reason.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “The Season” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
