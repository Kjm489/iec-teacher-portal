Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about numbers and number relationships using the original OIC lesson tasks.

  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Tell children that they are going to complete an activity independently. You are going to dictate some numbers (say how many) in quick succession. Children write the numerals on their whiteboards in the order that you say them. Ask children how many times they think they will need to listen to the numbers before they get the full sequence. Tell children to raise their hand when they have finished. See who manages to get all the numbers in the…

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Numbers” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Check meaning: If we add one more object, does the number stay the same? Show how you know.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Counting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.90. Listen to the audio (track 2.03 – sections headed ‘Numbers’ and ‘Counting’). Invite a confident volunteer to explain the difference between these numbers in their own words – even numbers (ending in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8) can be divided into two equal groups, but odd numbers (ending in 1, 3, 5, 7, 9) cannot. Model counting the children in the classroom in twos. Elicit that these are even numbers. Ask the class if they…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Are we counting the objects or guessing? How can we check the answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “How many …?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: The number is ___. / I know because ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Numbers” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate numbers and number relationships vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Counting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, suggest sentence starters for their quiz questions (e.g. ‘How many …?’). Ask more confident children to make a note of significant numbers in their lives (e.g. age, date of birth, house number, year group). Do they have more odd or more even numbers? 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Exit ticket: Solve or represent a new number example and explain the answer.

YEAR 1 Working with numbers
Week 22, Lesson 4: Odd and even numbers
Learning outcomes: 1.1a, 1.2c
Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Working with numbers’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by creating and completing a counting quiz.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Tell children that they are going to complete an activity independently.
You are going to dictate some numbers (say how many) in quick succession. Children write the numerals on their whiteboards in the order that you say them.
Ask children how many times they think they will need to listen to the numbers before they get the full sequence.
Tell children to raise their hand when they have finished. See who manages to get all the numbers in the correct order first. Were their predictions accurate?
Main activity
Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.90. Listen to the audio (track 2.03 – sections headed ‘Numbers’ and ‘Counting’).
Invite a confident volunteer to explain the difference between these numbers in their own words – even numbers (ending in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8) can be divided into two equal groups, but odd numbers (ending in 1, 3, 5, 7, 9) cannot.
Model counting the children in the classroom in twos. Elicit that these are even numbers. Ask the class if they think it’s easier to count things in small groups like this.
Hand out the worksheets or give the class the activity instructions. Children colour in the grids to see if a number is odd or even. This activity is designed to stretch children, helping them to better visualize the concept of these two types of number.
Put children into small groups to complete the quiz activity. There are five questions. Ask them how long they think they will need to complete the activity, then allow them enough time to complete it. They might be surprised at how much time they needed!
Check answers as a class.
Now ask children to write three extra questions for another group.
Groups swap questions and work together to answer them. Remind children to involve all of their group members in this activity by allowing their less confident classmates to suggest an answer.
Bring the class back together. Have a discussion about how children found the activity and how confident they are now feeling about odd and even numbers.
Count together in twos as a whole class, forwards and backwards.
Additional tasks
Complete exs.A–D in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.77. Tell children to count the items in the pictures and decide whether the total amount is an odd or even number.
Hand out the counters to groups. Say a number and children count out the counters, decide if the amount is odd or even, and decide if the total amount of remaining counters is odd or even.
Learning review
Children have tested their understanding of odd and even numbers in a quiz activity.
Differentiation
To support less able children, suggest sentence starters for their quiz questions (e.g. ‘How many …?’).
Ask more confident children to make a note of significant numbers in their lives (e.g. age, date of birth, house number, year group). Do they have more odd or more even numbers?
