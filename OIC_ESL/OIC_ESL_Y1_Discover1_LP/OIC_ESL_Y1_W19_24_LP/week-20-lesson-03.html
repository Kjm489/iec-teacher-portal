LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Seasons
Week: 20
Lesson: 3
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.3b, 1.3e

Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about seasons and weather using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Draw on the board a big square divided into four colours: blue, orange, green, and red. In the blue square, add the words ‘bored’ and ‘tired’. In the orange square, add the words ‘happy’ and ‘relaxed’. In the green square, add the words ‘silly’ and ‘worried’. In the red square, add the words ‘angry’ and ‘frightened’. Ensure that children understand the general feeling for each colour. Ask them to note down the colour that best represents how…

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “bored” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Check meaning: If a picture shows snow, is it more likely winter or summer? What clue helps?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “tired” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 pp.78–79. Tell children that they are going to read a story called ‘The Seasons of Arnold’s Apple Tree’. Before reading, give them some of the words from the story and see if they can predict what it will be about in pairs. Use the Think-Pair-Share technique. Invite volunteers to share their ideas and write them on the board. Provide handouts or ask children to open their books. Read each paragraph as a class.…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Do all places have the same weather in the same season? What do we see in this example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “happy” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “relaxed” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate seasons and weather vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “silly” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, pre-teach and write the meaning of some of the key words on the board as a reference first. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to find another story about something that changes in the seasons – for instance, of an animal in hibernation.

Extension: Compare two seasonal scenes and justify one difference.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “worried” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have predicted the content of and read a short story about the seasons. They have checked their understanding of their reading through comprehension exercises.

Exit ticket: Identify a season from a new picture and give one reason.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “bored” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
