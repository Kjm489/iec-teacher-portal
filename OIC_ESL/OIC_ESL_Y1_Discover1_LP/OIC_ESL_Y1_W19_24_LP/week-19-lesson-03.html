LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1

Week: 19

Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.3d, 1.3f

Measurable Learning Objectives

  1. Identify or demonstrate key ideas about seasons and weather using the original OIC lesson tasks.
  2. Use appropriate lesson language during a guided or paired response.
  3. Complete one independent task and explain or show the answer.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask children how they are feeling today. If this is the first time you are doing this sort of activity, begin with ‘happy’, ‘sad’, ‘tired’, or ‘OK’. Otherwise, you can add more words like ‘angry’, ‘worried’, and ‘excited’. Don’t ask children why – just accept their feelings. Show children the cloud cut-outs and invite them to draw a face showing how they are feeling inside the cloud. Support them to write the word to describe the feeling.

Diagnostic: Ask students to name, point to, or demonstrate one example before teaching.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “happy” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Demonstrate the first task from the original OIC main activity using a visual or concrete example. Think aloud and model the target language: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Check meaning: If a picture shows snow, is it more likely winter or summer? What clue helps?

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “sad” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Pretend to the class that you know absolutely nothing about spring and summer. Ask children to give you some words or phrases about these seasons. Write or draw their ideas on the board. Now tell them that you have two short texts about spring and summer. Which one would they like to read? Hold up the envelopes. Use the texts from Oxford Discover SB 1 p.70. Open one of the envelopes, look inside, and exclaim dramatically, Oh no! something has…

Complete one example together. Elicit an answer, ask for the evidence, and correct a deliberate mistake.

CCQ: Do all places have the same weather in the same season? What do we see in this example?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “tired” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In pairs, learners complete a fresh example using the pictures, book task or manipulatives. Partner A responds; Partner B checks and explains; then switch.

ICQs: Are you working alone or with a partner? Who checks the answer? Do you swap roles after one turn?

Support: It is ___. / In ___, I can see ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “OK” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Learners complete the corresponding original OIC book/activity task individually. Select an unfamiliar example to check transfer, rather than repeating the model.

Success criteria: accurate response, appropriate seasons and weather vocabulary, and evidence of independent understanding.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “angry” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Support less confident children in an adult-led small group. Before beginning the activity, elicit what begins a sentence (a capital letter) and what ends a sentence (a full stop). As an extension task for more able children, ask them to complete a Venn diagram about the two seasons, indicating similarities and differences.

Extension: Compare two seasonal scenes and justify one difference.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “worried” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have worked together to reconstruct two short texts about spring and summer.

Exit ticket: Identify a season from a new picture and give one reason.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “happy” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 19, Lesson 3: Spring and summer
Learning outcomes: 1.3d, 1.3f
Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by reading two texts about two different seasons. They will practise problem-solving in a fun activity.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children how they are feeling today. If this is the first time you are doing this sort of activity, begin with ‘happy’, ‘sad’, ‘tired’, or ‘OK’. Otherwise, you can add more words like ‘angry’, ‘worried’, and ‘excited’. Don’t ask children why – just accept their feelings.
Show children the cloud cut-outs and invite them to draw a face showing how they are feeling inside the cloud. Support them to write the word to describe the feeling.
Main activity
Pretend to the class that you know absolutely nothing about spring and summer. Ask children to give you some words or phrases about these seasons. Write or draw their ideas on the board.
Now tell them that you have two short texts about spring and summer. Which one would they like to read? Hold up the envelopes. Use the texts from Oxford Discover SB 1 p.70.
Open one of the envelopes, look inside, and exclaim dramatically, Oh no! something has happened … the sentences are all cut up! Place the words for just one of the sentences on the board and challenge children to help you to reorder them into a logical sentence.
Then put children into pairs and divide the class in two. One half will reconstruct the ‘spring’ sentences and the other half will reconstruct the ‘summer’ sentences. Hand out the pre-prepared envelopes.
Allow children enough time to make a good attempt at the activity.
Hold a whole-class feedback session and write the final sentences on the board.
Ask children to reflect on their initial predictions – were any of their words or ideas about the seasons mentioned in the sentences?
Tell children to read the two short texts, now complete, and to try to write another sentence for each season.
Additional tasks
Complete exs.A–B in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.58.
Draw a picture of a tree in spring or summer.
In pairs, children can play a game. One child says a sentence or a word and the other says which season it belongs to.
Learning review
Children have worked together to reconstruct two short texts about spring and summer.
Differentiation
Support less confident children in an adult-led small group. Before beginning the activity, elicit what begins a sentence (a capital letter) and what ends a sentence (a full stop).
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to complete a Venn diagram about the two seasons, indicating similarities and differences.
