Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.4a, 1.4b) through a task on “Country house, town house”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Elicit from the class some words and phrases to describe their house.

Put children into pairs to say as many sentences as they can about their house. Tell them to ‘talk’ to each other like a game of tennis – one child says something, then their partner says something different, and so on.

Invite volunteers to share facts about their partner’s house.

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “talk” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Ask a confident child: Is your neighbourhood (home) quiet or noisy? Then recast their answer using ‘his’ or ‘her’: e.g. His neighbourhood is …

Introduce children to possessive adjectives, giving them a simple grammar explanation.

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “his” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Complete Oxford Discover SB 1 p.141.

Hand out a picture of a home to each child. Each one should be different, if possible.

Tell children to think about what their home is like, what the neighbourhood is like, and if it is in the town or country. Encourage them to be creative with their ideas.

CCQ: Does a neighbourhood mean just one room, or the area around where people live? How does your example fit?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “her” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Invite a confident child to say something about their ‘new home’. Then model recasting their sentence using: His/Her home is [in a noisy neighbourhood. It is next to a playground].

Write these sentences on the board.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “new home” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Put children into pairs. First, encourage them to ask and answer questions about each other’s imaginary home.

Next, ask them to write two or three sentences about their partner’s home independently, based on what they heard.

Monitor actively, offering support and eliciting ideas where required. Give children time to complete and read through their sentences.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Country house, town house” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text. Spend more time teaching possessive adjectives in a small group.

As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to ask and write about the rooms in their partner’s home.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “talk” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have written a few sentences about a classmate’s imaginary home using possessive adjectives.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “Country house, town house” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “his” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 28, Lesson 5: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
