Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.3a, 1.3b) through a task on “Where is your home?”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

On the board, draw a block of cheese, a rat, and a cat. Then draw a house. Say: This is the house that Jack built. Then indicate to the cheese and say: This is the cheese that lay in the house that Jack built. Continue with as many lines as appropriate for your children. Mime as you present each item to aid understanding and make the rhyme more fun to say.

Encourage children to repeat each sentence after you.

Instruction check: ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Show the children pictures of a town, the country(side), and a city. Elicit the vocabulary.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.130. You can also play the audio, so children are listening and reading at the same time.

Model the first response aloud. Show real-life photos → point/name → model “There is/There are…” → pair practice → personal response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “there is” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Ask children to cover the text and to tell you what they think it will be about.

Use this moment to revise ‘there is’ and ‘there are’. For example, say: There are rivers near towns and cities. There is a boat on the river. Elicit more sentences about the pictures using ‘there is’ and ‘there are’.

Ask children to quickly read the texts, or read it through with them. Did they guess the content correctly?

CCQ: Is a bedroom a room or a piece of furniture? Which part of the picture proves your answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “there are” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Put children into groups of three and assign roles. Tell one child to read about towns, one child to read about the country, and one child to read about cities. Children read their text, then close their books. Encourage them to say one sentence about what they have just read, sharing it with their group.

Monitor actively and praise children for their effort in reading and trying to understand their text independently. Recognize how they are improving.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Where is your home?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

As a round-up, ask children if they enjoyed the reading tasks today. Invite them to share which parts they found most enjoyable or difficult.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, provide some questions to guide their reading.

Challenge fast finishers to think of another sentence they could add to the text.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “there is” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have read and recognized key vocabulary in a short text about different places to live.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “Where is your home?” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “there are” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
