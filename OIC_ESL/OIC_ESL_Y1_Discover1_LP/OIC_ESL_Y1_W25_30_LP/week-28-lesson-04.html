Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.2a, 1.3a) through a task on “The town mouse and the country mouse (2)”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask the class if they can remember last lesson’s story about the town mouse and the country mouse. Work together with children to retell the main events as far as you got up to. Elicit details that they can remember.

Ask: How do you think the town mouse is feeling? Would you feel the same in this situation?

Ask: What do you think will happen next? What would you like to happen?

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “moral” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Read the rest of the story with children (see Oxford Discover SB 1 p.139 or your chosen version of the text).

As a class, talk about how the ending compared with children’s ideas on the board. Which ending do they prefer?

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “moral” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.140.

Ask children to consider how the country mouse felt in the town. Was it the same as the town mouse in the country? What was the same and what was different? Encourage them to relate it to their own experience(s) of going somewhere different.

Retell the story with children using finger puppets.

CCQ: Does a neighbourhood mean just one room, or the area around where people live? How does your example fit?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “moral” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Then support them to make their own town mouse and country mouse finger puppet designs, based on the simple instructions you find.

Put children into pairs to retell the story using their finger puppets.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “moral” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Bring the class back together. Ask children: Who do you think has a better life – the country mouse or the town mouse? Why do the mice go back to their homes? What is the ‘moral’ of the story? (Explain that this is the lesson we can learn from the story – it is better to be happy and feel safe with little, rather than have a lot but feel unhappy and frightened.)

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “moral” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, write some key words or draw some pictures on the board to help them. You might want to look at the text more closely in an adult-led small group.

As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to think of other places the mice might have visited.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “moral” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have read the rest of the story and worked together to understand its moral. They have practised retelling it in pairwork.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “The town mouse and the country mouse (2)” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “moral” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 28, Lesson 4: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
