Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.2b, 1.2e, 1.2f) through a task on “What is your favourite place? (2)”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Revise place names with children by playing a flashcard game. Explain that by thinking again about newly learned vocabulary, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

Indicate to a flashcard and say a word. For instance, gesture to the park and say ‘park’. This is correct, so children should stand up. Then gesture to the library and say ‘museum’. Children should sit down or remain seated as it is incorrect.

Once everyone has grasped the game, you can quicken the pace. If children get it wrong (i.e. they stand when they are supposed to be sitting, or vice versa), they are out.

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “park” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Ask the class: What are your favourite places? Elicit some answers. Tell children that they are going to play a game about these favourite places.

Show them your flashcards. Then hand out paper and, in unison, show children how to fold and cut their paper into six flashcards of their own.

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “museum” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Give children time to draw the places and write the names underneath – one on each card.

Introduce the ‘Happy families’ game. Choose three confident children to model the game with you. All children put their cards together, and one child shuffles and deals out four cards to each player. The remaining cards are placed in a pile. The objective is to get a group of four (e.g. four museums or four supermarkets). To get a card, children must ask one of their classmates in turn: Have you got a library? If their classmate has a library flashcard (Yes, I have!), they must hand it over. If they haven’t (No, I haven’t!), the child who asks the question must select a card from the pile. This continues until there aren’t any cards left. The winner is the person with the most groups or ‘families’ at the end.

Check that everybody understands the rules. Ask some instruction-checking questions.

CCQ: Does a neighbourhood mean just one room, or the area around where people live? How does your example fit?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Happy families” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Put children into groups of four and encourage them to think of a fair way to decide who is going to start.

Monitor actively as children play, paying attention to their use of vocabulary, their ability to ask and answer questions, and if they take turns. Discreetly note down any mistakes.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “families” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Bring the class back together and quickly talk about how they found the game. Write examples of children’s mistakes on the board and encourage the class to identify the problem and to self-correct.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.142 and complete exs.C–D.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What is your favourite place? (2)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Ask more confident children to explain the rules of the game to their less confident peers, modelling each step in a practice round.

Fast finishers can tell you why the places are their favourites: e.g. The shopkeeper is friendly.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “park” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have played a game about favourite places in a neighbourhood.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “What is your favourite place? (2)” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “museum” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 29, Lesson 2: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
