LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES

Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)

Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.3d, 1.3e) through a task on “The town mouse and the country mouse (1)”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Put children into small groups of four or five. Tell them you are going to say some letters, and they need to make the letters with their bodies. Say the letters in ‘town’. Next, ask children to tell you the four letters. Elicit ‘town’.

Repeat for ‘country’.

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “town” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Tell children that they are going to read a story based on the Aesop’s fable ‘The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse’. Make sure that they recognize the word ‘mouse’, showing them a picture to support their understanding.

Put children into groups. Encourage them to imagine what a town mouse and a country mouse might look like – in terms of colour, clothing, surroundings, activities, etc.

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “country” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

They can draw and label a picture to show their ideas.

Share some descriptions as a class and focus on the contrast: e.g. My town mouse wears a smart, red coat. My country mouse wears an old, yellow hat.

Elicit suggestions on what the mice might eat and do, and where they might sleep.

CCQ: Does a neighbourhood mean just one room, or the area around where people live? How does your example fit?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “mouse” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

In their groups, give children time to predict what the story will be about.

Then turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.138, or your chosen version of the text, and read the first half of the story as a class. If possible, invite confident volunteers to take it in turns to read out a few sentences.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “town” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

After the first reading, discuss if children’s predictions were correct. In the same groups, give them time to compare their ideas with what happened in the story. How was it the same or different?

Tell groups they are going to work together to act out the first part of the story. They will need to invent additional characters, and perhaps add more conversation to incorporate new ideas. Encourage them to be as creative as they like!

Invite volunteer groups to perform in front of the class.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “country” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Create mixed-ability groups so children can support and encourage each other, modelling key topic vocabulary. For additional guidance in the prediction task, offer groups some ideas about the story, including one detail that isn’t there.

Challenge more able children to say some True/False statements about the story for their group.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “mouse” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have read a story that illustrates the differences between a city/town and the country. They have expressed themselves creatively in a drama activity.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “The town mouse and the country mouse (1)” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “town” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 28, Lesson 3: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
