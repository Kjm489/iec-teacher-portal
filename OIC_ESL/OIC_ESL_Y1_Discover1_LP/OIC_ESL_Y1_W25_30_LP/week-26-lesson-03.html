LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Homes

Lesson: 3
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
Oic Outcomes: 1.2a, 1.2b

Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.2a, 1.2b) through a task on “Same or different?”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask children whether they generally say something as soon as they think of it, or if they pause to reflect on their answer first. Tell them that today they are going to practise ‘waiting’. When they know the answer to something, encourage them to use their fingers to count to 5 first before putting their hand up.

Play a mime game. Mime an activity in a room for children to guess the room. Remind them to wait before responding.

Now increase the challenge by miming an activity in a room and, to answer, children must say an item of furniture in that room. For example, if you mime sleeping, a child might say ‘bed’. If they say the room, they are ‘out’. Remind children to wait before responding!

Instruction check: ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “waiting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Draw/Show a picture of a living room. Add some furniture items (e.g. a lamp, a sofa, a TV).

Elicit the names of these items and work together to make sentences using ‘there is’ and ‘there are’. If necessary, drill the sentences.

Model the first response aloud. Show real-life photos → point/name → model “There is/There are…” → pair practice → personal response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “bed” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Ask children to notice when we use ‘there is’ and when we use ‘there are’. Elicit some sentences about the classroom.

Instruct children to draw an outline of a bedroom. Draw one on the board for them to copy. Then do a picture dictation. Say:

In the bedroom, there is a desk. There is a lamp on the desk. There are three books on the desk. Next to the desk, there is a chair. There is a window and there is a bed under the window. Look! My teddy bear is on the bed.

CCQ: Is a bedroom a room or a piece of furniture? Which part of the picture proves your answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “out” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Invite children to look at each other’s pictures. They will be different, but the items should be the same.

In pairs, encourage children to make another picture. They must decide on a room together, as well as which items to draw in this room. However, they shouldn’t specify the number of items or their location. Give children time to draw their pictures.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “there is” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Invite children to look at their partner’s picture, note the differences, and say sentences to describe them: e.g. In my picture, there are three chairs. In your picture, there is one chair.

Bring the class back together. Ask children which of today’s activities they liked best and which they didn’t like. Consider whether you want them to share this information with you, with each other, or just to reflect on it on their own. They could talk to their partner, you could ask for a show of hands, or you could ask children to draw stars on their whiteboard (e.g. three stars – I liked everything; two stars – I liked most activities; one star – I liked one of the activities).

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “there are” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Provide some sentence starters to support less able children.

Encourage more able children to add more details when describing their room, for example, the colour and size: e.g. There is a small, red lamp on the desk.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have talked about the differences between two pictures.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “Same or different?” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “waiting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
