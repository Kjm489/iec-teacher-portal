Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.1a, 1.1c, 1.2c) through a task on “People live in different places”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Tell children that they are going to think about living in a town versus living in the country.

Depending on your location, ask children if they would like to go and live in the other place. How would they feel if their parents or carers told them that they were moving to this place next term?

Elicit some basic differences between the two places and invite children to say if they think one place is better to live in than the other. Encourage them to explain why.

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “In our town” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Using the flashcards, introduce the topic words one by one. (You may adapt the vocabulary to make it more relevant to your context.) Do a few drilling exercises – you say the word while indicating to the card, and children repeat. Then try saying them in different ways (e.g. slowly, quickly, lazily, in a very happy voice, in a sad voice, etc.).

Complete ex.A in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.136.

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Not in our town” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Now shuffle the flashcards. Stick them on the board face down and number them. Play a guessing game with children. Tell them to choose a number and guess what the card is by asking: Is it the [department store]?

Children continue to play the game in pairs. Monitor discreetly and support them with their pronunciation where required.

See if children can categorize the words by sorting them into two groups, such as ‘In our town’ and ‘Not in our town’. Challenge them to come up with as many different categories as possible. Share suggestions as a whole class.

CCQ: Does a neighbourhood mean just one room, or the area around where people live? How does your example fit?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “chatterbox” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Complete ex.B in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.136.

Hand out the square paper and, together, children make their ‘chatterbox’. Tell them to listen carefully to your instructions and to copy your actions. They can add colours and numbers, then add the vocabulary items underneath the flaps.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “People live in different places” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Pair up children to play the game. When they lift a flap, they say the word. Their partner has to say a sentence or do an action to show that they understand the word.

Monitor discreetly and support them with their pronunciation where required.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “In our town” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, sit them near you as you make the ‘chatterbox’ game, or prepare some in advance for these children.

As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to work out how to make the ‘chatterbox’ themselves by showing them your final version. Praise their problem-solving skills as they do this.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Not in our town” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned vocabulary for places in a town. They have practised using these new words in a variety of games. They have made a ‘chatterbox’ game.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “People live in different places” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “chatterbox” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 28, Lesson 1: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
