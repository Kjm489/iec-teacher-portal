Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.3d, 1.3f) through a task on “A day trip”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Say some numbers and ask children to tell you if they are odd or even numbers (refer back to children’s learning in Week 22, Lesson 4).

Throw two dice or spin a spinner twice. Ask children to say the sum out loud, and then to say whether the answer is an odd or an even number: e.g. Two plus five equals seven. That’s an odd number!

Tell children that for the game, an odd number means ‘country’ and an even number means ‘town’. Write this on the board.

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “country” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Show children the pictures. Elicit as much information about them as possible. Ask: What is it? Where is it? What can you see?

Say: Today we’re going to read a story that has some pictures like this.

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “town” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Ask: What do you think it’s about? Using the Think-Pair-Share technique, put children into pairs to predict the content of the text.

Turn to the story in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.144.

Read the text as a class and encourage children to check their predictions. Hold a whole-class discussion – did anyone manage to predict correctly?

CCQ: Does a neighbourhood mean just one room, or the area around where people live? How does your example fit?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “A day trip” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Ask children some comprehension questions to check their understanding of the main points in the text. You could also write the questions on the board for children to answer independently or in pairs.

Encourage children to come up with a title for the text in their pairs.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “country” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Ask children which of today’s activities they liked best, and why.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “town” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, go through the text section by section in an adult-led small group. Highlight and/or elicit key topic vocabulary to check children’s understanding. Encourage them to refer to the pictures.

Ask fast finishers to make more comprehension questions on the text for their partner.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “A day trip” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have read and answered questions about a neighbourhood.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “A day trip” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “country” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

Introductory activity

Say some numbers and ask children to tell you if they are odd or even numbers (refer back to children’s learning in Week 22, Lesson 4).

Throw two dice or spin a spinner twice. Ask children to say the sum out loud, and then to say whether the answer is an odd or an even number: e.g. Two plus five equals seven. That’s an odd number!

Tell children that for the game, an odd number means ‘country’ and an even number means ‘town’. Write this on the board.

Divide the class into two teams. Ask them to come up with a name for their team and to nominate the first person to write on the board.

Throw the dice or spin the spinner, and ask a child to say the sum. Each team’s nominee writes a word for that category on the board. If it is spelled correctly, they get two points for their team.

Main activity

Show children the pictures. Elicit as much information about them as possible. Ask: What is it? Where is it? What can you see?

Turn to the story in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.144.

Read the text as a class and encourage children to check their predictions. Hold a whole-class discussion – did anyone manage to predict correctly?

Ask children some comprehension questions to check their understanding of the main points in the text. You could also write the questions on the board for children to answer independently or in pairs.

Encourage children to come up with a title for the text in their pairs.

Ask children which of today’s activities they liked best, and why.

Additional tasks

Challenge children to retell the story using their own names and interests. They could also change it so that they go from the country to the city.

Write an acrostic poem about children’s home town, village, or city as a class or in pairs.

Learning review

Children have read and answered questions about a neighbourhood.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 29, Lesson 3: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
