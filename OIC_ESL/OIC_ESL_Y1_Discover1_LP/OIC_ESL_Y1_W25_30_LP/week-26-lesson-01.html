Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.2a, 1.3a) through a task on “Our homes have got furniture”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Ask children if they have lots of ‘things’. Elicit a few of these things and then ask: Can you imagine not having these items? Which is your favourite thing? Why?

Children make a quick list of their top five things.

Hold a whole-class discussion with children sharing their ‘can’t-be-without’ things.

Instruction check: ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “things” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Using the flashcards, teach the furniture words. Drill the words together and play some vocabulary games, such as ‘Stop!’. Say a word, then begin showing the flashcards one by one. When children see the flashcard of the word you said, they call out ‘stop!’.

Give children the opportunity to read and see the words.

Model the first response aloud. Show real-life photos → point/name → model “There is/There are…” → pair practice → personal response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “can” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Then invite children to match together the pictures and the words on the board.

Ask: What furniture have you got at home? [Judith], have you got a table?

Elicit the question ‘Have you got …?’ and drill it. Then put children into pairs to ask and answer questions about the furniture they have and haven’t got.

CCQ: Is a bedroom a room or a piece of furniture? Which part of the picture proves your answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Stop!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Ask children to take out a pencil and teach ‘in’, ‘on’, and ‘under’ by instructing them to put the pencil in, on, and under something on their desk, such as a book or a pencil case.

Then draw a picture of a bed on the board. Draw a ball under the bed, a sock on the bed, and a teddy in the bed. Elicit where the items are.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “stop!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Tell children to draw their own bed and to add their own items in, on, and under the bed. Encourage them to keep their work secret.

They then tell their partner the three items they need to ‘find’. Their partner will then pose a series of questions to establish the item’s location: e.g. Is the ball under the bed? Is the ball in the bed? Is the ball on the bed? When they ‘find’ the item, they draw it.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, tell them not to worry about saying full sentences. They can just say the preposition or indicate to their picture.

Encourage more confident children to give more details, for example, by using adjectives for colours and size.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Have you got …?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have talked about the furniture they have and haven’t got in their homes. They have learned prepositions of place to describe the location of objects.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “Our homes have got furniture” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “things” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 26, Lesson 1: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
