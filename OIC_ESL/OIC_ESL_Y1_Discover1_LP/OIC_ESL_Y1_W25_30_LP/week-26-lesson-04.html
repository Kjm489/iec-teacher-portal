Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.1a, 1.2c) through a task on “My neighbourhood”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Tell children that today they are going to be active throughout the lesson. Decide together on an appropriate signal you can make. When you make this signal, children should stand up quietly, space themselves out, and do five star jumps (or adapt to suit the abilities in your classroom). They then sit down and continue.

Practise once or twice so that everyone knows what to do.

Do this at regular intervals throughout the lesson.

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “neighbourhood” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Using the flashcards or Total Physical Response (TPR), introduce the adjectives.

Encourage children to think of something that can be all of these adjectives (although not at the same time). For example, a book can be boring or interesting, but not noisy. Accept all reasonable answers, but tell children you were thinking of a ‘neighbourhood’.

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “noisy” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Ask children how they would describe the neighbourhood where their school is. Ask: Is our neighbourhood noisy? Check children understand what ‘noisy’ is. Elicit ‘roads’ and ‘cars’.

Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.134. Invite volunteers to say what they think the neighbourhood is like for the houses in the pictures.

Revise/Elicit the names for places in a town. Stick these flashcards on the board and number them.

CCQ: Does a neighbourhood mean just one room, or the area around where people live? How does your example fit?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “roads” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Then tell children you are going to give them a problem. They will need to listen carefully to solve it.

Choose a card and write the number secretly. Say: I’m going to describe where I am. Explain that it is a place on one of the flashcards. For ‘park’, you might say: I’m sitting here and it’s quiet. There are lots of flowers and it’s interesting. Children are playing football.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “cars” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Invite a child to guess where you are and show them the number. Elicit from the class what you mentioned – what you and other people were doing, what you could see, and one or more adjectives.

Invite a confident volunteer to choose a card and do the same.

Once children have got the idea, they continue to play in pairs. The listening child should ask the question ‘What is it like?’.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “park” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, spend more time playing vocabulary games with the flashcards in a small, adult-led group.

Challenge more able children to say five sentences during the pairwork activity.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What is it like?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have asked and answered questions about a neighbourhood.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “My neighbourhood” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “neighbourhood” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
