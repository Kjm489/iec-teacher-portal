Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.1a, 1.2c, 1.3e) through a task on “What is your favourite place? (1)”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Take a few deep breaths with children. Then ask them to look around the classroom for five things that they don’t normally see, such as a shadow or a pattern on the carpet or floorboards. If they want to, they can share, but it is a quiet time for observation and reflection.

Using a soft voice, talk with children about the importance of ‘quiet time’. Ask: Do you like being quiet? What is easier when you are quiet? Do you feel calm?

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “quiet time” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Elicit from children some places in the neighbourhood, such as their school or shops. Say: Let’s find out about different shops and places. What different places do you know? Using the flashcards, elicit/pre-teach the lesson’s vocabulary. You can adapt this list accordingly to make it more relevant to your context.

Ask: What can you see when you go to the supermarket? Do the same for all the places.

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What is your favourite place? (1)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Play some flashcard games. Mouth the word and children say which word it is. You can also number the cards, and children say the number and then the word.

Complete Oxford Discover SB 1 p.142.

Put children into pairs to talk about their three favourite places in their neighbourhood. Prompt them to explain why they like to go there.

CCQ: Does a neighbourhood mean just one room, or the area around where people live? How does your example fit?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “quiet time” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Ask children to think of a new place for their neighbourhood. It should be something that doesn’t already exist there – and perhaps doesn’t exist anywhere! Give children some ideas and then time to think of a new amazing shop (or different kind of place) themselves.

Put them into groups to think of the name of their place and what it offers.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What is your favourite place? (1)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Invite groups to share their ideas with the rest of the class.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “quiet time” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Mix together abilities in pairwork and group work so more confident children can model newly learned vocabulary for their less confident peers.

As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to add more details. What other things are unusual about their imaginary place?

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What is your favourite place? (1)” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have learned the names of places in our neighbourhood. They have thought about their favourite places and why we go to them.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “What is your favourite place? (1)” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “quiet time” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
