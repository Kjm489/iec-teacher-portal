Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.4a, 1.4b) through a task on “Welcome to my home!”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Put children into small groups. Explain that you are going to give them a category and, in their groups, they must come up with three words for that category. Suggested categories: types of home, rooms in a home, furniture, or other topics such as colours, animals, etc.

You could make it competitive by awarding points.

Ask children to reflect on how they performed in the team game. Did they listen to their classmates’ ideas? Did they contribute some words? Did they help their classmates with spelling the words? Help them to identify their own strengths.

Instruction check: ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Tell children that today’s lesson will involve writing. They will have to think carefully and recall their learning from the past two weeks. The aim of the activity is to stretch them and extend their understanding.

Ask the following questions: What have we talked about? What words can you remember? How can you describe a home? Elicit some sentences.

Model the first response aloud. Show real-life photos → point/name → model “There is/There are…” → pair practice → personal response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Welcome to my home!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Explain to children that they are going to write about their own home. Talk about what kind of information they could include. Write some ideas on the board, for example:

My home

Where is it? It’s ______.

CCQ: Is a bedroom a room or a piece of furniture? Which part of the picture proves your answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

How many rooms are there? There are ______ rooms.

Describe your bedroom, kitchen, or living room. ______

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Welcome to my home!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Other information: There are ______ windows and ______doors in my home.

Reassure them that they don’t need to write about everything.

Give them enough time to complete the writing task. Monitor actively and support where necessary.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, provide the text as gapped sentences or show them a model text for inspiration.

As an extension task for more able children, ask them to structure their writing as a letter to a friend.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Welcome to my home!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have written about their own home, recalling what they have learned from the unit and self-correcting their work.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “Welcome to my home!” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

Original OIC source activities (reference)

Introductory activity

Put children into small groups. Explain that you are going to give them a category and, in their groups, they must come up with three words for that category. Suggested categories: types of home, rooms in a home, furniture, or other topics such as colours, animals, etc.

You could make it competitive by awarding points.

Ask children to reflect on how they performed in the team game. Did they listen to their classmates’ ideas? Did they contribute some words? Did they help their classmates with spelling the words? Help them to identify their own strengths.

Main activity

Tell children that today’s lesson will involve writing. They will have to think carefully and recall their learning from the past two weeks. The aim of the activity is to stretch them and extend their understanding.

Ask the following questions: What have we talked about? What words can you remember? How can you describe a home? Elicit some sentences.

Explain to children that they are going to write about their own home. Talk about what kind of information they could include. Write some ideas on the board, for example:

My home

Where is it? It’s ______.

How many rooms are there? There are ______ rooms.

Describe your bedroom, kitchen, or living room. ______

Other information: There are ______ windows and ______doors in my home.

Reassure them that they don’t need to write about everything.

Give them enough time to complete the writing task. Monitor actively and support where necessary.

When children have finished, encourage them to check their work for correct spelling and punctuation. Self-correction is an important skill. Then invite them to tell you what they found most challenging and where they think any problems are. Prompt them to also think about what strengths they displayed. You could model words like ‘perseverance’ during a challenging activity.

When they are happy with their writing, hand out the coloured paper for children to make a final draft.

Additional tasks

Encourage children to illustrate their final draft.

Find pictures of different homes, such as igloos or yurts, and imagine what they are like.

Learning review

Children have written about their own home, recalling what they have learned from the unit and self-correcting their work.

Differentiation

To support less able children, provide the text as gapped sentences or show them a model text for inspiration.

As an extension task for more able children, ask them to structure their writing as a letter to a friend.
