Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.1a, 1.2b) through a task on “Our homes have got rooms”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Revise the furniture words from last lesson.

Begin drawing one of the items and encourage children to guess what it is. If they guess really quickly, begin the drawing from a different place so that it takes them longer and they need to think harder.

Put children into pairs to play the game with each other on their whiteboards.

Instruction check: ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Using the flashcards, pre-teach/elicit the different rooms in a house. Play a mime game. You mime an action (e.g. cooking) and children guess which room you are in (the kitchen).

Children continue to play the game in small groups.

Model the first response aloud. Show real-life photos → point/name → model “There is/There are…” → pair practice → personal response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “bed” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Write the room words on the board for children to copy down.

Ask them to say, draw, and/or write the furniture they would find in each room (normally). Ask: Have you got a bed in your bathroom? Have you got a bed in your bedroom?

Then hand out a sheet of A4 paper to each child. Show them how to fold it into six sections and then cut the sections out to make cards.

CCQ: Is a bedroom a room or a piece of furniture? Which part of the picture proves your answer?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “bedroom” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Children now have six cards each. Each card will represent a room in a house. They can either draw the room or cut out pictures from magazines. Explain that by drawing a ‘bed’, this would mean the card is a ‘bedroom’.

Put children into pairs to play ‘Snap!’. One child takes the cards and shuffles them, then deals them out face down. Each child has six cards. The first child turns over their first card and says the room. Then the next child turns over their first card and says the room. If it is the same, the first one to call out ‘snap!’ keeps both cards. If not, the game continues. It is important that each child says the word before saying ‘snap!’.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Will you label, compare, or describe the home first? Point to the example you will use.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Snap!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Monitor actively and praise children for their efforts in recalling the room words.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “snap!” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Mix together abilities in the matching game so that more able children can model the vocabulary for less able children.

Encourage fast finishers to think of a different game using the vocabulary cards.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Our homes have got rooms” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have created and played a game to practise vocabulary for rooms in a house.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “Our homes have got rooms” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “There is/There are…” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
