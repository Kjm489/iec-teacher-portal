Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.3e, 1.4c) through a task on “What is your address?”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Show children the package, box, or envelope you have and show delight that it has arrived. Ask: How did the postal worker know to bring it here? Elicit that it has an ‘address’. Talk about what this is and when we need to use our address.

Ask children whether they get letters or parcels at home. Establish that they only get there because of the address. Stress the importance of knowing your own address.

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “address” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Pre-teach/Elicit the word ‘address’. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.131.

Discuss the different parts of an address. Make it relevant for your context (i.e. the order/way of writing it out), but include street number, street name, and town/village.

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “street numbers” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

On the board, write a few numbers, street names, and towns or villages in a random order. Ask children to copy this information down and to sort it into the categories: ‘street numbers’, ‘street names’, and ‘towns or villages’.

Write the school’s address on the board. Show children the number, the street name, and the town. Ask: What is the school’s address? Elicit: The school’s address is …

Underline ’s and ask children to notice the form and meaning. Say: It means the address of [the school]. Then pick up an item and ask: Whose pen is this? Elicit: It’s [Eva]’s pen!

Hand out envelopes and encourage children to write their name and a made-up address neatly on them. Their address can be as silly as possible, but it should follow the correct format.

(Optional) Elicit ‘stamp’ and what it is for. Give one to each child to stick on their envelope.

Collect all the envelopes for an activity in Week 27.

Show children how to make their own envelope.

Hold a Friendship Day in which everyone writes a note to each child in the class, and one child pretends to be the postal worker who delivers the mail.

Find out what other things we can do at the post office (e.g. pay bills or send parcels).
