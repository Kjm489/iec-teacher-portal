Measurable Learning Objectives

Learners will demonstrate the original OIC outcomes (1.3e, 1.4c) through a task on “How is a city different from the country?”, completing one independent response and explaining one choice.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Play a game to revise place names from Week 28, Lesson 1. Instruct children to stand in a line. Establish that they know the difference between left and right. Then say one of the words – if you have the place in your local area, they jump to the left; if you don’t, they jump to the right.

If this is too easy, say the word in a sentence so that children really have to listen before jumping. Alternatively, you could say True/False sentences: e.g. In our town, there are five department stores. If it is true, children jump to the left; if it is false, they jump to the right.

Instruction check: ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “contrasting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Ask the class: How is a town or city different from the country? If you live in a town or city, encourage children to think about their previous visits to the countryside, if possible, on a day trip, on holiday, or to visit family members and friends.

Write their ideas on the board.

Model the first response aloud. Use a picture/map → teacher think-aloud → labelled example → paired explanation → independent response.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “contrasting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Indicate to the board and say: This is called ‘contrasting’. Often, there is contrast in stories. This means differences between two things, places, or characters. Elicit some story examples from children. They might highlight that in some stories there is a good/kind character and a bad/naughty character.

Explain that they are now going to read a part of a story with contrast. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.137 ex.C or hand out the worksheets provided. Tell children to look at each table first so they know what contrast they are looking for.

Give children plenty of time to carry out this activity. Encourage them to do it on their own first, and then compare their answers with a partner.

CCQ: Does a neighbourhood mean just one room, or the area around where people live? How does your example fit?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “contrasting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Review answers and observations as a whole class.

Now ask children to change the key words so that the story is about themselves and a friend. Help them to identify the words that need to be changed and to substitute them with words about themselves. Do a few examples together first.

Use paired turns, with one learner explaining and the other checking the example. ICQ: Are you pointing to, drawing, or describing the place in this task? Show me where you will start.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “contrasting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Invite volunteers to read out their adapted stories. Remind listening children to be respectful and courteous towards their classmates as they read out different answers and perhaps express different preferences.

Remove sentence prompts gradually and collect one individual product for assessment.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “contrasting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

To support less able children, provide them with a gapped text to complete. Allow them to do the reading activity in pairs.

Encourage more able children to add more contrast to their writing.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “contrasting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Children have identified contrast in a short story.

Exit ticket: Give one accurate example connected to “How is a city different from the country?” and explain it in one sentence.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “contrasting” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
