LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: My family and friends
Week: 5
Lesson: 1
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g

Measurable Learning Objectives

Learning target: Students will introduce family members, using the language frame This is my ___. / He is my ___ / She is my ___.. Success criteria: identify at least three relevant examples; produce one meaningful response independently; respond to a partner.

Teacher preparation: Prepare family picture cards, fictional family tree, individual response cards and a board model. Verify exact vocabulary and page numbers in the primary Oxford Discover Student Book before delivery; supplementary Bright Ideas activities are optional.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Connect: Welcome learners with a familiar greeting. Show family picture cards but do not give answers immediately. Ask students to notice and predict: “What can you see?” “What do you already know?” Invite pointing or home-language-to-English bridging where needed. Use a 30-second partner rehearsal before asking for volunteers.

Check: Can students recognize the topic before direct teaching? Record two prior words or ideas on the board.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What can you see?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

I DO: Display family picture cards. Say the lesson frame slowly: This is my ___. / He is my ___ / She is my ___.. Point to the image/object, speak once naturally, then repeat with gestures. Think aloud about how you chose the word or response. Show a correct model and an intentionally incorrect example; ask which fits the visual. Model a full exchange with a puppet or volunteer, explicitly showing listening, turn-taking and pronunciation.

Teacher script: “Watch first. My turn. Now listen again. What did I say? I will point, say the sentence and check my partner understood.”

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What do you already know?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

WE DO: Put fictional family tree on the board. Students first point, then repeat a complete response together. Run three scaffolded examples: (1) teacher models and students echo, (2) teacher gives only the first word, (3) students produce the answer from the picture. Invite learners to correct one deliberately mismatched picture/sentence pairing. Use mini-whiteboards or finger signals to check every learner.

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

WE DO TO YOU DO TOGETHER: Run a picture sort with partners using family picture cards. Demonstrate one complete round with a student. Student A asks, describes or gives a clue; Student B answers, points or completes the task; partners swap. Display This is my ___. / He is my ___ / She is my ___. as a temporary scaffold, then hide it for a second round. Circulate and note accurate independent exchanges.

Feedback: Praise successful communication, then recast one common error without interrupting fluency.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What can you do now?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

YOU DO: Each learner completes an individual response tied to Family relations: choose or draw two relevant examples, label them where developmentally appropriate, and say a complete target sentence to the teacher or a partner. For a writing lesson, produce two connected sentences; for a story lesson, order three events and retell; for a review lesson, choose two prior skills and show evidence. Do not supply the full answer after the initial demonstration.

Evidence: Collect a short oral performance, mini-book, picture sequence or written sample. Score independently correct / correct with prompt / not yet demonstrated.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What can you see?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation: Access: give two picture choices, gesture support, a bilingual keyword if necessary and a sentence strip. Core: use the frame with one accurate detail. Stretch: add a second detail, reason or follow-up question; remove the word bank. For students reluctant to share personal family information, use a fictional family or characters instead.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What do you already know?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review and exit: Ask learners to teach a partner one example from Family relations. Each student responds to a new picture, prompt or question without copying the teacher. Ask “What can you do now?” and let learners choose confident / practicing / need help. Record one misconception and one concrete next-lesson action.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
