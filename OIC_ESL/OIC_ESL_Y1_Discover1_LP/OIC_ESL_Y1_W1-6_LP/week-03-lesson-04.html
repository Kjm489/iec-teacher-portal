LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Reflection and review
Week: 3
Lesson: 4
Duration: 90 minutes (2 × 45)
OIC outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g

Measurable Learning Objectives

Learning target: Students will retrieve, apply and reflect on prior learning, using the language frame I can ___ / I learned ___.. Success criteria: identify at least three relevant examples; produce one meaningful response independently; respond to a partner.

Teacher preparation: Prepare project checklist, student work samples, individual response cards and a board model. Verify exact vocabulary and page numbers in the primary Oxford Discover Student Book before delivery; supplementary Bright Ideas activities are optional.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Connect: Welcome learners with a familiar greeting. Show project checklist but do not give answers immediately. Ask students to notice and predict: “What can you see?” “What do you already know?” Invite pointing or home-language-to-English bridging where needed. Use a 30-second partner rehearsal before asking for volunteers.

Check: Can students recognize the topic before direct teaching? Record two prior words or ideas on the board.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What can you see?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

I DO: Display project checklist. Say the lesson frame slowly: I can ___ / I learned ___.. Point to the image/object, speak once naturally, then repeat with gestures. Think aloud about how you chose the word or response. Show a correct model and an intentionally incorrect example; ask which fits the visual. Model a full exchange with a puppet or volunteer, explicitly showing listening, turn-taking and pronunciation.

Teacher script: “Watch first. My turn. Now listen again. What did I say? I will point, say the sentence and check my partner understood.”

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What do you already know?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

WE DO: Put student work samples on the board. Students first point, then repeat a complete response together. Run three scaffolded examples: (1) teacher models and students echo, (2) teacher gives only the first word, (3) students produce the answer from the picture. Invite learners to correct one deliberately mismatched picture/sentence pairing. Use mini-whiteboards or finger signals to check every learner.

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What can you do now?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

WE DO TO YOU DO TOGETHER: Run a partner interview with partners using project checklist. Demonstrate one complete round with a student. Student A asks, describes or gives a clue; Student B answers, points or completes the task; partners swap. Display I can ___ / I learned ___. as a temporary scaffold, then hide it for a second round. Circulate and note accurate independent exchanges.

Feedback: Praise successful communication, then recast one common error without interrupting fluency.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What can you see?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

YOU DO: Each learner completes an individual response tied to Independent extended activity: choose or draw two relevant examples, label them where developmentally appropriate, and say a complete target sentence to the teacher or a partner. For a writing lesson, produce two connected sentences; for a story lesson, order three events and retell; for a review lesson, choose two prior skills and show evidence. Do not supply the full answer after the initial demonstration.

Evidence: Collect a short oral performance, mini-book, picture sequence or written sample. Score independently correct / correct with prompt / not yet demonstrated.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What do you already know?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation: Access: give two picture choices, gesture support, a bilingual keyword if necessary and a sentence strip. Core: use the frame with one accurate detail. Stretch: add a second detail, reason or follow-up question; remove the word bank. For students reluctant to share personal family information, use a fictional family or characters instead.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What can you do now?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Review and exit: Ask learners to teach a partner one example from Independent extended activity. Each student responds to a new picture, prompt or question without copying the teacher. Ask “What can you do now?” and let learners choose confident / practicing / need help. Record one misconception and one concrete next-lesson action.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “What can you see?” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

YEAR 1 Seasons
Week 3, Lesson 4: What do you know about seasons?
Learning outcomes: 1.1c, 1.1g
Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Seasons’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by watching a video about seasons.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
Introductory activity
Ask children to tell you the day and the month. Elicit or pre-teach the months of the year.
After eliciting the month, ask for the season. For example, say: December. Then ask: What season? Elicit: winter. If you are in a (sub)tropical area, adapt to your local context.
Play a game with children. Agree on some actions to represent each month (e.g. shivering for winter). Say a month and children mime the season.
Main activity
If you do not live in an area with four clear seasons, talk to children about different parts of the world with four seasons. They will need to know this for this unit.
Tell children they are going to watch a video about the four seasons.
Put children into groups. Ask them to think of four things they might see in each season. Children draw these items on their whiteboards.
Play the video. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.67.
Hold a whole-class feedback session. Write and draw vocabulary items on the board for children to copy down.
What can children see in the picture? Elicit ‘autumn leaves’ or ‘people playing outside’. Ask children what colour the leaves are and why. Talk to them about what happens to leaves in autumn. (The days are shorter and the leaves lose their green colour. The leaves turn red/orange/yellow and then they fall off the trees onto the ground.) If you live in a tropical region, see if there is a tree that is deciduous to show the class.
Children can complete the Big Question chart on p.67 of Oxford Discover SB 1.
Children complete the chart for the question: How are seasons different?
Encourage children to present their chart to a partner, then display them around the classroom.
Additional tasks
Complete the introductory questions in Oxford Discover WB 1 p.56.
They can draw a picture of themselves in their favourite season and label it.
Play the video again and stop at certain points, inviting children to comment on the frozen frame. What can you see? What season is it? What is the girl wearing?
Learning review
Children have learned about the four seasons in a short video.
Differentiation
To support less able children, ask them to list just one or two things for each season.
As an extension task for more able children, ask them to list more things for each season, including clothing and activities.
