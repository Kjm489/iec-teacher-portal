LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Me and my classroom
Week / Lesson: 1 / 3
Topic: Numbers and age
Duration: 90 minutes (45 + 45)
OIC Outcomes: 1.1e, 1.2a

Learning Objectives & Success Criteria

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Recognize, say and sequence numbers 1–20.
  2. Count classroom objects using number language.
  3. Ask and answer "How old are you?"
  4. Use number cards and physical actions to demonstrate number understanding.

Success: Students count at least 10 objects accurately, identify selected numbers up to 20 and answer an age question with appropriate support.

Target Language, Materials & Preparation

Vocabulary: Numbers 1–20, eraser, old, years.

Language: How old are you? I'm ___ years old. How many? There are ___.

Materials: Feely bag containing eight erasers, 20 small paper squares per student, number cards, Oxford Discover Student Book 1 and Workbook 1.

Textbook: Student Book 1 pp. 91 and 95 (Exercise E); optional Workbook 1 p. 81 (Word Study A–B).

Preparation: Prepare the feely bag and number-card sets. Check textbook exercises and have counting materials ready.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge — 8 minutes

Teacher: Review greetings from Lessons 1–2. Show the closed feely bag and ask, "What's inside?" Invite students to feel the bag and guess how many objects it contains.

Reveal the eight erasers one at a time. Count aloud with the class, touching each eraser once. Recount together.

Students: Predict, count and compare guesses with the actual quantity.

ICQ: "Do we look inside before guessing?" No. "Do we count each eraser once?" Yes.

CCQ: "If there are eight erasers, is the number seven or eight?" Eight.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO — 12 minutes

Teacher: Model counting from 1 to 20 with number cards and small paper squares. Point to each number as you say it. Demonstrate matching a written numeral to a group of objects.

Use Oxford Discover Student Book 1 p. 91 to reinforce counting. Then demonstrate the age exchange with a puppet or fictional child:

Teacher: "How old are you?"
Puppet: "I'm seven years old."
Teacher: "Seven!" (Shows number card 7.)

ICQ: "Do you watch and listen first?" Yes.

CCQs: "Is 12 bigger than 10?" Yes. "If I'm seven years old, am I saying my age or my name?" My age.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO — 15 minutes

Activity A — Number groups: Say a number and have students arrange the corresponding number of squares. Check by counting together.

Activity B — Number actions: Students trace numerals on a partner's back or form number shapes with their bodies, then identify the number.

Activity C — Age exchange: Practise "How old are you?" / "I'm ___ years old" chorally, using fictional ages where appropriate.

ICQs: "When I say five, do you show five squares?" Yes. "Do you ask about age or favorite food?" Age.

CCQs: "If I show 15, is that fifteen or fifty?" Fifteen. "Does 'How old are you?' ask about age?" Yes.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice — 10 minutes

Teacher: Give each student paper squares or number cards. Model a partner task: Student A shows a number; Student B says it and makes the matching quantity. Swap roles.

Partners then practise asking and answering ages. Use the age dialogue from Student Book 1 p. 95, Exercise E, as the textbook reference.

Scaffolding: Keep a 1–20 number line and age sentence frame visible.

ICQ: "Does each partner get a turn?" Yes.

CCQ: "If your partner shows nine objects, do you say eight or nine?" Nine.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO — 25 minutes

Activity A — My number cards (10 min): Students prepare and arrange their own 1–20 number cards. The teacher calls out numbers for students to locate independently.

Activity B — Number challenge (7 min): Students identify numerals, match quantities and complete counting tasks using their own paper squares.

Activity C — Age role-play (8 min): Students ask and answer "How old are you?" in pairs, using their own or fictional ages. Invite several independent exchanges.

ICQs: "Do you arrange the cards in number order?" Yes. "Do both partners ask and answer?" Yes.

CCQs: "Which comes after 13: 14 or 16?" 14. "Does 'I'm eight years old' mean my age is eight?" Yes.

Assessment evidence: Number recognition, one-to-one counting and accurate age exchanges.

6. Differentiation & Scaffolding — 10 minutes

Support:

  • Practise numbers 1–10 first.
  • Provide a visual number line.
  • Use physical counters and repeated counting.
  • Offer the frame "I'm ___ years old."
  • Allow pointing before speaking.

Extension:

  • Spell selected number words.
  • Make number puzzles.
  • Identify missing numbers in sequences.
  • Ask age questions without prompts.
  • Complete optional Workbook p. 81 tasks.

ICQ: "Can you use the number line if you need help?" Yes.

CCQ: "Is twenty the number after nineteen?" Yes.

7. Formative Assessment, Review & Exit Ticket — 10 minutes

Review: Show mixed number cards. Ask students to identify selected numbers, count objects and answer age questions.

Exit ticket: Each student:

  1. Identifies two number cards.
  2. Counts a small set of objects.
  3. Answers "How old are you?" with a complete or supported response.

Final CCQs: "Is 18 greater than 8?" Yes. "Does 'years old' describe age?" Yes.

Teacher records: Independent / With prompt / Not yet demonstrated.

TEACHER REFLECTION & NEXT STEPS

Post-Lesson Evaluation

Counting: Who needs more practice matching numbers to quantities?

Speaking: Can students ask and answer age questions?
