LESSON INFORMATION & OBJECTIVES
Curriculum: OIC ESL Year 1
Theme: Me and my classroom
Week / Lesson: 1 / 2
Topic: What's your name? (2)
Duration: 90 minutes (45 + 45)
OIC Outcomes: 1.1b, 1.2b

Learning Objectives & Success Criteria

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Listen carefully and identify the correct person or name from spoken information.
  2. Create a self-portrait poster to introduce themselves.
  3. Present their poster using simple English greetings and introductions.
  4. Listen respectfully and follow simple classroom activity instructions.

Success: Students identify a person or name from a listening prompt, complete a recognizable self-portrait and give a short introduction with appropriate support.

Target Language, Materials & Preparation

Language: Hello. What's your name? My name is ___. This is me. Nice to meet you.

Materials: Original OIC listening resource or teacher-read identification prompts, name/character picture cards, paper, crayons or colored pencils, and self-portrait poster examples.

Preparation: Prepare the listening identification task from the original OIC lesson. Create a simple teacher self-portrait as a model. Ensure students have suitable materials for drawing and presenting.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Objective Discussion — 8 minutes

Teacher: Welcome students and review greetings from Lesson 1 using a puppet. Ask: "What's your name?" and model the response. Invite several students to answer.

Show a sample self-portrait and explain: "Today we will listen, find the correct person, draw ourselves and introduce ourselves."

Students: Practise the greeting exchange and identify what they will make.

Scaffolding: Display "My name is ___" and use a puppet for reluctant speakers.

ICQ: "Will we draw ourselves or draw the teacher?" Expected: "Ourselves."

CCQ: "If I say 'My name is Mai,' is Mai my name?" Yes.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO — 12 minutes

Listening model: Display two character pictures with names. Say: "Listen: My name is Ben." Point to Ben. Repeat with another character, demonstrating how to listen before selecting.

Poster model: Show the teacher self-portrait. Point to the drawing and demonstrate:

"Hello! My name is Ms. Lan.
This is me. Nice to meet you!"

Think aloud: "I draw myself. I put my name on my poster. Then I tell my classmates my name."

ICQs: "Do you point before or after listening?" After. "Whose picture will you draw?" My own.

CCQs: "If the voice says 'My name is Ben,' should I choose Ben or Anna?" Ben. "Does 'This is me' refer to my own picture?" Yes.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO — 15 minutes

Listening identification: Conduct the original OIC listening task. Play or read each prompt, pause, and have students point to or select the matching person/name. Check responses together.

Guided poster preparation: Demonstrate drawing a face and adding a name. Invite students to describe the steps and rehearse the introductory sentence together.

Scaffolding: Repeat audio when needed, reduce picture choices, use pointing, and model the first response before students complete later examples.

ICQs: "Do you listen first and then point?" Yes. "Do you draw your portrait before presenting?" Yes.

CCQ: "If the speaker says 'I'm Anna,' should you point to the picture labeled Ben?" No.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice — 10 minutes

Teacher: Pair students. Partner A introduces themselves using a name card or drawing; Partner B listens and repeats the name. Students swap roles.

Then ask partners to explain what they will include in their self-portrait posters.

Students: Practise introductions and listen for names while preparing their poster ideas.

Scaffolding: Display the name sentence frame and allow rehearsal before independent presentation.

ICQ: "Does each partner get a turn to speak and listen?" Yes.

CCQ: "When someone tells you their name, should you listen for the name or their age?" The name.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO — 25 minutes

Activity A — Self-portrait poster (12 min): Each child draws themselves and adds their name. The teacher circulates without taking over the work.

Activity B — Presentation rehearsal (5 min): Students practise a short introduction using their completed poster. Allow visual prompts for those who need them.

Activity C — Student presentations (8 min): Students show their posters and introduce themselves to a small group or the class, depending on class size.

Student model:
"Hello! My name is ___.
This is me. Nice to meet you!"

ICQs: "Do you draw yourself or your partner?" Yourself. "When presenting, do you show your poster and say your name?" Yes.

CCQs: "If your poster says 'Linh,' should you introduce yourself as 'Ben'?" No. "Does your self-portrait show you?" Yes.

Assessment evidence: Collect the poster and record the student's ability to introduce themselves clearly.

6. Differentiation & Scaffolding — 10 minutes

Support:

  • Provide a partially prepared portrait outline.
  • Offer a name-writing model.
  • Allow a one-sentence introduction.
  • Use picture cues and partner rehearsal.
  • Repeat listening prompts and reduce choices.

Extension:

  • Present without reading a sentence frame.
  • Ask a classmate "What's your name?"
  • Respond with "Nice to meet you."
  • Identify several names independently during listening.

ICQ: "Can you use a name card or sentence frame if you need help?" Yes.

CCQ: "Is 'My name is...' used to introduce yourself?" Yes.

7. Formative Assessment, Review & Exit Ticket — 10 minutes

Listening check: Give a new spoken name prompt and ask students to identify the matching picture or name.

Speaking check: Invite students to introduce themselves using their posters.

Exit ticket: Each student:

  1. Identifies a name from a listening prompt.
  2. Shows their self-portrait and says "My name is ___."

Final CCQs: "When someone asks 'What's your name?', do you say your name?" Yes. "Do you listen carefully before choosing a picture?" Yes.

Teacher records: Independent / With prompt / Not yet demonstrated.

TEACHER REFLECTION & NEXT STEPS

Post-Lesson Evaluation

Listening: Which students could identify names accurately without repeated prompts?

Speaking: Which students could introduce themselves independently?

Scaffolding: Which students benefited from name cards, visual cues or sentence frames?

Next lesson: Reinforce listening and introductions as needed before progressing to numbers and age.
