Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Confirm children’s understanding of ‘left’ and ‘right’. Then ask them to stand in a line. If you say a fruit, they jump to the left. If you say a vegetable, they jump to the right. Tell children that today they are going to learn about food that comes from plants. Ask if they know any foods which are mainly plants (e.g. a vegetarian curry or a salad).

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to food from plants.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “left” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Model the key food vocabulary using pictures or real objects. Demonstrate the source task and think aloud about the food, its origin, or the target food expression.

Original OIC task reference: Main activity On the board, draw a plant showing the following: fruit, flower, leaves, stem, and root. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.96 and p.102. See if children can identify the fruit and vegetables in the pictures, and which part of the plant each item is. Introduce some movements for each of these words. For example, hands outstretched for a flower, hands by side for a stem, lying on the ground for a root, curled up in a ball for a fruit, and swaying for leaves. Say a fr

Concept check: Is this something we eat or drink? Does it come from a plant or an animal? Show the picture that proves your answer.

Language scaffold: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “right” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Use the original main activity with teacher prompts: Main activity On the board, draw a plant showing the following: fruit, flower, leaves, stem, and root. Turn to Oxford Discover SB 1 p.96 and p.102. See if children can identify the fruit and vegetables in the pictures, and which part of the plant each item is. Introduce some movements for each of these words. For example, hands outstretched for a flower, hands by side for a stem, lying on the ground for a root, curled up in a ball for a fruit, and swaying for leaves. Say a fruit/vegetable and children do the movement to represent the correct plant part. Put children into groups. Challenge them to think of three examples of food for each category (flower, fruit, leaves, root). Children share their ideas with the rest of the class. Tell everyone to actively listen and decide whether they agree or disagree. Hold a whole-class feedback sessio

Guide learners through one complete example; ask them to justify their choice. CCQs: Is this something we eat or drink? Does it come from a plant or an animal? Show the picture that proves your answer.

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Flower” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Will you point to the food before speaking? Are you matching foods or writing a full paragraph? Show me the first example.

Sentence frames: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Fruit” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Complete Oxford Discover WB 1 p.88 exs.B–C. Challenge children to sort fruit and vegetables into new categories (e.g. grow above ground, below ground, on a tree high up, on a plant low down, etc.). Draw a picture of their lunch and decide if they ate/will eat mostly roots, leaves, fruit, etc. On the board, draw four columns with the following headings: ‘Flower’, ‘Fruit’, ‘Leaves’, and ‘Root’. Draw and label fruit and vegetables that are in each category.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Leaves” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, encourage them to think of only fruit. As an extension task for more able children, you can introduce other parts of a plant (e.g. a vine for grapes and passion fruit, seeds for spices). Support less confident children by grouping them with more confident children, who can guide them through the task.

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Root” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have deepened their understanding of the different parts of a plant that we can eat.

Exit ticket: Identify one food or drink from a new picture and explain one fact about it.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “left” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
