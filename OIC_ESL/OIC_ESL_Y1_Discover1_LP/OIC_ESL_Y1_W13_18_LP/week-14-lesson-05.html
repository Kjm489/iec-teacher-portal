PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Before children come into class, place nine flashcards from the previous two weeks on the board, face down. Number them 1–9. To play, put children into teams. The first team chooses a number and you give a clue about the word (e.g. ‘It’s orange.’). Children make a guess. If they get it right, they get one point and the card is turned over. The objective is to get three in a row.

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to a book about food.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “It” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Model the key food vocabulary using pictures or real objects. Demonstrate the source task and think aloud about the food, its origin, or the target food expression.

Original OIC task reference: Main activity Tell children that they are going to make a zigzag book about their favourite foods. Encourage them to think back over the previous two weeks – they have learned about fruit and vegetables, parts of a plant that we can eat, and different meals (breakfast, lunch, and dinner). They can include all of these areas of learning in their zigzag book. Show them your example zigzag book so that they can see what it should look like. Hand out some strips of paper. In unis

Concept check: Is this something we eat or drink? Does it come from a plant or an animal? Show the picture that proves your answer.

Language scaffold: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “It” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Use the original main activity with teacher prompts: Main activity Tell children that they are going to make a zigzag book about their favourite foods. Encourage them to think back over the previous two weeks – they have learned about fruit and vegetables, parts of a plant that we can eat, and different meals (breakfast, lunch, and dinner). They can include all of these areas of learning in their zigzag book. Show them your example zigzag book so that they can see what it should look like. Hand out some strips of paper. In unison, fold the paper to create a zigzag book. Remind children that the first page is the cover and elicit the information that goes on the cover (title and name of author). Allow children time to think and plan before they start writing and drawing. They should do this in rough first. Children draw and write about their favourite foods in their zigzag books. Monitor chi

Guide learners through one complete example; ask them to justify their choice. CCQs: Is this something we eat or drink? Does it come from a plant or an animal? Show the picture that proves your answer.

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “It” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Will you point to the food before speaking? Are you matching foods or writing a full paragraph? Show me the first example.

Sentence frames: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “It” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Individually complete a new version of the original task for A book about food.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “It” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, provide gapped texts or words with letters missing. As an extension task for more able children, they can create a wordsearch of words for their classmates.

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “It” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have consolidated their learning about food and drink by creating a zigzag book.

Exit ticket: Identify one food or drink from a new picture and explain one fact about it.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “It” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
