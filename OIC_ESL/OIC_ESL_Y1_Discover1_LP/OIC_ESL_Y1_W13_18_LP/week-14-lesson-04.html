PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Complete Oxford Discover SB 1 p.100 and WB 1 pp.84–85. Act out the story using a range of props. Practise asking and answering some questions about the story. Draw a favourite scene from the story. Challenge a classmate to work out if it’s the beginning, middle, or end.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “carrot” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, allow them to keep their books open while retelling the story. Encourage more able children to read sections of the story aloud to the class. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think about what happens next.

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have read a story about food, compared it to their own, and identified the beginning, the middle, and the end.

Exit ticket: Identify one food or drink from a new picture and explain one fact about it.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Original OIC context Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Food and drink’. 
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by looking at the structure of a story and reading a story in their course books. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.

Introductory activity Play a quick warm-up game with children to revise the words from the story. This will help to build their long-term memory of new topic vocabulary. Demonstrate how to play. You mouth a key word, such as ‘carrot’, and children have to try to ‘read’ what you say. Children play the game in pairs. Invite children to briefly retell their stories from the previous lesson (Week 14, Lesson 3).

Main activity Tell children that they are now going to read the original story in their course book. Their task is to see how similar or different the story is to their own story. Stress that there is no right or wrong answer. Turn to Oxford DiscoverSB 1 pp.98–99 Children read the story, or alternatively, you read the story aloud and children follow. Say a sentence and children say who says it. Put children into pairs to think-pair-share any similarities and differences they notice. Then review as a class. Ask children if they liked the story, and why. Then elicit the beginning, middle, and end of the story. Write ideas on the board. Ask children to think about the moral (lesson) of the story. (Showing kindness and sharing [food] with each other is important – you end up receiving more than you give, such as tasty food, a sense of wellbeing, and friendship.) Now ask children to retell the story by changing the key words. For example, the carrots could be replaced. Children will have to think carefully about the replacement words they choose – the story must still make sense. Remind them that this task is designed to stretch their thinking and help them to understand the story better.

Additional tasks Complete Oxford Discover SB 1 p.100 and WB 1 pp.84–85. Act out the story using a range of props. Practise asking and answering some questions about the story. Draw a favourite scene from the story. Challenge a classmate to work out if it’s the beginning, middle, or end.

Differentiation To support less able children, allow them to keep their books open while retelling the story. Encourage more able children to read sections of the story aloud to the class. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think about what happens next.
