PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Play a quick warm-up game with children to revise the words from the story. This will help to build their long-term memory of new topic vocabulary. Demonstrate how to play. You mouth a key word, such as ‘carrot’, and children have to try to ‘read’ what you say. Children play the game in pairs. Invite children to briefly retell their stories from the previous lesson (Week 14, Lesson 3).

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to a story about food (2).

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “carrot” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Will you point to the food before speaking? Are you matching foods or writing a full paragraph? Show me the first example.

Sentence frames: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “read” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Complete Oxford Discover SB 1 p.100 and WB 1 pp.84–85. Act out the story using a range of props. Practise asking and answering some questions about the story. Draw a favourite scene from the story. Challenge a classmate to work out if it’s the beginning, middle, or end.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “carrot” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, allow them to keep their books open while retelling the story. Encourage more able children to read sections of the story aloud to the class. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think about what happens next.

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “read” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have read a story about food, compared it to their own, and identified the beginning, the middle, and the end.

Exit ticket: Identify one food or drink from a new picture and explain one fact about it.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “carrot” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
