Measurable Learning Objectives

Use the original OIC learning outcomes (1.3c, 1.3e, 1.3f) in the lesson: How do we look? (2).

Demonstrate understanding of the lesson vocabulary through the specified original activity.

Produce a supported response and complete an independent check related to how do we look? (2).

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Write a simple sentence on the board: ‘she’s wearing a red T-shirt’. Ask children if this is correct. Elicit and then add a capital ‘S’ and a full stop at the end of the sentence. Remind the class that these punctuation marks tell us where a sentence starts and finishes. Say: This is a capital letter (make yourself big) and this is a full stop (curl yourself up into a ball). Invite children to stand up. When you say ‘capital letter’, they make themselves big; and when you say ‘full stop’ (period), they curl themselves up into a ball.

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to how do we look? (2).

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “she” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Model the clothing words with picture cards or real garments. Demonstrate the source task while describing what a person is wearing.

Original OIC task reference: Main activity Explain to children that there are words around the classroom. They are going to complete a ‘running dictation’ in pairs. Use the following words, or create your own: I / am / at / the / beach / I / am / wearing / blue shorts / and / a / yellow T-shirt Say: One of you goes to the word card, reads it, and returns to your partner. They write it. Then you swap. There are 12 word cards to find and write. You must work quickly. Ask some instruction-checking questions

Concept check: Is this clothing worn on the head, feet, or body? Which item matches the picture? Is the person wearing it now?

Language scaffold: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “capital letter” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Use the original main activity with teacher prompts: Main activity Explain to children that there are words around the classroom. They are going to complete a ‘running dictation’ in pairs. Use the following words, or create your own: I / am / at / the / beach / I / am / wearing / blue shorts / and / a / yellow T-shirt Say: One of you goes to the word card, reads it, and returns to your partner. They write it. Then you swap. There are 12 word cards to find and write. You must work quickly. Ask some instruction-checking questions: Who is writing? Who is finding the word cards? How many word cards are there? Start the activity and monitor actively. When children have finished, tell them that the 12 word cards are a puzzle. Say: These words make two sentences. Can you make two sentences? Give children enough time to try to make the two sentences. Elicit sentences from children and write them on

Guide learners through one complete example; ask them to justify their choice. CCQs: Is this clothing worn on the head, feet, or body? Which item matches the picture? Is the person wearing it now?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “full stop” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Do you choose the clothing picture first or say the sentence first? Will your partner describe or point? Demonstrate one turn.

Sentence frames: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “running dictation” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Complete Oxford Discover WB 1 p.35 ex.A. Practise writing sentences on the topic of clothes and physical features, paying close attention to full stops and capital letters.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “she” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation Vary the difficulty level of the running dictation by changing the number of words. You may want to set up two games – one to support less confident children, one to stretch more able children. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to search for question marks and commas in their course books and write new sentences.

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “capital letter” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have revised the purpose of capital letters and full stops, and practised using them in sentences.

Exit ticket: Choose a new outfit picture, name two items and say one correct sentence.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “full stop” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
