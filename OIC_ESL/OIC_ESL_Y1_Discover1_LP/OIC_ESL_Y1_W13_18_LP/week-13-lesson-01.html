Produce a supported response and complete an independent check related to what do we eat?.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Introduce the topic of food to children. You could tell them what you ate the night before and say: These are all … Elicit ‘foods’. Ask children: What do you eat? Where does it come from? Accept all answers.

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to what do we eat?.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “foods” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Model the key food vocabulary using pictures or real objects. Demonstrate the source task and think aloud about the food, its origin, or the target food expression.

Original OIC task reference: Main activity Introduce the vocabulary items with flashcards or realia. Play some games. Divide the classroom into four colours – white, red/orange, green, and yellow. Say a food and children move to the appropriate colour corner (e.g. if you say ‘orange’, children go to the orange corner). Then ask children: Which foods come from animals? Language scaffold: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “orange” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Use the original main activity with teacher prompts: Main activity Introduce the vocabulary items with flashcards or realia. Play some games. Divide the classroom into four colours – white, red/orange, green, and yellow. Say a food and children move to the appropriate colour corner (e.g. if you say ‘orange’, children go to the orange corner). Then ask children: Which foods come from animals? Children share their ideas and feedback. Elicit that milk comes from cows. We can make butter from milk. Play your chosen video, asking children to note down and remember the foods mentioned. You could also pause the video each time there is a food item and elicit the word. You could also look at p.108 of the Oxford Discover SB 1 and talk about where meat and milk come from. Review known fruit and vegetable vocabulary and ask if these come from animals. Ask children if they know how milk becomes butter.

Guide learners through one complete example; ask them to justify their choice. CCQs: Is this something we eat or drink? Does it come from a plant or an animal? Show the picture that proves your answer.

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “foods” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Will you point to the food before speaking? Are you matching foods or writing a full paragraph? Show me the first example.

Sentence frames: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “orange” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Support children to write instructions for making butter. Draw a bar chart to illustrate children’s findings to the question ‘Do you like butter?’. Find out about other foods that come from animals (eggs, meat).

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “foods” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation Encourage less confident children by providing them with the food words in advance of the listening activity. Ask them to put them in order as they appear on the screen. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to find out about other foods we can make (e.g. cheese, yoghurt).

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “orange” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have watched a video about food and where it comes from. They have explored the origin of foods further in a practical task by making some butter.

Exit ticket: Identify one food or drink from a new picture and explain one fact about it.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “foods” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
