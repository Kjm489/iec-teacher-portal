Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Ask children if they enjoy going shopping for food with their parents/carers. Elicit ‘grocery store’ and/or ‘fruit and vegetable shop’. Ask children if they know how much an orange (or another common fruit/vegetable in your context) costs. Don’t worry too much about precise amounts – this is to give children an idea of the cost. Establish children’s understanding of money. Depending on your currency, it may or may not be feasible to introduce prices if they include very big numbers. Don’t worry too much about the reality of the prices children set.

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to shopping for food.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “grocery store” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Model the key food vocabulary using pictures or real objects. Demonstrate the source task and think aloud about the food, its origin, or the target food expression.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Will you point to the food before speaking? Are you matching foods or writing a full paragraph? Show me the first example.

Sentence frames: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “buy one get one free” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Children can draw a picture of themselves either as the shopkeeper or the customer. Complete a gapped text of the dialogue. Repeat the activity with different food items. Draw and write about a favourite shop.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “grocery store” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, encourage them to draw the food items for sale/on their shopping list. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to add up the prices and give change. Challenge more confident children to have an item on ‘special’ or a ‘buy one get one free’ offer. Additional information: Week 14, Lesson 1 Suggested grocery store dialogue (Insert the price in your currency.) Shopkeeper: Hello! Customer: Hello! I’d like six oranges, please. Shopkeeper: Here you are. Anything else? Customer: Two potatoes, please. Shopkeeper: Sorry. I haven’t got potatoes today. Would you like cucumber? Customer: Sorry. Can you repeat that, please? Shopkeeper: Yes. Would you like cucumber? Customer: Yes, please. Shopkeeper: That’s XX [insert currency/price]. Customer: Here you are. Shopkeeper: Thank you. Have nice day! Customer: Bye!

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “fruit and vegetable shop” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have practised new language from the unit by role-playing being a customer and a shopkeeper.

Exit ticket: Identify one food or drink from a new picture and explain one fact about it.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “special” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
