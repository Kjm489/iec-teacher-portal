5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Original OIC context Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Food and drink’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by role-playing a scene in a grocery store.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.

Introductory activity Ask children if they enjoy going shopping for food with their parents/carers. Elicit ‘grocery store’ and/or ‘fruit and vegetable shop’. Ask children if they know how much an orange (or another common fruit/vegetable in your context) costs. Don’t worry too much about precise amounts – this is to give children an idea of the cost. Establish children’s understanding of money. Depending on your currency, it may or may not be feasible to introduce prices if they include very big numbers. Don’t worry too much about the reality of the prices children set.

Main activity Elicit from children a possible dialogue in a shop. Using the example dialogue provided or your own ideas, act out the role of customer and shopkeeper. Elicit from children what they can say if they don’t understand: Sorry? Can you repeat, please? I don’t understand. Encourage children to use these phrases in today’s lesson. Put children into small groups to create their shops. They should discuss together what they have for sale and draw or write the items (e.g. four tomatoes, three potatoes, etc.). If pricing is appropriate, they can make price tags for the items. Hand out a shopping list to each child and ask them to choose three items they want to buy. You might need to emphasize that this is from a different shop – not their own. Ask one group to open their shop and one customer to go shopping. Instruct children to practise following the dialogue you modelled at the beginning of the class, prompting them where necessary. Now divide the class into two, so that one side of the class has the shops and the other side are customers. Before you start, remind children of the importance of taking turns and sharing in play. To avoid customers waiting, you can move things along by ringing a bell and encouraging them to move to a different shop. Set a reasonable time limit for this free play (dependent on the number of shops/customers you have). Hold a whole-class feedback to discover whether anyone managed to buy all the items on their list, or if any shop managed to sell everything.

Additional tasks Children can draw a picture of themselves either as the shopkeeper or the customer. Complete a gapped text of the dialogue. Repeat the activity with different food items. Draw and write about a favourite shop.

Learning review Children have practised new language from the unit by role-playing being a customer and a shopkeeper.

Differentiation To support less able children, encourage them to draw the food items for sale/on their shopping list. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to add up the prices and give change. Challenge more confident children to have an item on ‘special’ or a ‘buy one get one free’ offer.
