PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Play a letter game. Spell out a clothes item and ask children to draw it: e.g. ‘s-k-i-r-t’ – children draw a skirt. They can do this on their whiteboard. Dictate mixed-up letters that make clothes words to children: e.g. ‘a-t-h’ – hat. First, they write the letters on their whiteboard. Then they try to rearrange the letters to make a word. Write the correct spellings on the board.

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to my favourite clothes (1).

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “s-k-i-r-t” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Model the clothing words with picture cards or real garments. Demonstrate the source task while describing what a person is wearing.

Original OIC task reference: Main activity Put children into small groups to share resources. Hand out a selection of magazines to children. Tell them that they are going to make a collage poster using magazine pictures. Elicit that they need to look through the magazines and cut out clothes items that they like the look of. Language scaffold: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “a-t-h” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Use the original main activity with teacher prompts: Main activity Put children into small groups to share resources. Hand out a selection of magazines to children. Tell them that they are going to make a collage poster using magazine pictures. Elicit that they need to look through the magazines and cut out clothes items that they like the look of. Instruct children to stick these onto a piece of paper (they can draw a stick person and stick their collage on top). Praise children for their creativity as they produce their outfit. Prompt them to think about what they would like to add, what colours they would like to use, and what they need to look for next. Elicit an example sentence from a child. Guide learners through one complete example; ask them to justify their choice. CCQs: Is this clothing worn on the head, feet, or body? Which item matches the picture? Is the person wearing it now?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “s-k-i-r-t” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Do you choose the clothing picture first or say the sentence first? Will your partner describe or point? Demonstrate one turn.

Sentence frames: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “a-t-h” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Individually complete a new version of the original task for My favourite clothes (1).

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “s-k-i-r-t” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, provide gapped sentences. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to add detail to their descriptive sentences (e.g. by giving the person a name, explaining what foods they like, etc.).

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “a-t-h” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have produced a collage poster to illustrate favourite clothes and written simple sentences to describe their design.

Exit ticket: Choose a new outfit picture, name two items and say one correct sentence.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “s-k-i-r-t” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
