Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity If you have pinwheels, hand them out to children. If not, hand out a single piece of paper. Everyone sits in a circle. Tell children to sit with their backs straight and their bodies relaxed. Blow in one long, deep breath onto the pinwheel/piece of paper together. Ask how they felt. Hopefully, they felt calm and relaxed. Now ask children to blow with quick, short breaths and ask how they felt. Now blow normally onto the pinwheel. Ask children to notice how they feel with these different ways of breathing. Elicit that when they are nervous or worried, breathing deeply can help. Quickly revise clothes vocabulary. Indicate to an item of clothing you’re wearing or show one

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to how are we different? (1).

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “he” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Model the clothing words with picture cards or real garments. Demonstrate the source task while describing what a person is wearing.

Original OIC task reference: Main activity Play a game. Say: I’m going to say a clothes word. If you’re wearing it, stand up. Ready? Proceed to say different clothes items. Put children into groups to play the game with a more confident child saying the words. On the board, draw a boy and a girl wearing different clothes. Say, for example: She’s wearing a T-shirt. Ask: Is this the boy or the girl? Elicit that it’s the girl. Then ask: How do you know? And elicit ‘he’ for a boy and ‘she’ for a girl. Invite

Concept check: Is this clothing worn on the head, feet, or body? Which item matches the picture? Is the person wearing it now?

Language scaffold: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “she” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Use the original main activity with teacher prompts: Main activity Play a game. Say: I’m going to say a clothes word. If you’re wearing it, stand up. Ready? Proceed to say different clothes items. Put children into groups to play the game with a more confident child saying the words. On the board, draw a boy and a girl wearing different clothes. Say, for example: She’s wearing a T-shirt. Guide learners through one complete example; ask them to justify their choice. CCQs: Is this clothing worn on the head, feet, or body? Which item matches the picture? Is the person wearing it now?

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “He” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Do you choose the clothing picture first or say the sentence first? Will your partner describe or point? Demonstrate one turn.

Sentence frames: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “She” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks In pairs, draw some figures wearing different clothes and number them. A child says a sentence and their partner guesses which number figure it is. Cut out magazine pictures to create outfit collages.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “he” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, provide some sentence starters. As an extension task for more able children, encourage them to write more detailed sentences, including the colours of the clothes items.

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “she” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have practised saying what other people are wearing using he and she.

Exit ticket: Choose a new outfit picture, name two items and say one correct sentence.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “He” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
