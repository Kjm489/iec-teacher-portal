1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Play ‘Can I cross the river, please, Mr Crocodile?’. If possible, take children into the playground. You are the crocodile in the river and children need to get from one side to the other. (Mark this out with chalk.) They ask in chorus: Please Mr Crocodile, can I cross your river? You say: Yes, if you’re wearing [red socks]. These children are safe and cross the river. Everyone else now has to try and cross without getting caught. Once children have got the idea, encourage a confident child to take on the role of Mr Crocodile.

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Complete Oxford Discover WB 1 pp.32–33. Draw a favourite outfit. See if children can talk about clothes they don’t like wearing.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “trainers” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, pair them with more confident children. Write the sentences on the board as a reference. Challenge more confident children to think about winter clothes and summer clothes (if appropriate in your context). As an extension task for more able children, ask them to tell you why they like their clothes (e.g. they’re comfortable/fashionable/cool/colourful; they keep me warm/dry, etc).

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “scarf” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have revised and learned new clothes vocabulary and have identified them in speech.

Exit ticket: Choose a new outfit picture, name two items and say one correct sentence.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “dress” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Clothes I wear’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by listening for specific information. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
