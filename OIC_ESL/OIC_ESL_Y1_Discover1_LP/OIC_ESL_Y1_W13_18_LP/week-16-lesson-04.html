Original OIC context Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Clothes I wear’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by ordering words in a sentence.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.

Introductory activity Play a clothes game. Say a clothes item and children mime putting it on. Then invite a confident child to call out a word. Ask the class to help you spell words on the board: How do I spell ‘shoes’? Elicit ‘s-h-o-e-s’. Hand out mini whiteboards. Say a clothes item and children write the word secretly. Count 1-2-3, then everyone holds up their board to show what they’ve written. Remind children that they have to practise certain things over and over in order to master them.

Main activity Hand out the suggested exercises, or create your own. Say: We have a puzzle to solve. Do you like puzzles? The sentences are jumbled up. Can you help me? Do the first sentence together as an example. Write the correct sentence on the board. Remind children of the importance of word order in sentences. Let children work either individually or in pairs to put the words in the correct order. Review answers as a class. Say: But we don’t know about the children’s socks! Let’s write a jumbled sentence. Put children into groups of four. Challenge them to think of a sentence together. Then each child writes one word of the sentence on their mini whiteboard: e.g. He’s / wearing / pink / socks. Now ask each group to stand up, show each other their whiteboards (within the group), and put themselves in the wrong order. Invite groups to the front in turn. The rest of the class work together to put their classmates in the right order to make the sentence. Encourage children to use imperative phrases, such as: Liang, move next to Kim. Invite a confident child to read out the sentence. Return to the exercises handout and invite individual children to read a sentence aloud. On the board, write ‘sh’ and ask children to tell you what sound it makes. Elicit /sh/ and ask for example words with /sh/ (e.g. shirt, shorts, etc.). To end the lesson, line up children. If you say a word with a /sh/ sound, they jump to the left, and if not, to the right.
