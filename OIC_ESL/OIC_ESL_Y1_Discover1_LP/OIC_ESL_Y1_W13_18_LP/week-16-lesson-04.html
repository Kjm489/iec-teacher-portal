PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Play a clothes game. Say a clothes item and children mime putting it on. Then invite a confident child to call out a word. Ask the class to help you spell words on the board: How do I spell ‘shoes’? Elicit ‘s-h-o-e-s’. Hand out mini whiteboards. Say a clothes item and children write the word secretly. Count 1-2-3, then everyone holds up their board to show what they’ve written. Remind children that they have to practise certain things over and over in order to master them.

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to what are you wearing? (1).

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “shoes” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Do you choose the clothing picture first or say the sentence first? Will your partner describe or point? Demonstrate one turn.

Sentence frames: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “s-h-o-e-s” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Draw Lucía and Tomás in their clothes. Ask children to create a jumbled-up sentence for their partner to put in order.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “shoes” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, pair them with more able children so the latter can model the task and explain their thinking to the former. To challenge more able children, remove the capital letters from the first words in the sentences. Remind them to add full stops to the end of their sentences. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to search for more words with /sh/ in a dictionary and create an illustrated word list. 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have practised problem-solving by rearranging words into the correct order to make sentences about clothes.

Exit ticket: Choose a new outfit picture, name two items and say one correct sentence.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “shoes” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
