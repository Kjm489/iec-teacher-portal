4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Will you point to the food before speaking? Are you matching foods or writing a full paragraph? Show me the first example.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Individually complete a new version of the original task for Fruit and vegetables.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “sort” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, write some key words on the board to help them. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think of other food items and how they could be sorted. Encourage children to think about their last meal and how they could ‘sort’ the food items.

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “sort” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have learned about different fruit and vegetables. They have sorted the vocabulary into different groups and presented their findings on a poster.

Exit ticket: Identify one food or drink from a new picture and explain one fact about it.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “sort” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

Original OIC context Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Food and drink’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by presenting a poster to the class.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.

Introductory activity Show children an orange and a carrot (pictures or realia) and ask enquiringly how they are the same and how they are different. Accept all reasonable answers, including the fact that one is a fruit and the other is a vegetable.

Main activity Turn to Oxford Discover p.102 exs.A–B. Introduce the words. Identify which are fruit and which are vegetables, and which have a pit (stone) and which have seeds. Tell children they are going to create a poster about fruit and vegetables, which they will group in some way. Elicit different ways that fruit and vegetables can be grouped, for example, by colour, stone/seeds, fruit/vegetable, size, and any other reasonable ideas children have. Tell them to to choose one of these sorting criteria, then look for suitable examples in magazines to cut out and stick onto poster paper. Alternatively, children can draw the different fruit and vegetables. Allow them enough time to complete the activity, supplying any other vocabulary required. Invite children to present their posters to the class. Encourage them to say, for example: Our poster shows fruit with stones and fruit without stones. Give pairs time to rehearse and think what they are going to say. Remind the audience that they need to listen carefully and clap at the end. Ask children which foods on the posters they enjoy eating most. Additional task Complete Oxford Discover WB 1 p.88 ex.A.

Learning review Children have learned about different fruit and vegetables. They have sorted the vocabulary into different groups and presented their findings on a poster.

Differentiation To support less able children, write some key words on the board to help them. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to think of other food items and how they could be sorted. Encourage children to think about their last meal and how they could ‘sort’ the food items.

1. Fruit & Veg Market — Teacher Mistake Detective How to play: Teacher deliberately gives an incorrect description; students correct it using the lesson language. Use picture prompts for apple, banana, carrot, tomato; learners identify and say “fruit or vegetable”. ICQ: Do you repeat the mistake or correct it? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches fruit or vegetable and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

2. Fruit & Veg Market — Mini Showcase How to play: Pairs create a 20-second demonstration, drawing or role-play, then classmates give one positive response. CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches fruit or vegetable and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

3. Fruit & Veg Market — Picture Clue Relay How to play: Show or draw three partial clues; teams identify the target and justify their choice. Partners exchange clues about apple, banana, carrot, tomato; require a spoken response using “fruit or vegetable”. ICQ: Do you point or shout? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches fruit or vegetable and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

4. Fruit & Veg Market — Move-to-the-Corner How to play: Label four corners with suitable lesson choices; read a clue and learners move, then explain. CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches fruit or vegetable and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

5. Fruit & Veg Market — Partner Information Gap How to play: Give partners different picture cards; they ask questions without showing cards to find matches. Give a deliberately wrong example involving apple, banana, carrot, tomato; children correct it and say “fruit or vegetable”. ICQ: Do you show your partner your card? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches fruit or vegetable and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

6. Fruit & Veg Market — Build-and-Say Challenge How to play: Teams arrange picture or word cards into a correct sequence or set and say a complete target sentence. Pairs present a mini-dialogue or demonstration about apple, banana, carrot, tomato using “fruit or vegetable”. ICQ: Do you arrange cards first or speak first? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches fruit or vegetable and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.
