Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Ask children: What do you do when you see someone you don’t know? Elicit that we could ask somebody else who it is by describing them. Then ask children if they like making new friends. Talk briefly about the importance of friendship and the qualities of a good friend (e.g. kind, funny, helpful, shares their things).

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to how do we look? (1).

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “a green T-shirt” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Model the clothing words with picture cards or real garments. Demonstrate the source task while describing what a person is wearing.

Original OIC task reference: Main activity Using flashcards or pictures, review words for describing people and known clothes vocabulary. 3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Do you choose the clothing picture first or say the sentence first? Will your partner describe or point? Demonstrate one turn.

Sentence frames: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “long hair” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Make a spinner to practise new vocabulary. In pairs, children spin and ask and answer the question: Have you / Has [name of a child in the class] got [a red T-shirt]? Yes, I do. / No, they don’t. Draw a group of children with different clothes and write sentences to describe them.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: I am wearing ___. / He or she is wearing ___. / It is ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “a green T-shirt” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, write the role-play dialogue on the board for them to follow. As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write some sentences about several family members.

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “long hair” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have learned new vocabulary to describe physical features and applied their learning to a role-play activity. They have demonstrated friendship in group activities.

Exit ticket: Choose a new outfit picture, name two items and say one correct sentence.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “a green T-shirt” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
