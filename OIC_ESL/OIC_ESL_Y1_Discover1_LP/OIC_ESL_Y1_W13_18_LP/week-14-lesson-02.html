PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Make a circle with children. Play ‘Musical chairs’. Go around the circle, naming each child as a fruit. Choose five, such as apple, pear, banana, orange, and mango. Check that everyone knows which fruit they are. When you say their fruit, they must swap seats. When you say ‘fruit salad’, everyone swaps seats and you remove a seat, so that one child is ‘out’. Play this a couple of times and encourage the first child who is out to be the caller.

Original OIC context Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Food and drink’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by listening to a simple recipe and following the steps. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.

Introductory activity Make a circle with children. Play ‘Musical chairs’. Go around the circle, naming each child as a fruit. Choose five, such as apple, pear, banana, orange, and mango. Check that everyone knows which fruit they are. When you say their fruit, they must swap seats. When you say ‘fruit salad’, everyone swaps seats and you remove a seat, so that one child is ‘out’. Play this a couple of times and encourage the first child who is out to be the caller.

Main activity Ask the class if they know how to make a fruit salad. Pre-teach ‘peel’, ‘cut’, and ‘knife’. Elicit from children the ingredients of the fruit salad you’re going to tell them how to make. This is a listening lesson so you can either read out the recipe or play a recording. Ask children to listen for the ingredients and to note them down – number/which fruit. You can adapt the recipe with different fruit and quantities, if you wish. Elicit the method. Then read the method aloud and children check their ideas. If you have supervision, children can make the recipe themselves, following the instructions. Otherwise, you can demonstrate making the recipe (increasing the fruit!) and let children peel bananas, pour the juice, and serve with ‘a dollop of cream’. Before bringing food into the classroom to prepare, check that there are no children with any allergies or intolerances. Link the cream to the butter-making activity if you did it (see Week 13, Lesson 1), and where cream comes from. After tasting, ask children if they like the fruit salad and if they enjoyed making it/watching it being made. They can discuss in pairs before discussing as a class.
