PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Make a circle with children. Play ‘Musical chairs’. Go around the circle, naming each child as a fruit. Choose five, such as apple, pear, banana, orange, and mango. Check that everyone knows which fruit they are. When you say their fruit, they must swap seats. When you say ‘fruit salad’, everyone swaps seats and you remove a seat, so that one child is ‘out’. Play this a couple of times and encourage the first child who is out to be the caller.

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to making food.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Musical chairs” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Use the original main activity with teacher prompts: Main activity Ask the class if they know how to make a fruit salad. Pre-teach ‘peel’, ‘cut’, and ‘knife’. Elicit from children the ingredients of the fruit salad you’re going to tell them how to make. This is a listening lesson so you can either read out the recipe or play a recording. Ask children to listen for the ingredients and to note them down – number/which fruit. You can adapt the recipe with different fruit and quantities, if you wish. Elicit the method. Then read the method aloud and children check their ideas. If you have supervision, children can make the recipe themselves, following the instructions. Otherwise, you can demonstrate making the recipe (increasing the fruit!) and let children peel bananas, pour the juice, and serve with ‘a dollop of cream’. 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Will you point to the food before speaking? Are you matching foods or writing a full paragraph? Show me the first example.

Sentence frames: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “peel” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks Complete Oxford Discover SB 1 p.102 exs.C–D. Write out and illustrate the fruit salad recipe. Ask children to do the same for a savoury salad.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “cut” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children during the listening activity, provide a gapped text for them to complete (with a word bank or picture bank). As an extension task for more able children, they can write the recipe out and compare it with your version. They can also add more fruit (e.g. a fruit salad with ten ingredients). Additional information: Week 14, Lesson 2 Fruit salad recipe Ingredients 2 bananas 3 apples 1 orange ½ glass of orange juice a dollop of cream Method 1. Peel the fruit. 2. Cut up into small pieces with a knife. 3. Put in a bowl. 4. Add the orange juice. 5. Serve with a dollop of cream!

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “knife” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have listened to and prepared a recipe for a fruit salad.

Exit ticket: Identify one food or drink from a new picture and explain one fact about it.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “Musical chairs” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
