Original OIC context Context
This week, children will reflect on their individual learning from Weeks 16–17.
Children will also participate in an extended activity, practising the skills they have learned and focusing their work on areas of particular interest. 
Lessons this week will vary, depending on the scope of the chosen activity. Break up the activity into manageable stages to fit into the ESL lessons this week.

Source lesson activities Independent reflection Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used during Weeks 16–17. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, and Writing. Lead a discussion with children about these skills. For example: Tell me an activity when we practised speaking in English. When we [played XX game], what skills did we use? Did we speak? Did we listen? What did you find easy/difficult? Why? Help children to assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children who may find this self-reflective process quite challenging. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

Main activity Explain that this week, children will participate in one or more extended activities in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas: Children plan a fashion show. They bring in clothes, model their chosen items, and take turns describing what they are wearing. Alternatively, they can model their school uniform. You might want to invite their families/caregivers, for which children can make invitations. Children design an item of clothing (e.g. a sports jacket with lots of different pockets). Children make a class book, illustrating their learning in the unit. Children design a clothes game. Give them a blank racetrack template where they can draw pictures, write and decide on rules, and prepare for another group to play. Children create a survey to ask other people in the school community about their favourite clothes. Encourage them to think about the best way to present their results. Read some stories with children and talk about what the characters are wearing. Additional task If you made the clothes ‘washing line’ use it at line-up time or whenever you have a couple of spare minutes to review clothes vocabulary, asking children to find different items on the line and name them. (If you have duplicates of different colours, this also is a great opportunity to review colours.) As an extension, children can write labels for the items.

Learning review Children have consolidated their knowledge and learning on the topic of ‘Clothes I wear’.

Differentiation Encourage children who need more support to answer simple questions with tick/cross cards. Provide language and gapped texts, and put them into groups with more able children. For more able children, encourage them to do more. For example, they could write questions and true or false statements for the race track game.

Scaffolding · Model → Guide → Practise → Assess Pre-teach key language using the source vocabulary, pictures, realia and gestures. Model the task with one student; guide a class attempt, then move to pairs or groups. Differentiate using the source plan's guidance. ICQs — adapt to the exact activity “Do we work alone or with a partner?” “Do we point, say, draw, or write?” “What do we do first?” Check with a learner demonstration before beginning. CCQs — verify meaning, not just instructions Show two contrasting topic pictures from the source activity and ask learners to choose the correct one. Ask one short meaning question using the actual target word or phrase, and have learners demonstrate or explain their choice. Formative assessment Check whether learners can understand the target language and complete the source task independently, with prompts, or not yet. Close with a topic-related exit response.
