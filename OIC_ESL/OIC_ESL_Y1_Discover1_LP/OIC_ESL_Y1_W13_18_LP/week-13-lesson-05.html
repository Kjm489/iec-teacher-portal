Measurable Learning Objectives

Demonstrate understanding of the lesson vocabulary through the specified original activity.

Produce a supported response and complete an independent check related to breakfast, lunch, and dinner.

Teacher QA check: Confirm the named target words, textbook task and OIC codes against the original source. Timings refer to the seven IEC stages (8+12+15+10 / 25+10+10 = 90 minutes); original source activity descriptions may need shortening to fit. Six games are optional alternatives, not six additional required tasks.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Introductory activity Ask children to tell you what their absolute favourite food is. For example, if for one day they could only eat one food. Ask them if it’s healthy and introduce the concept of ‘five a day’ (a minimum of five portions of fruit/vegetables per day is considered healthy). Elicit from children if they regularly achieve ‘five a day’. Ask them to think about colour – ideally the fruit and vegetables eaten should be a variety of colours. As a class, create a chart to record what children ate the day before and its colour(s) to check that they had their five portions.

Diagnostic: invite learners to name or point to one example linked to breakfast, lunch, and dinner.

Task ICQ: Do you listen and look first, then tell your partner one idea? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “five a day” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Model the key food vocabulary using pictures or real objects. Demonstrate the source task and think aloud about the food, its origin, or the target food expression.

Original OIC task reference: Main activity Pre-teach ‘breakfast’, ‘lunch’, and ‘dinner’. Play the song in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.101. Say a typical food item that children in your context eat for these meals. Ask if it’s for breakfast, lunch, or dinner. For example, say: Cereal and milk? It’s usually breakfast. Ask which meal children like best and why. Tell children that they are going to find out what others eat for these three meals. Elicit the question and answer: What do you have for breakfast/lunch

Concept check: Is this something we eat or drink? Does it come from a plant or an animal? Show the picture that proves your answer.

Language scaffold: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Do you watch and listen to my example before trying it? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “breakfast” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Use the original main activity with teacher prompts: Main activity Pre-teach ‘breakfast’, ‘lunch’, and ‘dinner’. Play the song in Oxford Discover SB 1 p.101. Say a typical food item that children in your context eat for these meals. Ask if it’s for breakfast, lunch, or dinner. For example, say: Cereal and milk? It’s usually breakfast. Ask which meal children like best and why. Tell children that they are going to find out what others eat for these three meals. Elicit the question and answer: What do you have for breakfast/lunch/dinner? I have … Put children into small groups of three. First, ask children to predict what everyone eats (or most people) for breakfast, lunch, and dinner. Then tell them that they are going to find out by asking questions. Guide learners through one complete example; ask them to justify their choice. CCQs: Is this something we eat or drink? Does it come from a plant or an animal? Show the picture that proves your answer.

Task ICQ: Are we completing the first example together? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “lunch” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Partners take turns completing a new example from the source activity. One responds and the other checks, then they switch. ICQs: Will you point to the food before speaking? Are you matching foods or writing a full paragraph? Show me the first example.

Sentence frames: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Does each partner have a turn to speak or show an answer? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “dinner” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Additional tasks If children are not meeting ‘five a day’, encourage them to think what fruit/vegetables they could easily add. Make a collage of a healthy meal with fruit and vegetables by cutting out ingredients from magazines and labelling. Create a role-play about what children eat for breakfast, lunch, and dinner. You can give them three words to include in the role-play, or two questions.

Gradually remove the picture/word prompt. Assess the learner’s independent response using: This is ___. / I eat ___. / It comes from ___.

Task ICQ: Will you complete your own answer before asking for help? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “five a day” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Differentiation To support less able children, tell them to focus on asking just one of their questions. Encourage group members to support each other as they carry out their survey. As an extension task for more able children, they can research breakfast food in a different country.

Fade support from teacher model → picture cue → sentence starter → independent explanation.

Task ICQ: Can you use the picture or sentence starter if you need support? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “breakfast” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Learning review Children have asked and answered about what they eat for the three main meals of the day. They have been introduced to the idea of healthy eating.

Exit ticket: Identify one food or drink from a new picture and explain one fact about it.

Teacher records who can respond independently, with prompts, or needs reteaching.

Task ICQ: Do you show or say one answer without copying a partner? Lesson CCQ: In the lesson example, what does “lunch” mean? Point to or explain the matching picture/action.
