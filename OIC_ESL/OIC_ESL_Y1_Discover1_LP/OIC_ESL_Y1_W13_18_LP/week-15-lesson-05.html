Original OIC context Context
This week, children will reflect on their individual learning from Weeks 13–14.
Children will also participate in an extended activity, practising the skills they have learned and focusing their work on areas of particular interest. 
Lessons this week will vary, depending on the scope of the chosen activity. Break up the activity into manageable stages to fit into the ESL lessons this week.

Source lesson activities Independent reflection Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used during Weeks 13–14. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, and Writing. Lead a discussion with children about these skills. For example: Tell me an activity when we practised speaking in English. When we [played XX game], what skills did we use? Did we speak? Did we listen? What did you find easy/difficult? Why? Help children to assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children who may find this self-reflective process quite challenging. Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

Main activity Explain that this week, children will participate in one or more extended activities in small groups. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way. Teachers may choose one or more of the following activity ideas: Children plan a healthy menu for a day, including ‘five a day’. Bring in some simple recipes that children can follow and make. For example, ‘coconut pyramids’. Ensure no children have allergies or intolerances to any of the recipes beforehand. Children make a zigzag book showing what they have learned in Weeks 13–14. Find English language stories about food and drink, and read them with children. Children make spinners to revise vocabulary and activities from the unit. Alternatively, half the class creates spinners revising Week 13, and the other half of the class focuses on Week 14. Then pair a Week 13 child with a Week 14 child to play together. If you do not have materials to make a spinner, ask children to create wordsnakes. Using the course books, create a game. Children signal to pictures on the pages covered in Weeks 13–14, then ask their group: What’s this? Continue to play the whole-class shop role-play, introducing new food items and setting challenges.

Learning review Children have consolidated their knowledge and learning on the topic of ‘Food and drink’.

Differentiation Encourage children who need more support to answer simple questions with tick/cross cards. Provide language and gapped texts, and put them into groups with more able children. For more able children, encourage them to do more. For example, they could orally retell a food story they read for others.

Model the task with one student; guide a class attempt, then move to pairs or groups. Differentiate using the source plan's guidance. ICQs — adapt to the exact activity “Do we work alone or with a partner?” “Do we point, say, draw, or write?” “What do we do first?” Check with a learner demonstration before beginning. CCQs — verify meaning, not just instructions Show two contrasting topic pictures from the source activity and ask learners to choose the correct one. Ask one short meaning question using the actual target word or phrase, and have learners demonstrate or explain their choice. Formative assessment Check whether learners can understand the target language and complete the source task independently, with prompts, or not yet. Close with a topic-related exit response.

1. Food Learning Fair — Partner Information Gap How to play: Give partners different picture cards; they ask questions without showing cards to find matches. Use picture prompts for all unit food language; learners identify and say “I learned ...”. ICQ: Do you show your partner your card? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches I learned ... and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

2. Food Learning Fair — Build-and-Say Challenge How to play: Teams arrange picture or word cards into a correct sequence or set and say a complete target sentence. Create two contrasting examples from all unit food language; children explain their choices using “I learned ...”. ICQ: Do you arrange cards first or speak first? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches I learned ... and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

3. Food Learning Fair — Teacher Mistake Detective How to play: Teacher deliberately gives an incorrect description; students correct it using the lesson language. Partners exchange clues about all unit food language; require a spoken response using “I learned ...”. ICQ: Do you repeat the mistake or correct it? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches I learned ... and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

4. Food Learning Fair — Mini Showcase How to play: Pairs create a 20-second demonstration, drawing or role-play, then classmates give one positive response. Arrange cards illustrating all unit food language; learners explain their arrangement using “I learned ...”. ICQ: Do you work alone or with a partner? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches I learned ... and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

5. Food Learning Fair — Picture Clue Relay How to play: Show or draw three partial clues; teams identify the target and justify their choice. Give a deliberately wrong example involving all unit food language; children correct it and say “I learned ...”. ICQ: Do you point or shout? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches I learned ... and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.

6. Food Learning Fair — Move-to-the-Corner How to play: Label four corners with suitable lesson choices; read a clue and learners move, then explain. Pairs present a mini-dialogue or demonstration about all unit food language using “I learned ...”. ICQ: Do you move before or after the clue? CCQ: Show two examples using the lesson vocabulary; ask learners to select the one that matches I learned ... and explain or demonstrate why. Check: Listen for accurate use of the target words and sentence frame; model again when needed.
