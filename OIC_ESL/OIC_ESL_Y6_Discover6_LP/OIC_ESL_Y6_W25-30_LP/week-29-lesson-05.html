Lesson focus: Writing an information brochure

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a short video about life in a primary school. Search online for ‘A day in the life primary school video’ or similar. Reassure children it isn’t necessary for them to understand everything they hear.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What was the same as our school? What was different?

Oxford introductory task: Give children a few minutes to add any additional information to their planning notes.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Children will use this lesson to write and illustrate their school brochure.

Oxford task — teacher models: Briefly recap the purpose and audience of the task.

Oxford task — teacher models: Write the lesson’s vocabulary on the board. Check that children understand these adjectives in relation to the language they should use.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Remind children to use their planning notes, school brochures (Oxford Discover), and/or Bright Ideas AB 6 p.95 for reference.

Oxford task — guided practice: Encourage children to make their brochures as attractive as possible to their audience. They can add photos and illustrations.

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite children to take a short break after writing each section of their brochure. They may stand up and stretch or shake out their body before continuing their work.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Around two-thirds of the way through the task, give children five minutes to check their work so far with a partner. Check for activity requirements, accuracy, and repetition.

Oxford task — independent application: Give the class a reminder of the time five minutes before the end of the task.

Oxford task — independent application: Invite children to share a section of their brochure that they are proud of with a partner. Encourage them to learn from each other. Celebrate children’s hard work and creativity.

Oxford task — independent application: At the end of the lesson, remind children that at the beginning of the unit they ranked how confident they were about taking on new challenges, from 1 (not at all confident) to 10 (very confident). Ask them to think about whether they have changed their number, now that they have completed the unit. They do not need to share their answer.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide small-group support similar to in Week 29, Lesson 4. Consider providing a template for the brochure.

Oxford differentiation: Move around the room to support less confident children. Discuss their next steps.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to include at least three examples of verbs taking the infinitive or gerund: e.g. We recommend bringing a calculator for Maths lessons.

Oxford additional tasks: Look at your school’s website to find out what information it includes. In pairs, encourage children to discuss what works and what needs improvement.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written a primary school information brochure.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Challenges and successes’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by organizing written and visual information to meet the format, purpose, and audience of an information brochure.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
