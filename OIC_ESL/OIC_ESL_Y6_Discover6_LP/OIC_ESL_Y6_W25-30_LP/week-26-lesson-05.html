PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Divide the class into two teams. Invite a child from the first team to the front of the class. Give them a common fear to mime. Their team has 20 seconds to guess. Use a timer. If they guess correctly, they win a point. If not, the other team can guess for a bonus point.

Oxford introductory task: Now give a child from the second team a mime to complete. Repeat the process. Ask four or five children from each team to mime. The team with the most points at the end wins!

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Divide children into groups of three or four. Tell them they are going to work together to create a list of instructions to help someone overcome their fear. Display pictures of common fears on the board as prompts.

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children to use everything they have learned in this unit about fear, controlling fear, and suggesting solutions. Remind them that neural pathways in their brain are strengthened by repetition and practice.

Oxford task — teacher models: Review the structure of an instructional text. Use Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.168–169, or work together with the class to create an example text on the board, ‘How to overcome your fear of _____’. Use numbered instructions beginning with imperative verbs.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Give each group time to choose a fear to write about. Ask them to follow these steps. Classes using Oxford Discover may use the planner in WB 6 p.159 for reference.

Oxford task — guided practice: Think of ideas for overcoming the fear. Write notes on a large sheet of paper.

Oxford task — guided practice: Organize and number the key points in a logical sequence.

Oxford task — guided practice: Add supporting details to each point to explain why or how it works.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Each group member should write one or two of the numbered instructions on separate strips of paper.

Oxford task — independent application: Bring each other’s writing together to edit and finalize the text.

Oxford task — independent application: Tell children how long they have for the task. Challenge them to manage their own time, spending around one third each on on planning, writing, and finalizing.

Oxford task — independent application: Observe groups as they work. At the end of the lesson, ask one or two groups to evaluate how they worked together. What went well? What would you do differently next time?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability groups. Remind more able children to help their peers write their sentences once they have completed their own.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write an individual instructional text for a different fear.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to include questions in their advice to the reader: e.g. Could you take one small step to tackle your fear?

Oxford additional tasks: Invite each group to read their list of instructions to the class.

Oxford additional tasks: Read the instructional text in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.158 and answer the questions.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have worked in a group to write a list of instructions to help people to overcome a common fear.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Fears
Week 26, Lesson 5: How to overcome your fear
Learning outcomes: 6.2f, 6.4a, 6.4b, 6.4e

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Fears’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and writing skills by working in a group to produce a joint instructional text. In doing so, they will practise teamwork and time management skills.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
