PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Give children two minutes to think of challenging jobs. See if they can recall the emergency services. In addition, see if anyone mentions ‘local politician’ or ‘city official’.

Oxford introductory task: Tell children that managing a city is challenging. People often complain that public services and places are ‘too noisy, too dirty, or too dangerous’. They say that the park ‘isn’t big enough’ or ‘isn’t clean enough’. Write phrases on the board. Underline ‘too’ and ‘enough’. Establish their meaning.

Oxford introductory task: Elicit examples from children to describe problems in your own city or local area.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Oxford Discover should:

Oxford task — teacher models: teach the use of too and enough using examples that indicate problems in a community setting (e.g. the school playground or the city streets). For example:

Oxford task — teacher models: This road is too busy and too dangerous.

Oxford task — teacher models: The surface under the play equipment isn’t soft enough.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite children to say other adjectives that can be used with too or enough. Write them on the board and challenge volunteers to make sentences with these adjectives.

Oxford task — guided practice: Teachers using Bright Ideas should:

Oxford task — guided practice: ask children to use the story in CB 6 pp.90–91 to complete ex.1 on p.92.

Oxford task — guided practice: teach the use of too and enough using exs.2–3 on p.92 as a support.

Oxford task — guided practice: divide children into pairs. Ask them to do ex.4. Give them an opportunity to ask any questions if they are unsure of using too and enough.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: optionally, complete ex.5. Alternatively, move on to the exercise below.

Oxford task — independent application: Display a local news story about a current problem in the local area, such as litter in the streets, a dangerous road, or crowded public transport. Read and summarize the key points. Write important vocabulary on the board.

Oxford task — independent application: Divide children into groups of three or four. Encourage them to discuss the problem using too and enough, and to suggest possible solutions. They can use Bright Ideas CB 6 p.92, or Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.158 and 166 for support.

Oxford task — independent application: Give each group a sheet of paper. Instruct them to write brief sentences describing the problem and listing two or three solutions they want to suggest.

Oxford task — independent application: Display each group’s work around the classroom. Children walk around and read each other’s solutions. Encourage them to decide which group they think has the best ideas for solving the problem. They should explain their reasoning.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, jointly create one or two short model exchanges before the group discussion. Set out the problem and give advice using ‘verb + infinitive/gerund’.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to choose and to find solutions to their own news story.

Oxford additional tasks: Make a poster about a problem in the city. The aim is to encourage people to change their behaviour or to vote for a particular solution.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.91.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to use too and enough to describe a problem. They have applied their skills in suggesting solutions to address a real-world problem.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Week 28, Lesson 5: Problems and solutions
Learning outcomes: 6.2b, 6.2c, 6.2e

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘Challenges and successes’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by building on their learning in Weeks 25 and 28. They will go into depth about real problems which require solutions.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
