Lesson focus: What is success?

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete text-based answer with a quoted or paraphrased detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce an evidence-based reading response.

Evidence to collect: Collect an evidence-based reading response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford outcomes: 6.2a, 6.2d, 6.2f

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Display five or six pictures that illustrate success in different ways (e.g. individual sport, team sport, academic awards, learning to ride a bicycle, playing a musical instrument, a public figure [politician, activist, or similar]). Try to include examples from your own school, your own city or country, and another country.

Oxford introductory task: In pairs, ask children to discuss what the pictures have in common. Can they predict the topic of the new unit?

Oxford introductory task: Elicit ideas, then confirm: We will be looking at challenges and successes in this unit! Define or elicit the words ‘challenge’, ‘success’, and ‘succeed (at)’.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Pair up children for a five-minute discussion.

Oxford task — teacher models: Bright Ideas: Instruct each pair to look at the Talking Point and ex.5 in CB 6 p.87. Encourage them to ask and answer questions about when they succeeded at something.

Oxford task — teacher models: Oxford Discover: Invite children to share a time when they succeeded at something with their partner. Ask them to explain what they did to succeed, and how it made them feel.

Oxford task — teacher models: Invite volunteers to share their experiences with the class.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Bring pairs together to form groups of four for a longer discussion task. Give each group a large sheet of paper for making notes.

Oxford task — guided practice: Bright Ideas: Instruct each group to answer the questions in exs.3–4 in CB 6 p.87.

Oxford task — guided practice: Oxford Discover: Instruct each group to:

Oxford task — guided practice: make a list of successful people they know, and their area of success.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: decide if they agree or disagree with the statement: ‘Success is the same thing as winning.’ Prompt them to explain their choice.

Oxford task — independent application: make a list of different methods or people that can help them succeed.

Oxford task — independent application: Collate groups’ suggestions on the board. Identify common thoughts and ideas. Suggest any further examples that children may have missed.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask: Are you more likely to try a new challenge if you know ways to help you succeed? Establish if children have used the methods on the board to help them succeed in the past.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to think about how confident they are about taking on new challenges, from 1 (not at all confident) to 10 (very confident). Tell them to remember this number for the end of the unit. They do not need to share.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work in a small group with an adult to answer the questions.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to provide real-world examples to support their answers.

Oxford additional tasks: Ask children to write and illustrate a paragraph about a time they achieved success, and to describe who and/or what methods helped them to achieve it.

Oxford additional tasks: In pairs or small groups, ask children to discuss the statement: ‘To be successful in life, you need to become famous.’ Remind them to explain why they agree or disagree.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have discussed the meaning of ‘success’, drawing on their own personal experiences.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
