Lesson focus: Looking back

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Tell children there is an imaginary line across the classroom, which goes from ‘terrified’ at one end to ‘relaxed’ at the other.

Oxford introductory task: Read a list of six to eight common fears. Ask children to place themselves somewhere on the line for each fear.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: Did anything surprise you about the results of this activity, for yourself or for the class?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Allocate enough time in today’s lesson to teach the past unreal conditional. Explain that this tense allows us to talk about possible situations in the past that didn’t happen. Focus children’s attention on the two halves of the sentence: the condition and the result. Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.157. For example:

Oxford task — teacher models: If I hadn’t seen the lion, I would have walked in front of it.

Oxford task — teacher models: If my mother hadn’t watched the news, she wouldn’t have known about the earthquake.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Give children time to practise the past unreal conditional in pairs. You could use the exercises in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.157 and WB 6 pp.146–147 exs.B–E to support them.

Oxford task — guided practice: Tell children about a situation you have experienced in which you were scared. This can be real, or imagined. Make sure you use two or three examples of the past unreal conditional. Ask children to raise their hand when they hear you use it. For example:

Oxford task — guided practice: If I hadn’t been alert, I would have run into that truck.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: I wouldn’t have been so nervous if I had practised my presentation. (Note how in this example, the sentence begins with the result and ends in the condition.)

Oxford task — independent application: In pairs, children follow your example. They tell a partner about something scary that happened to them. They think of a sentence in the past unreal conditional that they can apply to that situation. Encourage pairs to help each other to make these sentences.

Oxford task — independent application: Observe pairs as they work and identify common mistakes. Review them anonymously as a class by writing correct and incorrect examples on the board. Remind children that making mistakes is part of the learning process and they aren’t something to be afraid of!

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability pairs for the reflection activity.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, give them a short news story to analyse (e.g. a volcano eruption: If they hadn’t evacuated the town, the people living there would have been terrified.).

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to take part in the introductory activity by reminding them that fear is natural. Remind the whole class to respect others’ feelings.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 6 p.147 ex.F, or write five sentences using the past unreal conditional.

Oxford additional tasks: Do the introductory activity again, this time inviting children to provide their own ideas.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned and practised using the past unreal conditional. They have reflected on past situations involving fear.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
