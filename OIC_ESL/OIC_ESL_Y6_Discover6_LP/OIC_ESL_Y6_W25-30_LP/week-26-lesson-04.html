Lesson focus: Adrenaline adventures

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate note of a heard detail and an inference, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a listening note plus supported inference.

Evidence to collect: Collect a listening note plus supported inference; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play the Wrap Up video in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.159 from 2:20 onwards (bungee jumping and skydiving). Alternatively, play a short video montage of dangerous, exciting sports. Search online for ‘video montage extreme sports’.

Oxford introductory task: See if children can identify any of the sports. Write a list of extreme sports on the board. Ask: Which sports can we do in our country? Which sports might take place in other countries?

Oxford introductory task: Encourage children to tell a partner one sport they would like to try.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Remind children of the word ‘adrenaline’ from Week 25. In pairs, give them two minutes to recall the effects of adrenaline on the body. Take feedback as a class.

Oxford task — teacher models: Do a listening activity about activities that create adrenaline in the body.

Oxford task — teacher models: Use Oxford Discover SB 6 p.166 exs.A–B or Bright Ideas CB 6 p.20 ex.3.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to identify how the speaker (Fred or Oli) feels about the activity or activities they describe.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask: Do you think the feelings of being ‘scared’ and ‘excited’ are connected? How?

Oxford task — guided practice: Follow up with a small-group discussion.

Oxford task — guided practice: Divide children into groups of four or five.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Give each group a set of pictures of scary sports and activities. Source real-life photos of extreme sports or use the following page references: Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.26, 46, 62, 113, 154, 158, 166; Bright Ideas CB 6 pp.18, 20, 30, 90–91.

Oxford task — independent application: Each group should nominate a leader. The leader will decide which picture to discuss, and when to move on to the next one. They should also ensure all children are able to contribute in a meaningful way, offering support and prompts where necessary.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to try to describe their feelings in detail: e.g. I would be afraid to go skydiving because I don’t like flying and I don’t like heights!

Oxford task — independent application: Invite each group leader to summarize their group’s discussion for the rest of the class.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide vocabulary banks with words for common fears and extreme sports, as well as adjectives to describe being scared or scary.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to stretch themselves and take a leadership role, and more confident children to let someone else lead. Remind less confident children that taking on this challenge can help them to grow and develop – this is an example of a good kind of ‘fear’!

Oxford additional tasks: Tell children to imagine you are planning a trip. Give them three or four options of scary sports or activities. Try to use real locations within travelling distance. Hold a class vote. If time allows, invite children to speak (for one minute first) to try to persuade their peers.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete exs.2–3 in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.18.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified how a speaker feels about scary sports. They have shared their own feelings about scary sports.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
