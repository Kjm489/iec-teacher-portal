PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Challenges and successes’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford introductory task: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford introductory task: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

Oxford task — teacher models: Motivational presentation (pair/group)

Oxford task — teacher models: Form groups of between two to five children.

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask groups to make a short presentation with the title ‘You can do it!’. The purpose of the presentation is to motivate other children at school to succeed.

Oxford task — teacher models: Children may include any information they wish from the unit. Encourage them to draw on their personal experiences to help them.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Groups will need to plan the outline of their presentation first. They can then work on separate parts individually, using relevant software, before combining them into one presentation. Encourage them to think about the best strategy.

Oxford task — guided practice: At the end of the week, allocate time for groups give their presentations. This could be to another class or in a school assembly.

Oxford task — guided practice: Challenge week (class)

Oxford task — guided practice: Dedicate the whole week to a range of active team and individual challenges, ideally in an outdoor or unfamiliar setting.

Oxford task — guided practice: Use the week as an opportunity for teambuilding and relationship building, encouraging children to support and give advice to their peers, and to offer solutions to problems.

Oxford task — guided practice: Celebrate children’s success in overcoming challenges. At the end of the week, ask them to identify their own achievements which they are proud of.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Podcast – ‘The advice show’ (group)

Oxford task — independent application: Form groups of four to five children.

Oxford task — independent application: Tell each group to plan and record a podcast in which the host interviews children facing different challenges or problems (e.g. starting a new school, moving to a new city, or feeling anxious about a test). The host of the show will then offer advice.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask each group to nominate one or two children to be the host(s), and three to be interviewed.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to break the task down into smaller goals by planning a timeline for the project. This should allocate time for planning, script writing, rehearsing, and recording.

Oxford task — independent application: Hold a ‘Listening lesson’ to finish the week. Play all the podcasts to the class. Elicit positive feedback and areas for improvement. Hold a class vote on the best podcast.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability groups and encourage group members to support each other. Allow them to use a detailed script when giving the presentation or recording the podcast.

Oxford differentiation: More able children can be asked to stretch themselves by speaking without detailed notes, or by improvising, during the presentation or podcast. Encourage them to think about what they want to say in advance and make brief notes.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Bright Ideas AB 6 pp.96–97.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
