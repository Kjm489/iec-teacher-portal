PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Write ‘fear’ on the board. See if children can tell you what this means. Then explain.

Oxford introductory task: Write the adjectives from the vocabulary bubble as well as ‘dangerous’ on the board. In pairs, ask children to decide which four words mean you are afraid, and which four words describe something to be afraid of. Check answers as a class (1. ‘afraid’, ‘nervous’, ‘scared’, ‘terrified’; 2. ‘dangerous’, ‘risky’, ‘scary’, ‘threatening’).

Oxford introductory task: Note that adjectives to describe being afraid are usually followed by ‘of’ (e.g. to be terrified of flying, to be nervous of public speaking). Discuss the differing strengths of the adjectives.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What are you afraid of? Encourage children to share one fear with a partner.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: How did the people on the bus feel? (really scared)

Oxford task — independent application: play the audio from CB 6 p.88 ex.3. Ask children to answer these questions:

Oxford task — independent application: What is Charlie nervous about? (doing a presentation)

Oxford task — independent application: What challenge does Rami want to do? (something sporty)

Oxford task — independent application: What activities does he suggest you do? (something new, like cooking a meal or scuba-diving)

Oxford task — independent application: Ask: Do you think fear can be useful? Why? Elicit answers to highlight the difference between dangerous things (e.g. poisonous animals, earthquakes) and activities which are usually positive despite being scary (e.g. doing a presentation or taking an exam). Ask: How does fear help us when taking ‘good’ risks? Elicit that it might help us to focus, motivate us, or give us energy.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a transcript of the listening activities. Write the questions they need to listen for on the board.

Oxford differentiation: Ask fast finishers to make a list of things people find scary, then categorize them into experiences they should avoid and experiences which can be positive.

Oxford additional tasks: Write answers to the three questions in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.142.

Oxford additional tasks: Look at the pictures in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.43 ex.1. Choose one picture. Encourage children to discuss in pairs how they would feel in this situation.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned key concepts and vocabulary around fear. They have considered how fear can sometimes be a positive thing.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
