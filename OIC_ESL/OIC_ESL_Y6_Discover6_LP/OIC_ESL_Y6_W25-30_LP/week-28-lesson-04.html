Lesson focus: Giving advice

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate note of a heard detail and an inference, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a listening note plus supported inference.

Evidence to collect: Collect a listening note plus supported inference; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: Giving advice

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children who they go to for advice at school or at home. Check that they know the names of staff members who support children in your school in a pastoral role.

Oxford introductory task: Encourage them to talk to their friends or to trusted adults whenever they are feeling worried or nervous about a challenging situation, even if they think it is ‘something small’.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: This lesson is composed of four short activities. Between each activity, encourage children to do five star jumps, stretch up, then touch their toes.

Oxford task — teacher models: Do a listening activity.

Oxford task — teacher models: Play the audio for ex.C in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.166, or for ‘Chat Room’ in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.89. Children listen and read along.

Oxford task — teacher models: Play the audio again. Instruct children to make notes on their whiteboard of the phrases that aren’t on the page (Oxford Discover), or to do ex.2 (Bright Ideas).

Oxford task — teacher models: Check answers as a class.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Teach/Review verbs that take the infinitive or the gerund (‘–ing’). You may refer to Bright Ideas CB 6 p.89 exs.3–4. Focus on verbs for advice or support. For example:

Oxford task — guided practice: I promise/agree/want/offer to help you practise English. (infinitive)

Oxford task — guided practice: I suggest/recommend/advise carrying a torch on a dark night. (gerund)

Oxford task — guided practice: Do a speaking activity to practise giving advice.

Oxford task — guided practice: In pairs, ask children to do the speaking activity in ex.C in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.166 (choose two or more activities), or exs.5–6 in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.89.

Oxford task — guided practice: Remind them to practise the grammar they have just learned. Children using Oxford Discover can also use the vocabulary from SB 6 p.158.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Play a class game. Remain in pairs.

Oxford task — independent application: Play a pre-prepared slide show. This should contain three to four pictures of challenging activities (e.g. scuba-diving, auditions for a play, a running race, building a robot).

Oxford task — independent application: Ask each pair to make notes for a very short exchange describing the challenge and giving advice, following this model:

Oxford task — independent application: (statement) I have decided to try scuba-diving!

Oxford task — independent application: (advice) I suggest practising in a swimming pool first.

Oxford task — independent application: For each picture, invite volunteers to share their sentences with the class. Discuss the different types of advice they give. Tell children that everyone will have a different perspective on how to tackle the challenge.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide mixed-up captions for each picture in the slide show: one statement and one piece of advice. Ask them to find the right captions for each photo.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to find out other common verbs that take the infinitive or the gerund. Discuss the difference in meanings for verbs which can take both: e.g. I stopped watering the flowers. / I stopped to water the flowers.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a mixture of correct and incorrect ‘verb + infinitive/gerund’ sentences on the board for children to identify and correct. Encourage children to make their own for a partner.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.89.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned key phrases for giving advice, using the construction ‘verb + infinitive/gerund’. They have practised giving advice in short exchanges.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
