Lesson focus: Planning an information brochure

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: Planning an information brochure

Oxford outcomes: 6.4a, 6.4b, 6.4c

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Write on the board ‘If I joined a new school, I would like to know …’.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What would you like to know if you joined a new school? Invite children to tell a partner how they would complete the sentence on the board.

Oxford introductory task: Then share answers as a class. Encourage any children who may have changed schools in recent years to share their experience.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children they are going to write a brochure for their primary school, aimed at new students.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Bright Ideas should follow exs.1–4 in AB 6 p.95. Note that writing the brochure will take place in Week 29, Lesson 5.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Oxford Discover should:

Oxford task — teacher models: review in more detail the structure of the brochure used in Week 29, Lesson 3. Look at the format of each section (headings, paragraphing), the use of photos and other visual information, and the layout on the page.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: review the purpose of the brochure (to provide practical information, to give recommendations and advice, to support new students who might be nervous).

Oxford task — guided practice: with children’s input, make a list on the board of topics to include in a school brochure.

Oxford task — guided practice: invite children to choose three to four topics to include in their brochure. Instruct them to make notes on each topic.

Oxford task — guided practice: Give each child a sheet of A3 or A4 paper folded in half to make a brochure. Show them how to use this to plan out their layout, thinking about how much room they need for each topic and leaving room for (photos or) drawings.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Observe children as they work. Praise those who are focused on the task, or who are helping their peers.

Oxford task — independent application: Remind children to put themselves in their audience’s shoes as they plan.

Oxford task — independent application: Who is your audience? What do they want to know?

Oxford task — independent application: How will they be feeling about starting a new school?

Oxford task — independent application: Can you include information about how your school will help them to be successful?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work in a small group with adult support on a shared list of two to three topics.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able and confident children to include different sections: one aimed at students, and one at their parents and carers. Ask: How will this affect the information you include, and the tone of your writing?

Oxford additional tasks: With the necessary permissions, allow children to take photos around the school to illustrate their brochure. Print these out before the next lesson.

Oxford additional tasks: In pairs or small groups, invite children to compare their plans and provide constructive feedback to help each other improve their work. Give children time to make changes or additions to their plan based on feedback.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have planned an information brochure for their primary school.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
