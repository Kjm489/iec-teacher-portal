Lesson focus: Guidance for success

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete text-based answer with a quoted or paraphrased detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce an evidence-based reading response.

Evidence to collect: Collect an evidence-based reading response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Display photos of secondary schools from different countries. Choose a varied selection. See if children can identify the purpose of these buildings. Then display a photo of your own local secondary school(s) to help children check their answer.

Oxford introductory task: Discuss similarities and differences between the photos.

Oxford introductory task: Briefly discuss your class’s potential secondary schooling path(s).

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Oxford Discover should:

Oxford task — teacher models: share an example of an English language secondary school brochure. Try to have one copy per pair. Many schools produce downloadable brochures if you are unable to source paper copies locally.

Oxford task — teacher models: focus children’s attention on the cover and the table of contents. Ask: What do you think the purpose of this brochure is? Who is the audience?

Oxford task — teacher models: ask pairs to quickly skim through the brochure to understand how it is structured. They should identify the main sections. Then ask them to read in more detail and to make notes. Suggested questions:

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: What facilities does this school have?

Oxford task — guided practice: What challenges do you think you would have at this school?

Oxford task — guided practice: What support does it offer to help pupils/students be successful?

Oxford task — guided practice: talk through the structure of the brochure with the class. Invite children to share their answers to the above questions. Add any information they have missed.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Teachers using Bright Ideas should:

Oxford task — independent application: ask children to complete exs.1–3 and the Our Values questions in CB 6 p.94.

Oxford task — independent application: encourage children to identify possible challenges at Yellow Hill Secondary School and the ways the school supports their pupils/students to be successful.

Oxford task — independent application: talk about the school or schools that children in your class might attend from age 11 to 16. Compare local schools with Yellow Hill Secondary School. Ask: What similarities and differences do you know? Then share any knowledge you have with the class.

Oxford task — independent application: For both sets of classes, give children time to respond to what they have read, either in pairs or as a class. Ask: How do you feel about going to secondary school? What are you looking forward to? What do you think will be challenging? Adapt the question if necessary to the school system your class is part of.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work in an adult-led small group to identify key information in the brochure. Highlight important vocabulary.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to compare the advantages and disadvantages of a brochure with a school website, video, or open day.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to make a list of questions they have about going to secondary school, and to try to find out the answers from your local school brochure(s) or website(s).

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.93.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have understood the purpose, audience, and main points of a school information brochure.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
