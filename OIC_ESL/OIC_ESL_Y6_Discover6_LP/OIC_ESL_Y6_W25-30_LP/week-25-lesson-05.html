Lesson focus: What makes things scary?

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to write a statement using the past unreal conditional on their whiteboard. Tell half of the class to erase their condition (‘If’…). The other half erases their consequence.

Oxford introductory task: Instruct children to walk around the room to see if they can find a partner whose half-sentence would work with theirs. (Silly sentences are fine, as long as they are logical!)

Oxford introductory task: To support less confident classes, prepare sentences in advance and cut them in two. Children find a partner whose half matches their half.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Oxford Discover should do the listening activity in SB 6 p.158 exs.A–B.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Bright Ideas should play the first audio item from ex.1 in AB 6 p.89 (scuba-diving). Ask children:

Oxford task — teacher models: Where and when are the scuba-diving lessons? (the local pool, Saturday, two o’clock)

Oxford task — teacher models: What does the first speaker say you can do if you scuba-dive? (explore the sea, coral reefs, and fish)

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: What is the second speaker afraid of? (putting their face under the water, swimming in the sea)

Oxford task — guided practice: For all classes, ask: What type of speech is this? Are the speakers being formal or informal? Are the speakers giving facts or opinions? How do you know? Consider vocabulary, tone of voice, and the intended audience.

Oxford task — guided practice: Discuss the fear(s) that the speakers had in the listening activity. With children’s input, make a list or mind map on the board of what makes each of them scary. Bright Ideas teachers may wish to add two more items to analyse, such as ‘heights’ and ‘the dark’.

Oxford task — guided practice: Give children an example solution for one of the items on the board: e.g. If you are afraid of the dark, you could turn on a small light at bedtime.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Share more useful vocabulary for suggesting solutions, using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.158 ex.C.

Oxford task — independent application: You should try … / Have you tried …? / You could … / Another solution might be …

Oxford task — independent application: In pairs or groups of three, encourage children to think of solutions for each of the fears on the board. Prompt them to use the suggested phrases.

Oxford task — independent application: Invite each pair or group to tell the class their solutions. Have a vote on the best piece of advice for dealing with each fear.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-abililty pairs or groups for the discussion task.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to think of alternative suggestions for each item: e.g. If you find that [turning on a light] doesn’t work for you, you could [have a drink of warm milk] to help you sleep.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to role-play discussing their fears and suggesting solutions in front of the class after the discussion task.

Oxford additional tasks: Try a guided meditation or breathing exercises that children can use when they are feeling afraid.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened for the level of formality and facts/opinions in a listening exercise about a scary activity. They have identified what makes something scary and worked together to come up with sensible solutions.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
