PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Divide the class into two teams. Ask each team to stand in a circle. Choose one of the following options.

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to put their circle in alphabetical or birthday order, racing the other team.

Oxford introductory task: Tell children to hold hands. Ask them to tangle themselves up as much as possible, without breaking hands. The two teams then race to untangle their circle first.

Oxford introductory task: Explain that communicating and working together will be important for today’s lesson.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Form teams of three to four children. Give them a team challenge to complete. Use the spaghetti and marshmallow challenge below, or your own idea. The challenge should test children’s teamwork, communication, critical thinking, and perseverance. Provide an oral list of instructions and then allow each team to work independently.

Oxford task — teacher models: Instructions for the spaghetti and marshmallow challenge:

Oxford task — teacher models: Give each team 20 pieces of spaghetti, one marshmallow, one metre of sticky tape, and one metre of string.

Oxford task — teacher models: Explain that children should use their spaghetti, sticky tape, and string to create as tall a structure as they can. The marshmallow must balance on top. Give them 20 minutes.

Oxford task — teacher models: Encourage teams to predict the height of their structure. Write predictions on the board.

Oxford task — teacher models: Encourage children to think about how all team members can be involved.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Remind them to find constructive ways to resolve conflict.

Oxford task — guided practice: Tell children when there are three minutes and one minute left.

Oxford task — guided practice: Observe teams as they work. Make notes. Observe factors such as the team dynamics, communication skills, leadership skills, learning from experience, and time management.

Oxford task — guided practice: During the project, remind children that they should be open to different ways of completing the challenge. Rigid thinking will lead to frustration and poor team working.

Oxford task — guided practice: At the end of the 20 minutes, stop the challenge. Measure each team’s structure. Take photos. Compare each group’s results with their predictions.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to make a few notes about things they think went well and not so well. Keep these notes for the next lesson.

Oxford task — guided practice: Now give children an individual challenge to complete in five minutes. Use the sweets balancing challenge or the balloon challenge below, or your own idea.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Instructions for the sweets balancing challenge:

Oxford task — independent application: Instructions for the balloon challenge:

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For the team challenge, create mixed-ability groups with supportive peers.

Oxford additional tasks: Set children another short team or individual challenge. Search online for ‘team challenges for kids’ or ‘kids world records to try’.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have completed a timed challenge, individually and as part of a team.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
