Lesson focus: Overcoming fear to help others

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete text-based answer with a quoted or paraphrased detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce an evidence-based reading response.

Evidence to collect: Collect an evidence-based reading response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Tell children to look at the photo of the firefighter in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.155 or the emergency services vehicles in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.76. Ask them to predict what this lesson will be about. Then give them the lesson title.

Oxford introductory task: Find out if any children have family or friends who work in the emergency services as firefighters, police officers, paramedics, or another role (e.g. coast guard).

Oxford introductory task: Check that children know the phone number for the emergency services in your country. Show them a list of countries with their emergency numbers. Can they find countries with the same number as their own?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Play a video to introduce the topic. Use the Wrap Up video in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.159 (from 1:30 to 2:18 – firefighters) or the video in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.77 ex.2.

Oxford task — teacher models: You may also wish to play short video clips of firefighters, police officers, and paramedics in action. Search online for ‘emergency services responding for kids video’ or similar.

Oxford task — teacher models: Encourage children to make notes on their whiteboard as they watch. They should think about their overall impression of the job(s) in the video(s) and how dangerous they are.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Divide children into groups of three or four to discuss the following questions. Remind them of their learning about controlling fear in Week 25. Children using Oxford Discover can refer back to SB 6 p.155 to help them answer the first question.

Oxford task — guided practice: How do you think people in dangerous jobs overcome their fear?

Oxford task — guided practice: What motivates them to overcome it? (helping people)

Oxford task — guided practice: Do you think you would be afraid to work as a [firefighter/police officer/paramedic]?

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: What things about the job would be scary? What would be rewarding?

Oxford task — independent application: Would you like to work for an emergency service? Which one?

Oxford task — independent application: Share answers as a class. Elicit varying opinions to encourage debate. Remind children to respect their peers’ opinions even when they disagree.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children interested in the emergency services as a career to find out more about it!

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, pause the video and repeat key phrases as they watch. Focus their attention on important vocabulary (equipment, clothing, vehicles, etc.).

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to read a news article about emergency services responding to a local situation. Encourage them to think about whether the responders were afraid, and how they might have overcome their fear to help others.

Oxford additional tasks: Look at a map of your local area to find out where the nearest fire stations, police stations, and hospitals are in relation to your school.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have watched a video about the emergency services. They have considered the emergency services as a potential career.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
