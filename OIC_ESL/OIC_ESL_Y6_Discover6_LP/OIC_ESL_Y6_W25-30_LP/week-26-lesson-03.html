Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children if they can remember the grammar they learned in Week 25 (the past unreal conditional). Reinforce that they have to practise new grammar over and over in order to master it.

Oxford introductory task: Write a complete sentence on the board using the past unreal conditional: e.g. If I hadn’t practised my presentation, I would have been really nervous.

Oxford introductory task: Write one sentence starter and one sentence ending on the board: e.g. If I hadn’t done my homework … and … I would have been afraid. Challenge children to complete both sentences using their own ideas. They then check and compare their sentences in pairs.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Introduce the expressions ‘If only …’ and ‘I wish …’ for imagining how you would like things to be, or to have been, that is different to reality (see Oxford Discover SB 6 p.165). Explain how to use these with different tenses. For example:

Oxford task — teacher models: If only you had been with me, I wouldn’t have been afraid!

Oxford task — teacher models: If only Andreas could swim underwater, he would be able to go scuba-diving.

Oxford task — teacher models: I wish I hadn’t jumped when I saw that spider.

Oxford task — teacher models: I wish a friend could come with me to the dentist.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Tell children they are in the stretch zone and it is OK if they are finding the work challenging. They should think carefully about tenses.

Oxford task — guided practice: Do a speaking activity to practise ‘If only …’ and ‘I wish …’.

Oxford task — guided practice: Display six to eight pictures illustrating common fears around the classroom. Suggestions: a snake, a shark, a high diving board, a skydiver, a dark forest, a volcano, a rollercoaster, a school hall set up for exams.

Oxford task — guided practice: Tell children to walk around the room and to choose a picture that inspires them to create an ‘If only …’ and/or ‘I wish …’ statement.

Oxford task — guided practice: Give them two to three minutes to think of their sentence.

Oxford task — guided practice: They then tell another child their sentence. Ask children to listen carefully to each other and to suggest any corrections to tenses if needed.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Invite a few volunteers to share their sentences with the class.

Oxford task — independent application: Children then move to another picture and repeat the exercise. Continue until they have visited at least four different pictures.

Oxford task — independent application: Discuss as a class:

Oxford task — independent application: How does saying ‘If only …’ make you feel?

Oxford task — independent application: When is it helpful to look back at the past? (e.g. when we can use what we learned to change our behaviour in future)

Oxford task — independent application: When is it unhelpful? (e.g. when we can’t change anything; it only makes us feel sad)

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them a list of ‘If only ...’ and ‘I wish …’ statements to match to the pictures, rather than creating their own.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to create an ‘If only ...’ and an ‘I wish …’ statement for each picture.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the grammar practice in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.154–155.

Oxford additional tasks: Write three ‘If only …’ and three ‘I wish …’ statements that relate to daily life.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used the expressions ‘If only ...’ and ‘I wish …’ in short exchanges.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Fears
Week 26, Lesson 3: If only …
Learning outcomes: 6.2b, 6.2f

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Fears’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by using the expressions ‘If only …’ and ‘I wish …’ in relation to the unit topic. They will have the opportunity to choose visual prompts that inspire them.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
