The Fox and the Lion

An Aesop’s fable about fear

One day, long ago, Fox was walking through the forest when he saw something he had never seen before. It was huge, with scary eyes and terrifying teeth.

Fox didn’t know what a lion was. He ran away as fast and as far as he could. He sat in a dark cave at the other side of the forest, shivering with fear. Fox didn’t come out of the cave until the moon and stars appeared in the sky.

The next day, Fox walked through the forest again. In the same place as before, he saw Lion. This time, Lion didn’t look so big. His eyes were less scary, and his teeth were less terrifying. Fox still ran away, but he didn’t run very far. He hid behind a big, old tree which stood halfway through the forest. Fox was brave enough to come out before dinner time.

On the third day, Fox began his journey through the forest again. This time, he knew what to expect. So, when he saw Lion again, Fox didn’t run away. He stood and looked at Lion for a while. Lion stared back.

‘Hello, sir,’ said Fox without fear. ‘I have things to do today. I’ll continue walking, if you don’t mind.’

‘I do, too!’ said Lion. ‘I will continue on my way, like you.’

And both creatures passed each other, and continued on their way.

Questions

How do Fox’s feelings about Lion change from the beginning to the end of the story?

What has Fox learned?

‘When we see or do something scary more often, it becomes less scary.’ Do you agree?

Do you think Fox is right to be less scared of Lion by the end? Or should he still be nervous of him?

What do you think will happen the next time Fox meets Lion? Why?
