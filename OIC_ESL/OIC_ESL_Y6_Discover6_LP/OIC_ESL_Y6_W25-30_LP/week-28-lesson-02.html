PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Briefly retell the story of ‘Robert the Bruce and the spider’. Display a suitable illustration. Robert the Bruce was a Scottish king who had fought the English king’s army many times and lost. He was ready to give up. Then, when he was hiding in a cave, he saw a spider trying to swing across the cave to make a web. It tried many times, each time getting a little better and further, until finally it was successful. This gave him the confidence to dare to try again. This time, his army won the battle against the English.

Oxford introductory task: See if children can relate the central idea of the story to a personal experience they have had. Ask: Is there a time you wanted to give up? How many times did you try before you succeeded?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Bright Ideas should:

Oxford task — teacher models: ask children to complete exs.1–6 in CB 6 p.88 (ex.3 is optional, as it was completed in Week 25).

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Oxford Discover should:

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: invite children to share examples of activities they are good at. Write key vocabulary on the board.

Oxford task — guided practice: ask children to tell a partner one activity on the board that they want to be better at.

Oxford task — guided practice: encourage them to think of someone who they can use as an example to help them, such as a member of the class, or a famous person. Ask: How can you improve? What do they do that you can do too? (e.g. work hard, practise, be a good team member)

Oxford task — guided practice: in pairs, tell children to ask and answer about things they are good at and want to be better at. After a few minutes, swap partners and repeat.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Instruct children to fold a piece of paper in two. On the front, tell them to write ‘My personal goals’. Inside, they should write:

Oxford task — independent application: One activity they will try to get better at, and what they will do to improve.

Oxford task — independent application: One new activity they will dare to try for the first time.

Oxford task — independent application: These can be different from the goals they discussed earlier. Children do not need to share them unless they wish to. If time allows, they can illustrate their goals.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide an illustrated vocabulary bank of school and leisure activities.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write their own story about not giving up.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to choose goals by reminding the class that all choices are personal and will not be shared. Encourage them to use the information on the board as a starting point for their choices.

Oxford additional tasks: Watch a child-friendly retelling of the story of ‘Robert the Bruce and the spider’ online.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.88.

Oxford additional tasks: Research a successful person. Identify what they are good at, and how they got (and continue to get) better. Make a list of things they do that can help others be successful.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified activities that they are good at and want to be better at. They have set themselves personal goals.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Challenges and successes’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by building on their learning in Week 28, Lesson 1 to talk about the topic in more detail.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
