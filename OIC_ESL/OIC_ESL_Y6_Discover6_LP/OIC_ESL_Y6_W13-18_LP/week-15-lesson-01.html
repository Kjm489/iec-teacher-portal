PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Masks’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford introductory task: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford introductory task: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

Oxford task — teacher models: Masks at work (class/individual)

Oxford task — teacher models: Arrange a visit to a local fire station, theatre, doctor’s surgery, factory, or other professional environment which uses masks. See if the visit can be as practical as possible (such as allowing children to try on firefighting masks and test water hoses).

Oxford task — teacher models: Before the visit, children research how masks are used in different work environments. They plan questions to ask during the visit.

Oxford task — teacher models: During the visit, encourage children to take photographs (if allowed) and make notes. Provide clipboards and cameras.

Oxford task — teacher models: Interview a member of staff. Include questions about the importance of reliability and commitment in relationships with others at work, and relate this to masks where appropriate.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Back in the classroom, spend some time allowing children to ask and answer questions in English in small groups about what they have learned. Provide dictionaries so they can translate new or difficult terms.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to write an illustrated report about how masks are used in the work environment they visited. Compare this to other work environments they have researched. Encourage them to include facts and opinions, and to use headings to organize their writing.

Oxford task — guided practice: At the end of the project, share copies of the reports with the workplace visited at the start of the week.

Oxford task — guided practice: A masked play (small group)

Oxford task — guided practice: Remind children of how a play is structured, using Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.50–51 or Bright Ideas CB 6 pp.70–71.

Oxford task — guided practice: Divide children into small groups of four or five. Ask them to plan and write a short play which uses masks as costumes.

Oxford task — guided practice: Provide materials to make simple masks, building on their work from Week 13.

Oxford task — guided practice: Encourage children to create a timeline for the project, to allow time for planning, writing, rehearsing, redrafting, and mask masking.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Perform the plays to the class (or to the wider school community) at the end of the week. Hold a class vote on the best play.

Oxford task — independent application: Masks in sports (class/individual)

Oxford task — independent application: Arrange a visit to a sports club which uses masks (such as cricket, ice hockey, skiing, motor sport, or fencing). Arrange to watch a match or race, to watch training, or if possible, for children to take part in a taster lesson.

Oxford task — independent application: Before the visit, children research how and why masks are used in the sport. They also research instructions to play the sport. This will support their knowledge of the tools needed to minimize risk in sport.

Oxford task — independent application: During the visit, children think about their experience of watching or playing, and the importance of masks.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children how they felt while watching or participating, and how this showed up in their body.

Oxford task — independent application: Back in the classroom, ask children to reflect on their visit and to write a personal narrative describing their experience (or part of it).

Oxford task — independent application: At the end of the project, children read their personal narratives to a small group or to the class.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: During the research tasks, give more guidance to children requiring extra support in a small group setting, to provide them with the tools to plan their work and make a confident start. Check on these children more frequently as they work. Create mixed-ability groups for the playwriting task.

Oxford differentiation: More able children can be asked to stretch themselves by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar into their work. At the end of the report writing or playwriting tasks, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures.

Oxford additional tasks: Show children the Wrap Up video they watched in Lesson 13, Week 1 (Oxford Discover SB 6 p.55). Encourage children to think about how far they have come since they first watched it. Ask: How much more can you understand? Do you feel more confident about the topic of masks now? Allow them to share some of their thoughts and achievements from the ‘Independent reflection’ task with their classmates.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
