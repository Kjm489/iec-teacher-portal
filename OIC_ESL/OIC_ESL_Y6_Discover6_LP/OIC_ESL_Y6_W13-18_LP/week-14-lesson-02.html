Lesson focus: Fact or opinion?

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Display around five newpaper headlines which reference masks in some way (health, culture, or sport, for example). Try to show a variety of newspaper styles, and include local, national, and international headlines. Include some headlines which state fact, and some which give opinions.

Oxford introductory task: Check understanding of each headline. Ask: Is this headline formal or informal? Is it reporting a fact or an opinion? How do you know?

Oxford introductory task: See if children can define a ‘fact’ and an ‘opinion’. Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.57 to provide a more exact definition.

Oxford task — teacher models: Who do you think wrote this text?

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Who is the intended audience?

Oxford task — guided practice: Is it formal, or informal? How do you know?

Oxford task — guided practice: Play a ‘fact or opinion?’ game. Read out three or four sentences from the reading text. Children must move to one side of the room if they think it is a fact, and to the other side if they think it is an opinion.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Teach children some of the rhetorical devices which can be used in a non-fiction text to give a fact or an opinion. Point out sentences from the text which use some or all of: personal pronouns, emotive language, rhetorical questions, or statistics. Ask: Which ones support an opinion? (the first three) Which one supports facts? (statistics)

Oxford task — independent application: In the same pairs, ask children to look at the detail of the text to identify facts and opinions. Children use two colours of highlighter pen to identify each sentence as a fact or an opinion, or write them in two different columns. Use dictionaries for support.

Oxford task — independent application: Observe children as they work. Ask questions to draw out their reasoning for their choices.

Oxford task — independent application: Take feedback on the task. Discuss any sentences children found difficult to categorize.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability pairs for the analysis tasks.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to annotate the text with different types of rhetorical language, in addition to highlighting facts and opinions.

Oxford additional tasks: Use the text in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.52 or in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.3. Ask children to identify which things in the article are fact, and which are opinion.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have analysed a non-fiction text. They have identified the register and why it is used for this text. They have distinguished between facts and opinions.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
