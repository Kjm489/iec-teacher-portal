Lesson focus: Writing a personal narrative

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Invite children to warm up by conducting a free writing exercise. Give each child a sheet of paper and a choice of coloured pens and pencils. Children have three minutes to write whatever comes into their mind. Reassure them that they do not have to share anything unless they wish to. The only rule is that they must write something, however short.

Oxford introductory task: You may choose to play relaxing music.

Oxford introductory task: At the end of the time, bring around a waste paper bin, so that anyone who wishes to can throw their paper away.

Oxford introductory task: Tell children that free writing is a useful exercise to get started, when you are not feeling motivated or inspired.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children to write their personal narrative, drawing on their notes from Week 14, Lesson 4 and Oxford Discover WB 6 p.59, as well as referring to the model text. Ask them to focus on three things:

Oxford task — teacher models: their audience and purpose;

Oxford task — teacher models: appropriate and interesting language choices;

Oxford task — teacher models: accuracy in spelling, punctuation, and grammar.

Oxford task — teacher models: Halfway through the time available for writing, ask children to pause. Tell them to stand up, stretch, and move around the classroom for a minute or two. Encourage them to rest their eyes, either by closing them, or by looking out of a window into the distance.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: When they have finished writing, divide children into pairs. See if they can check each other’s work for accuracy and meaning. Ask:

Oxford task — guided practice: Do you understand what your partner has written?

Oxford task — guided practice: Can you see any spelling, punctuation, or grammar mistakes?

Oxford task — guided practice: Can you see any repeated vocabulary, and suggest more interesting words?

Oxford task — guided practice: Remind children to give and receive feedback with respect, kindness, and tact.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to imagine how they would feel, if they were in their partner’s shoes. Encourage them to empathize with their partner about the events that happened to them. Give them model phrases to use, such as:

Oxford task — independent application: You must have felt [scared] when …

Oxford task — independent application: Was it very [exciting] when …?

Oxford task — independent application: I would have felt [happy], too!

Oxford task — independent application: Children make corrections based on their partner’s feedback, and then write a final draft.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident and able children, create a model first paragraph together on the board, before they begin to write. Make vocabulary banks and sentence starters available. Observe and support these children as they write.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident and able children to include grammar they have studied during the ‘Masks’ unit: the past perfect continuous and defining relative clauses.

Oxford additional tasks: Children read their narrative to the class and/or put it on display.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written a personal narrative. They have checked it is fit for purpose and audience, it uses appropriate and interesting language, and it is accurately written.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Masks
Week 14, Lesson 5: Writing a personal narrative
Learning outcomes: 6.4d, 6.4e, 6.4f

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Masks’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using their preparation notes from Week 14, Lesson 4 to write a personal narrative, using appropriate and interesting language choices to fit their purpose and audience.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
