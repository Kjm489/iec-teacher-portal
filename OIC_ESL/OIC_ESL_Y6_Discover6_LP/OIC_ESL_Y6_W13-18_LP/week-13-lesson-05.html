Lesson focus: Making a mask

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Share excitement and enthusiasm for today’s practical mask-making activity.

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to review their designs, materials lists, and step-by-step notes from Week 13, Lesson 4. Invite them to make any small changes, having had time to reflect overnight on their plans.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Children spend the majority of the lesson creating their masks. Allow them independence in this task, but provide support when requested.

Oxford task — teacher models: Encourage children to support each other during the task, to make suggestions and to willingly share resources. Remind them that they can find ways to communicate, even when they don’t know the exact word for what they need. Refer them again to Oxford Discover SB 6 p.54 for useful phrases.

Oxford task — teacher models: Encourage children to explore and test different solutions to problems they encounter during the mask-making process. Draw their peers into the decision-making process and highlight the value of shared problem-solving.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Allow time at the end of the lesson for evaluation of the activity.

Oxford task — guided practice: Praise children for working through any problems they encountered and highlight any particular achievements that you observed during the lesson.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children how they felt during the mask-making activity, perhaps when things were going well, and when they had difficulties. Ask: Did you feel this emotion in your body? How did it feel? Suggest strategies for dealing with difficult emotions, such as breathing techniques or taking a short walk.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Use the evaluation task to review the past perfect continuous. Ask questions to elicit responses which use this tense. Examples:

Oxford task — independent application: How long had you been looking for the right colour of wool before you found it?

Oxford task — independent application: What had you been doing when the decorations fell off?

Oxford task — independent application: Who had been helping you when you found the solution?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, sit them close to each other. Provide adult support to work through the steps together, and to provide language support such as naming materials or resources required.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write a short scene using their masks as costumes for their characters.

Oxford additional tasks: Classes using Oxford Discover who have made seasons-themed masks can perform the play in SB 6 pp.50–51 in small groups, using their masks.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a class display of the masks. Add labels explaining the theme or cultural significance of each mask, and the recycled and/or natural materials used to make it.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have made a mask, following instructions given in Week 13, Lesson 4. They have evaluated this activity, using the past perfect continuous to answer questions about it.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
