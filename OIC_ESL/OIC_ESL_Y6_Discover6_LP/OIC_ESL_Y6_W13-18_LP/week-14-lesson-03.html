PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a short video clip of an exciting fencing match, perhaps involving someone from your own country. Ask children to describe what they see and hear. Ask them why they think the fencers wear masks. If any children are involved in fencing, or have seen a match, ask them to share their experience.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Do the listening activity in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.62, or read aloud the listening script provided. For the latter, you could also pre-record a colleague or older child before class.

Oxford task — teacher models: Revise the use of the relative pronouns who, that, which, and where to add defining relative clauses. These provide important information that defines a noun in a sentence. Use your own subject knowledge, Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.61 and 75, and Oxford Discover WB 6 p.66 ex.A. For example:

Oxford task — teacher models: He was the fencer who/that wore a blue mask.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: This is the mask which/that she wore in the final.

Oxford task — guided practice: It’s the sports hall where fencing competitions are held.

Oxford task — guided practice: Divide children into pairs. Give each pair a set of photos of people, places, and items used for sport and cultural festivals, including masks.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Tell each pair to think and say sentences to describe each photo, using defining relative clauses. They may think of more than one sentence for each photo.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to listen attentively to their partner's sentences and provide constructive feedback. Each pair writes down their best sentences.

Oxford task — independent application: Share sentences as a class. Write common mistakes in using defining relative clauses on the board. Ask children to think about where they have gone wrong and why. Remind them that everyone makes mistakes, and that it is good to share and learn from them.

Oxford task — independent application: Play ‘defining relative clause fencing’. Ask children to line up in two teams, with the ‘leader’ in each line facing each other across a gap. Start the game by saying ‘This is the child …’. Each leader tries to be the first to score a ‘hit’ by finishing off the sentence using a defining relative clause correctly, such as ‘… who reads lots of books!’. These children then go to the back of the line, and the second children step up. Repeat with a different sentence starter. Keep a note of hits scored by each team. To make it more realistic, use cardboard tubes as ‘swords’. Ensure that children understand ‘defining relative clause fencing’ is a non-contact sport!

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them a sentence starter for each photo, with a choice of relative pronouns to circle, such as ‘This is the fencer who/which ________’.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to research their own sportspeople, places, and equipment using the internet or non-fiction books.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children by working through one or two examples first as a class.

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Masks’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening, speaking, and writing skills by doing a listening exercise about masks in sport before reviewing defining relative clauses.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
