Lesson focus: Using descriptive language effectively

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a short, animated film (three to five minutes) which has a clear and detailed setting, creating a certain mood (or changing moods throughout). Search online for ‘animated short film for children’ to find a suitable film.

Oxford introductory task: Teach the words ‘setting’ and ‘mood’ using your own subject knowledge and the ‘Learn’ box in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.99.

Oxford introductory task: Ask children how they would describe the setting of the film they saw. Ask: What mood does this create for the film? Is it an exciting/scary/calm setting?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: For Oxford Discover teachers, read ‘The Whistlers’ in SB 6 pp.100–102. Answer the comprehension questions.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: For Bright Ideas teachers, read ‘The dark tower’. Answer the comprehension questions.

Oxford task — guided practice: During the reading activity, remind children to focus on identifying descriptive language in the text. Observe them as they work. Ask questions to draw out deeper thinking from individual children about the role of each word or phrase in the text.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: During the time that children take to complete the reading and comprehension activities, encourage them to take mindful moments to take a break and reflect on their work so far.

Oxford task — independent application: At the end of the lesson, discuss children’s answers. Ask: What descriptive words and phrases would you like to use in your own writing? Encourage children to make a note of these.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children using Oxford Discover, use the short story in SB 6 p.99 instead of the longer story on pp.100–101. Work in a small group, with adult support, to answer the questions.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to break up the text into smaller sections. Reread each section several times and use peer-support or dictionaries to ensure understanding before moving on.

The dark tower

It was a dark, moonless night. Clouds covered the sky like a heavy blanket. Callum followed the rocky path using every sense he could: sight, sound, and touch. The forest was alive with whispers, which were carried from tree to tree. He was sure the trees were watching him.

Just then, Callum saw a gap in the forest. There was a building! He stared at the dark tower that appeared like a giant creature out of the night. In a narrow window, at the top of the tower, he saw a tiny light. The light looked hopeful. Perhaps there was somebody there?

First, Callum had to open the large, heavy door. He had to be strong. He nearly gave up. Finally, the door opened. Slowly, Callum climbed the icy stone stairs. When he reached the top, he saw something shiny on the floor. He picked up the object and turned it over in his hand. A metal key!

There was another door in front of him. Callum took a deep breath. He put the key into the lock. It wouldn’t move. He tried again. This time, it turned …
