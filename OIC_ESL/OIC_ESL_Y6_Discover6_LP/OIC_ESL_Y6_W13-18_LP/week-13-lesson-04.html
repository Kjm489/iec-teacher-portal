PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Give children a short list of instructions to follow. Tell them they must wait until the end of the list before completing all the steps in order. Some children may not be listening carefully. Don’t give them any advice at this point. Suggestion for instructions:

Oxford introductory task: One. Clap your hands five times.

Oxford introductory task: Two. Stamp your feet five times.

Oxford introductory task: Three. Do ten star jumps.

Oxford introductory task: Four. Put your hands on your head.

Oxford introductory task: Take feedback. Ask: Was it easy to remember the instructions? Why / Why not?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children that today they will plan a mask to make in the next lesson. Teachers using Oxford Discover may wish to link to the theme of seasons, using the masks in SB 6 pp.50–51 as a prompt. Teachers using Bright Ideas may wish to share the recycled art images in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.59 (text to be used in Week 14). Alternatively, show mask designs from local or global cultural festivals, theatre, or folklore.

Oxford task — teacher models: Explain to children that you will give them three instructions, which they should follow in sequence during this lesson. Say that there is an expected outcome for each instruction, and so children will need to listen carefully. Read the following instructions.

Oxford task — teacher models: One. Design a mask. Ask children to consider different designs for their mask. By the end of the lesson, they should have a completed sketch.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Two. Choose a method and materials. Encourage children to consider different strategies for making their mask, and to think about how using different materials will contribute to the overall effect. By the end of the lesson, they should have a list of materials. Ask: How will you make sure you can see / your mask is secure / your materials match your theme? Is there a different method or material which might be more effective?

Oxford task — guided practice: Three. Plan the task. Ask children to break down the project into steps to achieve their goal. By the end of the lesson they should have made brief notes on each step.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to repeat these three steps back to you and check understanding of the main points (in bold, above). Children then work independently throughout the rest of the lesson to complete the instructions. Observe and support them.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: During the lesson, allow children time to move around the room or the school grounds to collect and test different materials they want to use for their mask. Encourage them to discuss their designs and share ideas for different strategies as they do so.

Oxford task — independent application: Prompt children to refer to Oxford Discover SB 6 p.54 for useful phrases to use during their discussions, for paraphrasing and for filling in while thinking of a word.

Oxford task — independent application: At the end of the lesson, check that children have a completed design, materials list, and step-by-step process for making their mask.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a written or picture list of the three instructions. Provide a template for the mask and ideas on how to secure it. Provide extra support for choosing materials and making notes.

Oxford additional tasks: Provide additional time to evaluate how the lesson has gone today. Encourage children to share their plans for their masks, and to talk about how they found the planning process.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have followed a short list of detailed, oral instructions to plan making a mask.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Masks
Week 13, Lesson 4: Instructions for planning a mask 
Learning outcomes: 6.1e, 6.1h, 6.2d, 6.2e

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Masks’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by following a sequence of instructions to plan a mask. They will have the opportunity to be creative and to choose from a selection of man-made and natural materials, practising paraphrasing and using fillers as they do so.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
