Lesson focus: Uncovering masks

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: Uncovering masks

Oxford outcomes: 6.3b, 6.3d, 6.3g

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Spend a few minutes sharing a variety of bright and appealing non-fiction books with your class, including books about masks if possible.

Oxford introductory task: Give children the opportunity to discuss whether they like reading non-fiction or not, and what topics interest them.

Oxford introductory task: Draw children’s attention to the use of headings in the books to divide up the text into smaller sections. Compare the layout or ‘look’ of different texts on the page. Invite children to say which they prefer, and why.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Use ‘Uncovering Masks’ in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.58–59, ‘From Rubbish to Art!’ in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.59, or an alternative short non-fiction text about masks.

Oxford task — teacher models: Divide children into pairs. Give each pair a set of cut-up sections of the text. Ask them to use the title and the headings to put the text back together. Check answers as a class.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Give children time to read the full text in the book, focusing on the main points. Ask them to make notes on their whiteboard about the central idea of each section.

Oxford task — guided practice: Check understanding of the content as a class. Ask children to evaluate the headings used by the author. Ask: Did the headings help you to work out the main ideas in the text? How?

Oxford task — guided practice: Give children a choice of topics suitable for a non-fiction text. Use Oxford Discover SB 6 p.63 or ask children to suggest topics, perhaps using the books shared in the introductory activity for inspiration. Write them on the board.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to imagine they are writing about one of these topics. They should write three headings they would use for that topic on their whiteboard.

Oxford task — independent application: Divide children into small groups. Ask them to take turns reading out their headings (not the topic). The others in the group must guess which topic they have chosen.

Oxford task — independent application: Share the results of this exercise as a class. Ask each group to share examples of headings that were successfully guessed. Then encourage volunteers to share their own unsuccessful examples. Write them on the board. Invite the class to support them to improve their work. Praise any volunteers for taking this risk and point out to the class that they have benefited from doing so.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, read the text in a small group with adult support, checking understanding of the main gist of each section.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, tell them to consider the information in the text as a whole. Ask them to suggest an alternative way of organizing and presenting this information, using completely new headings.

Oxford additional tasks: Repeat the heading writing exercise and guessing game, asking children to choose a new topic for this round. Children learn from the first round to improve their work.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.57.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a non-fiction text about masks or recycled art, using headings to identify the main points. They have created headings for a new topic, and used them in a guessing game.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
