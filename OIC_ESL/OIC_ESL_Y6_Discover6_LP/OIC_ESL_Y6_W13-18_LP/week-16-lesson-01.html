Oxford introductory task: Tell children that the new unit is about how we use language. Show them the photo in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.88–89 or Bright Ideas CB 6 p.73 (the children talking). Ask: How are these people using language? What languages can you speak?

Oxford introductory task: Write three headings on the board: ‘Why we use language’, ‘Ways to use language’, ‘Different languages’. Add notes from the whole-class discussion.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Play the Big Question video in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.89, or ask children to look at all the photos in Bright Ideas CB 6 pp.72–75. You may wish to share additional photos of people communicating in different situations (such as teaching, giving directions, giving a speech, or giving safety instructions).

Oxford task — teacher models: How many different languages can you list?

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Why is language important?

Oxford task — guided practice: In how many different ways can we use language?

Oxford task — guided practice: Take feedback and add to the notes on the board.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: From the list on the board, see if children can identify the three languages spoken by the most people globally as their ‘first language’. Have a class vote on first to third place. (first – Mandarin Chinese, second – Spanish, third – English)

Oxford task — independent application: As part of the discussion, teach or revise the terms ‘communicate’, ‘facial expression’, ‘gesture’, and ‘speech’.

Oxford task — independent application: In their pairs, ask children to discuss times when they have used speech, facial expressions, and/or gestures to communicate. Children add to their whiteboard notes.

Oxford task — independent application: Take feedback. Allow children to compare their experiences. See if they can tell you about a time when they felt proud of how they used language (such as having a conversation in their second language, making a speech to others, or working out directions from a map).

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability pairs for the discussion tasks.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to imagine that they woke up in a world where each person spoke a different language to everyone else. Ask: What would it be like? How do you think humans would adapt?

Oxford additional tasks: Think about the questions in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.82.

Oxford additional tasks: Find out the top ten languages in the world by first language and by total number of speakers. Compare the differences. Discuss reasons why this might be the case.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have discussed what ‘language’ means and their experiences of using languages.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Using language effectively 
Week 16, Lesson 1: How do we use language?
Learning outcomes: 6.2a, 6.2f

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Using language effectively’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by contributing to a class discussion based on an input video or photos, and their own experiences.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
