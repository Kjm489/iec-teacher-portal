PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Using language effectively’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford introductory task: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford introductory task: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

Oxford task — teacher models: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: On the ‘day without speech’, run the teambuilding challenges. Children must not speak in any way, but can communicate using gestures, facial expressions, or other noises. These may have been decided in advance in their team discussions, or they can improvise.

Oxford task — guided practice: During the teambuilding, encourage children to find ways to ensure the whole team is included.

Oxford task — guided practice: Remind children that when they face conflict, they will need to find ways to communicate and negotiate with each other, without using words.

Oxford task — guided practice: After the teambuilding day(s), hold a debrief session. Allow children time in their small groups to evaluate how they found the teambuilding tasks without being able to talk. Then share feedback as a class.

Oxford task — guided practice: Interviewing our community (class/pairs)

Oxford task — guided practice: Make a connection with a community group which has a large number of speakers of English as a Second Language, or one which supports first language English speakers in a non-English-speaking country.

Oxford task — guided practice: In pairs, children plan questions to ask members of this group about their experience of learning and using English and other languages, as well as how language and communication has changed throughout their lives.

Oxford task — guided practice: Encourage children to evaluate their questions. Check that they are simple, accurate, and will elicit the required information.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Children meet the community group and conduct interviews, each pair interviewing one member of the group.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to reflect on their visit in their pairs. Ask them to think about any similarities and differences between their interviewee’s experience and how they use language themselves.

Oxford task — independent application: Each pair then writes a report about their interview and their reflections on it.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to use connectors to show similarities and contrast, and to use nondefining relative clauses to add detail.

Oxford task — independent application: English as a melting pot (pair/individual)

Oxford task — independent application: Explain the phrase ‘English is a melting pot of other languages’.

Oxford task — independent application: In pairs or individually, ask children to find out about words used in English which originally come from other languages. Children see if any words have been exported from their own first language. Then explore others.

Oxford task — independent application: Children create an illustrated poster highlighting the different cultures and languages which have ‘lent’ words to English. Encourage them to include passive language: e.g. The word [xxx] has been taken from [language].

Oxford task — independent application: Display the posters in class. Hold a class discussion about children’s favourite words in English, which have come from other languages.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability groups and pairs for the teambuilding and interviewing tasks.

Oxford differentiation: More able children can be asked to stretch themselves by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar into their work. At the end of the writing tasks, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures.

Oxford additional tasks: Show children the Wrap Up video from Oxford Discover SB 6 p.107. Encourage children to think about how far they have come in this unit. Ask: How much did you understand? Do you feel confident discussing language in English now? Allow them to share some of their thoughts and achievements from the ‘Independent reflection’ task with their classmates.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
