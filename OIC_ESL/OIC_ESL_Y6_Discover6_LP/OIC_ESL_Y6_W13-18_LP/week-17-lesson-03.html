Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: On the board, write five languages in a column on the left, and five countries on the right. Use the following pairs (mixed up): Spanish / Argentina, French / Canada, Swahili / Kenya, German / Germany, Flemish / Belgium.

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to match the languages to the countries. Children work in pairs, telling their partner what they think, using ‘is spoken’: Spanish is spoken in Argentina. Check answers as a class.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Briefly review prior learning of the passive voice using present tense statements. Refer to the introductory activity and other examples.

Oxford task — teacher models: e.g. Bananas are grown in hot countries.

Oxford task — teacher models: Then teach the use of the present perfect tense to construct passive statements. Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.103. Ensure that children understand the process for turning an active statement into a passive statement.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Active: I’ve taken a photograph. (photograph = object of sentence)

Oxford task — guided practice: Passive: A photograph has been taken. (photograph = subject of sentence)

Oxford task — guided practice: Play an observation game. Sit children in a circle. Place around ten objects in the middle of the circle. Give children a few minutes to discuss what they can see. Then tell them to close their eyes. Move, take away, or add a number of objects to the circle. Children open their eyes and tell you what has changed, using the present perfect tense in the passive voice. Remind them to use ‘has been’ or ‘have been’. You may wish to tell them how many changes you have made. For example:

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: The pencil has been taken away.

Oxford task — independent application: A book has been added.

Oxford task — independent application: Two coins have been moved to the left.

Oxford task — independent application: Divide children into small groups of three or four. Give each group objects to play their own observation game. Children take turns to be the leader. Ensure that each child has an opportunity to lead the game.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, encourage them to be the leader in the first round of the observation game, so that they hear the passive voice modelled by their peers before having to guess themselves.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by including a number of very similar objects, such as different coin denominations or several objects in the same colour, so that they have to include descriptive language in their response.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to make guesses by reviewing vocabulary before the game. Review the names of all the objects and relevant verbs required to construct the passive (such as ‘take away’, ‘add’, or ‘move’).

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.94–95, or source alternative worksheets to practise using the passive voice in the present perfect tense.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used the passive voice in the present perfect tense to play an observation game.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Using language effectively
Week 17, Lesson 3: Using the active and the passive 
Learning outcomes: 6.2b, 6.2f

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Using language effectively’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by using the passive voice in the present perfect tense to vary sentence construction. They will have the opportunity to lead a small-group game as a practice activity. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
