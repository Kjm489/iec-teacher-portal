Lesson focus: Can a mask be important?

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: Can a mask be important?

Oxford outcomes: 6.3b, 6.3d, 6.3e

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Show video clips of people from one or two professions using masks. These might include a surgeon wearing a mask for surgery, firefighters wearing masks to fight a fire, or air crew demonstrating how to use the oxygen mask in a cabin safety briefing.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What does this person do? Why are they wearing a mask? Is this mask important?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Divide children into pairs. Ask them to read the words and sentences in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.56 ex.A, or in the handout ‘How important is it?’. See if they can work out the meaning of the words using context, prior knowledge, teamwork, and dictionaries. Check understanding as a class.

Oxford task — teacher models: Discuss synonyms for ‘important’. Ask: Which words in the list mean ‘important’? Which other words contain a sense of importance? Discuss the differences between synonyms in terms of degree of importance and (for Oxford Discover teachers) urgency.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Hand out one photo to every child, so that each set of six is distributed equally.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to decide if their mask is important, essential, or not important. Tell them to move to one of three places in the classroom according to their decision. Invite them to explain their decision.

Oxford task — guided practice: Now tell children to find someone with the same mask. Give them a few minutes to discuss which other words they would use to describe their mask, from today’s lesson.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Take feedback as a class. Draw out reasons from children as to why they have chosen specific words to describe their mask. Ask: Is that a fact, or your opinion?

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to sort themselves into new groups. Each group should have one copy of each photo (in other words, one full set). Now ask children to rank their masks in order of importance. Compare and discuss results between different groups.

Oxford task — independent application: Summarize the learning from today’s lesson. Encourage children to think about recognizing and using synonyms in their work to vary meaning.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children during the exercise on synonyms, draw a line on the board with ‘Not important’ at one end and ‘Crucial’ at the other. Work together to see where ‘important’ and its synonyms could be placed on the line.

Oxford differentiation: To challenge more able children, encourage them to work out the meaning of new words from context alone, and to identify any specific words from that context that enabled them to do so.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used different strategies to work out the meaning of unknown words. They have assessed the differences in meaning between synonyms for ‘important’. They have categorized masks according to their importance.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
