Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: If possible, involve a member of your school community who uses sign language in planning this activity (staff, pupil, parent, or other). Alternatively, refer to videos uploaded by organizations such as the National Deaf Children’s Society (UK) or the American Society for Deaf Children (USA).

Oxford introductory task: Remind children that language isn’t always spoken.

Oxford introductory task: Teach children how to sign ‘Hello’ and one or two other simple words, using American Sign Language (ASL), British Sign Language (BSL), Australian Sign Language (Auslan), or another appropriate sign language for your community.

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to suggest other words or phrases they would like to learn to sign. Find videos to teach these to the class (or involve your school community member, if they are able to take part in the class). Show that you are learning at the same time.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: On the board, write: ‘Sign language, used across the world, allows people to communicate using their hands instead of speech.’ Explain that ‘used across the world’ is a nondefining relative clause, used to provide extra information in a sentence (usually written).

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach the use of nondefining relative clauses using your own subject knowledge, Oxford Discover SB 6 p.95, and WB 6 p.86. Refer to the difference between people and objects.

Oxford task — teacher models: The child, who was only three, was fluent in four languages.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: My friend, whose parents were born in Malaysia, was born in the UK.

Oxford task — guided practice: My mother tongue, which uses the Cyrillic alphabet, is Russian.

Oxford task — guided practice: The film, whose story was written by children, won a big award.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Play a game. Tell each child to write a short sentence on their whiteboard and leave it on their desk. Then play uplifting music. Ask children to move around the classroom, carefully holding their marker pen. Stop the music. Children must look at the sentence on the board closest to them and add a nondefining relative clause to it.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask each child to read their new sentence. See if they can identify and correct common mistakes. Remind them that everyone makes mistakes, and that it is good to share and learn from them.

Oxford task — independent application: Repeat the game as many times as you wish.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, add nondefining relative clauses to several sentences together in the first round of the game. Then encourage children to work individually.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to tell you how a nondefining relative clause is added to a sentence about a place (‘The school, where ...’ and ‘The school, whose …’).

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.86–87, or provide alternative worksheets to practise nondefining relative clauses.

Oxford additional tasks: Allow children more time to talk to your visitor or to view more videos of sign language.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned some simple signs in a common sign language. They have used nondefining relative clauses to add extra information to sentences.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Using language effectively 
Week 16, Lesson 4: Nondefining relative clauses
Learning outcomes: 6.2b, 6.4d

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Using language effectively’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and writing skills by using nondefining relative clauses to add extra information to sentences. They will also have the opportunity to learn simple sign language. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
