Lesson focus: Poetry writing

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete intelligible production and recognition of the target feature, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce an intelligible oral production and discrimination check.

Evidence to collect: Collect an intelligible oral production and discrimination check; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a rhyming game. Give each child a slip of paper with one word. Use the pairs provided, or make your own. Children find who has the word which rhymes with theirs. Remind them to read words aloud: spelling can’t be trusted!

Oxford introductory task: Check rhyming pairs as a class. Discuss any similar spellings which sound different, or similar sounding words which are spelled differently.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Lead children through the process of reading aloud and then analysing a poem. Use Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.106–107 (exs.A–C) or ‘A stormy night’ and your own subject knowledge.

Oxford task — teacher models: Remind children that the title of this unit is ‘Using language effectively’. Ask: Why is it important to use language effectively in a poem? (to communicate emotion or description, to create rhythm and rhyme so that it can be read aloud, to connect with your reader/listener)

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask: What language do you think is effective in this poem? Why? What effect does it create?

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite children to make notes about aspects of the poem that they would like to use in their own poem. This might include how the poem is organized, rhyme schemes, onomatopoeia, or any other aspects of poetry that have been discussed in the lesson.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to share their ideas with a partner.

Oxford task — independent application: Tell children that they will be writing their own poem. Invite them to think about an interesting subject to write about, and to reflect on it before the next lesson.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Differentiate the rhyming pairs in the introductory activity, to give less able children easier rhymes and more able children more difficult ones.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to experiment with sound to create their own onomatopoeic words.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to highlight the poem in different colours, to help them to identify onomatopoeia, rhyme, and incomplete or complete sentences.
