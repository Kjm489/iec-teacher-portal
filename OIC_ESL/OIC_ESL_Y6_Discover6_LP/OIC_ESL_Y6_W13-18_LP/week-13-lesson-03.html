Lesson focus: Relating events in the past

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Write on the board the sentence: ‘I make a mask.’

Oxford introductory task: Challenge children to edit this sentence as many times as they can, using different tenses and lengthening it if they wish. Give them a time limit and play suitable music (such as a countdown from a game show). Examples:

Oxford introductory task: I made a mask. I will make a mask. Today, I have made a mask.

Oxford introductory task: Share answers as a class. List all the tenses children know on the board.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Remind children of their learning of the past perfect tense from Week 5 (Inside the Earth). See if anyone wrote an example with this tense in the introductory activity, and praise them if so. Review how this tense is used.

Oxford task — teacher models: e.g. By the time we reached the port, the ship had already left.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach the past perfect continuous tense using your own subject knowledge, Oxford Discover SB 6 p.53, and WB 6 p.46. Compare and contrast its use to that of the past perfect. Teach children how to construct statements, negative statements, and questions.

Oxford task — teacher models: I’d been working for ten years before I became a firefighter.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: I hadn’t been practising spelling until I realized how important it was.

Oxford task — guided practice: How long had you been waiting before the concert began?

Oxford task — guided practice: For Oxford Discover teachers, use the exercises in SB 6 p.53 and WB 6 p.46 for practice, focusing in particular on SB 6 p.53 ex.F for conversation skills.

Oxford task — guided practice: For Bright Ideas teachers, prepare worksheets for initial practice. Then divide children into pairs. Ask them to create a list of questions to ask each other, using question words and the past perfect continuous. Then ask and answer. Model questions:

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Where had you been living before you moved to this city?

Oxford task — independent application: How long had you been playing football before you won a match?

Oxford task — independent application: What had you been doing when the telephone rang?

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to support their partner, working together to ensure accurate use of tenses and to construct appropriate answers to each question.

Oxford task — independent application: Invite a few volunteers to perform their exchange to the class.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children during the speaking practice, provide questions and answers on individual slips of paper. Ask children to match each question to its answer. Then read aloud to a partner to check.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to use as many different question words as they can. See if they can relate one or two questions to the unit topic.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.47, or provide other resources to practise the past perfect continuous.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed the past perfect and learned the past perfect continuous. They have practised the past perfect continuous in conversation.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
