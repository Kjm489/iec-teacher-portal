1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Stories’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford introductory task: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford task — teacher models: Reading for pleasure (class/individual)

Oxford task — teacher models: To underpin the importance of reading and stories, allocate the whole week to class and/or individual reading.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Library project (pairs/class/individual)

Oxford task — guided practice: Put children into pairs to research the history of libraries and/or unusual libraries (e.g. unusual building designs or mobile libraries using buses, boats, or elephants). They can then share their learning with others in a small group.

Oxford task — guided practice: As a class, discuss the role of public libraries, how they are funded, and different perspectives on the question of whether libraries should be supported by public funding.

Oxford task — guided practice: Arrange a visit to a public library or to the school library. Ask the librarian to explain how libraries are managed and organized today using electronic systems, and how they were organized in the past using paper systems. Allow children to explore the library and to borrow books if possible.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to write a report with the title ‘Libraries: Past and Present’.

Oxford task — guided practice: The Big Book Show (group)

Oxford task — guided practice: Put children into groups of four or five. Instruct groups to plan and record a TV news show about books and stories for children in Year 6.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: The show should cover any items relevant to the ‘Stories’ unit. Encourage children to think about how true stories are presented in the media.

Oxford task — independent application: Groups can choose different types of presentation to include in the show, such as a news report, an author interview, and book reviews. The total running time should be around 10 minutes.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to write scripts for each section of the show. They can add phrases to ask for clarification to make interview scripts sound more natural.

Oxford task — independent application: Give groups time to rehearse, during which they can make any changes that are needed to the scripts.

Oxford task — independent application: Observe the groups as they work. If there are any problems in a group, remind children that they all rely on each other. If one person does not complete their task, the show will not happen.

Oxford task — independent application: Record children’s shows in turn.

Oxford task — independent application: Hold a ‘TV watching lesson’ to finish the week. Play all the television shows to the class.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, help them to select reading materials which match their level of confidence and ability. Create mixed-ability groups for the TV project and encourage children to help each other when required.

Oxford differentiation: More able children can be asked to challenge themselves by selecting higher-level reading materials when reading for pleasure. During the library project, ask them to prepare and hold a debate about whether libraries should or should not be publicly funded. During the TV project, ask them to think about how they would present the show for a different audience, such as adults or very young children.

Oxford additional tasks: Hold a story quiz. Create multiple-choice questions in advance, based on any stories children have read or listened to during this unit or at other times in Year 6.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
