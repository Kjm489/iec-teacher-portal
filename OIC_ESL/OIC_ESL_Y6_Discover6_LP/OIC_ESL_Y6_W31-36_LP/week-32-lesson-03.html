Lesson focus: Writing dialogue

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to write a simple sentence beginning ‘I like …’ or ‘I often …’.

Oxford introductory task: Stand in a circle, or invite some children to stand in a line at the front of the class. Each child must say their sentence in a different way to the previous speaker (e.g. shouting, whispering, slowly, in a high voice).

Oxford introductory task: Explain to the class that in today’s lesson they are going to practise using words that describe how people speak in a story.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Identify reporting verbs in a story.

Oxford task — teacher models: Put children into pairs. Tell them to look at ‘Jorge, Filipe, and the Lonomia Caterpillar’ in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.164. If you use Bright Ideas, display the eBook Hide and Cheat or source another story which contains a variety of reporting verbs.

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask them to read the story, paying most attention to the dialogue. They do not need to understand everything they read.

Oxford task — teacher models: Encourage them to notice how dialogue is presented, and to make a list on their whiteboard of any words which are used in place of ‘said’. They can use a dictionary to check meaning.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Make a list together on the board of useful reporting verbs. Review definitions.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask: Why is it useful to use different reporting verbs? (to show how the speaker is speaking and feeling) How often do you think we should use them in place of ‘said’? (choose a few reporting verbs carefully, where they have the most effect)

Oxford task — guided practice: Practise using reporting verbs using Oxford Discover SB 6 p.179. Alternatively, choose five different reporting verbs from the board. Challenge children to write five sentences on their whiteboard using these verbs.

Oxford task — guided practice: Remind children to take a few breaths before they write each sentence, to focus their attention and to allow them to think carefully about the meaning of each reporting verb.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Teach the use of the suffix ‘–less’ to make nouns into adjectives. Use your own subject knowledge or Oxford Discover SB 6 p.179 ex.D. Explain that similar-looking words can have very different meanings depending on their prefix or suffix. You may wish to compare the meanings of ‘–less’and ‘–ful’.

Oxford task — independent application: hope hopeless (opposite of hopeful)

Oxford task — independent application: thought thoughtless (opposite of thoughtful)

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to practise writing ‘–less’ adjectives in sentences.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, focus on one section of the story. Work with them to identify and define the reporting verbs.

Oxford differentiation: Support less confident children to find the reporting verbs by talking about where they can be found (before or after the dialogue in inverted commas).

Oxford differentiation: Encourage fast finishers to think of more nouns which might take the suffix ‘–less’, and then to use a dictionary to check their ideas.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.168–169, or provide additional grammar exercises on the board for children to work through in pairs.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified reporting verbs in a story, and practised using reporting verbs themselves. They have learned new words with the suffix ‘–less’.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
