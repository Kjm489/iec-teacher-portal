PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to sit quietly and to pay attention to their breath. Then tell them the lesson title. Ask them to reflect on how confident they feel in choosing the correct tense in English. Ask: What questions do you have about tenses? What tenses do you find difficult? Do you feel confident using two tenses in one sentence?

Oxford introductory task: Encourage children to share any difficulties they have. Remind them that others in the class might share the same problem.

Oxford introductory task: Use this information to feed into your explanation of the grammar in the main activity.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Play the matching game provided. Check answers. Identify and correct any misunderstandings.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Write the names of the six target tenses on the board. Put children into groups of three or four. For each tense, ask them to discuss what they would use that tense for. Tell them to write an example sentence on their whiteboard.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Review the target tenses using your own subject knowledge and the grammar boxes in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.177 or Bright Ideas AB 6 pp.112–113. Check that each group has understood the role of each tense and written a correct sentence.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.166–167, or Bright Ideas AB 6 pp.112–113.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For a less able class, review the target tenses at the beginning of the lesson and complete the exercises together as a class. Then play the matching game. Finish with the group discussion to check children’s understanding.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to explain their reasoning in the matching game.
