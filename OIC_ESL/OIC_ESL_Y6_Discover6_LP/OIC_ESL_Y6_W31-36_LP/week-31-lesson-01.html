Additional information: Week 31, Lesson 1

Once upon a time …

‘Once upon a time …’ is a traditional way to begin a story in English. When we hear these words, we know we are ready for a story. That’s exciting! But why are stories so important?

Firstly, stories and storytelling are an important part of different cultures around the world. Stories help us to make connections with the people around us. They are part of our shared history. Telling traditional stories helps us to understand where we come from and what is important to us.

Of course, stories are also fun. Reading stories is a good way to spend your free time after school. You can help your brain to ‘switch off’, or relax, while you enjoy a good story.

Stories also help us to learn. We can explore new places, different times in history, and different people. Often, stories also have a special meaning, called a ‘moral’, which provides good advice for life. Every time we read a story, we help our brain to grow!

YEAR 6 Stories
Week 31, Lesson 1: Why are stories important?
Learning outcomes: 6.1a, 6.1b, 6.1g, 6.2f

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Stories’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by thinking about how they work out unknown words, and by identifying main and subsidiary points. They will develop their speaking skills in a small-group discussion.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
