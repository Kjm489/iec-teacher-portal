PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Write ‘science fiction’ on the board. See if children know or can guess what this means.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: Do you like stories set in the future? Why / Why not? What are some common themes from stories set in the future? (e.g. space travel, time travel, robots, life on other planets)

Oxford introductory task: Give children time to discuss these questions with a partner. Then discuss as a class.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Review future forms using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.185, or Bright Ideas AB 6 pp.110, 115. Check children understand which form to use for plans, predictions, facts, beliefs, and arrangements.

Oxford task — teacher models: It’s going to rain. (prediction) I’m going to be an author. (plan)

Oxford task — teacher models: I will be twelve next year. (fact) I think we’ll miss the train. (belief)

Oxford task — teacher models: Gabi is coming to my house tomorrow. (arrangement)

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children to complete the practice activities in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.174–175 or Bright Ideas AB 6 pp.110, 115. Check answers.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Play a speaking game.

Oxford task — guided practice: Divide children into groups of four to six.

Oxford task — guided practice: Give each group a set of cards. Ask them to lay out the cards face down.

Oxford task — guided practice: Explain the rules. In turn, children must turn over a card and make a sentence using a future form which matches the word on the card.

Oxford task — guided practice: Encourage children to take a few breaths before they respond.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: After each sentence, the rest of the group should discuss whether it is correct. If it isn’t, they will need to think about where their teammate has gone wrong and why. If they are unsure, they can ask for teacher support.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to find ways to make their teammates feel valuable. For example, by ensuring everyone’s input is welcomed in the discussion.

Oxford task — independent application: The game continues until all the cards have been taken.

Oxford task — independent application: Observe groups as they play. Note any common mistakes or difficulties.

Oxford task — independent application: Take feedback as a class. Review any common mistakes. Check children are confident with using future forms.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able or less confident children, make a list together on the board of suitable topics for the speaking game (e.g. free-time activities, holidays, weekend plans, future careers).

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to make sentences with two clauses during the speaking game: e.g. I think we’ll miss the train because our bus is going to be late!

Oxford additional tasks: Instruct children to draw a seven-day timetable, or draw a timeline for the next 20 years. Tell them to add activities and plans to the timetable or timeline, and then talk about it with a partner using future forms.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have completed writing activities and a speaking game to consolidate their understanding of using future forms to talk about plans, predictions, and arrangements.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 The importance of stories
Week 34, Lesson 5: Reviewing future forms
Learning outcomes: 6.2b, 6.4d

Context
This is the fifth lesson of the unit ‘The importance of stories’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and writing skills by consolidating their understanding of future forms. They will have the opportunity to collaborate in small groups for the speaking task, as well as doing a writing task individually.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
