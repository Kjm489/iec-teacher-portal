PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Write the word ‘retell’ on the board. See if children can work out what it means, using the prefix ‘re’ and the root word ‘tell’. Add the word ‘stories’ as a clue if needed.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: Why do we retell stories? (Ideas: for fun, to share a lesson, to show we have understood the story, to keep traditional stories alive)

Oxford introductory task: Choose one of these two options:

Oxford introductory task: Play a video which introduces how to retell a story. Search online for ‘how to retell a story for kids video’.

Oxford introductory task: Write these words on the board: ‘character’, ‘event’, ‘setting’, ‘beginning’, ‘middle’, ‘end’. Ask children to tell a partner why they think each of these things should be included in a retelling of a story. Then discuss as a class.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children they are going to retell the story they listened to in the previous lesson.

Oxford task — teacher models: Give them a few minutes to look at the story and to make notes.

Oxford task — teacher models: Then ask them to turn over the story transcript or to close their book.

Oxford task — teacher models: Put children into pairs to take turns retelling the story.

Oxford task — teacher models: Remind children that if they don’t remember the exact vocabulary, or how it was said, they can compensate by using their own words.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Teach children common phrases to use to ask for and provide clarification. Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.178 or Bright Ideas CB 6 p.73. For example:

Oxford task — guided practice: Sorry, what do you mean by …? / What I mean is … / Got it!

Oxford task — guided practice: Did you mean …? / No, not exactly. (To put it) in other words … / That’s clear. Thank you!

Oxford task — guided practice: Explain that asking for clarification is normal. Children should not be afraid to ask if they do not understand something the first time. It is a helpful strategy to avoid making mistakes.

Oxford task — guided practice: Read a very short story to the class. Use this to practise key phrases.

Oxford task — guided practice: Use a story from your classroom resources or the Oxford Owl free eBook library.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Choose a story with one or two difficult words or phrases.

Oxford task — independent application: Read one or two sentences quickly or hesitate in places.

Oxford task — independent application: Invite children to raise their hand to ask you for clarification. Encourage them to use the phrases you have taught.

Oxford task — independent application: Allow time for speaking practice.

Oxford task — independent application: Create a list of possible discussion topics together on the board (e.g. books, sports, trips).

Oxford task — independent application: Put children into pairs. Ask them to choose a topic to talk about. During their discussion, they should practise asking for and giving clarification.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less confident children, create mixed-ability pairs. Encourage the more confident child to retell the story first as an example for their classmate to follow.

Oxford additional tasks: Retell all or part of ‘The White Giraffe’ (Oxford Discover) or ‘Getting to the Top’ (Bright Ideas) to a partner. Practise asking for and giving clarification.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have retold a familiar story in their own words and listened to another short story. They have practised asking for and giving clarification in pairs.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Stories 
Week 32, Lesson 2: Retelling a story
Learning outcomes: 6.1b, 6.2a, 6.2d, 6.2f

Context
This is the seventh lesson of the unit ‘Stories’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by retelling the story from Week 32, Lesson 1 in their own words. They will then develop their listening skills by learning how to ask the speaker to clarify what they have said.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
