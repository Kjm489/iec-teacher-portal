PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Do a True/False quiz using five facts about the authors covered in the previous lesson. Ask children to move to one side of the room if they think the fact is true, and to the other side if they think it is false.

Oxford introductory task: Review answers.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask children to think about their own favourite author. They can discuss this in pairs.

Oxford task — teacher models: Give children ten minutes to research their favourite author and make notes on their whiteboard. As time is limited, you may wish to define simple research questions in advance (e.g. age, place of birth, genre of writing, popular book(s), any prizes won).

Oxford task — teacher models: For the research task, provide internet access. Remind children to think about online safety and the accuracy of sources (see Week 32, Lesson 5). Ask: Which sources of information might be helpful and trustworthy? (e.g. authors’ websites, publishers’ websites, reading charities such as BookTrust)

Oxford task — teacher models: Now ask children to add their personal opinions to their notes. Ask: Why would you recommend this author to others?

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Explain that children are going to give a short talk introducing and recommending their favourite author.

Oxford task — guided practice: Divide children into groups of four or five.

Oxford task — guided practice: Tell them to take turns to give their talk.

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite groups to give constructive feedback to each speaker. They should identify one thing the speaker did well, and one area for improvement.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Ideas for success criteria: speaking clearly and with expression, using accurate grammar, and persuading the audience to try the author.

Oxford task — independent application: Remind children to be respectful and kind when giving feedback. The aim is to help each other to improve and not to make their peers feel bad.

Oxford task — independent application: Move between the groups during the activity to observe and support.

Oxford task — independent application: Take feedback and questions from the speaking activity. Discuss any common questions or difficulties with the task. Celebrate the things children did well.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a list of useful vocabulary, topics, and sentence starters.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able and confident children to think about how they can use the content and delivery of their talk to persuade others more effectively.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children by giving them time to practise their talk in pairs before sharing with the group.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Bright Ideas AB 6 p.59 exs.3–5.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to create a poster about their favourite author to display in the classroom or school library.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have made notes about a favourite author, then given a short talk to recommend this author. They have evaluated their own speaking performance and that of their peers.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
