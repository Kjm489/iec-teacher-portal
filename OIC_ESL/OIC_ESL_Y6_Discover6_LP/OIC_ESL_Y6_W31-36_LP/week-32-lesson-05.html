PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a game. Ask children to think of three facts about themselves. However, two should be true while one should be false. They can make notes on their whiteboard. In pairs children must present their facts to their partner, who will try to identify the false one.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: Did you trust your partner? Was it difficult to identify the false fact? Why / Why not?

Oxford introductory task: Explain that today’s lesson is about checking the stories we hear or read.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Give each child either a word or its definition on a slip of paper. Tell them to find someone who has the word or definition to make a pair. Ensure enough matching pairs are distributed. When all words and definitions have been matched, check answers.

Oxford task — teacher models: Explain that we expect stories in the media to be accurate. They should not exaggerate or be based on lies. Usually, they are true and accurate. But we need to know how to check.

Oxford task — teacher models: Discuss how to check sources of information in the media and in social media.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: You may wish to refer to a current news story. Use newspaper article headlines and social media posts to illustrate how it is reported in different ways.

Oxford task — guided practice: You may wish to refer to sources of advice for keeping children safe online. Search online for ‘Safer Internet Day’ or ‘safer internet for children resources’.

Oxford task — guided practice: Questions to consider:

Oxford task — guided practice: Where do you get information and news stories from?

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: What sources of information do you trust?

Oxford task — independent application: Why is it important to check what you read?

Oxford task — independent application: How can you check if a source is accurate or truthful?

Oxford task — independent application: Why is it helpful to use more than one source of information?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, write examples for the True/False game on the board before playing. To support them during the class discussion, write key words or phrases on the board. Ask children to tell you what they mean to check understanding.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by giving them examples of news reports and social media posts about one story. The story and reports/posts can be real or imagined. Ask children which sources they would trust and why. Ask how they would check the facts.

Oxford additional tasks: Make an internet safety poster. Include advice for children on how to make sure what they read and hear is accurate.

Oxford additional tasks: Play online games which teach internet safety. Search online for ‘internet safety games for kids’. Look for games which focus on checking sources of information.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have considered the concepts of truth, exaggeration, and untruths in games. They have developed their understanding of ways to check the accuracy of stories in the media.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Stories
Week 32, Lesson 5: Checking a true story
Learning outcomes: 6.2a, 6.2d, 6.2e, 6.2f

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘Stories’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading and speaking skills by building on their analysis of newspapers in Week 32, Lesson 4 to think more deeply about checking true stories. They will do this through games and discussion.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
