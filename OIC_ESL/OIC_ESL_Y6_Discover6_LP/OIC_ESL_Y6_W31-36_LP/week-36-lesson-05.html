PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘The importance of stories’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford introductory task: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford introductory task: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to at the end of the school year to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

Oxford task — teacher models: Author visit (class)

Oxford task — teacher models: Arrange for an author to visit the class, or to speak to the class via video link, part-way through the week.

Oxford task — teacher models: Before the talk, read part or all of a story by this author to the class.

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask children to create a list of questions to ask the author. Possible topics to cover: the author’s life, their stories, and their future plans.

Oxford task — teacher models: Immediately after the visit or talk, children should make notes about the author’s responses.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Set children the task of writing up a report of the author visit, including a range of facts and opinions.

Oxford task — guided practice: Story exhibition (pairs)

Oxford task — guided practice: Put children into pairs and encourage them to choose a book or story they both enjoy. Explain that they are going to make a creative collage display about the book, its characters, its setting, and the author.

Oxford task — guided practice: Give them time to plan their display, then talk about their plans with another pair using future forms.

Oxford task — guided practice: Remind pairs to share both the planning and creation stages of their work equally. They should ensure that their partner feels that their input is valued.

Oxford task — guided practice: Provide collage and junk modelling materials, coloured paper, paint, or other suitable arts and craft materials for children to make their display. They can use strong cardboard as the backing material.

Oxford task — guided practice: At the end of the week, display all the story boards around the classroom. Encourage children to talk about their displays with other pairs.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Invite other members of the school community to visit the exhibition.

Oxford task — independent application: Literacy fundraising project (class/small groups)

Oxford task — independent application: Explain to children that they are going to research local, national, or global literacy charities. Discuss the impact of low literacy rates on different countries or social groups. Hold a class vote to decide on a charity to support.

Oxford task — independent application: Put children into small groups to decide on an activity they can do for fundraising or sponsorship. Ideas: read to younger children in school, do a non-stop storytelling challenge or ‘readathon’, write a personalized story for a small fee, or hold a story party.

Oxford task — independent application: Children can promote their fundraising efforts using posters and school news channels, such as via email or letters home.

Oxford task — independent application: Allocate at least one lesson or day for groups to complete their challenges and activities.

Oxford task — independent application: Finally, children should collect any donations or sponsorship. Tell them to write a short description of how this money will be used (using future forms).

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability groups or pairs and encourage children to help each other when required. Support these children to choose activities for the fundraising project which will stretch their skills in an appropriate way – for instance, by practising and then reading simple stories aloud to younger classes.

Oxford differentiation: More able children could challenge themselves by incorporating a greater element of writing into the author visit or story exhibition projects. Choose an author who writes more challenging stories for the author visit. For the fundraising project, ask children to speak about the project and the charity they have supported to others in the school community (e.g. in a school assembly).

Oxford additional tasks: Hold a class discussion and then write a paragraph about the future of stories using Oxford Discover WB 6 p.177 ex.C.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have  participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
