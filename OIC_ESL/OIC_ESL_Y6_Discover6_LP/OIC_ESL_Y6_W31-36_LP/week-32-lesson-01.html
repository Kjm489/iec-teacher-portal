Lesson focus: What lessons can stories teach us?

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate note of a heard detail and an inference, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a listening note plus supported inference.

Evidence to collect: Collect a listening note plus supported inference; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: What lessons can stories teach us?

Oxford outcomes: 6.1c, 6.1e, 6.1i

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Write the following anagrams and clues on the board.

Oxford introductory task: HEMET = the big idea in a story (theme)

Oxford introductory task: NOLESS = something that we learn (lesson)

Oxford introductory task: LORAM = a piece of advice, told through a story (moral)

Oxford introductory task: ALVUE = something important to us, which affects how we behave (value)

Oxford introductory task: Give children two minutes to solve as many as they can. Share answers as a class.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Do the listening activity in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.178 exs.A–B, or Bright Ideas CB 6 p.60 ex.3. For confident Bright Ideas classes, ask children to close their eyes and listen, rather than listen and read.

Oxford task — teacher models: Play the audio. Ask: What is the purpose of this listening text? (a short story with a lesson) Who is the audience? (children) How you do you know? (e.g. characters and setting, level of vocabulary, and level of formality)

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Play the audio again. Ask questions to check children’s understanding of the main events.

Oxford task — guided practice: Play the audio once more. This time, ask children to listen for words and phrases which link together different events in the story. These words might reference specific times, such as ‘one morning’, ‘that night’, and ‘sometime later’. Children can write these on their whiteboard. Check answers against the story transcript.

Oxford task — guided practice: Build on the previous tasks by creating a timeline of the story together on the board.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Optionally, complete the questions in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.178 exs.A–B, or Bright Ideas AB 6 p.58.

Oxford task — independent application: As a class, discuss the values covered by the story. Refer to the Oxford Discover Teacher’s Guide p.208 or to the Our Values box in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.60.

Oxford task — independent application: Do a ‘hot seat’ activity. Invite a volunteer to sit in a chair in front of the class. Give them a character from the story. Ask them questions to explore their thoughts and feelings: e.g. How did you feel when [event] happened? Why do you think you did [action]? They should answer as if they are that character. Repeat with other children and characters.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, pre-teach difficult vocabulary before listening. Provide a transcript for them to highlight the linking phrases as they listen.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able and more confident children in the ‘hot seat’ by giving them characters whose thoughts and feelings are less obvious.

Oxford additional tasks: Put children into pairs to make a list of all the phrases they can think of which describe the passing of time in a story. Begin with ‘Once upon a time …’.

Oxford additional tasks: Look again at ‘A Housemaid’s Diary’ in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.120–121. Identify the linking phrases which show the passing of time (e.g. ‘After breakfast …’).

Oxford additional tasks: Do the ‘Finished?’ activity in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.58.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened to a short story and identified key phrases for linking events. They have discussed the lesson of the story, as well as characters’ thoughts and feelings.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
