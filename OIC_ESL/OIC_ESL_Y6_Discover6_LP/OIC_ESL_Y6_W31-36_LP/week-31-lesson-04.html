PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to sit quietly and to close their eyes. Invite them to let their thoughts go, to relax, and to pay attention to their breath. After one or two minutes, ask children to open their eyes and to stretch their muscles.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Finish reading the short story from Week 31, Lesson 3. Tell children to add to their notes about the main points and themes as they read.

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask children to reflect on the story. They are going to complete a written analysis.

Oxford task — teacher models: If you use Oxford Discover, complete the activities in SB 6 p.176. Prioritize ex.A.

Oxford task — teacher models: If you use Bright Ideas, complete exs.3–4. Then ask children to complete a table including the following information:

Oxford task — teacher models: The main theme

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: How the theme is implied in the character’s actions and in their words

Oxford task — guided practice: What we can learn from the theme

Oxford task — guided practice: Think about risk-taking in the story. Discuss: What risks did the main character take in the story? How would you have felt and acted? Do you think they acted in the right way? Why / Why not? If not, what should they have done instead?

Oxford task — guided practice: Reflect on reading the story for pleasure.

Oxford task — guided practice: Tell children they are going to review the story. Ask: Would you recommend this story to a friend? Why / Why not?

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Before starting, create a list of useful adjectives and phrases together on the board. Review too and enough from Week 28. Refer to Bright Ideas CB 6 p.92 if required.

Oxford task — independent application: I think this story isn’t exciting enough.

Oxford task — independent application: In my opinion, the start of the story was too slow, but the ending was great!

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to discuss their opinions in pairs. Observe them as they talk and ask follow-up questions. Challenge them to explain their answers using evidence.

Oxford task — independent application: Briefly summarize the learning from Lessons 2–4 (story analysis). Explain that practising these skills on two different texts will have begun to establish new neural connections. Repetition and practice of these new skills will help them to strengthen and become automatic habits.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability pairs for the written analysis task.

Oxford differentiation: Ask fast finishers to create more sentences using too and enough.

Oxford additional tasks: Think creatively to write about the next time Martine meets the white giraffe (Oxford Discover).

Oxford additional tasks: Complete ex.5 in Bright Ideas ‘Getting to the Top’.

Oxford additional tasks: Encourage children to write a review of another story they have read recently, using too and enough.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read and understood a short story, and completed an analysis of its main theme. They have reviewed the story in pairs, using too and enough.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Stories’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by completing the extended reading tasks begun in Week 31, Lesson 3. They will share their personal opinions in pairwork, using previously learned structures.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
