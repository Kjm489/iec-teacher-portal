Lesson focus: Writing a story

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Explain that to write a good story, it is important to be able to clearly identify what you are trying to achieve in your own mind.

Oxford introductory task: Put children into pairs to explain the main idea of their story to their partner, briefly summarizing the problem and the resolution. Their partner should listen carefully and try to identify the internal and external conflict.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Give children the rest of the lesson to write their story, using their plans to guide them.

Oxford task — teacher models: Invite any children who feel less confident about their plan after doing the introductory activity to seek individual support before they write.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Guide children on time management by suggesting timings for writing the beginning, the middle, and the end of their story. Write these on the board. Then allow children to independently manage their time.

Oxford task — guided practice: Children should concentrate more deeply when writing a longer piece of writing. Prompt them to carefully check their vocabulary, grammar, spelling, and punctuation. Encourage them to find more interesting choices and to avoid repetition. Remind them that they are in the stretch zone.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Halfway through the lesson, ask children to pause. Tell them to take a moment to stretch and relax. Then refocus their attention on the task.

Oxford task — independent application: At the end of the lesson, evaluate Lessons 4 and 5 together. Give children the opportunity to discuss how successful they feel their planning, writing, alignment with activity requirements, and time management skills have been. Elicit what they have learned for future tasks.

Oxford task — independent application: Celebrate children’s achievements and effort. Invite volunteers to share some or all of their writing with the class.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide adult support in a small group. Guide them before and after each section of writing (beginning, middle, and end).

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to purposefully use characters’ dialogue to show their internal conflict and to move the action on.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children by being available to answer questions and provide support as they work.

Oxford additional tasks: Put children into pairs to check and provide constructive feedback on each other’s work. They can then produce a final, illustrated draft. Display their stories on a display board or in a display book.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written a story with internal and external conflict.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 6 The importance of stories
Week 35, Lesson 5: Writing a story
Learning outcomes: 6.4d, 6.4e, 6.4f

Context
This is the tenth lesson of the unit ‘The importance of stories’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by writing a story containing internal and external conflict, based on their plans from Week 35, Lesson 4. They will also practise time management skills.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
