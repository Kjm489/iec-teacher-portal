PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Give five children cards with the following phrases written on them:

Oxford introductory task: There is a tiger in my bathroom. (E)

Oxford introductory task: I am too tired to brush my teeth. (I)

Oxford introductory task: My bathroom is flooded. (E)

Oxford introductory task: My sister is using the bathroom and I cannot get in. (E)

Oxford introductory task: I want to clean my teeth. They feel horrible. (I)

Oxford introductory task: Tell the class that these are all problems for a child who wants to have clean teeth. Ask children to help you put their classmates holding the cards in order, from ‘smallest problem’ to ‘biggest problem’. (Suggested order: 5, 2, 4, 3, 1)

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Review the following parts of a story: setting, characters, problem, and resolution.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach children the phrases ‘internal conflict’ and ‘external conflict’. Explain how the problem in a story can relate to the character themselves (internal), or can come from other people / their environment (external).

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: See if children can identify which of the problems from the introductory activity are driven by internal or external conflict (marked ‘I’ and ‘E’ above).

Oxford task — guided practice: Read a mystery story. Use Oxford Discover SB 6 p.188 ‘The Mystery of Ursus Arctos’ or Bright Ideas Extended Reading Text 3: ‘Sherlock Holmes and the Red-headed League’. Read the story with the class or ask them to read quietly in pairs.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: In pairs, ask children to complete a simple table. Examples are included for Bright Ideas. Children using Oxford Discover can use the story annotations in blue to help them find and summarize the right information. This activity will stretch children and extend their understanding.

Oxford task — independent application: Discuss as a class: Why do you think conflict is important in a story? Imagine a story without any conflict. What would it be like?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able and less confident children, complete the table as a class, or in a small group with adult support.

Oxford differentiation: Fast finishers can complete another table for a favourite or recently read story.

Oxford additional tasks: Put children into pairs to act out the Oxford Discover story as Sid and Nadia.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete questions 3–4 in ‘After you read’ in the Bright Ideas Extended Reading pack.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read a mystery story. They have identified internal and external conflict.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Original OIC source sequence (reference only — expand to read)
YEAR 6 The importance of stories
Week 35, Lesson 1: A mystery story
Learning outcomes: 6.3c, 6.3d, 6.3f

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘The importance of stories’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by thinking about how internal and external conflict drives stories. They will also think deeply about story structure.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
