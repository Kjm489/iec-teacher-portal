Lesson focus: Reading a story

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: Reading a story

Oxford outcomes: 6.3b, 6.3c, 6.3f

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play one or two short video clips introducing the story context. If there is commentary, either mute it or tell children they do not need to understand everything they hear.

Oxford introductory task: Oxford Discover: search online for ‘white giraffe nature video’ and ‘cobra nature video’.

Oxford introductory task: Bright Ideas: search online for ‘kids on climbing wall video’.

Oxford introductory task: Invite children to ask questions and to respond to what they have seen. Allow them to share any personal experiences of seeing these animals or doing climbing activities.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Plan this activity in conjunction with Week 31, Lesson 4, to best suit your class and the time available. You may wish to adjust the balance across the two lessons.

Oxford task — teacher models: Children are going to read a short story. As they read, they should make notes about the main points and themes. Remind them to use a range of strategies to work out unknown words and key information.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: If you use Oxford Discover, ask children to read ‘The White Giraffe’ in SB 6 pp.174–175. Use SB 6 p.172 and dictionaries for vocabulary support. Read the whole text or stop at ‘… go to a school where I don’t fit in’.

Oxford task — guided practice: If you use Bright Ideas, ask children to read ‘Getting to the Top’. Read the whole text or stop at ‘Simon saw his dream slipping away’. As this is a longer text than children are used to, use the accompanying Teacher Notes to guide their reading.

Oxford task — guided practice: Halfway through the time available, pause the activity. Encourage children to stretch and move around the room for several minutes. Then continue.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Praise children for persevering when they find the work challenging. Remind them of the satisfaction they have felt in the past when completing a task which required a lot of concentration.

Oxford task — independent application: For the last five to ten minutes of the lesson, review the story as a class. Check understanding and identify key information. Ask: What do you think the main theme of this story is? Why?

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to keep their notes on the story safe for Week 31, Lesson 4.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work through the story together, pausing after each paragraph to check their understanding and to identify key points. Allow more time for discussion.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to work entirely independently.

Oxford differentiation: Fast finishers can research white giraffes (Oxford Discover) or interesting facts about climbing as a sport (Bright Ideas).

Oxford additional tasks: In pairs, encourage children to talk about the story so far, to compare notes about the main points and themes, and to share their opinions.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read (most or all of) a short story, identifying main points and themes. They have used a range of strategies to work out unknown words and key information.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
