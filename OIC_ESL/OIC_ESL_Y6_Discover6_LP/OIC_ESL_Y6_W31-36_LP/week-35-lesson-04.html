Lesson focus: Planning a story

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: Planning a story

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Briefly review learning from Week 35, Lesson 1. Refer to Oxford Discover SB 6 p.188 or Bright Ideas Extended Reading Text 3: ‘Sherlock Holmes and the Red-headed League’. Elicit the important elements of a story (characters, setting, problem, internal conflict, external conflict, and resolution). Ask questions to check children’s recall: e.g. What was the [internal conflict] for [character] in [story from Lesson 1]?

Oxford introductory task: Explain that children are going to plan and write their own story using internal and external conflict over the next two lessons.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Before children plan their story, give them questions to consider.

Oxford task — teacher models: Who is your audience? What is the story theme? (e.g. mystery, adventure, comedy, etc.)

Oxford task — teacher models: What is the problem and resolution? Where is the internal and external conflict?

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: How can you expand on your ideas to add detail?

Oxford task — guided practice: What can you use as inspiration from the story in Lesson 1? (e.g. ideas, vocabulary)

Oxford task — guided practice: Use Oxford Discover SB 6 p.189 ex.C and WB 6 p.179, or Bright Ideas AB 6 p.60 exs.1–3, for guidance and support in breaking down the story planning into sections. If you use Bright Ideas, adapt the plan in ex.3 to include internal and external conflict.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Divide the planning time in two. Designate the first half as ‘free thinking time’. If possible, allow children to find their own individual place to plan, either inside or outside. For example, they could find a comfortable chair in the library, or walk around the playground while they think. Be as flexible as possible in how and where children use this time, while being guided by your children’s capabilities and level of maturity.

Oxford task — independent application: For the second half of the time available, tell children to return to their seats to write their plan. They should use the book references listed above for guidance.

Oxford task — independent application: Children who finish early can begin to write their story. Encourage them to carefully check over their plan first to ensure it meets the activity requirements.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work through the planning process together in a small group with adult support. You may wish to allocate a shorter amount of time to ‘free thinking time’ and spend more time on guided planning. Focus on external conflict.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to plan a longer story with at least one ‘false resolution’ part-way through the story to trick the reader – for instance, in a mystery story, the reader believes they know who the villain is, until another clue shows they are innocent.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children by asking everyone in the class to write a mystery story, as in Week 35, Lesson 1. Create a mind map of possible settings, characters, problems, and conflicts together on the board before (or replacing) the ‘free thinking time’.

Oxford additional tasks: Read the story in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.178. Label the story. Answer the questions.

Oxford additional tasks: Read the short story in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.61 and complete exs.1–3.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have planned a story with internal and external conflict.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Learning outcomes: 6.4a, 6.4b, 6.4c

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘The importance of stories’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using their learning about internal and external conflict from Week 35, Lesson 1 to help them plan their own story. They will also have the opportunity to show growing independence.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
