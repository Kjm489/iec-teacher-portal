Lesson focus: Famous authors

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate note of a heard detail and an inference, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a listening note plus supported inference.

Evidence to collect: Collect a listening note plus supported inference; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Talk about opportunities your class has to visit libraries and borrow books. Encourage children to do so. Refer to the Week 33 library project, if your class took this option.

Oxford introductory task: Display pictures of public libraries. One of these should be local, and three or four from different places around the world. Include historic and modern libraries. Search online for ‘beautiful global libraries pictures’.

Oxford introductory task: Put children into pairs to discuss which library they would choose to visit, and why.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Do a listening activity to introduce (a) famous classic author(s).

Oxford task — teacher models: If you use Oxford Discover, play the Wrap Up video in SB 6 p.179 from 0:55 onwards. Before watching, write the names of Roald Dahl and Charles Dickens on the board. Ask children to listen for facts about these two people.

Oxford task — teacher models: If you use Bright Ideas, do the listening activity in AB 6 p.59 exs.1–2.

Oxford task — teacher models: Encourage children to consider:

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: how to identify the main points about the author from the subsidiary points;

Oxford task — guided practice: the target audience;

Oxford task — guided practice: whether the text is fact, opinion, or a mixture of both.

Oxford task — guided practice: Find out if any children have read books by the classic author(s) in the listening activity. See which other classic or modern authors children know. Invite them to share the name of the author they are currently reading.

Oxford task — guided practice: Do a shorter listening activity to briefly introduce (a) famous modern author(s).

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Display a picture and book cover(s) of one or two modern authors. Read out a short list of pre-prepared facts. Include your own opinion of this author.

Oxford task — independent application: Search online for ‘current authors for KS2’. Suggestions: Katherine Rundell, Malorie Blackman, Onjali Q. Raúf, Elle McNicoll, Jason Reynolds, Matt Haig.

Oxford task — independent application: Try to find out the key influences for this author, including personal experiences which might resonate with your class.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to make notes about what they hear. Check answers. See if they can separate the facts from your opinion.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to choose a new author next time they choose a book.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a transcript for them to follow as they listen.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to think about whether books written by authors who lived a long time ago are still relevant today. Discuss in pairs or as a class.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to think about what information they might hear about an author before listening (e.g. where they lived, year of birth, why they write/wrote stories).

Oxford additional tasks: Read and discuss a short extract from a book by Roald Dahl (Oxford Discover), from a retelling of Anne of Green Gables (Bright Ideas), or from one of the modern authors selected for the second activity.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about famous authors. They have broadened their awareness of modern authors writing for their age group in English.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
