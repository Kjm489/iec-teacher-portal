6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able or less confident children, identify the story theme and one or two pieces of evidence together first. Then complete the task in mixed-ability pairs.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to discuss common themes in the books they have read. Ask: What themes do you think are popular in current fiction for your age group? Why do you think authors have chosen these themes? (e.g. current affairs, societal or environmental challenges, personal experiences, relevance to children)

Oxford additional tasks: Use Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.164–165, or a familiar story from the Bright Ideas books. Identify the story’s theme and find supporting evidence.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned about the importance of themes or morals in a story. They have analysed a familiar story to identify its theme and how it is communicated.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
