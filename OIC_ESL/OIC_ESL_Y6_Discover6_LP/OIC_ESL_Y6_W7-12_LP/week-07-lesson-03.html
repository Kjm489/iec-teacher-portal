Lesson focus: What were you doing when …?

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Write the quote from the previous lesson on the board: ‘When I was a boy and I would see scary things in the news, my mother would say to me, “Look for the helpers. You will always find people who are helping.”’ – Mister Rogers.

Oxford introductory task: Invite children to share their responses to the quote.

Oxford introductory task: Tell children that when something scary happens, these helpers are often doing something else. They are interrupted by the big event. We need to know the grammar used to talk about interruptions – small or big.

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Discuss the questions in ex.2 as a class.

Oxford task — independent application: Do a role-play activity. Divide children into groups of four. Ask children to imagine they are on the TV news. Discuss possible topics and roles for a news report (e.g. Topic: a huge snowstorm; Roles: interviewer, bus driver, school pupil, car driver going to work).

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to allocate their roles within their group. Encourage them to divide roles fairly and ensure everyone can contribute. Remind more confident children to support less confident children so they have a clear understanding of the task and feel included.

Oxford task — independent application: Observe the role-plays. Praise groups that are working well together.

Oxford task — independent application: Invite several or all groups to share their TV news report with the class.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to identify common errors and how they can be corrected or improved. Remind children that making mistakes is part of the learning process.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, instead of using the Oxford Discover text, work in a small group with adult support to make correct sentences about interrupted actions. Then create mixed ability groups for the role-play activity.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a paragraph based on the title ‘What I was doing when …’.

Oxford additional tasks: Invite an older person to visit the class, or connect with them via video link. Encourage children to ask the visitor what they were doing when local or world events happened.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to use the past continuous with the past simple to describe one action interrupting another. They have role-played a news report.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Effects of nature 
Week 7, Lesson 3: What were you doing when …? 
Learning outcomes: 6.2b, 6.2c, 6.2f

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Effects of nature’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by using the past continuous and past simple tenses to describe an interrupted action. They will have the opportunity to practise using the correct grammar in pairs and in groups.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
