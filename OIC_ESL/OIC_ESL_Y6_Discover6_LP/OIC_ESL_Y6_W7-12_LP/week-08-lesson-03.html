PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Write the words ‘Animals … are MAGNIFICENT’ on the board. Display several different photos of particularly strong or beautiful animals which are impressive. Include a gorilla.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What do you think the word ‘magnificent’ means? (very beautiful and impressive) Help children to sound out the word together.

Oxford introductory task: Invite children to tell you which of these animals they think is the most magnificent.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: Why might we describe these animals as magnificent? What other things can be magnificent? (art, music, buildings, natural formations like mountains or rivers)

Oxford introductory task: Remind children of the word ‘inspiring’. Tell them that magnificent things are often also inspiring.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach or revise the use of the relative pronouns who, that, which, and where in defining relative clauses. These provide important information about a noun in a sentence. Use your own subject knowledge, Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.61 and 75, and Oxford Discover WB 6 p.66 ex.A.

Oxford task — teacher models: That is our neighbour who lives next door.

Oxford task — teacher models: We’re going to make posters which will tell people about why bees are important.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: This is a farm where the farmer keeps bees.

Oxford task — guided practice: As a class, read the text in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.51 ‘Gorillas: an endangered species’ or source an alternative text, which includes facts about gorillas and why they are endangered. Suggested search term: ‘National Geographic Kids Mountain Gorilla’. Ensure to include the word ‘Kids’ in your search.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask: What is the main problem facing gorillas today? Does the text mention any solutions?

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Divide children into pairs. Invite them to reread the text, then ask them to practise telling each other about gorillas using defining relative clauses. They can make notes on their whiteboards: e.g. Gorillas are animals that live in groups.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to listen attentively to their partner's sentences and provide constructive feedback.

Oxford task — independent application: Share sentences as a class. Write common mistakes in using defining relative clauses on the board. Ask children to think about where they have gone wrong and why. Tell them to make any corrections they think are needed.

Oxford task — independent application: Discuss as a class: What differences can you find between gorillas and bees (or butterflies)? Are there any similarities?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide highlighter pens which they can use to highlight key vocabulary in the text.

Oxford differentiation: Ask fast finishers to help less confident classmates understand defining relative clauses, explaining in which cases who, that, which, and where are used.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a paragraph about gorillas, using defining relative clauses.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have analysed an information text about gorillas. They have used relative pronouns who, that, which, and where in defining relative clauses.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
