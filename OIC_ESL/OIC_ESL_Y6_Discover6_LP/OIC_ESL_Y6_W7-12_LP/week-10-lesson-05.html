Lesson focus: Making predictions using modals

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete intelligible production and recognition of the target feature, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce an intelligible oral production and discrimination check.

Evidence to collect: Collect an intelligible oral production and discrimination check; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: Making predictions using modals

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Place a ‘time machine’ at the front of the classroom. This can be a simple, large cardboard box with ‘TIME MACHINE’ written on the side.

Oxford introductory task: Invite a confident volunteer to sit in the machine. Ask: Would you like to travel to the future? When and where in the world would you like to travel to?

Oxford introductory task: Imagine that the machine is working. Search online for ‘time machine sound effects video’ or ‘TARDIS sound effects video’. Play the sound effects. Ask the child to describe what they can see now they are ‘in the future’. Then bring them back to the present again.

Oxford introductory task: Repeat with several more volunteers.

Oxford introductory task: In pairs, give all children the opportunity to discuss whether they would like to travel to the future or not. They can also discuss when and where they would go.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Remind children of the ways they made future predictions in Week 10, Lessons 1 and 3 using the future simple tense. Elicit a few examples.

Oxford task — teacher models: Then remind children that in Week 4, they looked at modal verbs when they went on a mission to Mars. Tell them that they can use some modal verbs to make future predictions, too.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach the use of modals to make predictions. Use your own subject knowledge and Bright Ideas CB 6 p.68 ex.2 or Oxford Discover SB 6 p.33.

Oxford task — teacher models: I’ll be able to travel in a driverless car.

Oxford task — teacher models: We’ll have to wear clothes with microchips in them.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: You might not be able to live without the internet.

Oxford task — guided practice: Transport could be faster, quieter, and cleaner.

Oxford task — guided practice: That can’t be true!

Oxford task — guided practice: Before beginning their task, invite children to do a mindfulness exercise.

Oxford task — guided practice: Tell children to close their eyes and breathe deeply.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Ask them to imagine themselves and the world in the year 2050.

Oxford task — independent application: Let them sit still for several minutes.

Oxford task — independent application: You may wish to play calm or futuristic music while they think.

Oxford task — independent application: Invite children to draw their vision of 2050 on paper.

Oxford task — independent application: Divide children into groups of four or five. Challenge them to explain their drawing to their group using modal verbs. Observe the groups and correct any common mistakes at the end of the lesson.

Oxford task — independent application: Discuss as a class: Do you think your predictions will come true? Why / Why not?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able or less confident children, allow them to make notes on a whiteboard before explaining their drawing to others.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children to use a variety of modal verbs in their description and in their questions to classmates.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a short story about travelling to the future in a time machine.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have made a drawing of the future and described it using modal verbs to make predictions.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
