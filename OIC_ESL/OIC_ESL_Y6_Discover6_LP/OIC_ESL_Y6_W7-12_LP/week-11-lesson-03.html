1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Teachers using Bright Ideas can ask children to quietly read the play in CB 6 p.70 when they arrive. Then invite confident volunteers to read the play to the class. Check understanding of the text. Explain any new vocabulary.

Oxford introductory task: Teachers using Oxford Discover may search online for short interviews with current young music stars or actors, or find a text from English language children’s magazines. If texts are not easily obtainable, write ‘Being famous is important to me’ on the board. Ask children to discuss this idea quietly in pairs before sharing in the discussion point below.

Oxford introductory task: Discuss: Would you like to be famous in the future? Why / Why not?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children that today they will be thinking about their own future plans and dreams.

Oxford task — teacher models: Remind them that they talked about this topic in Week 1, Lesson 4. They were asked: What’s your dream future? Ask them to recall the dream they shared in Week 1. By thinking again about future plans and dreams, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning and any related language.

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask children to write down three predictions for their future. These may be related to the dream they shared in Week 1, or new ideas. You can provide further guidance, such as:

Oxford task — teacher models: a prediction about your skills, your hobbies, and your job;

Oxford task — teacher models: one prediction using the future simple and two using modal verbs.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite volunteers to share their ideas with the class.

Oxford task — guided practice: Correct common mistakes. If necessary, remind children of how to use the future simple tense and modal verbs to make predictions about the future.

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite children to discuss the following questions with a partner. Then discuss as a class.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask: How do your dreams make you feel? (e.g. excited, nervous, determined)

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask: Why is this your dream? What motivates you? (e.g. creativity, family, fame, money, happiness)

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Ask: How do we turn our dreams into reality? (e.g. hard work, learning new skills, practice, planning, connections with people, perseverance, luck)

Oxford task — independent application: Challenge children to think about any steps they might need to take to achieve their dreams.

Oxford task — independent application: Tell children to use their notes and their thoughts from the discussion to write a paragraph about their future dreams. Ask them to include steps they will take to achieve them.

Oxford task — independent application: Tell children to share their writing with a partner. Ask them to check each other’s use of the future tense and modal verbs. Make any corrections needed.

Oxford task — independent application: Invite volunteers to share their paragraph with the class. Celebrate and encourage children in their ambitions for the future.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, replace the introductory activity with the reading text ‘Our future dreams’. Discuss the text in a small group.

YEAR 6 Future technology
Week 11, Lesson 3: Future dreams 
Learning outcomes: 6.4a, 6.4c, 6.4d

Context
This is the eighth lesson of the unit ‘Future technology’.
In this lesson, children will develop their writing skills by using the prediction skills learned in Week 10, as well as recalling their future dreams from Week 1. They will draw on this learning to contribute to a class discussion and to produce a piece of writing.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
