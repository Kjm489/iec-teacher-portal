PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Watch one or more short video clips of age-appropriate professional plays in theatres. Search online for ‘children’s theatre video’.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What can you see in the video? Have you ever seen a play? Do you like to act?

Oxford introductory task: Tell children that today they will create a play called ‘Our future dreams’.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask children to reread their written work from Week 11, Lesson 3.

Oxford task — teacher models: Divide children into groups of three or four. Tell them that their play will be based on their dreams. The characters will discuss their dreams for the future.

Oxford task — teacher models: As a class, share ideas and inspiration for the play. Write key ideas on the board. Take suggestions for:

Oxford task — teacher models: Locations – such as at a friend’s house, in a café;

Oxford task — teacher models: Tone – such as friendly, funny, or an argument;

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Key lines – tell children that one of them must say ‘Great things will happen to me!’;

Oxford task — guided practice: Take suggestions for two or three other phrases or objects that must be mentioned in the play. Choose some sensible and silly ones!

Oxford task — guided practice: Allow children to explore ideas together for their play.

Oxford task — guided practice: Observe children as they work.

Oxford task — guided practice: Make suggestions to help them shape their play.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Remind them to use their writing as inspiration for their character’s lines.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask them to think about how they use their bodies to communicate feelings.

Oxford task — independent application: Children practise performing their plays.

Oxford task — independent application: Remind children that they have only had a short amount of time to prepare. Encourage them to watch each play respectfully, and to be positive in their responses.

Oxford task — independent application: Prompt children to evaluate each play and to give constructive feedback. Focus on speaking skills. Ask: What went well? What could be improved?

Oxford task — independent application: Hold a class vote on the most interesting or funny play.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, ensure they are in a group with confident and supportive children who will support them.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by putting them into one group and asking them to include an element of plot in their play. Their play should not only involve talking about future dreams, but a problem must also happen to the characters that they must solve.

Oxford additional tasks: Spend more time preparing the play. Finalize scripts, and make simple scenery, costumes, and props. Perform the plays for other members of the school community.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have planned, performed, and evaluated a play called ‘Our future dreams’.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
