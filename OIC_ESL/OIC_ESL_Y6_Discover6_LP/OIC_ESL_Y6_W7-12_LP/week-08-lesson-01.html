PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: The introductory activity will follow a similar pattern throughout Week 8.

Oxford introductory task: Write the words ‘Nature can … INSPIRE’ on the board. Display several different inspiring photos from nature, such as views of a landscape, the pattern on an insect’s body, or the shape of a flower. Include local and global examples.

Oxford introductory task: Invite children to tell you what they think the word ‘inspire’ means. Share the meaning (to give someone the idea or confidence to do something).

Oxford introductory task: Now write the words ‘INSPIRING’ (adjective) and ‘INSPIRATION’ (noun) on the board and explain the difference between the words.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What could people be inspired to make when they look at these photos? (e.g. clothes, furniture, art, poetry, music, buildings, cars)

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What things in nature do you find inspiring? Why?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children that there is often a connection between nature and science or engineering projects. Today’s text will cover this topic.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach the following vocabulary in relation to the nature–science link: ‘copy’, ‘create’, ‘design’, ‘inspire’, ‘invent’, ‘reproduce’, ‘symmetry’.

Oxford task — teacher models: Give children around ten minutes to read Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.72–73 ‘Summing Up Symmetry’ or Bright Ideas CB 6 p.49 ‘Ideas from nature’. Tell them to check unknown words using Oxford Discover SB 6 p.70 as well as taught strategies: breaking words into parts, looking at context, dictionaries, and/or talking it through with a partner.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask them to quickly make a list on their whiteboards of different inventions which have been inspired by nature.

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite children to share their answers. Ask: How was [this invention] inspired by nature?

Oxford task — guided practice: If time allows, find and share photos or videos of the inventions mentioned in the text.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Ask: Which of these inventions do you use? Did you know they were inspired by nature?

Oxford task — independent application: Invite children to share any other buildings or objects they know which they think are inspired by nature. Ask them to share their reasoning.

Oxford task — independent application: Use the remainder of the lesson to complete the questions in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.70 and/or p.74, or the questions in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.49 exs.3–4.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, stop at the end of each section of the text. Discuss the information they have read so far. Then continue with the next section.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to share examples from their own life by giving them categories to consider such as clothes, transport, buildings, and food.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to research architecture which uses nature for inspiration and write an information text about it.

Oxford additional tasks: Choose a photo from the introductory activity. Create an invention, a piece of art, or a piece of music inspired by the photo.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.62–63 or Bright Ideas AB 6 p.46.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read and understood a text about the link between nature and science. They have discussed what aspects of nature inspire them in their own lives.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Effects of nature’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by extracting main information from a text. They will reflect on how the topic relates to their own lives. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
