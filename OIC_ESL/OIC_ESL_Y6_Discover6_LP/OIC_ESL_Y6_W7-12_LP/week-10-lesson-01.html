PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Before the lesson, create a slideshow to introduce the topic. Search online for ‘futuristic pictures’ and ‘royalty free futuristic music’ to find appropriate pictures and music to create a futuristic atmosphere. Play this slideshow to the class.

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to tell a partner what they think the new unit topic will be. Invite a few volunteers to share their predictions with the rest of the class and give reasons why.

Oxford introductory task: Inform children that they will be thinking about three parts of the future in this unit: the future of technology, their own dreams and plans, and future changes to the world.

Oxford introductory task: Help children to understand that choices we make now affect the future of our own lives, the people around us, and the world.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children that today’s activity will involve making ‘predictions’. Elicit from children the meaning of this word and the verb form ‘to predict’. Then write the following questions from Bright Ideas CB 6 p.63 on the board:

Oxford task — teacher models: What are three things you know about the future?

Oxford task — teacher models: How do you know these things?

Oxford task — teacher models: Would you like to be able to predict the future?

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Can you predict what you will do when you finish school?

Oxford task — guided practice: Use audio or video input to start the discussion.

Oxford task — guided practice: Teachers using Oxford Discover can play track 3.23. Ask children to listen for the parts of the conversation which are blank in SB 6 p.138. Play twice, then share answers as a class.

Oxford task — guided practice: Teachers using Bright Ideas can play the video in CB 6 p.62. Tell children to make notes about what they hear, then share the main points with a partner. Check understanding as a class. Ask: What technology can you see in the photos in the video?

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Remind children that we do not always know what will happen in the future. Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.138 ex.C to briefly teach expressions of probability such as ‘I’ll probably be’ or ‘I might be/become’, and the adverb ‘definitely’.

Oxford task — independent application: Allow children time to make notes on their whiteboard in response to the questions on the board. Remind them to think about their reasoning and to use because in their answers. They should include at least one answer about themselves, one about their local area, and one about the world.

Oxford task — independent application: Divide children into pairs to discuss their responses. If time, repeat with a new partner.

Oxford task — independent application: Invite children to share their responses with the class. Identify any similarities or differences. Tell children that in this unit they will explore these ideas more.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide them with vocabulary banks for categories linked to the future such as different jobs or life events.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by asking ‘What if?’ questions to encourage them to think about linking cause and effect. Ask: What will you do if …? or What will happen if …?

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.63.

Oxford additional tasks: Imagine your school in the future. Draw the scene and write a paragraph about it.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used input materials and their own experiences to make predictions about the future.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
