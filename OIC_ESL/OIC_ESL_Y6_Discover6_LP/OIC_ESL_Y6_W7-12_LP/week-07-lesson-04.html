PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a memory game to revise the grammar from Week 7, Lesson 3.

Oxford introductory task: Say this sentence starter: I was walking through the city when …

Oxford introductory task: Ask a child to finish the sentence and to volunteer the next player. That child must repeat the sentence and then add on another action. Encourage the class to help players remember the actions as the game progresses. See how many actions they can remember.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask children to recall their discussion in Week 5, Lesson 1 about surviving in difficult environments (Bright Ideas: rainforests or Oxford Discover: volcanoes).

Oxford task — teacher models: Provide children with a short text about survival in a rainforest environment. For Oxford Discover, use SB 6 p.99. For Bright Ideas, ask children to review the story in CB 6 pp.46–47.

Oxford task — teacher models: Use the texts to start a class discussion about the items and skills you might need to survive in a rainforest environment. Remind children of the words ‘risk’, ‘safety’, and ‘planning’. Think about dangerous animals, mosquitoes, heat, water, food, and shelter. Write ideas and key vocabulary on the board.

Oxford task — teacher models: You may wish to add other environments from Week 5, such as volcanoes or the desert.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Divide children into pairs. Choose one of the following two activities:

Oxford task — guided practice: Children run a tour company to take tourists into the rainforest.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask them to talk about advice they would give to tourists going into the rainforest. They must agree on: something to bring, something to do, and something not to do. Share advice as a class.

Oxford task — guided practice: Now ask them to imagine the tour has happened. Ask: What happened to you when you were walking through the rainforest? What did you do? Use the sentence starter: ‘We were walking through the rainforest when …’

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Children plan a response to a potential natural disaster. This can relate to an earthquake zone, volcano, tsunami/flood risk zone, drought risk zone, or hurricane zone.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask them to talk about advice they would give to people living in this environment. They must agree on three key pieces of advice: items to keep in the house, something to do in an emergency, and something not to do. Share advice as a class.

Oxford task — independent application: Now ask them to imagine they lived during a natural disaster. Ask: What were you doing when the earthquake happened (or another natural disaster)?

Oxford task — independent application: During this exercise, encourage children to value their partner’s contribution. Remind them to listen attentively, show empathy, and gently correct their partner’s mistakes.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability pairs so peer-support can be provided.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by providing them with a real-life location or scenario.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a colourful information poster including tourism advice or emergency advice.

Oxford additional tasks: Learn vocabulary for the emergency services. Talk about how these people are ‘helpers’.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have discussed how to survive in a rainforest environment. They have worked in pairs to discuss safety strategies or emergency planning for difficult environments.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Effects of nature’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by drawing on the vocabulary and grammar learned in Weeks 5 and 7 to talk about difficult natural environments.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
