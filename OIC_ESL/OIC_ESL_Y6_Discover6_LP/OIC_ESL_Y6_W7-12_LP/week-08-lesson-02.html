Oxford differentiation: One special butterfly from North America is the monarch butterfly. The word ‘monarch’ means king or queen. Its wings are some of the biggest in the butterfly world, at up to ten centimetres wide. These butterflies are usually black, orange, and white. They eat a plant called milkweed, which makes them taste terrible! This keeps them safe from birds and other predators. Insects as pollinators

Insects have an important role to play in the food chain. Many crops and wild plants need animals and insects to pollinate them. To ‘pollinate’ means that they carry pollen from one plant to another. When the pollen reaches the new plant, it starts the process of making new seeds and fruits. It’s a useful and necessary process! Nearly half of the foods we eat are made through pollination. Some examples include tomatoes, apples, beans, tea, mangoes, strawberries, and pears. Most pollination is carried out by bees.

Did you know that butterflies are not just pretty to look at, with their bright colours and symmetrical patterns? In fact, butterflies are also pollinators, just like bees! They are not as efficient as bees because the pollen only sticks to their legs and some parts of their body, not to their whole body.

One special butterfly from North America is the monarch butterfly. The word ‘monarch’ means king or queen. Its wings are some of the biggest in the butterfly world, at up to ten centimetres wide.
