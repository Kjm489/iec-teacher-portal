Independent reflection

Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Future technology’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Highlight children’s achievements and encourage them to talk about what they are proud of. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

Main activity

Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Teachers may choose one of the following activity ideas:

‘My Future’ mood board (individual)

Show children a range of mood boards. Search online for ‘mood board pictures’. Explain that a mood board is a creative way of collecting and organizing your personal thoughts, projects, and plans. It can include text, photos, and drawings.

Provide a large range of materials from which photos and text can be cut out, such as magazines, catalogues, and newspapers. Give children access to the internet and to a printer.

Tell children to gather materials for their mood board.

Now make the mood board, using a large sheet of coloured paper or strong cardboard as a background.

Ask children to write several paragraphs explaining what they have put on their mood board and why. Prompt them to use the future simple and modal verbs to talk about their future goals.

Invite children to share their mood board and explanation with the class.

Celebrate children’s future plans and encourage them in their ambitions.

Recording a technology podcast (pairs)

In pairs, plan and record a podcast about future technologies.

The show should cover any types of technology of children’s choice.

Choose two or three different types of presentation to include in the show, such as a news report, a quiz, an interview, or an opinion piece. The total running time should be around ten minutes.

Remind children to include predictions using the future simple and modal verbs.

Encourage children to break the task down into smaller goals. They can plan a timeline for the project, including script writing, rehearsing, revising, and recording.

Hold a ‘Listening lesson’ to finish the week. Play all the podcasts to the class. Hold a class vote on the best podcast.

Energy in action (class/individual)

Arrange a visit to a location which produces or uses energy, such as a windfarm or geothermal site (producers) or shopping centre (user). This can be a local in-person visit, or a virtual visit online.

Before the visit, provide information about the location to the class. Tell children to prepare questions about:

the way energy is produced or used at that location,

the benefits and challenges of the energy sources,

future energy plans for the site.

Provide clipboards and cameras. During the visit, encourage children to take photographs (if allowed) and make notes.

Ask children to write an illustrated report about their visit. Encourage them to include sections in their report talking about the future and making predictions.

Invite children to present their reports to the class.
