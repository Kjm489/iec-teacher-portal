Observable success criteria: Learners can complete text-based answer with a quoted or paraphrased detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce an evidence-based reading response.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Read out the following statements to the class. Ask children to move to one side of the room if the answer is TRUE, and to the other side of the room if the answer is FALSE. You may wish to add one or two statements connected to your local context, such as a fact about energy use in your school or city.

Oxford introductory task: Solar energy is produced 24 hours a day. (F)

Oxford introductory task: Geothermal energy comes from hot rocks inside the Earth. (T)

Oxford introductory task: Tidal energy is collected in the world’s oceans. (T)

Oxford introductory task: If energy is ‘renewable’ it means that it will run out one day. (F)

Oxford introductory task: Renewable energy causes less pollution than non-renewable energy. (T)

Oxford introductory task: Ask: Where do you get your energy from? Is it renewable? (From food – plants and animals make our food using the sun’s energy, so it is renewable.)

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children to imagine that the class has been given a very large sum of money to ‘invest in’ (spend on) clean technology. Remind them that today they will present information about their energy source and how it can be used in the future. Ask children to consider everything they are about to hear from others in the class.

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask: Which form of energy do you think we should invest in? We can divide the money between up to three different energy sources. Tell children they can use percentages for this task.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Give children five minutes to review their presentation notes. Prompt them to prepare the main points they want to say, to help their energy source receive the investment.

Oxford task — guided practice: If time allows, invite all children to give their presentation to the class. Alternatively, divide children into small groups of five to six. They should each present to their group. Ensure that within each group, children are presenting a range of different energy sources. Allow enough time at the end of the lesson for the following discussion points.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to make a final decision on the investment based on what they have heard. Remind them that they can divide the money up to three ways (such as 70% / 20% / 10%). Allow children time to explain why they have made these choices to a partner.

Oxford task — independent application: Remind children that different perspectives on this problem are possible. Ask: What different ideas did you have from your partner? Why do you think you have different ideas?

Oxford task — independent application: Tell children that these decisions are ones that governments, companies, and investors have to make in real life. Ask: Is it difficult to make these decisions? Why?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, pair them with another more confident child who has researched the same energy source. Allow them to present as a pair.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children by suggesting that they use a highlighter pen to highlight main points from their research, to help them prepare the points they wish to say.

Oxford additional tasks: Make a poster about saving energy at home or school. You may choose to use Bright Ideas AB 6 p.68 exs.3–4 for reference.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have presented information about a clean energy source. They have made decisions about which energy sources to invest in for the future.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
