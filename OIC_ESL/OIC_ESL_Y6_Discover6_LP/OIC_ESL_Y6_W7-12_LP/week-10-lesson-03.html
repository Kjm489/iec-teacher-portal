Lesson focus: Making predictions for future technology

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: Making predictions for future technology

Oxford outcomes: 6.2b, 6.2f, 6.3a

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Search online for ‘basic robot dance’, ‘robot dance video’, or ‘Boston Dynamics robot family dance’ to find a suitable warm-up dance video. Encourage children to copy the dance moves.

Oxford introductory task: Search online for ‘flashmob robot dance Guinness World Record video’. Play the video. Ask: What kinds of technology are mentioned? (such as robots, mobile phones, drones)

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Revise the future simple tense using your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.185 or Bright Ideas CB 6 p.65. Focus on using it for making predictions.

Oxford task — teacher models: I’ll/I will work as a robotics engineer.

Oxford task — teacher models: People won’t use CD players anymore.

Oxford task — teacher models: Will smartphones still be popular in the year 2050?

Oxford task — teacher models: How will microchips be used in new objects?

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Read ex.1 of Bright Ideas CB 6 p.65 or reading text ‘Gina and Roberto’ to introduce the future simple in context. You may wish to invite two volunteers to read the parts of the children in the chat, or ask children to read it themselves in pairs.

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite children to say what types of technology company they know. Write names and types of company on the board (such as medical, entertainment, travel, manufacturing).

Oxford task — guided practice: Divide children into groups of four. Tell them they are now in research teams for a technology company. Their job is to imagine how a product can be improved.

Oxford task — guided practice: Write on the board: ‘Technology will get smaller, better, and faster.’

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask: What product do you want to get smaller, better, or faster? Invite children to share their answer with their team. As a team, they then choose one product to improve.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Teams should work together to think about what will happen to this product in the future. Use the questions ‘When?’, ‘Why?’, ‘How?’, and ‘Who?’ to explore what will happen.

Oxford task — independent application: Before they start, remind children that they will have different ideas. Ask them to suggest ways to resolve conflict more effectively. Provide some ideas (practise listening and empathy, look for things in common, give everyone a turn to speak, use voting).

Oxford task — independent application: Observe groups as they work. Help any teams that are finding the task difficult.

Oxford task — independent application: Invite groups to present their ideas to the class. Hold a class vote for the best ideas.

Oxford task — independent application: Evaluate the team task. Ask: Did you have any conflicts? How did you resolve them? What would you do differently next time?

Oxford task — independent application: Finish the lesson by repeating the robot dance activity.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create groups of mixed ability for the team task.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to participate by asking for these children’s views on the work so far when you observe the groups.

Oxford differentiation: Additional information: Week 10, Lesson 3

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned how to use the future simple tense to make predictions. In teams, they have planned the future development of a technology product.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Future technology’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking and reading skills by applying their knowledge of the future simple tense to predictions. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
