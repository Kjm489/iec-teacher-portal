Oxford introductory task: Play a video which references future technology – such as robots – in an age-appropriate way. Suggested search terms: ‘Asimo robot video’, ‘Wall-E trailer’, ‘Boston Dynamics robot family dance’, or ‘child-friendly robot video’.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What do you think robots will do for us in the future? How will they help us in our homes, in our city, and in the world?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children to make notes on their whiteboard in two columns: ‘Technology I use’ and ‘Other technology’. Ask them to read exs.2–3 in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.64 or the reading text ‘Martha’s blog’ and add new vocabulary to their columns.

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask: What types of technology did you learn about in this text? What extra information did you learn about them?

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: two to three subsidiary points (extra information about the technology).

Oxford task — independent application: Observe children as they carry out the research task. Prompt them to use their reading strategies to identify the most important points (e.g. identifying key words, skimming and scanning, using pictures and headings to predict content).

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide pre-selected simple texts or web pages for the research task, or pair them with a more able child.

Martha’s blog

Martha is the manager of an electronics shop.

It’s important for electronics companies to know about new trends in technology, so that we can give customers what they want.

These days, smartphones and smartwatches are popular in many countries. They contain tiny microchips that have as much power as huge computers had in the 1960s and 1970s!

Another trend for the future is wearable technology. These are electronic devices that can be worn as clothes or accessories (like glasses). They can also be attached to or placed in the user’s body. Wearable devices are used for information, entertainment, sports, or medical reasons.

My favourite new technology is the drone. I use my drone to make films of the mountains near my apartment. Context
This is the second lesson of the unit ‘Future technology’.
In this lesson, children will develop their speaking skills by talking about technology using new vocabulary. They will identify main and subsidiary points in an input text, before doing the same in an open research task. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
