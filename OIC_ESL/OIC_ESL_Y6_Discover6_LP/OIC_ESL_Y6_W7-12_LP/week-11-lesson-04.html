PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Give each child a different year (such as 1969, 1988, 2011, 2019, and so on).

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to stand up. They must put themselves in order by only saying their year. They cannot show other children their year, or say anything else.

Oxford introductory task: Check the correct order by asking children to say their year out loud. Invite children in the wrong place to find the correct place.

Oxford introductory task: Tell children that today they will learn a way of finding out about past years: time capsules.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Bright Ideas can follow the activities in CB 6 p.72. These introduce the concept of time capsules, with a focus on listening for information.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Oxford Discover can pre-record an alternative listening exercise which introduces the topic of time capsules, their potential contents, and purpose. Perhaps record a colleague talking about what they would put in a time capsule. Alternatively, give an oral presentation to children, demonstrating putting your chosen items in a shoe box. Try to cover the points in the bullet points below, then ask children to note down what they hear.

Oxford task — teacher models: What a time capsule is

Oxford task — teacher models: Who wants to make one

Oxford task — teacher models: What they are putting in their time capsule

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Why it’s important to choose items carefully

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to take a few minutes to complete a mind map of things they would put in a time capsule. Suggest categories such as News, Culture, and Technology.

Oxford task — guided practice: At the mid-point of the activity, ask children to share items they have added so far.

Oxford task — guided practice: At the end of the mind map activity, tell children the class time capsule can contain five items. Divide the class into pairs. Ask children to discuss which five items from their mind map they would add to the time capsule and why.

Oxford task — guided practice: Prompt children to use vocabulary to offer suggestions to their partner, such as ‘We could ...’ / ‘What if we ...?’ / ‘How about we ...?’ / ‘Shouldn't we ...?’.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Invite volunteers to share their answers with the class. Encourage deeper thinking from children by asking questions, such as:

Oxford task — independent application: What will people in the future think of [this item]?

Oxford task — independent application: What are the main points you want people to know about [this important event]?

Oxford task — independent application: Is it better to include a newspaper or an object that represents [this local news event]?

Oxford task — independent application: Is your choice personal to you?

Oxford task — independent application: Do you think children in another part of the world might choose differently? Why?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a transcript of the audio to follow while they listen. Alternatively, alter the speed of your presentation to suit the ability of your class.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to write their responses to the above discussion questions.

Oxford additional tasks: Ask older members of your family about items from their past that are no longer in use.

Oxford additional tasks: Create a personal time capsule in a shoe box.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened to and understood a listening text or oral presentation about time capsules. They have planned and discussed items that they would put in a class time capsule.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Context
This is the ninth lesson of the unit ‘Future technology’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by using a listening text or oral presentation as the main input for an unfamiliar topic. They will also have the opportunity to be creative and share ideas with their class. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
