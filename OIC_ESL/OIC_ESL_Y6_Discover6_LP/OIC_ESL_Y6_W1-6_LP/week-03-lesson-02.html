PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Bridges and tunnels’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford introductory task: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford introductory task: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Children choose the best design and write instructions for it.

Oxford task — independent application: They then display their designs to the class and tell them how to build it.

Oxford task — independent application: Before beginning the project, children consider how they will divide the research, design, testing, and reporting phases to create a project timeline.

Oxford task — independent application: If possible, arrange for a local civil engineer, university professor, or architect to view the children’s projects at the end of the week and to talk about their work in civil engineering.

Oxford task — independent application: Bridge and tunnel design: past, present, and future (individual or group)

Oxford task — independent application: As an individual or in small groups, children pick a historic bridge or tunnel to research, and then a contemporary bridge or tunnel. You may wish to provide children with a shortlist of suitable locations.

Oxford task — independent application: Children then imagine their own bridge or tunnel to be built in the year 2100 and create instructions for its construction. This can be one of the main bridge types or a completely new construction. Invite them to use their research of historic and contemporary bridges for inspiration. They may wish, for example, to design a future footbridge if they have already researched footbridges.

Oxford task — independent application: Children create a poster or short booklet comparing the past, present, and future bridges or tunnels. They practise future tenses and connectors for their poster’s text. Children also recall and produce the vocabulary learned in this unit, such as type of bridge, transport, uses, and building materials.

Oxford task — independent application: Display the posters or booklets in the classroom or share with others in school.

Oxford task — independent application: During this activity, remind children of the ‘fire together, wire together’ concept. By recalling and producing what they have learned about bridges and tunnels, they are helping to build their long-term memory of this learning.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support, or those with less confidence, to provide them with the confidence and tools to plan their work and make a confident start. This guidance could be provided in a small group setting. Check on these children more frequently as they work. Ensure they have vocabulary banks and relevant sentence starters to hand. More confident children can be given more independence to manage their work.

Oxford differentiation: More able children can be given additional tasks which contribute to the overall objectives of the week and require them to think about the learning process. For example, they could be asked to design and conduct a class survey, designing questions (with support if needed) which assess children's learning experience in this unit. They can then collate the reponses in a short report, using diagrams or charts.

Oxford additional tasks: Children can complete the review section in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.20–21. They can also complete any activities on pp.2–19 which have not already been completed.

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
