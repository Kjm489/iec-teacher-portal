Lesson focus: Persuading someone

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to take a breath and close their eyes. Congratulate them on finishing the first unit of Year 6. Ask them to reflect on everything they have learned.

Oxford introductory task: Tell children to open their eyes, stretch, and get ready to work.

Oxford introductory task: Explain that in this final lesson of the unit, they will be writing persuasive letters on an environmental issue.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Spend ten minutes teaching how to write a persuasive letter.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Oxford Discover can use Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.24–25 exs.A–C.

Oxford task — teacher models: Ask children to plan and write their letter using Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.18–19.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Bright Ideas can follow these steps:

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Discuss structuring the letter using an opening salutation, the purpose for writing, arguments for and against, the action or change the writer wants to achieve, and a closing signature.

Oxford task — guided practice: Provide a persuasive letter using this structure for children to use as a model. Write a simple letter on an environmental issue, or search for ‘persuasive letter example ESL’ to find general examples.

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to plan and write their letter on paper. Remind them to think carefully about purpose, audience, and why their arguments are important.

Oxford task — guided practice: Encourage children to take a short break after planning and writing each section of their letter. Children may stand up and stretch or shake out their body before continuing their work.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Give children five minutes to check their work with a partner. Check if they have used connectors and make suggestions about places to add them.

Oxford task — independent application: Tell children to write the final draft of their letter. Give them ten minutes for this task.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to share examples of persuasive language that they are proud of. Encourage children to learn from each other.

Oxford task — independent application: Discuss types of language that can be used to persuade others (such as emotion, facts, humour, exaggeration, rhetorical questions).

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, let them work with an adult in a small group to plan the letter, and to support them in the writing phase. Consider providing a simple template to be completed.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to adapt their letter to two different audiences (such as a politician and their neighbours).

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by asking them to write their letter about a real-life local issue.

Oxford additional tasks: Swap letters with a partner. Write a response to the letter received.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written a persuasive letter on an environmental issue. They have begun to think about what types of language are useful for persuading others.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
