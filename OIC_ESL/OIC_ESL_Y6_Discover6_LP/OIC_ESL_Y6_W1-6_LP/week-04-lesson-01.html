Lesson focus: What is the Earth made of?

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate note of a heard detail and an inference, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a listening note plus supported inference.

Evidence to collect: Collect a listening note plus supported inference; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: What is the Earth made of?

Oxford outcomes: 6.1a, 6.1d, 6.2f

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: If possible, begin the lesson outside. The activity may also take place inside.

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to close their eyes and think about their connection to the Earth through their feet. If appropriate in your culture and setting, ask children to remove their shoes.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What is under our feet? Encourage children to think about what might be far under the Earth’s surface, not just on the top layer.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: Do you think children in other countries have the same things under their feet?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Show a video which presents simple information about the structure of the Earth and its four layers. Use the video from Oxford Discover SB 6 p.27 or search online for ‘Structure Of The Earth The Dr. Binocs Show’ or ‘Structure of the Earth kids video’.

Oxford task — teacher models: Children watch and listen, noting new or difficult words on their whiteboards.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach and discuss vocabulary such as ‘crust’, ‘inner core’, ‘layer’, ‘mantle’, ‘outer core’, and ‘plate’. Teachers using Oxford Discover may also wish to use the exercises in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.28 and spend less time on the following activity.

Oxford task — teacher models: Do a modelling activity. Children work individually or in pairs.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Give each child (or pair) pieces of modelling clay in at least four colours. Suggested colours: brown, orange, yellow, and grey. If available, also include green and blue.

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite children to make a model of the Earth using grey for the inner core, yellow for the outer core, orange for the mantle, and brown for the crust. Green and blue (if used) can be used to represent land and ocean. Children can choose to make a flat or spherical model.

Oxford task — guided practice: As children work, question them using new vocabulary, such as Which layer are you going to make first? Is the mantle thicker than the inner core?

Oxford task — guided practice: Children who have chosen the spherical model carefully cut their model with a modelling knife, to create a cut-away model showing the four layers of the Earth.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: All children label the layers using small flags made from stickers, which are secured by small wooden sticks and pushed into the clay.

Oxford task — independent application: Children tell each other about their models.

Oxford task — independent application: Reflect as a class. Invite children to explain which type of model they chose and why.

Oxford task — independent application: Discuss the advantages or disadvantages of flat or spherical models. Hold a class vote on the best methodology.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: For children who cannot use modelling clay, or if it is unavailable, draw or paint a large, colourful poster of the Earth’s layers.

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them a visual reminder of the layers and/or pre-made labelling flags to help them to build their model.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to choose one of the Earth’s layers and find out more about it.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.22–23.

Oxford additional tasks: Draw and label a diagram of the Earth.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a paragraph about the Earth’s layers using the vocabulary learned in the lesson.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have begun to think about the structure of the Earth and words used to describe it. They have made a model of the Earth’s layers.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Inside the Earth
Week 4, Lesson 1: What is the Earth made of?
Learning outcomes: 6.1a, 6.1d, 6.2f

Context
This is the first lesson of the unit ‘Inside the Earth’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening and speaking skills by discussing what they know about the unit topic and learning new vocabulary linked to it. They will also have an opportunity to practise model-making skills.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
