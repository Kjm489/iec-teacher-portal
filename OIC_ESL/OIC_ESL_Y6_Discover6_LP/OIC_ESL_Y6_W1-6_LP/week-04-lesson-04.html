Oxford introductory task: Invite children to close their eyes and listen to some quiet, relaxing music. Search online for ‘relaxing classical music’. Ideas: ‘Venus’ from The Planets by Gustav Holst or ‘Air on a G String’ by J.S. Bach. While they listen, ask these questions. Pause between each.

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Oxford Discover should follow the listening activity (A/B) in SB 6 p.34.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Bright Ideas should read the text about Tim Peake in CB 6 p.85 to the class. Ask questions to check comprehension of key facts, dates, and numbers.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach the use of parentheses to add extra information to a sentence. Use your own subject knowledge and the Writing Study in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.35.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to write sentences on their whiteboards about Lake Vostok (Oxford Discover) or Tim Peake (Bright Ideas) using parentheses.

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite children to share their sentences with a partner. Remind children to use polite expressions to correct their partner if they see any mistakes.

Oxford task — guided practice: Invite children to share their partner’s best sentence with the class.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Additional task(s)

Oxford task — independent application: Complete the Word Study (common homonyms) in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.35.

Oxford task — independent application: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.28–29 or Bright Ideas AB 6 pp.84–85.

Oxford task — independent application: Research an isolated area of the world which is unexplored. Find out number-related facts. Write sentences about it using parentheses.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a copy of the transcript for them to read as they listen to the text.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write a paragraph about a place they would like to explore themselves. Encourage them to write about the reasons behind their choice.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have listened for large numbers in a text related to unexplored places on Earth or in space. They have learned about parentheses and used them to include extra information in sentences.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Inside the Earth
Week 4, Lesson 4: Unexplored places 
Learning outcomes: 6.1d, 6.1e, 6.4d

Context
This is the fourth lesson of the unit ‘Inside the Earth’.
In this lesson, children will develop their listening skills by focusing on listening for large numbers in a topic-related text. They will link this to their writing skills by learning how to use parentheses to include extra information in sentences.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
