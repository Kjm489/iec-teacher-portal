PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Inside the Earth’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Oxford introductory task: Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Teachers should support lower-attaining children.

Oxford introductory task: Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Our Big Earth Television Show (groups)

Oxford task — independent application: In groups of four to five children, plan and record a television show.

Oxford task — independent application: The show should cover any items of children’s choice from the ‘Inside the Earth’ topic. Encourage children to think about local and global issues.

Oxford task — independent application: Choose different types of presentation to include in the show, such as a news report, a quiz, an interview, a set of instructions, or an opinion piece. The total running time should be around ten minutes.

Oxford task — independent application: Allocate different sections of the show to different members of the group to research and prepare. Allocate roles such as director, presenter, camera person, and sound.

Oxford task — independent application: Plan any props, costumes, locations, or other items which might be needed.

Oxford task — independent application: Choose a title for the programme and record a theme song.

Oxford task — independent application: Write scripts for each section of the show. Ensure the sections are connected together well.

Oxford task — independent application: Rehearse. Make any changes that are needed to the scripts.

Oxford task — independent application: Observe the groups as they work. If there are any problems in a group, remind children that they all rely on each other. If one person does not complete their task, the show will not happen.

Oxford task — independent application: Record the show.

Oxford task — independent application: Hold a ‘TV watching lesson’ to finish the week. Play all the television shows to the class. It might be fun to supply appropriate snacks and comfy cushions!

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: Give more guidance initially to children requiring extra support, or those with less confidence, to provide them with the confidence and tools to plan their work and make a confident start. This guidance could be provided in a small group setting. Check on these children more frequently as they work. Ensure they have vocabulary banks and relevant sentence starters to hand. More confident children can be given more independence to manage their work.

Oxford differentiation: More able children can be given additional tasks which contribute to the overall objectives of the week and require them to think about the learning process. For example, they could be asked to design and conduct a class survey, designing questions (with support if needed) which assess children's learning experience in this unit. They can then collate the reponses in a short report, using diagrams or charts.

Oxford additional tasks: Children can complete the review section in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.40–41. They can also complete any activities from Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.22–39 which have not already been completed.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have identified the core English language skills that they would like to improve or consolidate, and have done so over the week's activities. They have participated in an extended activity which has helped to develop and reinforce these skills, while firmly placing them in a real-world context.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
