PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a warm-up game about predictions. Play a fun and entertaining video which asks children to predict what happens next. Search online for ‘what happens next video’. You can also search for ‘Mr Bean clips’, ‘famous football penalties’, or any other suitable videos which can be paused to allow children to make predictions.

Oxford introductory task: Explain that this lesson will use prediction skills for reading. Children have warmed up their brains to use this skill by asking ‘What happens next?’.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Oxford Discover should:

Oxford task — teacher models: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.37–40.

Oxford task — teacher models: Invite children to contribute the ways they think Romans were affected by living close to Vesuvius. Write a list on the board.

Oxford task — teacher models: Then use the photos in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.27 (cave) and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.31 (mountains) or other photos such as deserts, rainforests, and snowy environments to discuss how humans might need to adapt to explore other difficult environments.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Add new columns to the list as you discuss.

Oxford task — guided practice: Teachers using Bright Ideas should:

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: After reading, invite children to contribute ways humans adapt to the rainforest (such as boiling water, camouflage, mosquito spray). Write a list on the board.

Oxford task — independent application: Then use the photos in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.42 (snow and volcano) or similar photos to discuss how humans might need to adapt to explore other difficult environments. Ask: How are these different to the rainforest?

Oxford task — independent application: Add new columns to the list as you discuss.

Oxford task — independent application: During the class discussion, praise children for their effort in working on the problem.

Oxford task — independent application: Remind children that they talked about making risky journeys in Week 1. Ask them to explain how the ways people act in difficult environments increase or reduce the risk they are taking. Use the notes on the board to guide the discussion.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, place them in a small group with an adult to help them understand and analyse the text.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to write an information text on how humans adapt to difficult environments.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the listening activity in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.36 and the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.30–31.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Bright Ideas AB 6 p.44.

Oxford additional tasks: Research the exploration of cave systems. Find out how cave explorers adapt to this difficult environment.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have revised their prediction strategies and applied them to a text about volcanoes (Oxford Discover) or rainforests (Bright Ideas). They have begun to think about how humans adapt to challenging environments.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

YEAR 6 Inside the Earth
Week 5, Lesson 1: What can challenging environments teach us?
Learning outcomes: 6.3b, 6.3d, 6.3e

Context
This is the sixth lesson of the unit ‘Inside the Earth’.
In this lesson, children will develop their reading skills by revising strategies for prediction and applying them to a topic-based text. They will have the opportunity to contribute to a class discussion about exploring challenging environments. 
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
