Lesson focus: There might be life on Mars

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: There might be life on Mars

Oxford outcomes: 6.1a, 6.2b, 6.4d

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a video showing the excitement of human missions to Mars. Search for ‘Mars mission video’ or ‘Mars landing celebration’.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: How do you think the people in the video feel? How can you tell?

Oxford introductory task: Ask: How does this video make you feel? Did you notice anything happen to your body as you watched? (I smiled. My heart rate got quicker. I couldn’t stop moving.)

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Set out five chairs as a ‘Mars space shuttle’. Invite four volunteers to join you. Identify the driver, engineer, geologist, and doctor. Carry a clipboard and say you are the captain of a mission to Mars.

Oxford task — teacher models: Read the script below slowly and clearly. This can be attached to your clipboard.

Oxford task — teacher models: Teachers using Bright Ideas may prefer to read the story in Bright Ideas CB 6 pp.80–81 instead.

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children to write any modal verbs they hear on their whiteboard.

Oxford task — teacher models: It's time to get ready to fly to Mars. We must make sure everything works properly.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: [to engineer] Could you please check the engines? Thank you!

Oxford task — guided practice: [to doctor] I can see the boxes of food and medicines in the corner. You will find medicines and blankets in the biggest box.

Oxford task — guided practice: [to driver] Be careful – that big red button might send a message to other spaceships, asking for help! You can only press it if we are in danger on the flight.

Oxford task — guided practice: [to geologist] Are you ready to collect some exciting new rock types? There could be hundreds of new rocks on Mars!

Oxford task — guided practice: [to all] I've checked everything, so we must be ready! Can I close the door? Yes? Let's fly!

Oxford task — guided practice: Check answers. What are the common mistakes? Encourage children to reflect on this.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Spend ten minutes revising modals of ability, permission, request, obligation, possibility, and deduction using your subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.33 or Bright Ideas CB 6 p.68.

Oxford task — independent application: During the revision activity, remind children of the ‘fire together, wire together’ concept. They are helping to build their long-term memory of modal verbs.

Oxford task — independent application: Divide children into groups of five. Tell them to role-play being on the mission, in the same way as you did at the start of the activity or in the Bright Ideas story. Encourage them to practise modal verbs.

Oxford task — independent application: That must be the moon outside that window!

Oxford task — independent application: We might see some aliens!

Oxford task — independent application: Children complete the activities in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.33 or write sentences about their Mars mission using a variety of modal verbs. Encourage children to check their sentences.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able or less confident children, provide them with a table of modal verbs to use in the role-play activity, as well as some suggestions for topics to talk about.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, they can explore the many Mars-related resources online. Try searching for ‘Fun Kids Mission to Mars’ or ‘NASA Mars for Kids’.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.26–27 or Bright Ideas AB 6 pp.67 and 115.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed modal verbs and their different uses in speech and in writing. They have role-played being on a Mars mission.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
