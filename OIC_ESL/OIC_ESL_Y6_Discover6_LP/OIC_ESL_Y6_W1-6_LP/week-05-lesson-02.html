Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a sorting game. Divide children into groups of three or four. Give each group a set of seven cards. Print each event on a different piece of card (remove the dates first). Use other events of local, national, or global importance if preferred.

Oxford introductory task: Mount Vesuvius destroyed Pompei (AD 79).

Oxford introductory task: Ferdinand Magellan was the first person to go around the world by boat (1519).

Oxford introductory task: Gertrude Ederle was the first woman to swim the English Channel (1926).

Oxford introductory task: Sir Edmund Hillary and Sherpa Tenzing Norgay were the first people known to climb Mount Everest (1953).

Oxford introductory task: Neil Armstrong was the first person to walk on the moon (1969).

Oxford introductory task: Colin O’Brady crossed Antarctica without any support (2018).

Oxford introductory task: Victor Vescovo became the first person to dive to the deepest points of each of the world's five oceans (2019).

Oxford introductory task: Ask each group to agree on how to arrange the events in order from oldest to newest.

Oxford introductory task: Check answers.

Oxford introductory task: Explain that this lesson will teach the past perfect tense. This helps us to talk about events in the past, in the right order.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach the past perfect tense. Teach the affirmative, negative, and questions. Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.41.

Oxford task — teacher models: The bus had just left when we arrived at the bus stop.

Oxford task — teacher models: (First the bus left. Then we arrived at the bus stop.)

Oxford task — teacher models: Before she took the exam, Anita hadn’t studied hard.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: (First Anita didn’t study. Then she took the exam.)

Oxford task — guided practice: Had the volcano erupted by the time the rescue boats arrived?

Oxford task — guided practice: (Did the volcano erupt before or after the boats arrived?)

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask children to use the story from Lesson 1 (Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.38–39 or Bright Ideas CB 6 pp.46–47).

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: In pairs, encourage children to say sentences using the past perfect tense to describe the sequence of events in that story.

Oxford task — independent application: After the sailors had lifted Vulcan out of the boat, they laid him in the cabin.

Oxford task — independent application: Before they boiled the water, they had made a fire.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to use polite expressions to praise or correct their partner. Pairs should write their sentences on their whiteboards.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to share a good sentence that their partner created with the class.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, give them two halves of sentences to match together (using past perfect and simple past).

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to research a famous explorer and write a paragraph about their life using the past perfect tense.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the activities in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.34–35.

Oxford additional tasks: Use the dialogue in Bright Ideas CB 6 p.45. Invite children to rewrite it using past perfect tense.

Oxford additional tasks: Practise using the past perfect tense to sequence events in children’s own lives.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have learned the past perfect tense and used it to sequence events in the past.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
