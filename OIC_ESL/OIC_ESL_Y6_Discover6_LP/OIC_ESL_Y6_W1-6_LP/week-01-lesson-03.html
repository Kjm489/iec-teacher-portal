PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a warm-up game: ‘Bridges and tunnels’.

Oxford introductory task: Create the following three paths across the classroom using large sheets of white and coloured paper. Alternatively, ask children to copy the paths onto their whiteboards.

Oxford introductory task: Bridge

Oxford introductory task: Tunnel

Oxford introductory task: Cliff

Oxford introductory task: Explain to children that they must choose which path to take. If you complete the bridge, you win three points. If you complete the tunnel, you win two points. If you complete the cliff, you win one point. The first person to finish gets a bonus point.

Oxford introductory task: Use a random number generator or double-sided object to call out a number from 1–2.

Oxford introductory task: Children move forward (on the paper path or on their whiteboards) the correct number of spaces.

Oxford introductory task: If they land on a black space, their bridge or tunnel has collapsed and the game is over. Note: The bridge and tunnel are shorter and more rewarding, but have a higher risk of failure. Children who choose the cliff path are sure to finish, but less likely to win!

Oxford introductory task: The winner is the child who completes their path with the most points, once everyone has finished or is out.

Oxford introductory task: Discuss the game. Ask: What choice did you make and why? Do you prefer to take a risk or to be safe? What kinds of journey can be risky?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Read the story in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.9–11 or Bright Ideas CB 6 pp.90–91.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: On the left of the class whiteboard, write RISK and on the right, write REWARD. Invite children to put a phrase on the board under RISK or REWARD, using the information in the story.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Discuss the risks in the story and widen the discussion to the children’s own experiences. Ask: Were the characters in the story right to be scared? Can risk-taking be positive? How do you know when to take risks? What can you do to reduce the risk?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide small-group support for the story reading and discussion, led by another adult.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage more able children to broaden the discussion from travel and transport to other areas of daily life, such as starting a new school or learning a new sport.

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to contribute to the discussion by encouraging them to talk about good experiences, when they have successfully overcome risk.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to identify a risk in the school environment and suggest ways to reduce it.

Oxford additional tasks: Answer the questions in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.12 or Bright Ideas CB 6 p.92.

Oxford additional tasks: connect an isolated region to a more-populated region, or

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read and analysed a short story. They have thought about the concept of risk-taking, and begun to identify situations involving risk in their own lives.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
