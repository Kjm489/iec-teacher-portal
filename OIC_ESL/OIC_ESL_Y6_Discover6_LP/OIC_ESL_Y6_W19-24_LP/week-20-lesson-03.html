PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play a game. Children sit in a circle. Choose four children to stand outside the circle. Everyone else must close their eyes. The four chosen children move quietly around the outside of the circle and tap one child each on the shoulder, saying ‘It’s me!’ as they do. They may disguise their voice any way they wish. All children then open their eyes. Those who were tapped on the shoulder stand up and guess who tapped them. If they are correct, they swap places with the tapper for the next round. Play two or three rounds.

Oxford introductory task: Tell children that it is difficult when we don’t know who did something and have to work it out! See if they can remember what type of sentence we use when we know what action was done, but don’t know who is responsible (the passive).

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Teach the use of the passive voice in the past perfect tense. Remind children that we use the passive when we don’t know, or don’t want to say, who did the action. Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.123. For example:

Oxford task — teacher models: Active: Anne had written the diary secretly.

Oxford task — teacher models: Passive: The diary had been written secretly.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Divide children into groups of three or four. Encourage them to work together so that everyone feels their contribution is valued by the team.

Oxford task — guided practice: Explain that last year you had attended a wedding for two lovely people: Eva and Paul. But everything had gone wrong! And no-one had wanted to be named. Give each group the cut-up and mixed-up statements 1–6 and a–f from ‘Who did it?’. Ask them to be detectives. They must match up the sentences to see who was responsible for each action. They must then make an active sentence in the past perfect tense to ‘accuse’ that person. Encourage them to say the sentence, write it on their whiteboard, and check it with their group. Then check sentences as a class.

Oxford task — guided practice: e.g. It was Gordon. Gordon had burned the meal!

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Now tell children that, happily, some things had gone well at the wedding. Hand out statements A–F. Explain that the people didn’t want to take any credit for their actions because the wedding should be all about the happy couple. This time, groups put each sentence into the passive voice. Again, encourage children to say the sentence, write it on their whiteboard, and check it with their group. Then check as a class.

Oxford task — independent application: e.g. A delicious chocolate ice cream had been made.

Oxford task — independent application: If time allows, each group can then create their own sentences in the passive voice to show things that had gone well or badly at the wedding. Share as a class.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, remind them that their passive sentences should include the word ‘been’, while active sentences should not. Use individual word flashcards to show how the subject and verb parts move or are added/removed between active and passive.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, ask them to practise the question form for each example: e.g. Who had the meal been burnt by?

Oxford learning review: Children have worked in groups to practise the passive voice in the past perfect tense.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
