Lesson focus: The poetry of birds

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate note of a heard detail and an inference, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a listening note plus supported inference.

Evidence to collect: Collect a listening note plus supported inference; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: The poetry of birds

Oxford outcomes: 6.3b, 6.3d

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Give each child a bird name. Suggestions: flamingo, owl, penguin, peacock, duck, chicken, parrot, eagle (or other bird of prey).

Oxford introductory task: Tell children to mime the bird silently. Children must look or move around the room and find classmates who have been given the same bird.

Oxford introductory task: Extension: Each group must guess what the others’ birds are.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children that today they are going to analyse one or more poems about birds. Remind them that they also did this in Week 17. Elicit what they can remember. Repeat the question from Week 17, Lesson 4: Why is it important to use language effectively in a poem? (to communicate emotion or description, to create rhythm and rhyme so that it can be read aloud, to connect with your reader/listener)

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Tell children that today’s poetry is classic poetry with a higher level of difficulty, so they will need to think deeply to extend their understanding of language.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Work through the reading activities in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.133–136. If time is limited, focus on one to three poems from pp.133–135. You may wish to use the video in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.139 again. Play the first section about poets.

Oxford task — independent application: During the lesson, briefly introduce examples of alliteration, metaphor, and similes. Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.139 and 147.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able or less confident children, consider providing a translation in their own language.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more confident children by seeing if they can understand the gist of the poem(s) independently before the class reading.

Oxford differentiation: As an extension task for more able children, highlight the date(s) when the poetry was written. Ask: Do you think these poems are still relevant today? Why / Why not?

Oxford additional tasks: Read more poems about birds.

Oxford additional tasks: Write a short poem about birds. Use descriptions that the reader can visualize.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have read (a) famous poem(s) about birds. They have visualized the poem(s) using the descriptions.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
