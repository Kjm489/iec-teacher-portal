6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability groups for the newspaper project. For the ‘Birds in the wild’ project, provide language support from an adult to a small group of children during the visit. Consider providing a template for the leaflet activity.

Oxford differentiation: More able children can be asked to stretch themselves by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar in their work. At the end of the writing tasks, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures.

Independent reflection

Ask children to reflect on the English language skills they have used over the last couple of weeks, while working through the ‘Birds’ unit. Encourage them to think about the core communicative skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.

Children discuss in pairs when they used the skills. They can assess areas that need further work, what they are comfortable with, and where they feel they were stretched and challenged. Support lower-attaining children.

Children may wish to keep a log of their goals – no matter how big or small – which they can refer back to during future ‘Reflection and review’ weeks to track their progress and achievements.

Main activity

Explain that this week, children will participate in an extended activity. They will practise their English language skills in a practical and relevant way.

Choose one of the following activity ideas:

Birds in the wild (class/individual)

Children visit a bird sanctuary, zoo, wildlife park, or nature reserve.

In small groups, they plan questions in advance.

During the visit, they take notes and photograph the birds. Encourage them to choose one or two bird species to focus on.

After the visit, they write a leaflet about the birds they have observed. It can include text, photos, and drawings. Give children the freedom to design their leaflet as they wish. Provide examples of appealing non-fiction leaflets and books to inspire them. (Some leaflets might be available at the site of the visit.)

At the end of the week, children present their leaflet to the class.

A creative exhibition (small group)

Children respond to the topic creatively, taking inspiration from the work of poets, photographers, and artists they have seen during this unit.

In small groups, ask children to choose a picture or theme which can be related to birds through metaphor (e.g. freedom, beauty, intelligence, hope).

Each group decides how they would like to show their theme in an art exhibition. They can decide to work together on larger pieces, or work individually on pieces that complement each other.

Children can choose to use any medium available to them (paint, pencil, clay, recyclable material modelling, photography, music, etc.).

Each child should also write a short poem to go with their artwork.

Remind children to plan their own time. They should appoint a leader to ensure that tasks are allocated well and the project is completed on time.

At the end of the week, create a class exhibition. Each group has its own display area. Children can walk around and view each other’s work. They should talk about the theme that inspired them and how they turned it into art and poetry.

Write an edition of Nature News (small group)

In groups of four, plan and write an imaginary newspaper, Nature News. If this has been used as a project in previous review weeks, you may wish to keep the same groups or change them. Tell children that this issue will cover news about birds.

Ask children to research current news stories involving birds, looking at local and global news.

As a group, they should choose which stories to include in the newspaper and divide the work between them fairly.

They write their articles and opinion pieces for the newspaper. Prompt them to practise using the grammar learned in Weeks 22–23.

Encourage children to work together. Remind them of the importance of reliability and commitment in relationships with others.

Invite each group to share their newspaper with the class.

Discuss what children have learned about current news stories involving birds.

Differentiation

To support less able children, create mixed-ability groups for the newspaper project. For the ‘Birds in the wild’ project, provide language support from an adult to a small group of children during the visit. Consider providing a template for the leaflet activity.

More able children can be asked to stretch themselves by consciously incorporating new and difficult English words or grammar in their work. At the end of the writing tasks, they can evaluate their writing and think about ways they can improve it through, for example, the use of synonyms or more complex sentence structures.
