Lesson focus: Interviewing about past events

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate note of a heard detail and an inference, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a listening note plus supported inference.

Evidence to collect: Collect a listening note plus supported inference; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Write the following pairs of sentences on the board. Underline the similar words.

Oxford introductory task: Of course! Of coarse!

Oxford introductory task: I wander why you think that? I wonder why you think that?

Oxford introductory task: Do you have any good advice? Do you have any good advise?

Oxford introductory task: How did that event affect you? How did that event effect you?

Oxford introductory task: In pairs, ask children to note down the correct word for each phrase. Then check answers (course, wonder, advice, affect). Explain the meaning of the words with the alternative spelling (coarse, wander, advise, effect). Complete Oxford Discover SB 6 p.117 ex.D, as required.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Remind children that interviews are an important way to record personal history. Explain that today they will interview each other about their lives so far.

Oxford task — teacher models: Use your own subject knowledge and Oxford Discover SB 6 p.116 to explain how to use follow-up questions and common fillers to extend the conversation and to make the interviewee feel comfortable. Draw children’s attention to questions and fillers from the introductory activity that could be useful.

Oxford task — teacher models: Where did you …? How old were you when …? What happened next?

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Oh, really? I see! That’s interesting!

Oxford task — guided practice: With children’s input, create a mind map of topics that they might want to ask about (such as school, family, places they have lived, or favourite memories) on the board. Model an example question, with a filler and a follow-up question.

Oxford task — guided practice: Where were you born? I see. Where is that exactly?

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Give children 5–10 minutes to make notes on their whiteboard about their own lives.

Oxford task — independent application: Divide children into pairs. Give them 10 minutes for one child to interview the other. Then swap roles. Encourage them to try out follow-up questions and fillers.

Oxford task — independent application: Remind the class that curiosity is an important skill for an interviewer. Encourage them to listen carefully to their partner’s answers and to base their follow-up questions on them. Make sure each pair is supportive of each other in both asking and answering.

Oxford task — independent application: Encourage children to practise a range of follow-up questions and fillers. Remind them that making mistakes is part of learning. It is more important to keep the conversation flowing than to be 100% accurate. Mistakes can be corrected afterwards.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work together to create longer lists of basic fillers and follow-up questions which they can use, after the mind map activity.

Oxford differentiation: To challenge more able children, provide audiovisual recording equipment for the interview. Ask them to watch each interview and to identify strengths and areas for improvement in their work.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete Oxford Discover WB 6 p.108 exs.A–C, or provide more practice exercises on easily confused words.

Oxford additional tasks: Invite a colleague to visit the class to be interviewed by children.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have checked their understanding of common words with similar spellings. In pairs, they have conducted interviews about their lives and practised using follow-up questions and common fillers to extend the conversation.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
