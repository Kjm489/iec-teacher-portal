Lesson focus: Comparing different sources

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Play the slideshow of historical sources. For ideas, search online for ‘primary historical sources pictures’, and ‘secondary historical sources pictures’. Include one or more pictures related to your local community or country.

Oxford introductory task: Ask children to move to one side of the room if they think each item or document shown is a primary source, and to the other if it is a secondary source.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Do the reading activity in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.112–114 to examine and compare primary and secondary sources.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, work in a small group with adult support. Work through each section of the text, explaining new vocabulary and discussing any difficult concepts. You may wish to select a shorter section of the texts for children to compare (such as reaching the top of the mountain).

Oxford differentiation: Encourage less confident children to use context to help them work out the meaning of any unknown words in the text. Provide dictionaries for support.

Oxford additional tasks: Write an account of the events in the Oxford Discover text. Use the sources available to support children’s writing with historical facts.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have compared primary and secondary sources about the same historical period or event.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Original lesson-stage directions (reference)

Stage 1: 1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min Original introductory activity: Play the slideshow of historical sources. For ideas, search online for ‘primary historical sources pictures’, and ‘secondary historical sources pictures’. Include one or more pictures related to your local community or country. Ask children to move to one side of the room if they think each item or document shown is a primary source, and to the other if it is a secondary source. Scaffolding: Visuals, gesture, word bank and think–pair–share before whole-class responses. ICQs: “Are we thinking first?” “Does everyone share an idea?” CCQs: “What do you already know about comparing different sources?”

Stage 2: 2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min Teacher model grounded in the original activity: Do the reading activity in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.112–114 to examine and compare primary and secondary sources. Additional task Write an account of the events in the Oxford Discover text. Use the sources available to support children’s writing with historical facts. Demonstrate one complete example aloud, highlight useful language, show a visual or text example, and think aloud about how to check accuracy. CCQs: “What did I do first?” “Which detail proves my answer?”

Stage 3: 3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min Guided original task: Do the reading activity in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.112–114 to examine and compare primary and secondary sources. Additional task Write an account of the events in the Oxford Discover text. Use the sources available to support children’s writing with historical facts. Complete the first task together, prompt learners to explain their choices, then gradually remove sentence frames. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What evidence supports this answer?”

Stage 4: 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min In pairs, apply the language from Comparing different sources to the original reading, listening, picture, dialogue, investigation or writing task. Partners take alternating turns and must ask a follow-up question or justify a decision. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Is your response supported by the lesson example?”

Stage 5: 5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min Original additional/extension task: Do the reading activity in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.112–114 to examine and compare primary and secondary sources. Additional task Write an account of the events in the Oxford Discover text. Use the sources available to support children’s writing with historical facts. Students produce an original written, spoken or visual response; assess against the stated Year 6 outcomes.

Stage 6: 6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Emerging: Provide pictures, keywords and an oral sentence frame. Developing: Complete a full response with one supporting detail. Extending: Add a reason, comparison, evidence or alternative explanation. Original differentiation guidance: To support less able children, work in a small group with adult support. Work through each section of the text, explaining new vocabulary and discussing any difficult concepts. You may wish to select a shorter section of the texts for children to compare (such as reaching the top of the mountain). Encourage less confident children to use context to help them work out the meaning of any unknown words in the text. Provide dictionaries for support.

Stage 7: 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Original learning review: Children have compared primary and secondary sources about the same historical period or event. Exit ticket: Give one accurate example, explanation or application of comparing different sources . Record I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet.

Stage 8: Evidence & Next Steps Evidence: Collect a response sample and note common misconceptions. Next steps: Reteach key language, offer targeted practice, and prepare the following Year 6 lesson.

Context
This is the third lesson of the unit ‘Recording history’.
In this lesson, children will practise their skills in comparing primary and secondary sources, building on their learning from Week 19, Lesson 2.
The core lesson should take around 45 minutes to an hour, with extension activities provided for extending to 90 minutes over two lessons. The timing is flexible to suit different timetables.
