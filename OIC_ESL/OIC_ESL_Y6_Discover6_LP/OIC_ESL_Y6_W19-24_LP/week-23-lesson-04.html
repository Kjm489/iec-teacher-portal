Lesson focus: Taking action on conservation

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete accurate target-language example used in context, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce two accurate sentences and an explanation.

Evidence to collect: Collect two accurate sentences and an explanation; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Introduce bird conservation as the lesson topic. Display photos of bird sanctuaries and conservation projects, both locally and from other countries.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: What do you remember about conservation? See if pupils can recall their learning from the unit ‘Effects of nature’.

Oxford introductory task: Review key vocabulary on the board such as ‘conservation’, ‘destroy’, ‘endangered’, ‘habitat’, ‘protect’, and ‘species’.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Review the use of the passive voice in the present perfect, past perfect, and future with the construction ‘by + person’. You can use Oxford Discover SB 6 p.145 as a support. Explain to children that they have to practise grammar over and over in order to master it.

Oxford task — teacher models: e.g. My bird poem has been / had been / will be read by the teacher.

Oxford task — teacher models: Play the ‘by birds’ circle game. Ask the first child to say a sentence in the passive: e.g. My lunch has been stolen. The whole circle then responds: by birds! Repeat. Encourage children to try different tenses.

Oxford task — teacher models: Divide children into pairs. Ask them to read the action plan in Oxford Discover SB 6 p.148. Alternatively, hand out the suggested ‘Brilliant birdwatching’ text.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: As a class, discuss who might do actions to birds and their habitats, either positive or negative. Write children’s ideas on the board. They might suggest types of people, animals, weather events, climate change, etc.

Oxford task — guided practice: Discuss what actions they might do. Children might suggest destroy habitats, protect nests, provide food, etc. More ideas can be found in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.135.

Oxford task — guided practice: Tell children to create sentences on their whiteboards about the conservation of birds using the passive in different tenses and ‘by’. For example:

Oxford task — guided practice: Before this year, birds had been seen by many visitors to the forest.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Recently, their habitats have been destroyed by farmers.

Oxford task — independent application: In the future, safe places to nest will be created by conservationists.

Oxford task — independent application: Tell children to swap their whiteboard with a partner. Prompt them to check mistakes and to give and receive feedback with respect. See if children can work together to correct any mistakes.

Oxford task — independent application: Ask children to reflect on how confident they are when using the passive voice in speaking and in writing. Explain any common concerns or mistakes.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide sentences cut into sections. Work together in a small group to match them up: e.g. Bird boxes / have been built / by volunteers.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to create three linked sentences, as shown in the example above.

Oxford additional tasks: Complete the exercises on the passive voice in Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.134–135.

Oxford additional tasks: Find out whether there are any projects to count bird populations in your city or country.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have reviewed the use of the passive voice. They have read an action plan for bird conservation and practised applying the passive voice to this topic.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
