PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Display two pairs of photos showing a local place (such as your school, or a city street) and a contrasting place from another country, both now and in the past. Aim for a clear difference between the two time periods. You may choose to use the photos in Oxford Discover SB 6 pp.108–109.

Oxford introductory task: Ask children if they recognize the locations. Elicit responses about how they have changed through time. Ask: What differences do you see? Why do you think it’s important to take photographs? What does it tell us about the history of these places?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Play the Wrap Up video in SB 6 p.117. Ask the following questions:

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Why was the moon landing filmed? (so that future generations could see this important event)

Oxford task — guided practice: What are two benefits of modern technology? (people around the world can quickly find out about events; people in the future can see our record of events)

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: What are two ways to share personal memories? (photographs, diaries)

Oxford task — independent application: Divide children into groups of three or four. Discuss the questions in SB 6 p.109 ex.C. Share answers as a class.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, provide a transcript of the listening exercise. Encourage them to use a highlighter to highlight important information.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children by encouraging them to see themselves and current events in the world as part of history. Ask: What will you remember about this time in your life? What current events will future generations learn about? Explain your reasoning.

Oxford additional tasks: Answer the three Big Question questions in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.102.

Oxford additional tasks: Recall the work from Week 11, Lesson 4 about time capsules. Discuss what time capsules are and how they contribute to recording history.

Oxford additional tasks: Find and discuss additional historical photographs of the school, local area, or city. Compare buildings, transport, technology, and clothes.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have discussed their existing knowledge of recording history. They have done a listening exercise related to methods of recording history.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
