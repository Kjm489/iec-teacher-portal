Observable success criteria: Learners can complete intelligible production and recognition of the target feature, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce an intelligible oral production and discrimination check.

Evidence to collect: Collect an intelligible oral production and discrimination check; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Do a short quiz with three very simple questions about birds, based on children’s learning and knowledge of local birds. The aim is to practise multiple choice questions.

Oxford introductory task: Example: Do birds imitate sounds from a) humans, b) nature, or c) humans and nature?

Oxford introductory task: Check answers. See if anyone got them all right.

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children that today they are going to create their own quiz questions for a Bird Quiz.

Oxford task — teacher models: Spend 5 minutes reviewing topics linked to birds from the unit. Write topic headings on the board, such as ‘Migration’, ‘Nest-building’, ‘Bird sounds’, ‘Poems about birds’.

Oxford task — teacher models: Divide children into groups of three to four. Give each group a different topic.

Oxford task — teacher models: Give the groups 10 to 15 minutes to create two or three questions for a multiple choice quiz. They may use all the resources from the unit as background information, as well as additional information from Oxford Discover WB 6 pp.124, 132, 138; or the free Oxford Owl eBook Animal Conflicts (series: Project X Origins). Search for this title in the Oxford Owl eBook Library (www.oxfordowl.co.uk/for-school/oxford-owl-ebook-collection).

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Put together the class quiz. Either:

Oxford task — guided practice: groups input their questions to an online quiz tool designed for the classroom, or

Oxford task — guided practice: groups provide the teacher with their questions on separate question cards.

Oxford task — guided practice: Hold the quiz, either using the online quiz tool or by reading out the quiz cards. Optionally, divide it into themed ‘rounds’.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Each team works together to answer all the questions except for their own. Remind children to discuss their answers with respect. If there is conflict in the group, they must find ways to decide their final answer calmly and fairly. For instance, by using known facts to support a choice, or by choosing the most popular answer.

Oxford task — independent application: Check answers. The team with the most points is the winner.

Oxford task — independent application: For a tie-break question, ask: How many bird species can you name in 20 seconds? Set a timer. Teams write them on their whiteboards. The team with the most birds wins.

Oxford task — independent application: Discuss: How confident did you feel speaking in your group? How well did your group work together? Could you have communicated better? How?

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, create mixed-ability groups. Encourage more able children to support them during the team tasks.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to include a question using the passive voice.

Oxford additional tasks: Make a ‘Did you know?’ poster about one or more of the facts learned in the quiz.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have used information about birds to design and hold a short quiz.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.
