Lesson focus: Writing an action plan

Observable success criteria: Learners can complete short organized response with a clear main idea and supporting detail, explain a decision using a relevant detail, and independently produce a revised written response.

Evidence to collect: Collect a revised written response; mark accurate / partially accurate / reteach and record one misconception.

Oxford title: Writing an action plan

Oxford outcomes: 6.4c, 6.4d

PERIOD 1 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 1 · 45 MINUTES — Warm-up, I DO, WE DO, Pair Practice

1. Warm-up & Activate Prior Knowledge · 8 min

Oxford introductory task: Search online for ‘charity event posters’. Display posters for four to five charity events (such as clean-up days, open days, or fundraising events) around the classroom. (It isn’t essential for these to be bird-themed.)

Oxford introductory task: Encourage children to move around the classroom to look at each of the posters. See if they can find answers to the questions: What? Why? Who? How? Where? When?

Oxford introductory task: Invite volunteers to share some of their findings with the class.

Oxford introductory task: Ask: Did you find it easy to find information? Was anything missing?

ICQ: What is the first action you will take?

2. Teacher Modeling — I DO · 12 min

Oxford task — teacher models: Tell children that today they are going to write their own action plan for bird conservation. Ask: What is an action plan?

Oxford task — teacher models: Review the action plans from Week 23, Lesson 4. You may also choose to spend more time reading the action plan in Oxford Discover WB 6 p.138.

Modeling move: Demonstrate one worked example, verbalizing the reasoning before learners try.

3. Guided Practice — WE DO · 15 min

Oxford task — guided practice: Ask: Why is it important to treat birds fairly? How can an action plan help?

Oxford task — guided practice: Encourage children to think about their own local area. Ask: What action can we take to support birds locally? How can we lead other people? Invite them to contribute to a list of ideas on the board. Tell them to choose one of these ideas to write about.

CCQ: Which example or detail from the task supports your answer?

4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min

Collaborative practice (IEC scaffold): In pairs, rehearse the task demonstrated in guided practice; compare responses and give one reason for each choice. This is an IEC teaching adaptation, not a separate Oxford activity.

ICQ: Will both partners contribute and listen?

PERIOD 2 — 45 MINUTES
PERIOD 2 · 45 MINUTES — YOU DO, Differentiation, Review

5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min

Oxford task — independent application: Children plan and write their action plan using Oxford Discover SB 6 p.149 and WB 6 p.139, or using the same layout and headings as in ‘Brilliant birdwatching’.

Oxford task — independent application: Prompt children to check that they have included all the relevant information.

Oxford task — independent application: Children then read their action plan to a partner or to the class.

Independent evidence: Learners complete an individual response demonstrating the original OIC learning outcomes.

6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min

Oxford differentiation: To support less able children, highlight useful sentence starters and phrases from the model action plan(s) which they can use in their own writing.

Oxford differentiation: Challenge more able children to provide facts and statistics to support their action plan.

Oxford additional tasks: Have a class vote on the action plans. If possible, put the winning plan (or a combination of similar plans) into practice.

Oxford additional tasks: Create posters to promote the chosen action plan in the school community.

7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min

Oxford learning review: Children have written an action plan to support bird conservation in their local area.

Assessment: Check an individual response against the original learning outcome; note achieved, developing, or needs reteaching.

Children plan and write their action plan using Oxford Discover SB 6 p.149 and WB 6 p.139, or using the same layout and headings as in ‘Brilliant birdwatching’. Prompt children to check that they have included all the relevant information. Children then read their action plan to a partner or to the class. Demonstrate one complete example aloud, highlight useful language, show a visual or text example, and think aloud about how to check accuracy. CCQs: “What did I do first?” “Which detail proves my answer?”

Prompt children to check that they have included all the relevant information. Children then read their action plan to a partner or to the class. Complete the first task together, prompt learners to explain their choices, then gradually remove sentence frames. ICQs: “Are we doing the first example together?” CCQs: “What evidence supports this answer?”

Stage 4: 4. Supported Pair/Group Practice · 10 min In pairs, apply the language from Writing an action plan to the original reading, listening, picture, dialogue, investigation or writing task. Partners take alternating turns and must ask a follow-up question or justify a decision. ICQs: “Who starts?” “When do you switch?” CCQs: “Is your response supported by the lesson example?”

Stage 5: 5. Independent Practice — YOU DO · 25 min Original additional/extension task: Have a class vote on the action plans. If possible, put the winning plan (or a combination of similar plans) into practice. Create posters to promote the chosen action plan in the school community. Students produce an original written, spoken or visual response; assess against the stated Year 6 outcomes.

Stage 6: 6. Extension & Differentiation · 10 min Emerging: Provide pictures, keywords and an oral sentence frame. Developing: Complete a full response with one supporting detail. Extending: Add a reason, comparison, evidence or alternative explanation. Original differentiation guidance: To support less able children, highlight useful sentence starters and phrases from the model action plan(s) which they can use in their own writing. Challenge more able children to provide facts and statistics to support their action plan.

Stage 7: 7. Review, Formative Assessment & Exit Ticket · 10 min Original learning review: Children have written an action plan to support bird conservation in their local area. Exit ticket: Give one accurate example, explanation or application of writing an action plan . Record I = independent, P = prompted, N = not yet.

Stage 8: Evidence & Next Steps Evidence: Collect a response sample and note common misconceptions. Next steps: Reteach key language, offer targeted practice, and prepare the following Year 6 lesson.
